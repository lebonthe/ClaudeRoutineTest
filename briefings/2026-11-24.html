ENEnglish version

Topic: three verbs that Chinese speakers often translate as 說 or 講 — hablar (talk, speak), decir (say, tell something) and contar (tell a story, recount; also count).

🎯 Learning Goal: talk with someone and about something (Hablé con Marcos, Hablamos de política), report what someone said (Me dijo que no puede venir), and tell what happened or tell a story (Te cuento lo que pasó, Cuéntame un chiste). The key test: hablar = the activity of talking; decir = the content that is said; contar = telling a whole story or sequence from start to end.

Vocabulary: hablar (regular: hablo, hablé) · hablar con / de / sobre (talk with / about) · decir (irregular: digo, dices, dice…; past dije, dijo, dijeron; participle dicho; command di → dime) · contar (o→ue: cuento, cuentas, cuenta, contamos; past conté; command cuenta → cuéntame) · contar con (to rely on: Puedes contar conmigo) · un chiste / una historia / un secreto (a joke / a story / a secret) · decir la verdad / mentiras (tell the truth / lies) · ¿Cómo se dice…? (How do you say…?) · ¿Qué me cuentas? (What's new?) · hablar por teléfono (talk on the phone).

Grammar Point: (1) hablar focuses on communication itself: Hablé con mi jefe, Habla tres idiomas. Languages normally take no article (Hablo español). Hablar cannot introduce a que-clause: ✗ Habló que viene → ✔ Dijo que viene. (2) decir focuses on content: decir algo a alguien, with the indirect-object pronoun in front: Le dije la verdad a mi madre, ¿Qué te dijo?; it is the verb for reported speech (Day 62): Me dijo que estaba cansado. It never combines with con: ✗ Dije con él. (3) contar means telling an account from beginning to end, or counting: Te cuento lo que pasó, Mi abuelo cuenta historias de la guerra, Cuenta hasta diez. The o→ue change happens only when the stem is stressed (cuento but contamos, Day 87). (4) Choosing, using Chinese as a guide: 我們聊了兩小時 → Hablamos dos horas (activity); 他說他累了 → Dijo que estaba cansado (content); 他講了他的旅行 → Nos contó su viaje (the account). With a secret both work — Me dijo / me contó un secreto — but contar sounds more like "shared, confided". (5) Pronouns with commands (Days 25, 45, 126): dime, dímelo, cuéntame, cuéntamelo, háblame de ti; negative: No se lo digas a nadie (le + lo → se lo).

Example Sentences:

  • ¿Hablaste con Marcos? ¿Qué te dijo? — Did you talk to Marcos? What did he tell you?
  • Me dijo que no puede venir el sábado. — He told me he can't come on Saturday.
  • ¡Cuéntamelo todo! — Tell me everything!
  • Es una historia larga. Te la cuento en el café. — It's a long story. I'll tell you at the café.
  • ¿Cómo se dice "scooter" en español? — How do you say "scooter" in Spanish?
  • No se lo digas a nadie. — Don't tell anyone.
  • Puedes contar conmigo. — You can count on me.

主題:三個中文常翻成「說」或「講」的動詞——hablar(講話、說語言)、decir(說出某個內容、告訴)、contar(講述故事或經過;也有「數數」的意思)。

🎯 學習目標:能說和某人講話、談某件事(Hablé con Marcos 我跟 Marcos 談過了、Hablamos de política 我們聊政治),能轉述別人說的內容(Me dijo que no puede venir 他跟我說他不能來),也能講發生了什麼事或講故事(Te cuento lo que pasó 我跟你講發生什麼事、Cuéntame un chiste 講個笑話給我聽)。判斷關鍵:hablar = 講話這個活動;decir = 說出的內容;contar = 把一整件事從頭講到尾。

單字:hablar(規則動詞:hablo、hablé)・hablar con / de / sobre(和……說話/談論……)・decir(不規則:digo, dices, dice……;過去式 dije, dijo, dijeron;過去分詞 dicho;命令 di → dime)・contar(o→ue:cuento, cuentas, cuenta, contamos;過去式 conté;命令 cuenta → cuéntame)・contar con(依靠:Puedes contar conmigo 你可以靠我)・un chiste / una historia / un secreto(笑話/故事/秘密)・decir la verdad / mentiras(說實話/說謊)・¿Cómo se dice…?(……怎麼說?)・¿Qué me cuentas?(最近怎樣?有什麼新鮮事?)・hablar por teléfono(講電話)。

文法重點:(1)hablar 重點在溝通這件事本身:Hablé con mi jefe(我跟老闆談過)、Habla tres idiomas(他會說三種語言)。說語言通常不加冠詞(Hablo español)。hablar 後面不能直接接 que 子句:✗ Habló que viene → ✔ Dijo que viene。(2)decir 重點在內容:decir algo a alguien,間接受詞代名詞放在前面:Le dije la verdad a mi madre(我跟媽媽說了實話)、¿Qué te dijo?(他跟你說什麼?);轉述句(第 62 天)也用它:Me dijo que estaba cansado(他跟我說他很累)。它不和 con 搭配:✗ Dije con él。(3)contar 是把一段經過從頭講到尾,或是數數:Te cuento lo que pasó(我跟你講發生了什麼)、Mi abuelo cuenta historias de la guerra(爺爺會講戰爭的故事)、Cuenta hasta diez(數到十)。o→ue 只在重音落在詞幹時發生(cuento,但 contamos 不變,第 87 天)。(4)怎麼選,用中文來對照:「我們聊了兩小時」→ Hablamos dos horas(活動);「他說他累了」→ Dijo que estaba cansado(內容);「他講了他的旅行」→ Nos contó su viaje(經過)。說秘密兩個都可以——Me dijo / me contó un secreto——但 contar 更有「透露、分享」的感覺。(5)命令句加代名詞(第 25、45、126 天):dime、dímelo、cuéntame、cuéntamelo、háblame de ti(跟我談談你自己);否定:No se lo digas a nadie(le + lo → se lo)。

Chapter 30 — 以道佐人主者 (Yǐ dào zuǒ rén zhǔ zhě) — "One Who Assists a Ruler with the Way"

Text (received Wang Bi edition, with variants noted):

以道佐人主者,不以兵強天下,其事好還。師之所處,荊棘生焉。大軍之後,必有凶年。善有果而已,不敢以取強。果而勿矜,果而勿伐,果而勿驕,果而不得已,果而勿強。物壯則老,是謂不道,不道早已。

Translation: "One who assists a ruler with the Way does not use weapons to dominate the world, for such deeds tend to come back. Where armies have camped, thorns and brambles grow. After great wars, bad harvest years are sure to follow. The skilled one achieves the result and stops there; he does not dare to use it to seize domination. Achieve the result but do not boast; achieve it but do not brag; achieve it but do not be arrogant; achieve it only because there was no other way; achieve it but do not force. When things reach their peak of strength they begin to age. This is called 'not the Way', and what is not the Way ends early."

Key words:

  • 佐人主 — "assist a ruler": the chapter is addressed to ministers and advisers, not to the ruler himself.
  • 其事好還 — "such deeds like to return": violence comes back to the one who uses it — revenge, resentment, counter-attack. This is one of the clearest statements of "what goes around comes around" in early Chinese thought.
  • 師之所處,荊棘生焉。大軍之後,必有凶年 — farmland is abandoned when men are taken for war, so fields return to thorns; armies eat the harvest and leave famine behind. Text note: 大軍之後,必有凶年 is absent from the Guodian bamboo slips and the Mawangdui silk manuscripts, and many scholars regard it as an early commentary that entered the main text. The idea is old even if the line may not be.
  • 果 — the key word, repeated six times. Wang Bi glosses 果猶濟也 — "果 means to accomplish, to bring something through". It is the result that ends the trouble, not victory as a display of power. Some editions read 善者果而已; Wang Bi has 善有果而已.
  • 勿矜、勿伐、勿驕 — three ways of showing off: being proud of oneself, claiming credit, looking down on others. Chapter 22 and Chapter 24 already warned that 自伐者無功,自矜者不長.
  • 果而不得已 — "achieve it because it could not be avoided". Here 不得已 has its normal meaning, "having no choice" — compare yesterday's Chapter 29, where the same words meant "will not succeed". Force is acceptable only as a last resort.
  • 物壯則老,是謂不道,不道早已 — this closing line appears again, word for word, at the end of Chapter 55. 早已 = "comes to an early end".

Background: the Daodejing was compiled during the Warring States period (5th–3rd century BCE), when states mobilised whole populations for war. Chapter 30 continues the political chapters: Chapter 29 said the world is a sacred vessel that cannot be worked on; Chapter 30 applies this to the most extreme way of "working on" the world — military force; Chapter 31 (兵者不祥之器) will go further and say that victory should be treated with funeral rites. The chapter is not pacifist in an absolute sense: it accepts force "when there is no other way", but it limits force to the result and forbids using the result to dominate. Commentators have compared this with Sunzi's 不戰而屈人之兵 ("subduing the enemy without fighting") — both value a quick, limited result over glory.

Philosophical implications:

  • Stop at the result. The test of 果 is: did you solve the problem, then stop? Going further — to humiliate, to expand, to prove superiority — turns a necessary act into the start of the next conflict (其事好還).
  • Hidden costs come later. Thorns and famine are delayed consequences. The chapter trains the reader to look past the moment of victory to the fields a year later.
  • Peak strength is the beginning of decline. 物壯則老 links politics to nature: anything pushed to its maximum begins to age. Softness and restraint (Chapter 76: 柔弱者生之徒) keep things alive longer.

Modern reflection: the line fits the past week in the markets uncomfortably well: aggressive bets that "dominate" a market tend to come back (其事好還), and the strongest stocks fall the hardest when they are at their peak (物壯則老). It also fits everyday work. When you win an argument in a code review or a meeting, 果而勿矜、果而勿伐、果而勿驕 is a good rule: get the fix merged, then stop. Don't add the extra comment that proves you were right. When a regulator or a government uses force to calm a market — like Taiwan's short-selling ban this week — the chapter's question is useful too: is this a limited measure that will be removed once it has done its job, or will it stay and create new problems later?

第 30 章 以道佐人主者(Yǐ dào zuǒ rén zhǔ zhě)——「用道輔佐君主的人」

原文(通行王弼本,並註明異文):

以道佐人主者,不以兵強天下,其事好還。師之所處,荊棘生焉。大軍之後,必有凶年。善有果而已,不敢以取強。果而勿矜,果而勿伐,果而勿驕,果而不得已,果而勿強。物壯則老,是謂不道,不道早已。

白話翻譯:「用道來輔佐君主的人,不靠武力在天下稱強,因為這種事很容易遭到回報。軍隊駐紮過的地方,會長滿荊棘。大戰之後,一定會有荒年。善於用兵的人只求達成結果就停止,不敢藉此逞強。達成了結果不要自負,不要誇功,不要驕傲;達成結果是出於不得已,達成結果不要逞強。事物強盛到極點就開始衰老,這叫做不合於道,不合於道就會早早結束。」

關鍵字詞:

  • 佐人主——「輔佐君主」:這一章是寫給大臣與謀士看的,不是寫給君主本人。
  • 其事好還——「這種事容易回報到自己身上」:暴力會回到使用者身上——報復、怨恨、反擊。這是先秦思想中「冤冤相報」最清楚的表述之一。
  • 師之所處,荊棘生焉。大軍之後,必有凶年——男人被徵去打仗,田地荒廢,長回荊棘;軍隊吃光收成,留下饑荒。版本註記:「大軍之後,必有凶年」不見於郭店楚簡與馬王堆帛書,很多學者認為它是早期注文混入正文。即使這一句可能是後加的,這個想法本身很古老。
  • 果——本章的關鍵字,重複了六次。王弼注:「果猶濟也」——「果」是完成、把事情辦成。重點是結束麻煩的「結果」,不是展示力量的「勝利」。有些版本作「善者果而已」;王弼本作「善有果而已」。
  • 勿矜、勿伐、勿驕——三種炫耀:自負、誇功、看不起別人。第 22 章與第 24 章已經警告過「自伐者無功,自矜者不長」。
  • 果而不得已——「達成結果是因為無法避免」。這裡的「不得已」就是一般的「沒有辦法、被迫」——可以和昨天第 29 章對照,同樣三個字在那裡是「達不到」的意思。武力只能作為最後手段。
  • 物壯則老,是謂不道,不道早已——這一句在第 55 章結尾原句重現。「早已」= 早早結束。

背景:道德經成書於戰國時代(西元前 5 至 3 世紀),當時各國動員全體人民打仗。第 30 章延續政治篇章:第 29 章說天下是不能刻意改造的神器;第 30 章把這個道理用在最極端的「改造天下」方式——武力上;第 31 章(「兵者不祥之器」)會更進一步,說打了勝仗要用喪禮來對待。本章並不是絕對的和平主義:它接受「不得已」時使用武力,但把武力限制在達成結果,並禁止藉結果去稱霸。注家常把它和孫子的「不戰而屈人之兵」相比——兩者都重視快速、有限的結果,而不是榮耀。

哲學意涵:

  • 達成結果就停。「果」的檢驗是:問題解決了嗎?解決了就停。再往前走——羞辱對方、擴張、證明自己更強——就會把必要的行動變成下一場衝突的開端(其事好還)。
  • 隱藏的代價會晚一點出現。荊棘與饑荒都是延遲出現的後果。本章訓練讀者把目光越過勝利的那一刻,看到一年後的田地。
  • 最強盛的時候就是衰退的開始。「物壯則老」把政治和自然連在一起:任何被推到極限的東西都會開始老化。柔軟與節制(第 76 章「柔弱者生之徒」)讓事物活得更久。

現代反思:這一章和上週的股市對照起來,準得讓人不太舒服:想「稱霸」市場的激進押注很容易反噬(其事好還),漲到最高點的強勢股跌得最重(物壯則老)。它也適用於日常工作。當你在 code review 或會議中贏了一場爭論,「果而勿矜、果而勿伐、果而勿驕」是很好的規則:把修正合併進去就好,然後停下來,不要再補一句證明自己是對的。當主管機關或政府用強力手段穩定市場——例如本週台灣的限空令——本章的問題也很有用:這是一個完成任務後就會撤除的有限措施,還是會一直留著,日後製造新問題?

Some Palauan politicians, such as Senate President Hokkons Baules, have argued for switching to Beijing, so the issue is debated inside Palau.

History

  • Early settlement: people reached the islands at least 3,000 years ago. Palauan society developed around matrilineal clans and village councils, with large meeting houses called bai.
  • Spain and Germany (a link for German readers): Spain claimed the Caroline Islands; when Germany challenged this in 1885, Pope Leo XIII arbitrated and confirmed Spanish sovereignty. After losing the 1898 war with the US, Spain sold the Carolines, including Palau, to Germany in 1899. The Germans mined phosphate on Angaur island.
  • Japanese period (1914–1944): Japan took the islands in 1914, and they became part of Japan's League of Nations South Seas Mandate. Koror was the headquarters of the South Seas Government (南洋廳) from 1922, and Japanese settlers came to outnumber Palauans. Many Palauan families still have Japanese names, and Palauan has many Japanese loanwords.
  • World War II: the Battle of Peleliu (15 September–27 November 1944) was one of the bloodiest island battles of the Pacific War, with very heavy losses on both sides.
  • US trusteeship and independence: after 1947 Palau was part of the UN Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, run by the US. Its 1981 constitution was the world's first "nuclear-free" constitution, which clashed with the US defence agreement; Palauans voted eight times before the Compact of Free Association was approved in 1993. Palau became independent on 1 October 1994 and joined the UN in December 1994.

Culture and environment

  • Bai and storyboards: traditional bai have high painted gables that tell legends. Carved wooden "storyboards", now a typical souvenir, developed in the 1930s, often credited to encouragement by the Japanese artist Hijikata Hisakatsu.
  • Stone money: the famous giant stone discs (rai) used as money on Yap were quarried in Palau's Rock Islands and carried across the sea by canoe.
  • Conservation leader: Palau created the world's first shark sanctuary in 2009; its National Marine Sanctuary, passed in 2015 and in force since 2020, closes most of its ocean area to commercial fishing; since 2017 visitors sign the "Palau Pledge" in their passport to behave responsibly; reef-harming sunscreens have been banned since 2020. The Rock Islands Southern Lagoon became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012, and Jellyfish Lake is famous for swimming among stingless jellyfish (its population collapsed after a 2016 drought and later recovered in part).

Current challenges

  • Climate change and sea-level rise threaten low islands, coral reefs and water supplies.
  • Great-power competition: Palau sits on the "second island chain". It hosts expanding US defence projects (including a long-range radar system) and faces Chinese pressure over its Taiwan ties, especially through tourism.
  • A small economy that depends on tourism and outside funding, with young people often moving to the US for work and study, which the Compact allows.

  • Name and variations: "Evil Kermit", also called "Me to Me", "Inner Me" or "Dark Kermit". A two-part caption format: "Me: [a sensible plan]" / "Me to me: [a tempting bad idea]".
  • Origin: the image comes from Muppets Most Wanted (2014, Disney), in which Kermit the Frog meets his evil look-alike, Constantine, "the world's most dangerous frog". In the still, Kermit faces a figure who looks exactly like him but wears a dark hooded cloak, like a Sith lord from Star Wars.
  • How it became popular: the still circulated as a reaction image after the film, but the "Me / Me to me" caption format spread widely on Twitter in late 2016 and was quickly copied on Reddit, Tumblr and Instagram. It worked because it needs no explanation: everyone knows the voice in your head that says "just one more episode".
  • Meaning and usage: it shows an inner conflict between self-control and temptation. The hooded Kermit is your worst impulse, speaking in your own voice. Typical examples: "Me: I should sleep early tonight. / Me to me: watch a 3-hour video about medieval castles." Or for developers: "Me: I'll write the tests first. / Me to me: push straight to production on Friday." The humour is self-mocking: the joke is on the poster, not on anyone else.
  • Why the image works: the two figures are the same character, so the conflict is clearly internal; the hood borrows the visual language of "the dark side", which turns small temptations (snacks, sleep, shopping) into a mock-epic battle between good and evil.
  • Variations and evolution: the roles are sometimes reversed (the hooded Kermit gives good advice); some versions add a third Kermit or other characters; the same "inner voice" idea later appears in formats like "my brain at 3 a.m." It belongs to the same family as the older "angel and devil on your shoulder" image, which was common in cartoons long before the internet.
  • Current status: its peak was 2016–2018. Today it is an evergreen, widely understood format rather than a trending meme; people still use it, especially on X and Reddit, but it is not new. It is distinct from "Kermit Sipping Tea / But that's none of my business" (introduced on an earlier day): that meme is about passive-aggressive commentary on others; Evil Kermit is about arguing with yourself.
  • Cultural note: the format translates easily, so Chinese-language users also make versions such as 「我:今天要早睡。/ 內心的我:再滑一下就好」. The idea maps closely onto the familiar Chinese phrase 天人交戰 (an inner battle between reason and desire).

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 7: in / on / at for time (Stage 2 begins).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese uses 在 (or nothing at all) for every time expression — 在早上、在星期一、在三點. English chooses between in, on and at depending on the size of the time period, and sometimes uses no preposition at all.

Unit 1 — 介系詞第 7 課:時間的 in / on / at(進入第二階段)

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:時間越大用 in,一天用 on,一個點用 at。中文幾乎所有時間都用「在」,甚至什麼都不加——「在早上」「星期一」「三點」。英文則要依照時間的大小,在 in、on、at 之間選一個,有時候還完全不能加介系詞。

2. 核心概念:把月曆一層一層放大

  • in = 一段可以「待在裡面」的長時間:in 2027、in February、in spring、in the 1990s、in the morning / afternoon / evening。
  • on = 一天、一個日期,就像月曆上的「一頁」:on Monday、on 10 October、on my birthday、on Lunar New Year's Eve、on Monday morning。
  • at = 時鐘上的一個點,或一個很短、有名稱的時刻:at 7:30、at noon、at midnight、at night、at lunchtime、at the moment。
  • 這和第 1 課的「地點」邏輯一樣:in = 在一個範圍裡面;on = 在一個平面上;at = 在一個點上。月份和年份像一個大房間(in);一天像月曆上的一頁紙(on);幾點鐘像時鐘上的一個點(at)。

特殊情況:

  • in the morning,但 on Monday morning——加上「星期一」,就變成月曆上的一頁,所以用 on。
  • at night(一般的晚上、夜裡),但 in the night(某一個特定夜裡發生的事:I woke up in the night.)。
  • at Christmas / at Chinese New Year(整個節日期間)vs. on Christmas Day / on Lunar New Year's Day(那一天)。
  • at the weekend(英式)vs. on the weekend(美式)——兩種都正確。
  • 不加介系詞:this / next / last / every / today / tomorrow / yesterday 前面不加:next week、this Friday、every Sunday、last year。

  1. It's in late January or February:月份(以及「一月下旬」這種一段時間)是大容器,用 in。如果說 on February 就錯了,因為二月不是一天。

Section 7 — Film & Performing Arts: Diaghilev's Ballets Russes (1909–1929)
第 7 段 — 電影與表演藝術:佳吉列夫的「俄羅斯芭蕾舞團」(Ballets Russes,1909–1929)

ENEnglish version

  • What it is: the Ballets Russes ("Russian Ballets") was a touring ballet company founded and run by the Russian impresario Sergei Diaghilev (1872–1929). It never performed in Russia; it was based in the West, mainly Paris and later Monte Carlo, and it existed only as long as Diaghilev lived — from its first Paris season in May 1909 (at the Théâtre du Châtelet; the name "Ballets Russes" was used for the permanent company from 1911) to his death in Venice in August 1929.
  • Key figures: choreographers Michel Fokine, Vaslav Nijinsky, Léonide Massine, Bronislava Nijinska and George Balanchine; dancers Nijinsky, Tamara Karsavina and (briefly) Anna Pavlova; composers Igor Stravinsky, Maurice Ravel, Claude Debussy, Erik Satie, Sergei Prokofiev and Francis Poulenc; designers and painters Léon Bakst, Alexandre Benois, Natalia Goncharova, Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse, André Derain, Joan Miró, Giorgio de Chirico and Georges Rouault; and Coco Chanel for costumes.
  • Historical and cultural background: Diaghilev began as an art editor and exhibition organiser in St Petersburg (the magazine Mir iskusstva, "World of Art"). After showing Russian art and opera in Paris, he brought dancers from the Imperial Theatres during their summer break. Paris before the First World War was hungry for "exotic" Russian colour; after the war, the company turned to the modernist avant-garde of Paris itself.
  • Important works (content overview):
    • The Firebird (1910, Fokine / Stravinsky) — a Russian fairy tale; Stravinsky's first big success.
    • Petrushka (1911, Fokine / Stravinsky / Benois) — a puppet at a Shrovetide fair who has a human soul; Nijinsky's great role.
    • L'Après-midi d'un faune (1912, Nijinsky / Debussy / Bakst) — about 12 minutes of flat, profile movement like an ancient Greek frieze; its erotic ending caused a scandal.
    • The Rite of Spring (1913) — covered in an earlier briefing; the most famous premiere riot in theatre history.
    • Parade (1917, Massine / Satie / Picasso / Cocteau) — a circus side-show turned into Cubist theatre; Guillaume Apollinaire's programme note used the word sur-réalisme, one of its earliest uses.
    • Les Noces (1923, Nijinska / Stravinsky) — a peasant wedding as a hard, machine-like ritual.
    • Apollon musagète (1928) and The Prodigal Son (1929), both by Balanchine — the start of his clean, neoclassical style.
  • Artistic style and techniques: the core idea was the Gesamtkunstwerk — a "total work of art" in which choreography, new music and painting are equal partners. Diaghilev commissioned new scores instead of using old ballet music; he hired easel painters, not specialist theatre decorators, to design sets and costumes; and he kept ballets short (often three in one evening) so each one could be a concentrated experiment. Choreographically, the company moved away from long 19th-century story ballets (like Swan Lake) toward one-act works, turned-in and angular movement, and a greater role for the male dancer.
  • Cultural and historical significance: it made ballet a central art of modernism rather than a decorative court entertainment. Many of the 20th century's most important scores (The Firebird, Petrushka, The Rite of Spring, Ravel's Daphnis et Chloé) were written for it. It brought Cubism and Surrealism onto the stage, influenced fashion (Bakst's "Oriental" colours affected Paris dress designers such as Paul Poiret) and showed how an impresario can be a creative force.
  • Influence and legacy: after Diaghilev's death, the dancers scattered and founded much of world ballet: Balanchine co-founded the School of American Ballet (1934) and later New York City Ballet (1948); Ninette de Valois, a former member, founded what became Britain's Royal Ballet; Marie Rambert founded Ballet Rambert; Serge Lifar led the Paris Opéra Ballet; and companies called "Ballet Russe de Monte Carlo" toured the world in the 1930s–50s. The film The Red Shoes (introduced on Nov 16) is partly a portrait of a Diaghilev-like impresario.
  • Interpretation vs. fact: the dates and creators above are historical facts. Calling the company the "birth of modern ballet" is a critical judgement, widely shared but still an interpretation; some historians stress that it also relied on wealthy patrons and on an exoticised image of Russia and "the East" that looks problematic today.
  • Where to experience it today: Petrushka, Faune, The Firebird, Apollo and The Prodigal Son remain in the repertoire of many major companies (Balanchine's works through New York City Ballet and others). Original costumes and designs are held by museums such as London's Victoria and Albert Museum, which staged the exhibition "Diaghilev and the Golden Age of the Ballets Russes, 1909–1929" in 2010–11. Diaghilev and Stravinsky are both buried on the cemetery island of San Michele in Venice.

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 113: Reading a Film as a Historical Document — the Era Depicted vs. the Era of Production
第 8 段 — 看電影的方法第 113 課:把電影當成歷史文件來讀——「描寫的時代」vs.「製作的時代」

Distinct from Section 7: Section 7 introduces a subject; this section teaches a transferable method. Previous lessons on ideological, psychoanalytic, structuralist, postcolonial, queer and ecocritical readings looked at what values a film carries; today's lesson asks when those values come from.

ENEnglish version

1. Today's Analytical Method

Reading a film as a historical document. Every film carries at least three dates: the time it depicts (the story world), the time it was made (its production context), and the time it is watched (its reception). Historians of cinema argue that a film is most reliable as evidence of the second — the fears, hopes and assumptions of the moment it was made — even when it is set in the past or the future. Siegfried Kracauer's From Caligari to Hitler (1947) read Weimar films as signs of the German "collective mind" before Nazism; Marc Ferro (Cinéma et histoire, 1977) treated films as a "counter-analysis" of society that can reveal what official history hides; Pierre Sorlin (The Film in History, 1980) and Robert Rosenstone (Visions of the Past, 1995) studied how historical films construct the past. This matters because it turns any film — even a B-movie — into evidence about its own time.

2. Detailed Explanation

  • Step 1 — find the three dates. When is the story set? When was it written, shot and released? When and where are you watching it?
  • Step 2 — list what the film takes for granted. Which jobs, gender roles, technologies, enemies and dangers does the film treat as normal? Assumptions reveal the production era more than declarations do.
  • Step 3 — look for pressure from the period. Censorship rules, studio demands, war, elections, economic crises, and the people who funded the film. Changes forced on a film (new endings, cuts, framing devices) are especially revealing.
  • Step 4 — test competing readings. A film can be read in opposite ways by different audiences. Note what evidence supports each reading, and what the filmmakers actually said (with caution: creators' statements are evidence too, not the final answer).
  • Step 5 — separate levels of claim. Objective: dates, production history, what is on screen. Interpretive: "this monster stands for X". Speculative: "audiences felt Y". Mark each one honestly.
  • Common misconceptions: (1) "A historical film tells us about the past it depicts." Mostly it tells us how its makers saw that past. (2) "Allegory must have one correct meaning." Strong allegories are often ambiguous, which is why they survive. (3) "This is just reading politics into everything." The method requires evidence from the production context, not just a feeling that a monster "looks like" something.

3. Concrete Film Example — Invasion of the Body Snatchers (1956), dir. Don Siegel

Produced by Walter Wanger for Allied Artists, written by Daniel Mainwaring from Jack Finney's novel The Body Snatchers (serialised in Collier's, 1954), starring Kevin McCarthy as Dr. Miles Bennell and Dana Wynter as Becky Driscoll. In the small California town of Santa Mira, people are replaced by emotionless copies grown from giant seed pods.

  1. The three dates. The story is set in an ordinary present-day 1950s town; it was made in 1955–56, during the Cold War, just after the peak of Senator Joseph McCarthy's anti-communist hearings (he was censured by the Senate in December 1954); we watch it today, after decades of debate about it.
  2. What the film takes for granted. A small-town doctor as the natural hero; a world of front porches, family doctors and trust in neighbours. The horror is that this familiar world looks exactly the same after the invasion — only the feeling has gone. The pod people's promise is a life "without love, without desire, without faith" — calm, conformist and identical.
  3. The key scenes. (a) In Miles's greenhouse, the pods burst open with foam and half-formed copies of the characters: the threat grows at home, not from outer space battles. (b) From a hiding place, Miles and Becky watch the town square, where trucks distribute pods and the townspeople calmly organise their spread to neighbouring towns — a community acting as one. (c) In the tunnel, after a moment of sleep, Becky becomes a pod; Miles's voice-over says he never knew the real meaning of fear until he kissed her. (d) On the highway at night, Miles runs between cars shouting "They're here already! You're next!", and turns toward the camera — so he is shouting at us.
  4. Pressure from the period — the frame story. Siegel's version reportedly ended on the highway. After poor preview reactions, the studio added a prologue and epilogue in a hospital, where Miles tells his story to doctors; at the end, a crash report confirms him, and a doctor calls for the authorities. The frame turns an open, desperate warning into a story with official rescue. That change is itself historical evidence: in 1956, a studio thought audiences needed reassurance that institutions would respond.
  5. Competing readings. Reading A: the pods are communists — an enemy that looks like your neighbours, infiltrates quietly and erases individuality. Reading B: the pods are McCarthyist America — a community that demands conformity, where everyone suspects everyone and dissent is "cured". Both readings use the same images. The novelist Jack Finney said he intended no political allegory, and the filmmakers' later comments vary, so neither reading can claim to be the "intended" one. What both readings agree on is the production era's central anxiety: losing your individuality inside your own community.
  6. Why this works on the viewer. Because the invaders look normal, the camera trains us to scan faces for small signs of coldness — the same suspicious way of looking that the period encouraged. The film both depicts paranoia and makes us practise it.
  7. Deeper meaning. The film is less useful as evidence of "what happened" in the 1950s and very useful as evidence of what 1950s Americans feared. Its later remakes (1978, 1993, 2007) each changed the setting and the type of fear — a good sign that each one records its own production era.

4. Practical Viewing Exercise

Watch the original Godzilla (ゴジラ, 1954, dir. Ishirō Honda). Note the three dates (story / production / your viewing). Then find two scenes where the film seems to take something for granted about postwar Japan — for example, the fishing boat disaster at the start (made in the same year as the real Lucky Dragon No. 5 incident after the US Bikini Atoll H-bomb test of March 1954), or the images of a burning city and of victims in hospital. For each scene, write one objective observation and one interpretive claim, and say what production-era evidence supports the claim.

5. Key Takeaways

  • Every film has three dates: depicted, produced, watched. It is best evidence of the time it was made.
  • Look at assumptions and at changes forced on the film (endings, cuts, frames): they reveal the period's pressures.
  • Allow competing readings, support each with evidence, and keep objective observations separate from interpretation.

Ask what it records about the year it was made (the ruins, black markets and hunger are real), and what it shows about how an Italian director in 1947 understood German guilt and Nazi ideology.

1. 今日分析方法

把電影當成歷史文件來讀。每部電影至少帶著三個時間:它描寫的時代(故事世界)、它製作的時代(製作背景)、以及它被觀看的時代(接受背景)。電影史學者認為,電影最可靠的,是作為第二種時間的證據——也就是它被拍出來那一刻的恐懼、希望與預設——即使故事設定在過去或未來。克拉考爾(Siegfried Kracauer)的《從卡里加利到希特勒》(1947)把威瑪時期的電影讀成納粹崛起前德國「集體心理」的徵兆;費侯(Marc Ferro)的《電影與歷史》(1977)把電影當成社會的「反分析」,可以揭露官方歷史隱藏的東西;索蘭(Pierre Sorlin)的《歷史中的電影》(1980)與羅森史東(Robert Rosenstone)的《過去的影像》(1995)研究歷史電影如何建構過去。這很重要,因為它讓任何電影——甚至是 B 級片——都成為它那個時代的證據。

2. 詳細說明

  • 步驟一——找出三個時間。故事設定在什麼時候?劇本在什麼時候寫、什麼時候拍、什麼時候上映?你在什麼時間、什麼地方看它?
  • 步驟二——列出電影視為理所當然的事。電影把哪些職業、性別角色、科技、敵人與危險當成「正常」?比起電影明白說出的主張,這些預設更能透露製作的時代。
  • 步驟三——找出時代施加的壓力。審查規範、片廠要求、戰爭、選舉、經濟危機、出資的人。被迫加在電影上的改變(新結局、刪剪、外框敘事)特別有參考價值。
  • 步驟四——測試互相競爭的解讀。不同觀眾可能把同一部電影讀成相反的意思。記下支持每一種解讀的證據,以及創作者實際說過的話(但要小心:創作者的說法也是證據,不是最終答案)。
  • 步驟五——區分主張的層次。客觀的:日期、製作經過、畫面上有什麼。詮釋的:「這個怪物代表 X」。推測的:「觀眾當時感到 Y」。每一種都要誠實標示。
  • 常見誤解:(1)「歷史電影告訴我們它描寫的那段過去。」大多時候,它告訴我們的是創作者怎麼看那段過去。(2)「寓言一定只有一個正確意思。」強而有力的寓言常常是模稜兩可的,這正是它們能流傳下來的原因。(3)「這只是把政治硬套到所有東西上。」這個方法要求來自製作背景的證據,不能只憑感覺說某個怪物「看起來像」什麼。

3. 具體電影範例——《天外魔花》(Invasion of the Body Snatchers,1956),導演唐・席格(Don Siegel)

由華特・旺格(Walter Wanger)為聯合藝術家公司(Allied Artists)製作,丹尼爾・曼韋林(Daniel Mainwaring)改編自傑克・芬尼(Jack Finney)的小說《The Body Snatchers》(1954 年在《Collier's》雜誌連載),由凱文・麥卡錫(Kevin McCarthy)飾演醫師邁爾斯・貝內爾、黛娜・溫特(Dana Wynter)飾演貝琪・德里斯科爾。在加州小鎮聖米拉,居民一個個被巨大種莢培育出來、沒有情感的複製人取代。

  1. 三個時間。故事設定在一個普通的 1950 年代當代小鎮;電影拍攝於 1955–56 年的冷戰時期,剛好在參議員約瑟夫・麥卡錫的反共聽證會高峰之後(他在 1954 年 12 月遭參議院譴責);我們則是在今天,經過數十年的討論之後觀看它。
  2. 電影視為理所當然的事。小鎮醫生理所當然是英雄;一個有門廊、家庭醫師、信任鄰居的世界。恐怖之處在於,這個熟悉的世界在入侵之後看起來一模一樣——只是感情不見了。種莢人承諾的生活是「沒有愛、沒有慾望、沒有信仰」——平靜、順從、彼此一模一樣。
  3. 關鍵場景。(a)在邁爾斯家的溫室裡,種莢冒著泡沫裂開,露出主角們尚未成形的複製體:威脅在家裡長出來,而不是來自外太空的戰爭。(b)邁爾斯和貝琪躲起來,看著鎮上的廣場:卡車在發送種莢,居民冷靜地組織,把種莢運往鄰近城鎮——整個社區像一個人一樣行動。(c)在隧道裡,貝琪只睡著了一下就變成了種莢人;邁爾斯的旁白說,直到吻了她,他才知道恐懼真正的意思。(d)夜晚的公路上,邁爾斯在車陣之間奔跑,大喊「它們已經來了!下一個就是你!」,然後轉向攝影機——所以他是在對我們大喊。
  4. 時代的壓力——外框故事。據說席格的版本是以公路那一幕結束。試映反應不佳後,片廠加上醫院裡的序幕和尾聲:邁爾斯向醫生們講述他的遭遇;最後一份車禍報告證實了他的說法,一位醫生打電話通知有關當局。這個外框把一個開放、絕望的警告,變成一個有官方出手拯救的故事。這個改變本身就是歷史證據:在 1956 年,片廠認為觀眾需要被安撫,需要相信體制會有反應。
  5. 互相競爭的解讀。解讀 A:種莢人是共產黨——一個看起來像你鄰居、悄悄滲透、抹除個人特質的敵人。解讀 B:種莢人是麥卡錫主義下的美國——一個要求順從的社區,人人互相懷疑,異議會被「治好」。兩種解讀用的是同樣的畫面。原著作者傑克・芬尼說他並沒有要寫政治寓言,電影創作者後來的說法也不一致,所以哪一種解讀都不能自稱是「原意」。兩種解讀都同意的,是製作那個時代的核心焦慮:在自己的社區裡失去個體性。
  6. 為什麼對觀眾有效。因為入侵者看起來很正常,鏡頭訓練我們去掃視每一張臉,尋找細微的冷漠跡象——這正是那個時代鼓勵的多疑目光。這部電影既描寫了偏執,也讓我們親身練習偏執。
  7. 更深層的意義。這部電影作為 1950 年代「發生了什麼事」的證據用處不大,但作為 1950 年代美國人「害怕什麼」的證據非常有用。它後來的重拍版(1978、1993、2007)每一次都改變了背景和恐懼的類型——這正好說明每一個版本都記錄了它自己的製作時代。

4. 實際觀影練習

看原版《哥吉拉》(ゴジラ,1954,導演本多豬四郎)。記下三個時間(故事/製作/你觀看的時間)。然後找兩個電影似乎把戰後日本的某件事視為理所當然的場景——例如開場的漁船災難(這部片與真實的第五福龍丸事件同一年:1954 年 3 月美國在比基尼環礁試爆氫彈),或是城市燃燒、醫院裡受害者的畫面。每個場景寫下一個客觀觀察和一個詮釋主張,並說明有什麼製作時代的證據支持這個主張。

5. 重點整理

  • 每部電影都有三個時間:描寫的、製作的、觀看的。它最能作為「製作時代」的證據。
  • 注意電影的預設,以及被迫加上的改變(結局、刪剪、外框):它們透露了那個時代的壓力。
  • 允許不同的解讀,每一種都要有證據支持,並把客觀觀察和詮釋分開。

6. 延伸觀賞推薦
