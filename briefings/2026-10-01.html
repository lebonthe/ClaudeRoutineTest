4) Zhuangzi — Essay 13: 天道 (Tiān Dào) — "The Way of Heaven"

The sixth chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇), following "Heaven and Earth." It opens with the claim that the Way of Heaven turns without piling up anything (天道運而無所積), just as the way of the emperor and the sage turns without piling up, so all under heaven return to them. The chapter describes emptiness, stillness, tranquility, quietness and non-action (虛靜恬淡,寂漠無為) as the level of Heaven and Earth and the substance of the Way. A still mind is like a mirror: it reflects the myriad things, and the ruler who rests in it lets his ministers act while he does not. The best-known story is Wheelwright Bian (輪扁) telling Duke Huan that the books the duke is reading are only "the dregs of the ancients" (古人之糟魄): the knack of cutting a wheel cannot be put into words or handed to his own son, so whatever the sage really knew died with him. Another episode has Shi Chengqi (士成綺) visit Laozi and meet with ridicule of reputation, and a later passage ranks Dao, virtue, benevolence, righteousness, names and penalties in a descending order that cannot be reversed.

5) Country / Region Spotlight — Belgium (比利時)

  • Location & territory size: Belgium lies in northwestern Europe on the North Sea, bordering the Netherlands, Germany, Luxembourg and France. At about 30,700 km², it is roughly 85% the size of Taiwan (about 36,200 km²) and about the size of Maryland.
  • Population: About 11.8 million, among the most densely populated countries in Europe; roughly 60% live in Flanders, 30-ish% in Wallonia and the rest in the Brussels-Capital Region.
  • Religion: Historically Catholic, and Catholics are still the largest single group by self-identification, but secularization is advanced; there are sizeable non-religious and Muslim (roughly 6-7%) communities.
  • Language: Three official languages: Dutch (spoken by roughly 60% of people), French (roughly 40%) and German (about 1%, in the east). Brussels is officially bilingual.

The medieval Flemish cloth cities (Bruges, Ghent) were among Europe's wealthiest; the Low Countries passed to Burgundy, then the Habsburgs, then Spain and Austria, and were annexed by revolutionary France. After a 1815-1830 union with the Netherlands, the 1830 Belgian Revolution produced independence, with Leopold I as king in 1831. Leopold II's personal Congo Free State (1885-1908) became the Belgian Congo until independence in 1960. Occupied in both world wars, Belgium was a founding member of the EU's predecessors (1957) and federalized in stages, completed in 1993.

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — Rage Comics (暴怒漫畫)

  • Name: Rage Comics — crudely drawn, MS Paint-style comics built from a cast of reusable faces ("Rage Guy," "Forever Alone," "Me Gusta," "Okay Guy," "Y U NO").
  • Origin / history: The format is usually traced to 2008 on 4chan, where an anonymous user posted a comic about a man flying into a furious rant after being splashed by toilet water, ending in a screamed "FFFFFUUUUUUUU—." The shouting face became "Rage Guy," and the template was quickly copied by other users.
  • How it became popular: Reddit's dedicated subreddit, 9GAG and Facebook pages carried it from 2009 to 2012, helped by the fact that anyone could make one in minutes, and by online "rage comic builder" tools.
  • Meaning and usage: Short, relatable four-to-eight panel comics about awkward everyday situations, embarrassing moments, school and family life, with each face acting as a fixed emotion (anger, loneliness, smugness, disbelief).
  • Variations / evolution: The cast grew into hundreds of characters, and faces such as Trollface and Forever Alone became independent memes; later image-macro formats and social-media video replaced the multi-panel comic.
  • Current status: Largely dormant since the mid-2010s and mostly used today for nostalgia or ironic retro humor, though the individual faces survive.

Sources: general reference knowledge (Know Your Meme entry on Rage Comics); exact dates and details were not re-verified live for this run.

7) Film / Performing-Arts Spotlight — Raise the Red Lantern (大紅燈籠高高掛, 1991, Zhang Yimou)

  • Name and key figures: Raise the Red Lantern, directed by Zhang Yimou, starring Gong Li as Songlian, with Ma Jingwu as the unseen-faced Master Chen, and cinematography by Zhao Fei. It is adapted from Su Tong's 1990 novella Wives and Concubines (妻妾成群) and was financed with Taiwanese backing (producer Chiu Fu-sheng) as a Chinese-language co-production.
  • Era / background: Made in 1991, after Zhang's earlier Red Sorghum (1987) and Ju Dou (1990), and set in the warlord era of the 1920s. It was filmed largely at the Qiao Family Compound in Shanxi Province.
  • Plot overview: After her father's death and her family's poverty, 19-year-old university student Songlian becomes the fourth wife of the wealthy Master Chen. In the household, red lanterns are lit outside the quarters of whichever wife he chooses for the night, and that wife receives foot massages and orders her own menu. This turns the compound into a contest of jealousy, scheming and pretense among the wives, and Songlian's growing obsession ends in tragedy.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Strict symmetry and stage-like framing of the courtyards; saturated red for the lanterns against cold grey walls; a rigid seasonal structure (Summer, Autumn, Winter, Spring); the master is almost never shown in a clear close-up, so power is felt as an institution rather than a person; and a tightly controlled, mostly studio-like sound design with a recurring Peking-opera influence.
  • Cultural / historical significance: A critique of patriarchal tradition and of rituals that turn people into objects, read by many as allegory for wider authoritarian structures. It made Zhang and Gong Li internationally known.
  • Influence and legacy: It won the Silver Lion at the 1991 Venice Film Festival and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film; it helped define "Fifth Generation" Chinese cinema abroad, and a stage ballet version was created by the National Ballet of China.
  • Current status / where to experience it: It is available on home video including a Criterion Collection release; streaming availability varies by region, and the Qiao Family Compound in Shanxi can be visited as a tourist site.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Intertextuality, Homage & Allusion (Lesson 63)

Earlier lessons taught how to read images, sound, cutting, dialogue and character arcs inside a single film; this one teaches how to read a film against other films. Intertextuality means a film's meaning is partly built from references to earlier works. Distinguish four modes: allusion (a brief nod), homage (a respectful re-creation of a specific earlier scene), parody (an imitation that mocks) and pastiche (an imitation of style without mockery). To analyse it, ask four questions: (1) What exactly is being referenced, and how do I know? (2) What is kept and what is changed? (3) What does the reference add that the scene could not say alone? (4) Does a viewer who misses it still follow the scene?

Worked example: The Untouchables (1987, dir. Brian De Palma), the Chicago Union Station shootout. Eliot Ness waits at the top of a grand flight of steps while a mother struggles with a baby carriage; when the gunfight begins, the carriage starts to roll down the steps and time stretches into slow motion as Ness, exposed, must decide between covering the fight and saving the child. This is a direct homage to the Odessa Steps sequence in Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin (1925), where a baby carriage rolls down the steps during the massacre (see the 2026 spotlight on that film). What is kept: the staircase, the descending carriage, the intercutting of faces, the stretched time. What is changed: Eisenstein's camera follows a mass of victims as political horror, while De Palma narrows the focus to one hero's suspense and adds an old-fashioned movie gunfight, with synchronized sound and a swelling orchestral score. Why it works: for viewers who know Potemkin, the scene borrows cinema history's most famous image of helpless innocence and makes the audience expect disaster; for viewers who do not, it still plays as a self-contained suspense sequence, which is the sign of a well-judged homage.

4)莊子——第 13 篇:天道(Tiān Dào)

《外篇》第六篇,承接〈天地〉。開篇主張天道運行而不積滯(天道運而無所積),帝王與聖人之道也是運行而不積滯,所以天下歸附。本篇把虛靜恬淡、寂漠無為說成天地的水準與道德的本質。靜止的心像一面鏡子,映照萬物;君主安住其中,臣下做事而君主無為。最著名的故事是輪扁(輪扁斲輪)對齊桓公說,公所讀的書不過是「古人之糟魄」:斲輪的手藝無法用言語傳達,也無法傳給自己的兒子,所以聖人真正知道的東西早已隨他一起死去。另一段則是士成綺拜訪老子而遭到對名聲的嘲諷,後文還把道、德、仁、義、名、刑罰依次排列,說其順序不可顛倒。

  • 名稱與關鍵人物:《大紅燈籠高高掛》,導演張藝謀,鞏俐飾頌蓮,馬精武飾始終看不清面孔的陳老爺,攝影趙非。改編自蘇童 1990 年中篇小說《妻妾成群》,由台灣資金(製片邱復生)參與出資,是華語合拍片。
  • 時代背景:1991 年出品,在張藝謀先前的《紅高粱》(1987)與《菊豆》(1990)之後,故事設定於 1920 年代軍閥時期,主要在山西的喬家大院拍攝。
  • 劇情概要:父親過世、家道中落後,19 歲的大學生頌蓮成為富有的陳老爺的四太太。在這個家中,老爺當晚選中哪位太太,就把紅燈籠掛在她的院落前,那位太太可以受到捶腳並自己點菜。這讓大院變成太太們之間嫉妒、算計與偽裝的競爭,頌蓮日漸加深的執念最後以悲劇收場。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:庭院採嚴格對稱、舞台般的構圖;飽和的紅色燈籠對比冷灰色牆面;以夏、秋、冬、春構成嚴整的季節結構;老爺幾乎不給清楚的特寫,讓權力感成為一種制度而不是一個人;聲音設計精準克制,帶有京劇的影響。
  • 文化與歷史意義:對父權傳統與把人變成物件的儀式提出批判,許多人將其解讀為對更廣泛威權結構的寓言。它讓張藝謀與鞏俐在國際上廣為人知。
  • 影響與遺產:獲 1991 年威尼斯影展銀獅獎,並入圍奧斯卡最佳外語片;它協助定義了海外眼中的「第五代」中國電影,中國國家芭蕾舞團也製作了芭蕾舞劇版本。
  • 現況/在哪裡欣賞:已有家庭影音版本,包括 Criterion Collection 版;串流平台依地區而異,山西的喬家大院可作為觀光景點造訪。

8)看片方法——互文性、致敬與引用(Intertextuality, Homage & Allusion)(第 63 課)

先前的課程教你在單一影片內部讀影像、聲音、剪接、對白與角色弧線;這一課教你把一部電影放在其他電影旁邊閱讀。互文性指的是電影的意義有一部分來自對前作的指涉。請區分四種模式:引用(allusion,短暫的一瞥)、致敬(homage,恭敬地重現某個特定的前作場景)、諷刺模仿(parody,帶嘲弄的模仿)與風格仿作(pastiche,模仿風格但不嘲弄)。分析時問四個問題:(1)被指涉的到底是什麼,我怎麼知道?(2)保留了什麼、改變了什麼?(3)這個指涉加了什麼是場景本身說不出來的?(4)沒看出指涉的觀眾,還能看懂這場戲嗎?

實例分析:《鐵面無私》(The Untouchables,1987,導演布萊恩·狄帕瑪)芝加哥聯合車站槍戰。艾略特·尼斯在寬闊階梯頂端等候,一位母親正吃力地推著嬰兒車;槍戰爆發時,嬰兒車開始沿階梯滾落,時間被拉長成慢動作,暴露在外的尼斯必須在掩護槍戰與救孩子之間做選擇。這是對愛森斯坦《波坦金戰艦》(1925)敖德薩階梯段落的直接致敬,該片中嬰兒車也在屠殺中滾下階梯(參見本系列對該片的介紹)。保留的部分:階梯、滾落的嬰兒車、交叉剪接的臉孔、被拉長的時間。改變的部分:愛森斯坦的鏡頭把一大群受害者當作政治恐怖來跟拍,狄帕瑪則把焦點縮到單一英雄的懸疑,並加入老派的電影槍戰、同步音效與澎湃的管弦配樂。為什麼有效:對熟悉《波坦金戰艦》的觀眾,這場戲借用了電影史上最著名的無助無辜的影像,讓觀眾預期災難;對不熟悉的觀眾,它仍是一段自成一體的懸疑段落,這正是致敬拿捏得宜的標誌。
