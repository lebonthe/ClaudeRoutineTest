3) Spanish Lesson — Day 42 (10 min, comparatives & superlatives)

Days 1-41 built an almost complete verb system — every major indicative and subjunctive tense, both moods' commands, and the three-type conditional system. Today deliberately steps outside verb conjugation into a new grammar area that has not yet been covered: comparing and ranking nouns and adjectives. Spanish comparatives split into three patterns depending on whether something is "more/less," "as...as," or "the most/least": más/menos + adjective + que (more/less than), tan + adjective + como (as...as), and for superlatives, el/la/los/las + más/menos + adjective (the most/least). A handful of adjectives (bueno, malo, grande, pequeño) have irregular comparative forms that must be memorized rather than built with más/menos.

  • Mi hermano es más alto que yo. — My brother is taller than me.
  • Este café no es tan caro como ese. — This coffee isn't as expensive as that one.
  • Ella es la más inteligente de la clase. — She is the smartest in the class.

Grammar note: the irregular comparatives are the main trap — bueno/bien → mejor (not "más bueno"), malo/mal → peor (not "más malo"), grande (age) → mayor, pequeño (age) → menor; but grande/pequeño keep their regular más grande/más pequeño forms when talking about physical size rather than age. Also note that superlatives use de, not en, to mean "in/of" the group being ranked (la más inteligente de la clase, not en la clase) — a common English-interference error.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 42: 益 (Yì) — "Increase" (Wind above, Thunder below — Xùn over Zhèn). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary pivots directly off yesterday's Hexagram 41: "損而不已必益,故受之以益。" ("Decrease that does not stop must eventually turn into increase; hence it is followed by Yì [Increase].") Where Hexagram 41 taught that every gain draws down a reserve elsewhere, Hexagram 42 completes the cycle: sustained, sincere decrease below eventually produces increase, and here it is the upper trigram that gives way to benefit the lower — the exact mirror of 41's "decrease below, increase above." The Judgment reads: "益,利有攸往,利涉大川。" ("Increase: it is favorable to have somewhere to go; it is favorable to cross the great river.") The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) explains: "益,損上益下,民說無疆,自上下下,其道大光。利有攸往,中正有慶。利涉大川,木道乃行。益動而巽,日進無疆,天施地生,其益無方。凡益之道,與時偕行。" ("Increase means decreasing what is above to increase what is below; the people's joy then knows no bounds. When those above lower themselves before those below, their way shines greatly. It is favorable to have somewhere to go, for the central and correct bring cause for celebration; it is favorable to cross the great river, for the way of wood [the trigram Xùn] can then proceed. Increase moves together with compliance, advancing daily without limit; heaven bestows and earth generates, and this increase has no fixed direction. In every case, the way of increase must move together with the times.") The Image commentary draws the lesson for conduct: "風雷,益;君子以見善則遷,有過則改。" ("Wind and thunder: this is the image of Increase. Thus the superior person, seeing good, moves toward it, and having erred, corrects it.") Read together, Hexagrams 41 and 42 form a complete teaching on where real gain comes from: 41 shows that willingly accepting loss below purchases benefit above, while 42 shows the reverse motion completing the cycle — those above voluntarily giving way to enrich those below is itself what generates lasting, boundless increase, so that decrease and increase are ultimately two phases of a single, continuous exchange rather than opposites.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Rwanda (盧安達)

  • Location: East-Central Africa, landlocked; bordered by Uganda to the north, Tanzania to the east, Burundi to the south, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west. Known as the "Land of a Thousand Hills" for its mountainous, terraced terrain.
  • Territory: About 26,338 km² — roughly 0.7 times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km², i.e. somewhat smaller), about a tenth the size of Kenya (~580,000 km²), and comparable in area to the US state of Maryland (~32,100 km²) — despite this small footprint it is one of Africa's most densely populated countries.
  • Population: Roughly 14.3 million (2026 estimate), giving a population density among the highest in mainland Africa.
  • Religion: Predominantly Christian (roughly 90%+, split between Roman Catholic and various Protestant denominations, including a large Seventh-day Adventist community), with small Muslim (~5%) and traditional-belief minorities.
  • Economy: One of the fastest-growing economies in Africa over the past two decades, built on services, tourism (notably mountain-gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park), coffee and tea exports, and a deliberate government push to position Kigali as a regional tech and conference hub (sometimes dubbed "Africa's Singapore" for its cleanliness, low corruption ranking, and business-friendly reforms); still faces high poverty and heavy dependence on foreign aid despite the growth.
  • History: A German, then Belgian colony (League of Nations/UN trust territory) that entrenched an ethnic hierarchy between Hutu and Tutsi populations. Independence came in 1962; decades of ethnic tension and periodic violence culminated in the 1994 Rwandan genocide, in which an estimated 800,000-1,000,000 people, overwhelmingly Tutsi, were killed by Hutu extremists over roughly 100 days before the Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF), led by Paul Kagame, took control and ended the killing. The following decades saw an internationally noted reconciliation and rebuilding effort, including community-based gacaca courts for genocide-related justice, alongside significant, sustained economic growth.
  • Current leader: President Paul Kagame, in power since 2000 (having been the de facto leader as Vice President/Defence Minister from 1994), re-elected repeatedly with overwhelming majorities; his government is credited with the country's stability and growth but has also drawn international criticism over restrictions on political opposition and press freedom, and over Rwanda's alleged involvement in conflict in the neighboring Democratic Republic of the Congo.
  • Language: Kinyarwanda, French, English, and Swahili are all official languages, with Kinyarwanda spoken natively by nearly the entire population; English replaced French as the primary language of instruction in schools in 2008, part of a broader post-genocide shift away from Francophone institutional ties.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, cia.gov World Factbook

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — "Ain't Nobody Got Time for That" (Sweet Brown)

  • Name: "Ain't Nobody Got Time for That," most often associated with the phrase "I got bronchitis!"
  • Origin/history: Taken from a local Oklahoma City news interview in April 2012 with Kimberly "Sweet Brown" Wilkins, a resident who had escaped an apartment fire. In her animated, widely quoted interview, she recounted running from the building and needing to catch her breath, declaring "I got bronchitis, y'all. Ain't nobody got time for that."
  • How it became popular: The clip was quickly clipped and shared on YouTube and Twitter, where its rhythmic delivery and quotability made it an instant viral sensation; within days it had been Auto-Tuned into remix songs (in the style of the Gregory Brothers' "Bed Intruder Song"), turned into image macros, and picked up by national talk shows and news outlets covering internet culture.
  • Meaning/usage: Used as a general-purpose exclamation to dismiss something as not worth one's time or effort — "ain't nobody got time for that" — often deployed sarcastically in response to tedious tasks, bureaucratic hurdles, or unnecessary drama, independent of its original fire-related context.
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned numerous Auto-Tune remixes and parody videos, entered everyday spoken slang well beyond its news-interview origin, and became one of several early-2010s "reaction interview" memes (alongside "Hide yo kids, hide yo wife") that turned unscripted local-news moments into national internet phenomena.
  • Current status: Largely retired as an actively shared meme format today, but the phrase itself persists in everyday colloquial English as a stock dismissive expression, and the clip remains a frequently cited touchstone in retrospectives on the early 2010s "viral local news interview" meme wave.
    Sources: knowyourmeme.com, en.wikipedia.org

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Bicycle Thieves (1948, dir. Vittorio De Sica)

  • Name/key figures: Bicycle Thieves (Ladri di biciclette), directed by Vittorio De Sica, screenplay co-written by De Sica and Cesare Zavattini (adapting Luigi Bartolini's 1946 novel), starring non-professional actors Lamberto Maggiorani as Antonio Ricci and Enzo Staiola as his son Bruno.
  • Era/background: Made in 1948 amid the devastation of postwar Italy, it is the definitive landmark of Italian Neorealism — a movement, alongside Rome, Open City (1945) and La Terra Trema (1948), built on shooting real locations, casting non-actors, and depicting the everyday struggles of the working poor, in deliberate contrast to the elaborate studio-bound "White Telephone" films of the Fascist era. Fellini's 8½ (spotlighted here Aug 20) later dramatized precisely the arthouse turn away from this style that De Sica helped define.
  • Plot/content overview: Antonio, an unemployed man in Rome, finally lands a job hanging film posters, which requires a bicycle he has just pawned his family's bedsheets to redeem. On his very first day, the bicycle is stolen. He and his young son Bruno spend the following day wandering Rome's streets and markets searching for it, encountering indifferent police, a psychic, and a brief, humiliating temptation for Antonio to become a thief himself before the film's quietly devastating final shot.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Location shooting on real Roman streets and markets rather than studio sets; a wholly non-professional cast (Maggiorani was an actual factory worker); a plain, unembellished visual style that refuses melodrama, sentimental score cues, or a conventional redemptive resolution; and a narrative built from small, accumulating, everyday indignities rather than dramatic plot turns.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely considered the movement's masterpiece and the film that carried Italian Neorealism's aesthetic and moral principles to the rest of the world, cementing the idea that cinema could dignify ordinary poverty and postwar hardship as worthy of serious art rather than melodrama.
  • Influence and legacy: Directly shaped the French New Wave, the Dardenne brothers, Iranian social-realist cinema (notably Abbas Kiarostami), and generations of filmmakers pursuing location-based, non-professional-cast social realism; awarded an honorary Academy Award in 1950 (before a competitive Best Foreign Film category existed) and consistently ranks among the greatest films ever made in critics' polls, including topping the 1952 Sight & Sound poll.
  • Current status/where to experience it: Restored and distributed by the Criterion Collection; a staple of film-school curricula and Neorealism retrospectives worldwide as the movement's foundational text.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, criterion.com

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Voice-Over Narration & Narrator Reliability (Lesson 28)

Voice-over narration places a verbal storyteller's voice over the visual track, and the first thing to ask of it is simple but easy to skip past: whose voice is this, and where are they speaking from — inside the story's own timeline, or from some later, reflective vantage point? The second and more analytically important question is reliability: does the narration simply describe what we also see (redundant, often a weakness), does it add information the images cannot show (interiority, irony, foreknowledge), or does it actively mislead the audience about what is actually happening on screen? This is distinct from Day 6's Narrative Structure (which addresses the sequencing/shape of the plot itself, voice-over or not) and from Day 4's Sound Design lesson (which covers score and diegetic/non-diegetic sound broadly, not the specific device of a speaking narrator); voice-over is a narrator's claim on the story, and the analytical work is testing that claim against what the images actually show. Four things to check: whether the narrator is a character inside the story or an external, all-knowing voice; whether narration and image ever visibly contradict each other (a productive tension, not a mistake); whether the narration's tense and tone reveal how much time and distance separate the narrator from the events described; and whether the film eventually reveals the narration itself to have been unreliable, forcing a retroactive re-reading of everything heard before that point.

Worked example: Fight Club (1999, dir. David Fincher) — the unnamed Narrator's voice-over runs continuously from the film's opening frame, confidently explaining his insomnia, his job, and his growing friendship with the charismatic Tyler Durden in a wry, detached, know-it-all tone that invites total trust. The third-act revelation that Tyler Durden is a dissociative alter-ego the Narrator has literally been talking to (and about) throughout — never disclosed by the narration itself despite the Narrator's claimed omniscience over his own story — retroactively exposes the entire voice-over as an unreliable narrator's self-deceiving account, and rewards a rewatch by making visible how carefully Fincher had planted subliminal single-frame flashes of Tyler throughout the "normal" early scenes, hidden in plain sight beneath a narration that never once mentioned them.

3)西班牙文課程——第 42 天(10 分鐘,比較句與最高級)

第 1~41 天已建構出幾乎完整的動詞系統——所有主要的直陳式與虛擬式時態、兩種語氣的命令式,以及完整的三類條件句系統。今天刻意跳出動詞變化的範疇,進入一個尚未教過的全新文法領域:比較與排序名詞、形容詞。西班牙文的比較句依語意分成三種固定句型:「más/menos + 形容詞 + que」(比……更/較不……)、「tan + 形容詞 + como」(和……一樣……),以及最高級「el/la/los/las + más/menos + 形容詞」(最……/最不……)。少數幾個形容詞(bueno、malo、grande、pequeño)有不規則的比較級形式,必須整組記下來,而不是靠 más/menos 規則推導。

  • Mi hermano es más alto que yo. —— 我哥哥比我高。
  • Este café no es tan caro como ese. —— 這杯咖啡不像那杯那麼貴。
  • Ella es la más inteligente de la clase. —— 她是班上最聰明的。

文法筆記:最大的陷阱是不規則比較級——bueno/bien(好)→ mejor(更好,不是「más bueno」);malo/mal(壞)→ peor(更壞,不是「más malo」);grande(用於年齡時)→ mayor(年紀較大);pequeño(用於年齡時)→ menor(年紀較小)——但 grande/pequeño 若指的是實際物理尺寸大小,則仍可使用規則形式 más grande/más pequeño。另外要注意,最高級句型中「在……範圍裡」要用 de,不是 en(la más inteligente de la clase,不是「en la clase」),這是英文使用者很容易因母語干擾而犯的錯誤。

4)易經卦象

第 42 卦:益 (Yì) ——「增益」(風在上,雷在下——巽上震下)。《序卦傳》從昨天的第 41 卦直接接續下來:「損而不已必益,故受之以益。」(減損若持續不止,終將轉化為增益,因此損卦之後接的是益卦。)如果說第 41 卦教導的是「每一次獲得,都要在別處相應付出減損」,那麼第 42 卦則補完了整個循環:下方持續、誠心的減損,終將帶來增益,而這裡讓步的一方換成了上方的卦體,去增益下方——恰好是第 41 卦「損下益上」的完全鏡像。卦辭寫道:「益,利有攸往,利涉大川。」(益卦:適合有所前往,適合渡過大河。)《彖傳》進一步解釋:「益,損上益下,民說無疆,自上下下,其道大光。利有攸往,中正有慶。利涉大川,木道乃行。益動而巽,日進無疆,天施地生,其益無方。凡益之道,與時偕行。」(益卦,是減損上方以增益下方,百姓的喜悅便沒有邊界。當上位者願意謙卑自處於下位者之下,其道便會大放光明。適合有所前往,是因為居中守正之德值得慶賀;適合渡過大河,是因為「木」(巽卦所象徵的舟木)之道才能順利運行。增益之時要能行動又能順從,便能日日精進而無有窮盡;天施予、地生成,這樣的增益沒有固定的方向。凡是增益之道,都必須與時勢同步而行。)《象傳》則點出實際行事的教訓:「風雷,益;君子以見善則遷,有過則改。」(風與雷交會,這就是益卦的卦象。因此君子看見益卦,遇見良善之事便會主動靠近效法,發現自身有過錯便立即改正。)將第 41 卦與第 42 卦合觀,兩卦共同構成一套關於「真正的獲得從何而來」的完整教誨:第 41 卦顯示,甘願在下方承受損失,便能換取上方的獲益;第 42 卦則呈現相反方向的動作,完成整個循環——上位者主動讓步以充實下位者,這個舉動本身正是產生持久、無窮增益的原因,因此減損與增益,終究是同一場連續交換的兩個階段,而非彼此對立的兩件事。

5)國家/地區/政體介紹——盧安達(Rwanda)

  • 位置:位於非洲中東部,為內陸國;北鄰烏干達,東接坦尚尼亞,南邊是蒲隆地,西邊是剛果民主共和國。因境內丘陵、梯田地形密布,素有「千丘之國」之稱。
  • 領土:約 26,338 平方公里——約為台灣(約 36,193 平方公里)的 0.7 倍,略小於台灣;約為肯亞(約 58 萬平方公里)的十分之一;面積與美國馬里蘭州(約 32,100 平方公里)相近——儘管面積不大,卻是非洲大陸人口密度最高的國家之一。
  • 人口:約 1,430 萬人(2026 年估計),人口密度在非洲大陸國家中居於前列。
  • 宗教:以基督教為主(合計超過九成,分屬天主教與多個新教教派,其中包含規模龐大的基督復臨安息日會社群),另有少數穆斯林(約 5%)與傳統信仰人口。
  • 經濟:過去二十年間為非洲成長最快速的經濟體之一,主要仰賴服務業、觀光業(尤其是火山國家公園的山地大猩猩健行觀光)、咖啡與茶葉出口,加上政府有意將首都吉佳利打造成區域科技與會議中心(因市容整潔、腐敗程度低、營商環境友善,有時被稱為「非洲新加坡」);儘管經濟持續成長,仍面臨高貧窮率與高度依賴外國援助的挑戰。
  • 歷史:曾先為德國、後為比利時殖民地(國際聯盟/聯合國託管地),殖民統治期間強化了胡圖族與圖西族之間的族群等級劃分。1962 年獨立;數十年的族群緊張與間歇性暴力,最終在 1994 年釀成盧安達大屠殺——短短約一百天內,估計有八十萬至一百萬人(絕大多數為圖西族)遭胡圖族極端分子殺害,直到由保羅·卡加米(Paul Kagame)領導的盧安達愛國陣線(RPF)掌控局勢、結束殺戮。此後數十年間,盧安達推動了國際上頗受矚目的和解與重建工程,包括設立以社區為基礎的加卡卡法庭(gacaca courts)處理大屠殺相關司法案件,同時經濟也持續顯著成長。
  • 現任領導人:總統保羅·卡加米(Paul Kagame),自 2000 年起執政(1994 年至 2000 年間已以副總統/國防部長身分成為實質領導人),歷次連任皆以壓倒性多數勝選;其政府因帶來國家穩定與經濟成長而受到讚譽,但也因限制政治反對勢力與新聞自由,以及盧安達被指涉入鄰國剛果民主共和國衝突等爭議,而遭到國際社會批評。
  • 語言:盧安達語(Kinyarwanda)、法語、英語與斯瓦希里語皆為官方語言,幾乎全國人口皆以盧安達語為母語;2008 年起,英語取代法語成為學校主要教學語言,是大屠殺後盧安達整體疏遠法語圈體制連結的政策轉向之一。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、cia.gov 世界概況(World Factbook)

6)網路迷因介紹——「沒有人有時間管那個」(Ain't Nobody Got Time for That / Sweet Brown)

  • 名稱:「Ain't Nobody Got Time for That」(沒有人有時間管那個),最常與台詞「我得了支氣管炎!」(I got bronchitis!)一起被引用。
  • 起源/歷史:出自 2012 年 4 月美國奧克拉荷馬市一則地方新聞採訪,受訪者金柏莉·「甜甜布朗」·威爾金斯(Kimberly "Sweet Brown" Wilkins)剛從一場公寓火災中逃出。在這段語氣生動、被廣泛引用的採訪中,她描述自己逃出建築物、上氣不接下氣的過程,並脫口說出:「我得了支氣管炎,你們知道吧。沒有人有時間管那個。」
  • 如何走紅:這段訪談片段迅速被剪輯並在 YouTube 與 Twitter 上瘋傳,其節奏鮮明、極易被引用的說話方式,使其迅速成為病毒式話題;短短幾天內就被改編成 Auto-Tune 混音歌曲(風格類似 Gregory Brothers 樂團的《Bed Intruder Song》)、製作成圖片迷因,並被多個全國性談話節目與新聞媒體以「網路文化現象」為題報導。
  • 意義/使用情境:作為一句通用的感嘆語,用來表示某件事不值得投入時間或精力——「ain't nobody got time for that」——常被用來以戲謔、諷刺的語氣回應繁瑣的任務、官僚程序或無謂的紛擾,使用情境早已脫離原本的火災事件本身。
  • 變體/演化:衍生出大量 Auto-Tune 混音與模仿影片,這句台詞也早已脫離新聞採訪的原始語境,融入日常口語表達之中;它與另一句同期竄紅的「Hide yo kids, hide yo wife」並列,同屬 2010 年代初期「反應式新聞採訪」迷因浪潮的代表作,將未經排練的地方新聞片段,轉化為全國性的網路現象。
  • 目前狀態:作為一種主動被積極轉傳的迷因格式,今日已大致退場,但這句台詞本身仍持續存在於日常口語中,成為一句固定的戲謔式推託用語;這段影片至今仍是回顧 2010 年代初期「病毒式地方新聞採訪」迷因浪潮時,經常被引用的重要案例。
    來源:knowyourmeme.com、en.wikipedia.org

7)電影/表演藝術介紹——《單車失竊記》Bicycle Thieves(1948,導演:維多里奧·狄西嘉)

  • 名稱/關鍵人物:《單車失竊記》(義大利文原名 Ladri di biciclette),由維多里奧·狄西嘉(Vittorio De Sica)執導,劇本由狄西嘉與凱撒·扎瓦蒂尼(Cesare Zavattini)共同改編自路易吉·巴托里尼(Luigi Bartolini)1946 年出版的同名小說,由業餘演員蘭貝托·馬齊奧拉尼(Lamberto Maggiorani)飾演父親安東尼奧·里奇(Antonio Ricci)、恩佐·斯泰奧拉(Enzo Staiola)飾演兒子布魯諾(Bruno)。
  • 時代/背景:拍攝於 1948 年,正值戰後義大利社會滿目瘡痍的時期,是義大利新寫實主義(Italian Neorealism)最具代表性的奠基之作——該運動與《羅馬,不設防的城市》(1945)、《大地在波動》(1948)並列,主張在真實場景拍攝、任用非職業演員、刻劃底層勞動者的日常困境,刻意與法西斯時期精緻、棚內拍攝的「白色電話片」形成強烈對比。8 月 20 日介紹過的費里尼《8又1/2》,後來所戲劇化呈現的,正是脫離狄西嘉所奠定的這種寫實風格、轉向藝術電影的過程。
  • 內容概述:安東尼奧是羅馬一名失業男子,終於獲得一份張貼電影海報的工作,但這份工作需要一輛自行車——他剛才才典當了家中床單,才把車贖回來。就在上班第一天,自行車就被偷走。他與年幼的兒子布魯諾接下來一整天在羅馬街頭與市集間四處奔走尋找,遇上冷漠的警察、一位靈媒,以及安東尼奧一度受到誘惑、幾乎自己也淪為竊賊的羞辱時刻,最終迎來全片沉靜卻令人心碎的結尾鏡頭。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:在羅馬真實的街道與市集實景拍攝,而非棚內搭景;全片啟用非職業演員(馬齊奧拉尼本人其實是一名真正的工廠工人);風格樸素、不加修飾,拒絕灑狗血的通俗劇效果、催淚配樂,以及傳統意義上帶有救贖意味的結局;劇情由一連串日常、細微卻不斷累積的屈辱堆疊而成,而非仰賴戲劇性的劇情轉折。
  • 文化/歷史意義:普遍被視為新寫實主義運動的代表傑作,也是將這場運動的美學與道德理念,傳播至全世界的關鍵作品,確立了「電影足以賦予底層貧困與戰後苦難以嚴肅藝術價值,而非僅止於通俗劇題材」這一核心信念。
  • 影響與傳承:直接影響了法國新浪潮、達頓兄弟(Dardenne brothers),以及以阿巴斯·基阿魯斯達米(Abbas Kiarostami)為代表的伊朗社會寫實主義電影,也啟發了世代以降、以實景拍攝與非職業演員為核心的社會寫實主義創作者;本片於 1950 年獲頒奧斯卡榮譽獎(當時尚未設立競賽性質的最佳外語片獎項),並持續在包括 1952 年《視與聽》雜誌影史票選在內的多項權威影史最佳影片評選中名列前茅。
  • 現況/如何欣賞:目前已由 Criterion Collection 完成數位修復並發行;至今仍是全球電影學校課程與新寫實主義回顧展中,用以講授該運動核心理念的基礎教材。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、criterion.com

8)看片方法——旁白敘事與敘事者可信度 Voice-Over Narration & Narrator Reliability(第 28 課)

旁白敘事是在影像軌之上,再疊加一位口述說故事者的聲音,第一個該問、卻很容易被忽略的問題很單純:這是誰的聲音?他此刻說話的位置,是身處故事本身的時間軸之內,還是站在某個更晚、帶著回顧視角的位置發聲?第二個、也是分析上更關鍵的問題,是「可信度」:這段旁白究竟只是重複描述我們眼睛已經看到的畫面(這通常是一種弱點),還是補充了畫面本身無法呈現的資訊(內心世界、反諷、預先知情),又或者,它是否其實在主動誤導觀眾,讓觀眾誤判畫面上真正發生的事?這與第 6 天的敘事結構(處理的是劇情本身的排列順序,無論是否搭配旁白)不同,也與第 4 天的聲音設計課(廣泛涵蓋配樂與畫內外聲音,而非「說話的敘事者」這項特定裝置)不同;旁白本質上是敘事者對這個故事所提出的一種主張,而分析的工作,就是拿這個主張去對照畫面實際呈現的內容,檢驗兩者是否一致。可檢查四件事:這位敘事者是故事內的一個角色,還是外部、全知全能的聲音;旁白與畫面是否曾出現明顯矛盾(這是一種富有意義的張力,不是失誤);旁白使用的時態與語氣,透露出敘事者與所描述事件之間,相隔了多少時間與距離;以及,這部電影最終是否揭露這段旁白本身其實不可信,逼使觀眾回頭重新解讀先前聽到的一切。

實例演練:《鬥陣俱樂部》(Fight Club,1999,導演:大衛·芬奇)——片中無名敘事者(the Narrator)的旁白,自電影第一格畫面便持續運作,以一種帶著嘲諷、疏離、無所不知的語氣,自信地解釋自己的失眠問題、他的工作,以及他與魅力十足的泰勒·德登(Tyler Durden)日益加深的友誼,這種語氣邀請觀眾對他全然信任。第三幕揭露泰勒·德登其實是敘事者本人一個解離型的另一個自我人格——換句話說,他自始至終都是在跟(也是在談論)自己說話——而旁白本身,儘管敘事者自稱對自己的故事無所不知,卻從未揭露這個事實,這回頭揭穿了整段旁白其實出自一位自我欺騙的不可信敘事者。重看此片時,觀眾會發現芬奇早已在前段那些看似「正常」的場景中,刻意埋入以單一畫格、幾乎難以察覺的速度快閃過的泰勒身影——這些畫面明明就藏在眼前,卻完全未被旁白提及過一次。
