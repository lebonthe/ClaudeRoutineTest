ENEnglish version

Topic: the five senses as verbs — oler a (smell of/like), saber a (taste of/like), sonar (sound; ring a bell), tener buena pinta (look good), and the nouns el olor, el sabor, el sonido.

🎯 Learning Goal: say what something smells, tastes and sounds like (Huele a café, Sabe a coco), give quick judgements (¡Qué bien huele!, Suena bien), say that a name or face is familiar (Me suena), and say that food looks good (¡Qué buena pinta!).

Vocabulary: oler (to smell; irregular: huelo, hueles, huele, olemos, oléis, huelen) · oler a + noun (to smell of/like) · el olor (the smell) · saber a + noun (to taste of/like) · el sabor (the flavour) · sonar (o→ue: suena) (to sound; to ring) · me suena (it rings a bell / sounds familiar) · el sonido (the sound) · tener buena / mala pinta (to look good / bad — Spain) · se ve rico (it looks tasty — Latin America) · probar (to taste, try — Day 120) · estar rico / estar bueno (to taste good) · soso (bland) · picante (spicy).

Grammar Point: (1) The thing is the subject, and "like" = a. English says "it smells like coffee"; Spanish says huele a café, sabe a coco. Do not use como here: ✗ huele como café is heard but sounds translated; huele a café is the natural form. (2) Good or bad = adverb, not adjective: huele bien, sabe mal, suena bien — never ✗ huele bueno. (3) Oler has a written h in the stressed forms: o → hue (huelo, huele, huelen) because Spanish words do not begin with ue-; olemos / oléis keep plain o. (4) Transitive vs. intransitive: Huelo algo raro = I smell something strange (I am the smeller); Esto huele raro = this smells strange (it gives off the smell). Do not confuse Yo huelo a ajo = "I smell of garlic"! (5) Saber a ≠ saber. Sé la respuesta = I know the answer; Sabe a limón = it tastes of lemon. The a tells you which meaning. (6) Me suena works like gustar: the familiar thing is the subject and the person is the indirect object: Tu cara me suena (your face looks familiar to me), ¿Te suena el nombre?, No me suenan esos nombres (plural verb for a plural subject). (7) Suena bien is the standard reply to a plan: —¿Quedamos a las ocho? —Suena bien.

Example Sentences:

  • ¡Qué bien huele! ¿Qué estás cocinando? — It smells so good! What are you cooking?
  • Huele a ajo y a jengibre. — It smells of garlic and ginger.
  • Prueba la sopa. ¿A qué sabe? — Taste the soup. What does it taste like?
  • Sabe un poco a coco, pero está sosa. — It tastes a bit of coconut, but it's bland.
  • Ese restaurante me suena. ¿No fuimos allí el año pasado? — That restaurant rings a bell. Didn't we go there last year?
  • Esta tarta tiene muy buena pinta. — This cake looks really good.
  • —¿Cenamos el viernes? —¡Suena genial! — "Shall we have dinner on Friday?" "Sounds great!"

Chapter 28 — 知其雄 (Zhī qí xióng), "Know the Masculine, Keep to the Feminine"

Original text (Wang Bi received text):
知其雄,守其雌,為天下谿。為天下谿,常德不離,復歸於嬰兒。
知其白,守其黑,為天下式。為天下式,常德不忒,復歸於無極。
知其榮,守其辱,為天下谷。為天下谷,常德乃足,復歸於樸。
樸散則為器,聖人用之,則為官長,故大制不割。

Translation: Know the masculine, keep to the feminine, and be the ravine of the world. Being the ravine of the world, constant virtue never leaves you, and you return to being an infant. Know the white, keep to the black, and be the model of the world. Being the model of the world, constant virtue does not err, and you return to the limitless. Know glory, keep to humility, and be the valley of the world. Being the valley of the world, constant virtue is sufficient, and you return to the uncarved block. When the uncarved block is split, it becomes vessels; the sage, using it, becomes the head of officials. Therefore the great cutting does not cut.

Structure and key images:

  • Three parallel stanzas. Each pairs one quality you know (strong, bright, honoured) with its opposite that you keep (soft, dark, humble), names a low or receptive place (ravine 谿, model 式, valley 谷), and ends with a "return" (復歸): to the infant (嬰兒, Ch. 10, 20, 55), to the limitless (無極), and to the uncarved block (樸, Ch. 15, 19, 32, 37).
  • "Know" but "keep". The chapter does not tell you to be weak or ignorant. You fully understand strength and brightness — and then you choose to stand on the lower, softer side. Wang Bi explains 雄 as "the kind that goes first" and 雌 as "the kind that goes last": the sage knows how to lead but positions himself behind (Ch. 7: 後其身而身先).
  • Ravine and valley. Water flows down into low places; the low place receives everything (Ch. 6 谷神, Ch. 8 上善若水, Ch. 66 江海所以能為百谷王者 以其善下之).
  • 樸 and 器 — the uncarved block and the vessel. Raw wood can become any tool; once carved, it is only one tool. People who "become vessels" are useful but limited (compare Confucius, Analects 2.12, 君子不器 "the gentleman is not a vessel"). The sage uses people according to their shapes but himself stays uncarved.
  • 大制不割 — "the great cutting does not cut". Wang Bi: the great ruler "takes the hearts of all under heaven as his own heart, so there is no cutting." Good government, like a skilled craftsman, works with the grain instead of chopping things into artificial pieces (compare Cook Ding, Zhuangzi Ch. 3, discussed in this series earlier).

Textual history (scholarly views, not settled facts): the Zhuangzi "Tianxia" chapter quotes Lao Dan with only two couplets, roughly "know the masculine, keep the feminine… be the ravine; know the white, keep to humiliation (辱)… be the valley". Several modern scholars (for example Yi Shunding 易順鼎 and Gao Heng 高亨) therefore argue that the received middle stanza (白/黑, 無極) and the 榮 in the third stanza were added later; 白 paired with 辱 (in the old sense of "soiled, dark") fits the Tianxia quotation. The Mawangdui silk manuscripts already contain three stanzas, so any change happened early. The term 無極 later became central in Song Neo-Confucian cosmology (Zhou Dunyi's 無極而太極).

Philosophical implications: (1) Power that holds back is stronger than power that pushes: whoever stands in the low place collects the flow. (2) Knowledge and humility are not opposites — real humility requires knowing exactly what you are giving up. (3) Potential (樸) is worth more than any single function (器); keep a part of yourself uncarved.

Modern reflection: in a team, the person who can do the impressive work but chooses to listen, review and support often becomes the "valley" everyone brings problems to. In a career, specialisation makes you a useful vessel; Chapter 28 asks whether you also keep some "uncarved" capacity — curiosity, beginner's mind — so that you are not only one tool. And as a leader, "the great cutting does not cut" means designing rules along the grain of how people actually work, instead of slicing them into boxes.

白話翻譯:知道什麼是雄強,卻守住雌柔,就成為天下的溪谷。成為天下的溪谷,恆常的德就不會離開,回到嬰兒般的狀態。知道什麼是光明,卻守住幽暗,就成為天下的法式。成為天下的法式,恆常的德就不會出差錯,回到無窮無極。知道什麼是榮耀,卻守住卑辱,就成為天下的山谷。成為天下的山谷,恆常的德才會充足,回到未經雕琢的原木。原木剖開就成為各種器物;聖人運用它,就成為百官之長。所以最好的裁製是不切割的。

結構與關鍵意象:

  • 三段平行結構。每段都把一個你「知道」的特質(雄強、光明、榮耀)和你「守住」的相反面(雌柔、幽暗、卑辱)配對,點出一個低下、能容受的位置(谿、式、谷),最後都以「復歸」作結:回到嬰兒(第 10、20、55 章)、回到無極、回到樸(第 15、19、32、37 章)。
  • 「知」而「守」。這一章不是叫你軟弱或無知。你完全懂得什麼是強與明——然後選擇站在較低、較柔的那一邊。王弼解釋:「雄,先之屬;雌,後之屬」——聖人懂得如何領先,卻把自己放在後面(第 7 章:後其身而身先)。
  • 谿與谷。水往低處流;低處能接納一切(第 6 章谷神、第 8 章上善若水、第 66 章「江海所以能為百谷王者,以其善下之」)。
  • 樸與器——原木與器物。原木可以做成任何工具;一旦雕成,就只是一種工具。「成器」的人有用但受限(可對照《論語·為政》2.12「君子不器」)。聖人依每個人的形狀來用人,自己卻保持未雕琢的狀態。
  • 大制不割。王弼:「大制者,以天下之心為心,故無割也。」好的治理就像高明的工匠,順著紋理做事,而不是把東西硬切成人為的碎塊(可對照本系列先前介紹過的《莊子·養生主》庖丁解牛)。

文本史(學者看法,並非定論):《莊子·天下》引老聃只有兩組對句,大意是「知其雄,守其雌,為天下谿;知其白,守其辱,為天下谷」。因此近代多位學者(例如易順鼎、高亨)主張,通行本中間那段(白/黑、無極)以及第三段的「榮」是後人增補;「白」與「辱」(古義為「污黑」)相對,正好符合〈天下〉的引文。馬王堆帛書已經有三段,所以若有改動,也發生得很早。「無極」一詞後來成為宋代理學宇宙論的核心概念(周敦頤「無極而太極」)。

哲學意涵:(1)懂得收斂的力量比一味推進的力量更強:站在低處的人,才能匯聚眾流。(2)知識與謙卑並不對立——真正的謙卑,需要清楚知道自己放棄的是什麼。(3)潛能(樸)比任何單一功能(器)更有價值;讓自己保留一部分未經雕琢的空間。

現代反思:在團隊裡,明明能做出亮眼成果、卻選擇傾聽、審查與支援的人,往往會成為大家帶著問題來找的「山谷」。在職涯上,專業化讓你成為有用的「器」;第 28 章則問:你是否還保留一些「未雕琢」的能力——好奇心、初學者之心——讓自己不只是一種工具?而身為領導者,「大制不割」意味著順著人們實際工作的紋理來設計規則,而不是把人切進一個個格子裡。

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 5: next to / beside / by / near (Stage 1, basic spatial concepts).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese uses 旁邊 for "next to / beside / by" and 附近 for "near", and learners often treat all four as the same. The real question is how close and whether anything is in between.
  • Core concept — a distance ruler: next to = directly side by side, touching or with nothing in between (the next building, the next seat). beside = the same distance as next to, slightly more written or literary. by = right at the side of a feature — a window, a door, the sea, a river, the road — with an "at the edge of" feeling. near = in the general area; there may be streets or other things in between (often walking distance). close to is a near-synonym of near.
  • Examples: I live next to a temple, so I hear the drums every morning. / She sat beside me on the train. / Can we get a table by the window? / They have a small house by the sea in Taitung. / My office is near Taipei Main Station — about a ten-minute walk. / Is there a convenience store near here? / Park your scooter by the road, not on the sidewalk.
  • Meaning change: "I live next to the station" = my building is right beside it (very loud!); "I live near the station" = I can walk there; "I live by the station" = right at it, close beside it (similar to next to, less exact).
  • Common mistakes: (1) "near from / near to my home" → near my home (no extra preposition; "near to" exists in British English but sounds formal). (2) "the shop next my house" → next to my house (next needs to; or use next door: "the shop next door"). (3) "Beside, I also like hiking" → Besides (with s = "also, in addition"; beside without s = at the side of).

  • Key takeaways: next to / beside = 0 distance; by = at the edge of a feature; near = same neighbourhood, things in between.

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:「旁邊」和「附近」在英文裡到底差多遠?中文的「旁邊」可以對應 next to、beside、by,「附近」對應 near / close to。很多人把四個當成同一個字隨便換,但英文母語者選字時,腦中其實有一把尺:到底多近?中間有沒有隔著東西?

2. 核心概念:一把距離的尺

  • next to:緊緊並排,中間什麼都沒有。隔壁那棟樓、旁邊那個座位。想像兩個人肩並肩站著。

  • Historical and cultural background: the French New Wave was at its height (1959–62). Varda had made La Pointe Courte (1955), often called a precursor of the New Wave, before Truffaut and Godard; she belonged to the "Left Bank" circle with Alain Resnais and Chris Marker, who were closer to literature, documentary and politics. The film is set on 21 June 1961, the longest day of the year, while the Algerian War was still going on — a war that is mostly off-screen but present in news on the radio and in Antoine, who must return to Algeria that night.
  • Content overview: Cléo, a young and successful pop singer, is waiting for the results of a biopsy and fears she has cancer. The film follows her through Paris between about 5:00 and 6:30 p.m. — despite the title, it ends before 7 — in 13 titled chapters with on-screen clock times, close to real time. She visits a tarot reader (the opening, the only colour scene), shops for a hat, rehearses with her composer and lyricist, rides in a taxi, visits Dorothée, sees a short silent comedy in a cinema projection booth, and in Parc Montsouris meets Antoine, a talkative soldier. Together they go to the Salpêtrière hospital, where a doctor tells her almost casually that she will need two months of radiotherapy and should be fine.
  • Style and techniques: (1) Near real time: chapter titles count the minutes, so we feel time as Cléo feels it — slow, anxious, then suddenly open. (2) Mirrors and looking: in the first half Cléo constantly checks herself in mirrors and is looked at as a pretty object ("being beautiful is being alive", she believes). (3) The turning point: after her songwriters tease her and she sings the sad song "Sans toi" — the camera moves in, the background goes black, and she breaks down — she takes off her blonde wig, changes into a black dress and goes out alone. From then on she looks at the city, its faces and strangers, instead of being looked at. (4) Documentary street shooting: real Paris streets and passers-by, often with hidden or handheld camera. (5) Point of view through subjective shots (the faces staring at her in the café). (6) Film within a film: the silent short "Les fiancés du pont Mac Donald", starring Jean-Luc Godard and Anna Karina, about a man who sees the world as dark because of his black sunglasses — a playful mirror of Cléo's own fear.
  • Cultural and historical significance: it is one of the few major New Wave films made by a woman and told from a woman's inner experience. Feminist critics later read it as a study of the gaze: a woman moves from being an image to being a subject. It also connects personal fear of death with a society that is quietly at war.
  • Influence and legacy: it shaped real-time and "one afternoon in a city" films (from Richard Linklater's walking films to many indie dramas), and it is central to Varda's reputation as "the grandmother of the New Wave". Varda continued for six more decades (Vagabond, 1985; The Gleaners and I, 2000; Faces Places, 2017) and received an honorary Academy Award in 2017. In Sight and Sound's 2022 critics' poll, Cléo from 5 to 7 ranked in the top 20 (No. 14).
  • Where to see it today: restored by Varda's company Ciné-Tamaris; released by the Criterion Collection (including in its Complete Films of Agnès Varda box set) and regularly screened in Varda retrospectives and on arthouse streaming services (availability differs by country).
  • Facts vs. interpretation: the dates, credits, real-time structure and chapters are facts. Reading the wig scene as a feminist "awakening" is a widely shared critical interpretation, not something the film states.

  • 影響與傳承:它影響了「即時」與「城市裡的一個下午」類型的電影(從李察·林克雷特的漫步電影到許多獨立劇情片),也是華達被稱為「新浪潮祖母」的核心作品。華達之後又創作了六十年(《無法無天》Vagabond,1985;《拾穗者》The Gleaners and I,2000;《最酷的旅伴》Faces Places,2017),並於 2017 年獲頒奧斯卡榮譽獎。在《視與聽》(Sight and Sound)2022 年影評人票選中,《五點到七點的克萊歐》名列前 20(第 14 名)。

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 111: Ecocriticism — Reading Nature, Land and Non-Human Life on Screen
第 8 段 — 看片方法第 111 課:生態批評——解讀銀幕上的自然、土地與非人類生命

This continues the "critical lenses" unit (psychoanalytic, structuralist, postcolonial and queer readings in Lessons 107–110). 本課延續「批評視角」單元(第 107–110 課:精神分析、結構主義、後殖民、酷兒解讀)。

ENEnglish version

1. Today's Analytical Method

Ecocriticism asks how a film represents the natural world, land, animals and the relationship between humans and their environment — and what values those choices carry. The term was coined by William Rueckert (1978) in literary studies and popularised by Cheryll Glotfelty and Harold Fromm's The Ecocriticism Reader (1996). Film scholars such as David Ingram (Green Screen, 2000), Sean Cubitt (EcoMedia, 2005), Scott MacDonald ("Toward an Eco-Cinema", 2004) and Nadia Bozak (The Cinematic Footprint, 2012) brought it to cinema. It matters because almost every film shows a landscape, weather, animals or resources — and the way it frames them teaches audiences, often invisibly, what nature is for.

2. Detailed Explanation

Ask six questions:

  1. Backdrop, resource, character or agent? Is nature just scenery behind the humans, something to be used (timber, oil, land), a character with a point of view, or a force that acts on its own?
  2. Whose land, who benefits, who pays? (environmental justice) Who profits from changing the environment, and whose body, home or culture absorbs the damage? This links to Lesson 109 (postcolonial) and Lesson 102 (ideological criticism).
  3. Scale and time. Does the film stay at human scale (a few days, one hero) or show geological, seasonal or generational time? Long takes and slow pacing can make us dwell on a place; MacDonald argues this "retrains perception".
  4. How are animals framed? As props, food, threats, cute human-like companions (anthropomorphism) — or as beings with their own lives? Whose gaze do we share?
  5. What does the ending do to the environment? Is balance restored, does everything return to normal, or does the film admit irreversible loss?
  6. (Optional) The film's own footprint. How was it made — real locations disturbed, animals used, digital worlds instead? Bozak reminds us that cinema itself consumes energy and materials.

Common misconceptions: (1) Ecocriticism is not only for "environmental films" — a car-chase action film is a rich text about oil, roads and speed. (2) It is not a moral test of whether the film is "green enough"; it describes how the film thinks about nature, including contradictions. (3) A beautiful landscape is not automatically pro-nature: postcard beauty can also turn land into a commodity. Keep separate what you observe (shots, sounds, plot outcomes) and what you interpret (the film's values).

3. Concrete Film Example — Princess Mononoke (もののけ姫, 1997), dir. Hayao Miyazaki, Studio Ghibli; music by Joe Hisaishi

Sequence: the climax at the forest lake at dawn: Lady Eboshi shoots the Forest Spirit (Shishigami) as it changes into the giant, translucent Night-Walker (Didarabotchi); its head is cut off; the headless body spreads a deadly flow that kills the forest and floods Iron Town; Ashitaka and San return the head; the god collapses into a great wind, and green life returns.

  1. What to look for — nature as agent: the Forest Spirit is not scenery. It walks, gives and takes life (flowers bloom and die under its feet earlier in the film). When it is wounded, the environment itself reacts — the film turns "nature" into the most powerful actor in the story. Observation.
  2. Who benefits, who pays: Eboshi is not a simple villain. Iron Town gives work and dignity to women freed from brothels and to people with leprosy; its prosperity depends on cutting the forest for charcoal and iron. The samurai and the emperor's hunters want the god's head for power and supposed immortality. The film shows environmental damage tied to real human needs, which makes the conflict harder. Interpretation grounded in plot.
  3. Scale and image: the Night-Walker is shown in huge wide shots against the night sky, transparent and star-like; humans are tiny. Then the death-flow is animated as dark, oily matter crawling over moss and trees — the frame fills with destruction at landscape scale, not just one character's death.
  4. Sound: during the rampage the music largely gives way to wind, roaring and silence; when the head is returned, Hisaishi's theme comes back as the land turns green.
  5. The ending — restoration or loss? The returning life is grass and flowers on bare hills, not the ancient forest. San says (in the English version) roughly that even if the trees grow back, it won't be the Forest Spirit's forest any more; Ashitaka answers that the Forest Spirit is life itself and cannot die. Eboshi, who lost her arm, says they will start over and build a better village. A single small kodama (tree spirit) appears in the forest in the final scene.
  6. Deeper meaning: the film refuses both easy answers — "humans are evil" and "everything heals". Something sacred is permanently gone (an irreversible ending), yet coexistence is still possible. The lone kodama is open: a sign of hope, or a sign of how little is left. That ambiguity is the ecocritical point.

4. Practical Viewing Exercise

Watch the first ten minutes of the Taiwanese documentary Beyond Beauty: Taiwan from Above (看見台灣, 2013, dir. Chi Po-lin 齊柏林), shot from helicopters. Write down three shots of beautiful landscape and three shots of damage (landslides, sand mining, illegal farms on slopes). Then ask: does the aerial "God's-eye" view make the damage feel urgent, or does it make destruction look beautiful too? Who is shown on the ground — and who is not?

5. Key Takeaways

  • Ask what role nature plays: backdrop, resource, character or agent.
  • Follow the cost: who benefits from changing the land and who pays.
  • Check the ending: restored balance, return to normal, or admitted loss.

6. Suggested Further Viewing

Koyaanisqatsi (1982, dir. Godfrey Reggio; music by Philip Glass) — a film with no dialogue that contrasts landscapes with accelerated cities; ideal for practising questions 3 (scale and time) and 1 (nature as backdrop vs. agent).

中繁體中文版

1. 今日分析方法

生態批評(Ecocriticism)探討一部電影如何呈現自然世界、土地、動物,以及人與環境的關係——還有這些選擇背後帶著什麼價值觀。這個詞由威廉·魯克特(William Rueckert)於 1978 年在文學研究中提出,並因格羅特費爾蒂(Cheryll Glotfelty)與弗洛姆(Harold Fromm)編的《生態批評讀本》(The Ecocriticism Reader,1996)而普及。大衛·英格拉姆(David Ingram,《綠色銀幕》Green Screen,2000)、肖恩·丘比特(Sean Cubitt,《生態媒體》EcoMedia,2005)、史考特·麥唐納(Scott MacDonald,〈邁向生態電影〉,2004)與娜迪亞·博扎克(Nadia Bozak,《電影的足跡》The Cinematic Footprint,2012)等學者把它帶進電影研究。它之所以重要,是因為幾乎每部電影都會出現風景、天氣、動物或資源——而電影框取它們的方式,常常在不知不覺中教觀眾:自然是「拿來做什麼的」。

2. 詳細說明

問六個問題:

  1. 背景、資源、角色,還是行動者?自然只是人物背後的布景、被利用的東西(木材、石油、土地)、有自己觀點的角色,還是會自己行動的力量?
  2. 誰的土地?誰得利?誰付出代價?(環境正義)改變環境讓誰獲利?誰的身體、家園或文化承受損害?這和第 109 課(後殖民)與第 102 課(意識形態批評)相連。
  3. 尺度與時間。電影停留在人的尺度(幾天、一個英雄),還是呈現地質、季節或世代的時間?長鏡頭與緩慢節奏能讓我們在一個地方停留;麥唐納認為這能「重新訓練知覺」。
  4. 動物如何被框取?是道具、食物、威脅、像人一樣可愛的夥伴(擬人化)——還是擁有自己生命的存在?我們借用的是誰的目光?
  5. 結局對環境做了什麼?平衡被恢復了?一切回到原狀?還是電影承認了無法挽回的失去?
  6. (選用)電影本身的足跡。它怎麼拍的——干擾了真實的外景地、使用了動物,還是改用數位世界?博扎克提醒我們,電影本身也在消耗能源與材料。

常見誤解:(1)生態批評不只適用於「環保電影」——一部飛車動作片,其實是關於石油、道路與速度的豐富文本。(2)它不是檢查電影「夠不夠環保」的道德考試;它描述電影如何看待自然,包括其中的矛盾。(3)美麗的風景不等於親近自然:明信片式的美,也可能把土地變成商品。請把你觀察到的(鏡頭、聲音、劇情結果)和你詮釋的(電影的價值觀)分開。

3. 具體電影實例——《魔法公主》(もののけ姫,1997),導演宮崎駿,吉卜力工作室;配樂久石讓

段落:黎明時分森林湖畔的高潮戲:黑帽大人(艾波西)在山獸神(シシ神)變身為巨大透明的「夜行巨人」(ディダラボッチ)時開槍射擊;祂的頭被砍下;無頭的身體湧出致命的流體,殺死森林、淹沒達達拉城;阿席達卡與小桑把頭歸還;神化為一陣大風倒下,綠色生命重新回來。

  1. 要注意什麼——自然是行動者:山獸神不是布景。祂會行走、會給予與奪走生命(片中稍早,花草在祂腳下綻放又枯萎)。祂受傷時,環境本身會產生反應——電影把「自然」變成故事中最強大的行動者。觀察。
  2. 誰得利、誰付出代價:黑帽大人不是單純的反派。達達拉城讓從妓院贖身的女性與痲瘋病患得到工作與尊嚴;城的繁榮卻建立在砍伐森林燒炭煉鐵上。武士與天皇派來的獵人想取神的頭,為了權力和傳說中的長生不老。電影讓環境破壞與真實的人類需求綁在一起,使衝突更難解。以劇情為根據的詮釋。
  3. 尺度與影像:夜行巨人以巨大的遠景襯著夜空出現,透明、如星辰般;人類渺小如點。接著死亡之流被畫成黑色、油狀的物質,爬過苔蘚與樹木——畫面被地景尺度的毀滅填滿,而不只是某個角色的死亡。
  4. 聲音:暴走過程中,音樂大多讓位給風聲、咆哮與寂靜;頭被歸還時,久石讓的主題旋律隨著大地轉綠而回來。
  5. 結局——恢復還是失去?回來的生命是光禿山丘上的草與花,而不是古老的森林。小桑(英文版)大意是說:就算樹木長回來,那也不再是山獸神的森林了;阿席達卡回答:山獸神就是生命本身,祂不會死。失去一條手臂的黑帽大人說,大家要重新開始,建一個更好的村子。最後一場戲,森林裡出現一隻小小的木靈(こだま)。
  6. 更深的意義:電影拒絕兩種簡單答案——「人類是邪惡的」和「一切都會癒合」。某種神聖的東西永遠消失了(不可逆的結局),但共存仍然可能。那隻孤單的木靈是開放的:是希望的徵兆,還是剩下多麼少的徵兆?這份曖昧正是生態批評要抓住的重點。

4. 實作看片練習

看台灣紀錄片《看見台灣》(Beyond Beauty: Taiwan from Above,2013,導演齊柏林)的前十分鐘,這部片以直升機空拍。寫下三個美麗風景的鏡頭和三個破壞的鏡頭(山崩、盜採砂石、山坡上的違法農地)。然後問自己:空拍的「上帝視角」讓破壞顯得迫切,還是讓毀滅也變得好看?地面上出現了誰——又有誰沒出現?

5. 重點整理

  • 問自然扮演什麼角色:背景、資源、角色,還是行動者。
  • 追蹤代價:改變土地讓誰得利、誰付出。
  • 檢查結局:恢復平衡、回到原狀,還是承認失去。

6. 延伸觀賞建議

Koyaanisqatsi(片名是霍皮族語「失衡的生活」,1982,導演高佛雷·雷吉奧;配樂菲利普·格拉斯)——一部沒有對白的電影,把自然風景與加速的城市對照;非常適合練習第 3 題(尺度與時間)和第 1 題(自然是布景還是行動者)。
