3) Spanish Lesson — Day 79 (10 min, Salir / Dejar / Irse: Three Ways to "Leave")

Today covers the verbs of leaving: salir (go out of a place, depart; also "go out with" someone), dejar (leave something behind, let, or quit with dejar de) and irse (go away from where you are). Key trap: you salir from a place but you dejar an object, and "I'm leaving now" is usually me voy.

  • ¿A qué hora sales del trabajo? — What time do you leave work?
  • Salgo a las seis, pero antes dejo estos papeles en tu mesa. — I leave at six, but first I'll leave these papers on your desk.
  • ¿Ya te vas? — Are you leaving already?
  • Sí, me voy a casa. No dejes la puerta abierta. — Yes, I'm heading home. Don't leave the door open.

4) Zhuangzi — Essay 15: 刻意 (Kè Yì) — "Constrained in Will"

The eighth chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇), following "The Turning of Heaven." It sorts people by what they strain after: mountain-dwellers who cultivate lofty, withdrawn conduct and scorn the world; scholars who preach humane government and moral training; statesmen who chase achievement and fame; river-and-sea hermits who idle at leisure; and the "Dao yin" (導引) practitioners who breathe exercises to live long like the Peng ancestor Pengzu. Zhuangzi says each is "constraining the will" (刻意), pushing against the grain. The ideal is instead the one who needs none of these: "without constraining the will, yet lofty; without benevolence and righteousness, yet cultivated; without achievement and fame, yet well governed; without rivers and seas, yet at leisure; without breathing exercises, yet long-lived; forgetting everything, yet possessing everything" (不刻意而高,無仁義而修,無功名而治,無江海而閒,不導引而壽,無不忘也,無不有也). The chapter closes on the sage's way of living: life is flowing movement, death is transformation; keep the spirit pure, unmixed, calm and unclouded, and it remains whole. This is the origin of the idea of "purity of spirit" (純素之道) in later Daoist self-cultivation. (Summarized from general knowledge of the text; verify wording against an edition.)

5) Country / Region Spotlight — The Philippines (菲律賓)

  • Location & territory size: An archipelago of more than 7,600 islands in Southeast Asia, lying between the Philippine Sea and the South China Sea, south of Taiwan across the Bashi Channel (the Batanes islands are only about 190 km from Taiwan's southern tip). Land area is about 300,000 km², roughly 8 times Taiwan (about 36,200 km²) and close to Italy or the US state of Arizona. The largest islands are Luzon (home of the capital, Manila) and Mindanao.
  • Population: About 116.3 million (2025), making it one of the ten or so most populous countries in Asia; Metro Manila alone has roughly 14 million people.
  • Religion: Predominantly Christian: roughly 80% Roman Catholic, with Protestant and independent churches (such as Iglesia ni Cristo), and a Muslim minority of around 6% concentrated in Mindanao and the Sulu archipelago (the Bangsamoro region). It is one of only two majority-Catholic countries in Asia (with Timor-Leste).
  • Language: Filipino (standardized from Tagalog) and English are official; more than 180 other languages are spoken, including Cebuano, Ilocano and Hiligaynon.
  • Economy: GDP of about US$461.6 billion in 2024 and GDP per capita of about US$3,985 (per a World Bank-based indicator listing). The World Bank projects growth of about 4.6% in 2026, below the government's downgraded 5-6% target; 2025 growth was estimated at around 4.8-5%. Key sectors are business-process outsourcing (call centers), overseas-worker remittances, electronics and agriculture.
  • History: Austronesian settlers arrived thousands of years ago; Ferdinand Magellan reached the islands in 1521 and Spain colonized them from 1565 (named after King Philip II) until 1898. After the Spanish-American War the US ruled until 1946, with a Japanese occupation in 1942-45. Independence was recognized on July 4, 1946. Ferdinand Marcos Sr. declared martial law in 1972 and was ousted by the 1986 People Power Revolution, which became a model for peaceful democratic change.
  • Current leader: President Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr., in office since June 30, 2022, and limited to a single six-year term (ending 2028); Vice President is Sara Duterte (a separately elected office).
  • Fun fact: The Philippines is among the most storm-exposed countries on earth, sits on the Pacific "Ring of Fire," and its Banaue Rice Terraces are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — The Hampster Dance (倉鼠舞)

  • Name: The Hampster Dance (also written "Hamster Dance"), one of the earliest viral web phenomena: a page of rows of animated hamster GIFs dancing to a looped, sped-up song.
  • Origin / history: Created in 1998 by Deidre LaCarte, a Canadian art student, as a personal GeoCities page. By her own account it began as a friendly contest with her sister and a friend over whose page could get the most visitors; she picked hamsters because she had kept them as pets. A soundtrack was added later: a sped-up sample of "Whistle Stop," a song by Roger Miller from Disney's Robin Hood (1973).
  • How it became popular: It spread by email chains and word of mouth in the late-1990s web, when sharing a link was the main way to go viral; the page's traffic reportedly reached many millions of visits. A commercial "Hampton the Hampster" song release followed around 2000.
  • Meaning and usage: It has no deeper meaning: it is pure, absurd, repetitive joy, shared as a wink at the strangeness of the early web. It is remembered as a symbol of "old internet" nostalgia.
  • Variations / evolution: It inspired many animated-GIF-and-loop pages, "dancing" parody sites and later spiritual successors such as Nyan Cat; the exact list of spin-offs was not verified this run.
  • Current status: It lives on mostly as a nostalgic reference and in web archives and retrospectives about early internet culture.

7) Film / Performing-Arts Spotlight — Rakugo (落語, Japanese Comic Storytelling)

  • Name and key figures: Rakugo (落語, literally "fallen words," referring to the "ochi" punchline), a traditional Japanese sit-down comic storytelling art performed by one performer, the rakugoka. Historical figures include Anrakuan Sakuden (1554-1642), often called an ancestor of the form, and Sanyūtei Enchō (1839-1900), a master of ghost stories and new works.
  • Era / background: It grew out of the entertainers who told humorous anecdotes to feudal lords and townspeople, and took its recognizable form in the Edo period (17th-19th c.), with distinct Edo (Tokyo) and Kamigata (Osaka/Kyoto) traditions.
  • Content overview: A performance has three parts: the makura (a light preface that warms up the audience), the hondai (the main story, usually a comic tale of merchants, wives, landlords, clever fools and tricksters) and the ochi (the punchline). Famous stories include "Jugemu" (about a child with an absurdly long name), "Meguro no Sanma" (the saury of Meguro, about a lord who tastes a humble grilled fish) and "Shibahama" (a drunkard's dream about finding money). Some stories are ghost tales or tearjerkers, so the art is not only comic.
  • Artistic style and techniques: The performer kneels on a cushion (zabuton) with no costumes or sets, using only a folded hand towel (tenugui) and a paper fan (sensu) as props that become a pipe, chopsticks, a book, a sword or a letter. A single person plays every character by turning the head left and right (kamishimo) and changing voice, posture and gaze. The audience's imagination builds the world.
  • Cultural / historical significance: It is regarded as one of Japan's major performing arts alongside kabuki and bunraku, and a key form of urban commoner humor and language; a teacher-and-apprentice system continues, and ranks run from zenza (opening act) to futatsume and shin'uchi (headliner).
  • Influence and legacy: It influenced Japanese comedy, manzai and television; the manga and anime Showa Genroku Rakugo Shinju (by Haruko Kumota) brought it to a new generation. English-language rakugo has been performed internationally, for example by the Canadian-born Katsura Sunshine.
  • Current status / where to experience it: Regular performances are held at yose theaters in Tokyo, such as Suehirotei (Shinjuku), Asakusa Engei Hall, Suzumoto Engeijo (Ueno) and Ikebukuro Engekijo, and in Osaka at Tenma Tenjin Hanjotei. Recordings and streaming clips with subtitles exist; check current schedules.

Sources: general performing-arts reference knowledge; names, dates and venue details were not re-verified live, so confirm schedules locally.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — The Establishing Shot & Spatial Orientation (Lesson 65)

Previous lessons dealt with cuts, angles, sound and pacing within a scene. This lesson asks the first question of any scene: where are we, and how is the space laid out? An establishing shot (usually a wide or aerial view at the start of a scene or film) shows the location, its scale and sometimes the time of day; a re-establishing shot returns to that wide view later to reorient us after close-ups. How to use it: (1) note what the first shot of each scene tells you about place, time and mood, and what it deliberately withholds; (2) compare the scale of people to the setting, since a tiny figure in a vast space says "isolated" or "overpowered" before anyone speaks; (3) notice when the film re-establishes the space, because that usually marks a change in who is present or who has power; (4) ask whether the film maps the geography clearly (so we can follow the action) or keeps it confusing on purpose; (5) look for the subversion, such as an establishing shot that shows an impossible or misleading space.

Worked example: The Shining (1980, dir. Stanley Kubrick), the opening and the hedge maze. The film opens with aerial shots over a mountain lake (Saint Mary Lake in Glacier National Park, with Wild Goose Island in view) following a tiny yellow Volkswagen along a winding road, to an eerie electronic version of the "Dies Irae" chant. The wide, aerial view does two jobs: it establishes that the Torrance family is traveling into remote wilderness, and it makes the car small, almost insect-like, against a huge landscape, so isolation is communicated before the Overlook Hotel even appears. Later, Kubrick re-establishes the geography: Jack looks down at a scale model of the hotel's hedge maze, and the film dissolves from the model to the real maze where Wendy and Danny walk, so we learn the layout from above before it becomes a trap on the ground. The pattern is: establish scale and place, then reuse that map when the danger arrives. (Film details are from general knowledge; confirm exact locations and credits against a source.)

6)網路迷因介紹——Hampster Dance(倉鼠舞)

  • 名稱:Hampster Dance(也寫作 "Hamster Dance"),最早的病毒式網路現象之一:一個網頁上排滿一排排跳舞的倉鼠動態 GIF,配上循環播放、加速的歌曲。
  • 起源/歷史:1998 年由加拿大藝術系學生 Deidre LaCarte 以個人 GeoCities 網頁的形式創作。依她自己的說法,起因是與姊妹和朋友打賭看誰的網頁流量更高;她選倉鼠是因為養過倉鼠當寵物。配樂是後來才加入:把 Roger Miller 為迪士尼《羅賓漢》(1973)所唱的 "Whistle Stop" 加速後取樣。
  • 如何流行:在 1990 年代末的網路上靠電子郵件連鎖信與口耳相傳散播,當時分享連結是爆紅的主要方式;網頁流量據稱達數百萬次造訪。約 2000 年又有商業化的「Hampton the Hampster」歌曲發行。
  • 意義與使用情境:沒有更深的含意:就是純粹、荒謬、重複的快樂,用來對早期網路的怪異風格眨眼致意。它被視為「老網路」懷舊的象徵。
  • 變體/演變:啟發了許多 GIF 加循環音樂的網頁、「跳舞」惡搞網站,以及後來的精神繼承者,例如 Nyan Cat;具體衍生清單本次未查證。
  • 現況:主要作為懷舊梗,以及網路存檔與早期網路文化回顧中的參考而存在。

來源:網路歷史的一般知識;名稱、日期與銷量數字未即時重新查證,細節請視為概略。

7)電影/表演藝術介紹——落語(Rakugo,日本單人喜劇說書)

  • 名稱與主要人物:落語(字面意思「落下的話」,指「落ち」即結尾包袱),日本傳統的坐式喜劇說書藝術,由一位表演者「落語家」獨自演出。歷史人物包括常被視為始祖的安樂庵策傳(1554–1642),以及擅長怪談與新作的三遊亭圓朝(1839–1900)。
  • 時代/背景:源自對大名與市井百姓講述幽默軼事的藝人,在江戶時代(17–19 世紀)成形,並有江戶(東京)與上方(大阪/京都)兩大傳統。
  • 內容概述:一場演出分三部分:「枕」(輕鬆的開場白,暖場)、「本題」(主要故事,多為商人、妻子、房東、聰明的傻子與騙子的喜劇)與「落ち」(結尾包袱)。名段包括〈壽限無〉(一個名字長得離譜的孩子)、〈目黑的秋刀魚〉(殿下嚐到庶民烤魚的故事)與〈芝濱〉(酒鬼夢到撿到錢)。也有鬼故事與催淚的人情噺,所以並非只有搞笑。
  • 藝術風格與技法:表演者跪坐在坐墊(座布團)上,沒有服裝與布景,只用摺好的手巾與一把扇子當道具,它們可以變成菸管、筷子、書、刀或信。一個人靠左右轉頭(上下)、變換聲音、姿態與視線扮演所有角色。世界由觀眾的想像建立。
  • 文化/歷史意義:被視為日本主要表演藝術之一,與歌舞伎、文樂並列,也是都市庶民幽默與語言的重要載體;師徒制延續至今,階級從前座、二目到真打(壓軸)。
  • 影響與傳承:影響了日本喜劇、漫才與電視;漫畫與動畫《昭和元祿落語心中》(雲田晴子作)讓新一代認識落語。英語落語也在國際上演出,例如加拿大出身的桂 Sunshine。
  • 現況/何處可看:東京的寄席定期演出,如新宿末廣亭、淺草演藝廳、上野鈴本演芸場與池袋演芸場,大阪則有天滿天神繁昌亭。也有附字幕的錄影與串流片段;請查詢最新場次。

來源:表演藝術的一般參考知識;名稱、日期與場地細節未即時重新查證,場次請自行確認。

8)看片方法——開場鏡頭與空間定位(第 65 課)

之前的課程談的是場景內的剪接、角度、聲音與節奏。這一課問任何場景的第一個問題:我們在哪裡?空間是怎麼配置的?開場鏡頭(establishing shot)通常是場景或影片開頭的遠景或空拍,交代地點、規模,有時也交代時間;重新定位鏡頭(re-establishing shot)則是在一連串特寫之後,再回到遠景幫觀眾重新找回方位。使用方法:(1)記下每場戲第一個鏡頭告訴你的地點、時間、氣氛,以及它刻意隱藏什麼;(2)比較人物與環境的比例,在任何人開口前,廣大空間中的小人影就已說出「孤立」或「被壓倒」;(3)留意影片何時重新定位空間,那通常意味著在場人物或權力關係改變;(4)問影片是把地理說得清楚(讓我們跟得上動作),還是故意讓人迷失;(5)找出顛覆之處,例如開場鏡頭呈現不可能或具誤導性的空間。

範例:《鬼店》(The Shining,1980,導演史丹利·庫柏力克)的開場與樹籬迷宮。影片開場是越過山間湖泊(冰河國家公園的聖瑪麗湖,可見野鵝島)的空拍,跟隨一輛小小的黃色金龜車沿著蜿蜒公路前進,配上詭異的電子版《震怒之日》(Dies Irae)。這個遼闊的空拍畫面一舉兩得:交代托倫斯一家正駛入荒僻的野外,同時讓汽車在巨大風景中小得像隻昆蟲,在瞭望飯店還沒出現之前,就已傳達孤立感。後來,庫柏力克重新定位地理:傑克俯視飯店樹籬迷宮的縮尺模型,影片隨後從模型溶接到溫蒂與丹尼走進的真實迷宮,於是我們先從上方認識它的格局,之後它才在地面上變成陷阱。模式是:先建立規模與地點,等危險來臨時再重用那張地圖。(影片細節出自一般知識;確切地點與人員請對照資料來源。)
