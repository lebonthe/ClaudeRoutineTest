3) Spanish Lesson — Day 46 (10 min, reflexive verbs & reflexive pronouns)

Today's lesson finally formalizes something used silently since Day 1: reflexive verbs and reflexive pronouns (me/te/se/nos/os/se), where the subject and the object of the verb's action are the same person. Me llamo Ana ("I call myself Ana" — i.e. "My name is Ana") from Day 1 was already a reflexive construction; today generalizes the pattern to verbs like levantarse (to get (oneself) up), ducharse (to shower), vestirse (to get dressed), and acostarse (to go to bed), each conjugated normally but with a matching reflexive pronoun placed before the conjugated verb (me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, os levantáis, se levantan) or attached to an infinitive/gerund/affirmative command, following the exact same placement rules already learned for object pronouns on Days 44-45. The lesson is deliberately sequenced right after Day 45 to draw a sharp contrast: yesterday's se was a forced spelling substitution (le/les → se before a third-person direct object pronoun, e.g. Se lo doy), involving two distinct participants (a giver, a receiver, and a thing); today's se is a true reflexive pronoun, where the subject acts on itself and no separate direct-object pronoun (lo/la/los/las) appears alongside it. Both share identical spelling but serve entirely different grammatical functions — spotting which one you're looking at just requires checking whether a lo/la/los/las immediately follows.

  • ¿A qué hora te levantas? — What time do you get up?
  • Me levanto a las siete, me ducho y me visto rápido. — I get up at seven, shower, and get dressed quickly.
  • Después de ducharme, tengo que acostarme temprano hoy. — After showering, I have to go to bed early today.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 46: 升 (Shēng) — "Pushing Upward / Ascending" (Earth above, Wind/Wood below — Kūn over Sun). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary draws a direct line from yesterday's Hexagram 45: "聚而上者謂之升,故受之以升。" ("That which gathers and then rises is called Shēng [Ascending]; hence Cuì [Gathering Together] is followed by Shēng.") The logic is organic and literal: once things have massed together (45), the accumulated substance and energy of that mass naturally begins to press and rise upward — exactly as the hexagram's picture shows wood (the Sun trigram) growing steadily up through the earth (Kūn) that sits above it, a tree pushing its way toward light from within the soil that both contains and nourishes it. The Judgment reads: "升,元亨。用見大人,勿恤,南征吉。" ("Ascending: supreme success. It is favorable to see the great man; have no anxiety. A southward campaign brings good fortune.") "Seeing the great man" and "having no anxiety" together frame ascent as something to be pursued through legitimate access to wise guidance rather than anxious striving alone, while "a southward campaign" (south being associated with light, warmth, and outward-facing progress in the traditional Chinese symbolic compass) affirms that deliberate, forward motion — not passive waiting — is what completes the rise. The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) elaborates: "柔以時升,巽而順,剛中而應,是以大亨。用見大人,勿恤,有慶也。南征吉,志行也。" ("Through gentleness, one ascends in accordance with the time; there is docile compliance, and firmness centered with response — hence great success. 'It is favorable to see the great man; have no anxiety' means there is cause for celebration. 'A southward campaign brings good fortune' means that the will is being carried into action.") The Image commentary draws the hexagram's central practical teaching directly from its picture: "地中生木,升;君子以順德,積小以高大。" ("Wood grows within the earth: this is the image of Ascending. Thus the superior man, in accordance with virtue, accumulates small things in order to achieve something lofty and great.") A tree does not leap upward; it rises the only way growth ever can, by patient, continuous small increments compounding over time until the result is unmistakably tall — and the hexagram's real counsel is that true ascent, whether of a person, an idea, or a fortune, is built the same way: not by a single decisive leap, but by the steady accumulation of small, virtuous, well-timed actions that eventually break through into visible height.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Cuba (古巴)

  • Location: The Caribbean's largest island nation, at the entrance to the Gulf of Mexico, roughly 150 km south of Florida (US) across the Straits of Florida, north of Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, east of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, and west of Haiti.
  • Territory: About 109,884 km² including the main island, Isla de la Juventud, and roughly 4,000 smaller islands and cays — the largest island in the Caribbean, and about three times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km²); comparable in area to Portugal (~92,212 km²) or the US state of Tennessee (~109,153 km²). The US retains a long-term lease on Guantánamo Bay in the island's southeast, dating to a 1903/1934 treaty, still in effect today over Cuban objection.
  • Population: Roughly 11 million, concentrated in the capital Havana (~2 million) and along the coasts; Cuba's population has been shrinking and aging rapidly since around 2015 due to low birth rates and one of the largest sustained emigration waves in the Americas, especially accelerating since 2021.
  • Religion: Officially declared an atheist state from 1962-1992, then redefined as secular under 1992 constitutional reform; predominantly nominally Roman Catholic (roughly 60%), heavily blended in practice with Santería, a syncretic Afro-Cuban religion descended from Yoruba tradition brought by enslaved West Africans; smaller Protestant communities also exist.
  • Economy: A centrally planned socialist economy where the state controls most production and employment, shaped for over six decades by the US trade embargo (in place since 1960, formalized 1962). Key pillars include tourism, nickel and cobalt mining, medical-services diplomacy (exporting Cuban doctors abroad for revenue), and remittances from the large diaspora (especially in Miami); sugar, historically the dominant export, has sharply declined. Cuba has faced a severe economic crisis since around 2019-2020 — worsened by the pandemic's collapse of tourism, tightened US sanctions, and the loss of subsidized Venezuelan oil — resulting in chronic blackouts and shortages of food, fuel, and medicine.
  • History: Originally home to Indigenous Taíno peoples; reached by Columbus in 1492 and colonized by Spain for roughly four centuries, built on a sugar-and-slave plantation economy. The 1898 Spanish-American War ended Spanish rule; nominal independence followed in 1902 under the US-imposed Platt Amendment, which included the Guantánamo lease. Decades of US-aligned governance culminated in Fulgencio Batista's dictatorship, overthrown by the 1959 Cuban Revolution led by Fidel Castro and Che Guevara. Castro's alignment with the Soviet Union and nationalization of industry triggered the US embargo, the failed 1961 US-backed Bay of Pigs invasion, and the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis. Soviet subsidies propped up the economy through the Cold War; the USSR's 1991 collapse triggered Cuba's severe "Special Period" economic crisis. Fidel Castro transferred power to his brother Raúl in 2006 (formally 2008); a brief 2014-2015 Obama-era diplomatic thaw reopened embassies before subsequent US administrations re-tightened sanctions. Raúl Castro stepped down as Communist Party First Secretary in 2021, ending direct Castro family rule after six decades; that same year saw unprecedented nationwide protests over economic hardship, followed by the largest wave of Cuban emigration since the 1980 Mariel boatlift.
  • Current leader: Miguel Díaz-Canel Bermúdez, President since 2018 and, since April 2021, First Secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba (succeeding Raúl Castro in the party's top role). Cuba remains constitutionally a one-party socialist state; the Communist Party is the only legally permitted political party.
  • Language: Spanish (official), spoken in a distinctive Caribbean dialect featuring rapid speech, dropped consonants, and vocabulary influenced by West African languages carried by enslaved populations.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, cia.gov World Factbook

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — Dat Boi

  • Name: "Dat Boi" (stylized eye-dialect spelling of "that boy").
  • Origin/history: The source image — a cartoonish green frog or toad riding a unicycle — originated around 2010 on a skateboarding-themed Tumblr/imgur post and circulated in near-total obscurity for years.
  • How it became popular: It resurfaced explosively in spring 2016 when reposted with the caption "dat boi" on platforms like 9GAG, Instagram, and Vine, often paired with the exclamation "here come dat boi!!" — spreading with unusual speed and becoming a defining case study of that era's fast-cycling "meme economy," where the meme's own sudden, engineered-feeling ubiquity and anticipated backlash became part of the joke.
  • Meaning/usage: Deployed as a gleefully nonsensical reaction or announcement image posted in response to almost anything, with little or no literal connection to the triggering content — the humor lies in the shared, absurdist in-joke recognition rather than any specific meaning.
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned countless edits (the frog riding other vehicles/objects, crossovers with other viral characters, "O shit waddup" follow-up captions), and became a frequently cited textbook example in discussions of meme lifecycle theory: years of quiet incubation on a niche platform, a sudden explosive viral spike, rapid oversaturation, self-aware fatigue and backlash, and eventual settling into ironic nostalgia.
  • Current status: Largely a nostalgic touchstone of mid-2010s internet culture today, invoked retrospectively ("remember dat boi?") as shorthand for that specific meme-cycle aesthetic rather than in fresh, active circulation.
    Sources: knowyourmeme.com, en.wikipedia.org

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Sunset Boulevard (1950, dir. Billy Wilder)

  • Name/key figures: Sunset Boulevard, directed by Billy Wilder and co-written with Charles Brackett and D.M. Marshman Jr.; starring William Holden as struggling screenwriter Joe Gillis and Gloria Swanson as faded silent-film star Norma Desmond, with Erich von Stroheim (himself a real, once-major silent-era director) as Norma's devoted butler Max; cinematography by John F. Seitz; score by Franz Waxman.
  • Era/background: Released by Paramount in 1950, at a moment when the human wreckage of Hollywood's brutal silent-to-sound transition twenty years earlier was still a living memory; the film knowingly casts real industry figures as themselves, including director Cecil B. DeMille, silent star Buster Keaton, and gossip columnist Hedda Hopper.
  • Plot/content overview: Narrated by a dead man — Joe Gillis's corpse floats face-down in a swimming pool in the opening scene — the film flashes back to show broke screenwriter Joe drifting into the decaying mansion of Norma Desmond, a once-immense silent star now delusionally convinced a "return" (never "comeback," she insists) is imminent. Joe becomes her kept ghostwriter on a disastrous Salome script and, increasingly, her possession; when he tries to break free for an ordinary life, Norma shoots him, then descends into total delusion, famously addressing the newsreel cameras covering her arrest with "All right, Mr. DeMille, I'm ready for my close-up."
  • Artistic style and techniques: Opens with the then-taboo device of a dead narrator, fusing film-noir shadow and hard-boiled voice-over with Gothic-mansion decay; Norma's mansion is shot in deep shadow amid cobwebbed opulence and a looming pipe organ, staging the house itself as a kind of live burial; the film's real Paramount-lot locations and non-actor industry cameos give its Hollywood satire an unusually sharp, self-implicating edge.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made about Hollywood eating its own; earned 11 Academy Award nominations, winning three (screenplay, score, art direction); selected in 1989 for the US National Film Registry's inaugural class of preserved works.
  • Influence and legacy: Norma's lines "I am big. It's the pictures that got small" and the closing "ready for my close-up" rank among the most quoted in film history; adapted into a Tony- and Olivier-winning Andrew Lloyd Webber stage musical (1993); frequently cited as the direct template for later Hollywood-devours-itself films.
  • Current status/where to experience it: Restored and widely available via Paramount home video and major streaming platforms; a fixture of film-school curricula on noir technique and Hollywood's self-mythologizing.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, loc.gov National Film Registry

8) Film-Appreciation Method — The Dutch Angle / Canted Framing (Lesson 32)

A Dutch angle (or canted frame) tilts the camera off its normal horizontal axis so the horizon line runs diagonally rather than level, immediately signaling to the audience — before any dialogue or action tells them so — that something in the scene is off-balance, unstable, or morally/psychologically skewed. This is distinct from Day 8's Framing & Composition (which covers symmetry, one-point perspective, and negative space within a level frame) and Day 3's Cinematography & Lighting (light quality and color, not the camera's rotational axis): the Dutch angle's entire effect comes from a single mechanical choice — rotating the camera around its lens axis — rather than from what is lit, colored, or arranged within an otherwise normal frame. Three things to check when you spot one: whether the tilt is used sparingly as a punctuation mark for one specific moment of disorientation, or sustained across an entire sequence/film as a pervasive stylistic signature of a corrupted or unstable world; whether the degree of tilt is subtle (a slight unease) or extreme (near-vertiginous, signaling severe crisis); and whether the tilt is motivated by a character's literal physical/psychological state (vertigo, drunkenness, a blow to the head) or is purely an authorial, non-diegetic commentary on the scene's moral content that no character could "feel."

Worked example: The Third Man (1949, dir. Carol Reed, cinematography by Robert Krasker) — set across the rubble and shadow of Allied-occupied postwar Vienna, the film returns again and again to canted framing, from interrogation-room scenes to the climactic sewer chase, tilting the frame to visually encode a city, and a friendship, whose moral and physical foundations have been knocked off level by war and betrayal. The tilts are not motivated by any character's literal disorientation but function as pure authorial commentary — a visual thesis that this entire world is off its axis — deployed so insistently across the film that it became, along with the picture's famous zither score, one of its most instantly recognizable stylistic signatures; the technique was considered daringly excessive by some contemporary critics (a producer is said to have joked about sending Reed a spirit level) yet is now taught as one of the textbook reference cases for canted framing in film schools.

3)西班牙文課程——第 46 天(10 分鐘,反身動詞與反身代名詞)

今天的課程,終於把從第一天就悄悄使用、卻從未正式解說的文法主角攤開來教:反身動詞與反身代名詞(me/te/se/nos/os/se),用於「主詞對自己做出動作」的情境。第一天教過的 Me llamo Ana(字面直譯「我把自己稱為安娜」,也就是「我叫安娜」)其實早就是反身句型;今天把這個模式一般化,套用到 levantarse(起床)、ducharse(洗澡)、vestirse(穿衣服)、acostarse(上床睡覺)等動詞上——這些動詞本身仍照一般規則變化,只是每個人稱前面都要加上對應的反身代名詞(me levanto、te levantas、se levanta、nos levantamos、os levantáis、se levantan),放置位置規則也和第 44、45 天學過的受詞代名詞完全一致(放在已變化動詞前,或黏在原形動詞/現在分詞/肯定命令式字尾)。今天的課程刻意排在第 45 天之後,是為了製造一個鮮明的對比:昨天的 se 是一種「強制改寫拼法」——第三人稱間接受詞 le/les 遇到第三人稱直接受詞 lo/la/los/las 時被迫改成 se(例如 Se lo doy),句中牽涉到兩個不同角色(給予者、接受者、以及一個東西);今天的 se 卻是貨真價實的反身代名詞,主詞的動作對象就是自己,句中不會另外出現 lo/la/los/las 這類直接受詞代名詞。兩者拼法完全相同,文法功能卻截然不同——只要檢查 se 後面是否緊跟著 lo/la/los/las,就能立刻判斷是哪一種。

  • Me levanto a las siete, me ducho y me visto rápido. —— 我七點起床,洗澡然後很快穿好衣服。
  • Después de ducharme, tengo que acostarme temprano hoy. —— 洗完澡後,我今天得早點睡覺。

4)易經卦象

第 46 卦:升 (Shēng) ——「上升」(地在上,風/木在下——坤上巽下)。《序卦傳》從昨天的第 45 卦直接接續下來:「聚而上者謂之升,故受之以升。」(凡是先聚集、然後向上發展的,就稱為升;因此萃卦之後接的是升卦。)這個邏輯十分直白且具體:當事物先聚集成群(第 45 卦)之後,匯聚起來的力量與能量,自然會開始向上頂升、向外發展——正如卦象所示,木(巽卦所代表的意象)在大地(坤)之中逐漸向上生長,一株樹苗持續從滋養、包容著它的土壤內部,朝著陽光的方向奮力向上突破。卦辭寫道:「升,元亨。用見大人,勿恤,南征吉。」(升卦:大為亨通。適宜求見德高望重的大人物,不必憂慮;向南方出征則吉祥。)「求見大人物」與「不必憂慮」兩句合觀,點出真正的上升應當透過正當途徑求得賢者的指引與提攜,而不是單靠焦慮不安的蠻力硬撐;「向南出征」(在傳統中國方位象徵中,南方對應著光明、溫暖與向外開展的進取之意)則進一步強調:唯有主動、堅定地向前行動——而非被動等待——才能真正完成這股上升之勢。《彖傳》進一步解釋:「柔以時升,巽而順,剛中而應,是以大亨。用見大人,勿恤,有慶也。南征吉,志行也。」(以柔順之姿,順應時勢而上升;謙遜而柔順配合,剛健居中而有所呼應,因此能大為亨通。「適宜求見大人物,不必憂慮」,是說這樣做值得慶賀。「向南出征吉祥」,是說心中的志向正在被付諸實行。)《象傳》則直接從卦象本身,點出這一卦最核心的實踐教訓:「地中生木,升;君子以順德,積小以高大。」(樹木在大地之中向上生長,這就是升卦的卦象。因此君子藉此順應美德而行,透過日積月累的小善小行,最終成就崇高遠大的成果。)樹木從不會一躍而起;它唯一能夠成長的方式,就是靠耐心、持續、細微的增量,經年累月不斷疊加,直到最終長成有目共睹的參天大樹——而這一卦真正的教誨,正是無論是一個人、一個理念,還是一份事業的真正上升,走的都是同一條路:不是靠某一次決定性的躍升,而是靠日復一日、把握時機、腳踏實地的微小善行不斷積累,最終才能突破而顯現出真正的高度。

(第四十六個卦象登場——已記錄於 ROUTINE_LOG.md 以避免重複。目前已介紹:1 乾 Qián、2 坤 Kūn、3 屯 Zhūn、4 蒙 Méng、5 需 Xū、6 訟 Sòng、7 師 Shī、8 比 Bǐ、9 小畜 Xiǎo Chù、10 履 Lǚ、11 泰 Tài、12 否 Pǐ、13 同人 Tóng Rén、14 大有 Dà Yǒu、15 謙 Qiān、16 豫 Yù、17 隨 Suí、18 蠱 Gǔ、19 臨 Lín、20 觀 Guān、21 噬嗑 Shì Kè、22 賁 Bì、23 剝 Bō、24 復 Fù、25 无妄 Wú Wàng、26 大畜 Dà Chù、27 頤 Yí、28 大過 Dà Guò、29 坎 Kǎn、30 離 Lí、31 咸 Xián、32 恆 Héng、33 遯 Dùn、34 大壯 Dà Zhuàng、35 晉 Jìn、36 明夷 Míng Yí、37 家人 Jiā Rén、38 睽 Kuí、39 蹇 Jiǎn、40 解 Xiè、41 損 Sǔn、42 益 Yì、43 夬 Guài、44 姤 Gòu、45 萃 Cuì、46 升 Shēng。)

5)國家/地區/政體介紹——古巴(Cuba)

  • 位置:加勒比海地區面積最大的島國,位於墨西哥灣入口處,隔佛羅里達海峽與美國佛羅里達州相距約 150 公里,北鄰美國,南接牙買加與開曼群島,西邊隔海為墨西哥猶加敦半島,東邊則與海地相望。
  • 領土:約 109,884 平方公里,包含主島、青年島(Isla de la Juventud)以及約四千個小島與沙洲——是加勒比海面積最大的島嶼,約為台灣(約 36,193 平方公里)的三倍;面積與葡萄牙(約 92,212 平方公里)或美國田納西州(約 109,153 平方公里)相近。美國在古巴東南端的關塔那摩灣(Guantánamo Bay)依 1903 年/1934 年條約長期租借一處軍事基地,至今仍在使用,古巴政府對此持續表達異議。
  • 人口:約 1,100 萬人,高度集中於首都哈瓦那(約 200 萬人)及沿海地區;自 2015 年前後起,由於出生率偏低,加上美洲地區規模數一數二的持續人口外移潮(自 2021 年起更明顯加速),古巴人口正快速萎縮且高齡化。
  • 宗教:1962 至 1992 年間曾正式宣告為無神論國家,1992 年修憲後改定性為世俗國家;名義上以羅馬天主教為主(約佔 60%),但實際信仰上高度融合了源自西非約魯巴(Yoruba)傳統、由被奴役非洲人帶入古巴的混合宗教「桑特里亞」(Santería);另有規模較小的新教社群。
  • 經濟:採中央計畫社會主義經濟體制,國家掌控絕大多數生產與就業,並長達六十餘年受美國貿易禁運(自 1960 年起實施,1962 年正式化)所形塑。主要經濟支柱包括觀光業、鎳與鈷礦業、以「醫療外交」形式輸出古巴醫師換取外匯,以及龐大海外僑民(尤其邁阿密僑社)匯回的僑匯;歷史上曾長期居出口首位的糖業,則已大幅萎縮。古巴自 2019 至 2020 年前後陷入嚴重經濟危機——疫情導致觀光業重挫、美國制裁再度收緊,加上委內瑞拉補貼石油供應中斷等因素加劇危機——造成長期停電與食品、燃料、藥品的持續短缺。
  • 歷史:最早為原住民泰諾人(Taíno)的家園;1492 年哥倫布抵達後,西班牙展開長達約四百年的殖民統治,建立以蔗糖與奴隸勞動為核心的種植園經濟。1898 年美西戰爭結束西班牙統治;1902 年在美國強加的「普拉特修正案」(Platt Amendment,其中即包含關塔那摩灣租借條款)框架下取得名義獨立。數十年親美政權統治,最終演變為富爾亨西奧·巴蒂斯塔(Fulgencio Batista)的獨裁政權,在 1959 年由菲德爾·卡斯楚(Fidel Castro)與切·格瓦拉(Che Guevara)領導的古巴革命中被推翻。卡斯楚政府轉向與蘇聯結盟並將產業收歸國有,觸發美國實施貿易禁運、1961 年美方支援卻慘敗的豬玀灣入侵行動,以及 1962 年古巴導彈危機。冷戰期間蘇聯的經濟補貼長期支撐古巴經濟;1991 年蘇聯解體後,古巴陷入嚴重的「特殊時期」經濟危機。菲德爾·卡斯楚於 2006 年(正式移交於 2008 年)將權力交棒給弟弟勞爾·卡斯楚(Raúl Castro);2014 至 2015 年歐巴馬政府任內曾短暫推動外交解凍、重新開設大使館,但後續美國政府又再度收緊制裁。勞爾·卡斯楚於 2021 年卸下古巴共產黨第一書記職務,結束卡斯楚家族長達六十年的直接統治;同一年古巴爆發史無前例的全國性抗議活動,起因於嚴峻的經濟困境,隨後引發自 1980 年「馬列爾偷渡船事件」以來規模最大的古巴人出走潮。
  • 現任領導人:米格爾·迪亞斯-卡內爾·貝穆德斯(Miguel Díaz-Canel Bermúdez),自 2018 年起擔任總統,並自 2021 年 4 月起接替勞爾·卡斯楚,擔任古巴共產黨第一書記(黨內最高職務)。古巴憲法上仍是一黨制社會主義國家,古巴共產黨為唯一合法政黨。
  • 語言:官方語言為西班牙文,使用具有鮮明特色的加勒比海方言——語速快、輔音經常省略,詞彙也受到當年被奴役的西非族群語言深刻影響。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、cia.gov 世界概況(World Factbook)

6)網路迷因介紹——Dat Boi(那個男孩)

  • 名稱:「Dat Boi」(刻意以口語化拼法拼寫「that boy」,意為「那個男孩」)。
  • 起源/歷史:圖片來源是一隻卡通風格的綠色青蛙(或蟾蜍)騎著單輪車的插畫,約在 2010 年首次出現於一篇滑板主題的 Tumblr/imgur 貼文中,此後多年幾乎完全無人問津。
  • 如何走紅:直到 2016 年春天才突然爆紅——圖片被重新配上「dat boi」字幕,在 9GAG、Instagram、Vine 等平台大量轉發,常搭配驚呼句「here come dat boi!!」(那個男孩來了!!),以異常驚人的速度擴散開來,成為當年那種「快速循環的迷因經濟」最具代表性的案例之一——這個迷因本身突如其來、彷彿刻意營造出來的無所不在感,以及隨之而來、幾乎可預期的反感反彈,本身也成了笑點的一部分。
  • 意義/使用情境:被當作一張帶著歡樂、毫無邏輯的反應圖或宣告圖,幾乎可以回應任何內容,與被回應的內容本身通常毫無實質關聯——笑點並非來自任何具體意涵,而是來自大家共同心領神會的荒誕式集體玩笑。
  • 變體/演化:衍生出無數改圖版本(青蛙改騎其他交通工具/物品、與其他爆紅角色的跨界合體、搭配「O shit waddup」接續字幕的版本),並成為討論「迷因生命週期理論」時經常被引用的教科書級案例:先在小眾平台上多年默默潛伏,接著突然爆炸式病毒傳播,隨後迅速氾濫飽和,再演變成迷因本身自帶反諷意味的疲乏與反感,最終沉澱為帶著懷舊色彩的過往符號。
  • 目前狀態:如今大致已成為 2010 年代中期網路文化的懷舊代表符號,多半只在「還記得 dat boi 嗎?」這類回顧性語境中被提及,用來指代那個特定迷因週期的美學風格,而非仍在活躍、新鮮地流通使用中。
    來源:knowyourmeme.com、en.wikipedia.org

7)電影/表演藝術介紹——《日落大道》Sunset Boulevard(1950,導演:比利·懷德)

  • 名稱/關鍵人物:《日落大道》(Sunset Boulevard),導演為比利·懷德(Billy Wilder),與查爾斯·布拉克特(Charles Brackett)、D.M.馬歇曼(D.M. Marshman Jr.)共同編劇;威廉·荷頓(William Holden)飾演潦倒的編劇喬·吉利斯,葛洛麗亞·史璜森(Gloria Swanson)飾演風華不再的默片女星諾瑪·戴斯蒙;艾里希·馮·斯特羅海姆(Erich von Stroheim,本人正是曾經舉足輕重、後來沒落的真實默片時代導演)飾演諾瑪忠心的管家馬克斯;攝影指導為約翰·塞茨(John F. Seitz);配樂由法蘭茲·瓦克斯曼(Franz Waxman)創作。
  • 時代/背景:1950 年由派拉蒙影業發行,拍攝當時,二十年前默片轉有聲片這場殘酷轉型所造成的人才殞落與時代創傷,仍是業界鮮明的集體記憶;本片刻意讓多位真實影壇人物本色出演,包括導演西席·地密爾(Cecil B. DeMille)、默片時代巨星巴斯特·基頓(Buster Keaton),以及八卦專欄作家海達·霍珀(Hedda Hopper)。
  • 內容概述:全片由一名死者擔任旁白敘述者——開場畫面即是喬·吉利斯的屍體俯臥漂浮於游泳池中——隨後以回溯手法呈現窮困的編劇喬,如何意外闖入曾經紅極一時、如今已然沒落的默片女星諾瑪·戴斯蒙那座逐漸崩壞的豪宅。諾瑪堅信自己即將迎來「重返影壇」(她堅持絕不使用「復出」一詞)的一天,喬淪為她豢養的代筆編劇,替她那部注定失敗的《莎樂美》劇本效力,並逐漸成為她愈發偏執的執念與所有物;當喬試圖擺脫她、追求平凡生活時,諾瑪開槍將他射殺,自此徹底陷入瘋狂幻想,面對前來拍攝她被捕畫面的新聞攝影機,留下那句經典台詞:「好了,地密爾先生,我已經準備好拍我的特寫鏡頭了。」
  • 藝術風格與技巧:本片開場即大膽採用當年極為罕見、近乎禁忌的「死者旁白」敘事手法,將黑色電影式的陰影美學與冷硬派旁白,和哥德式豪宅的頹敗氛圍融為一體;諾瑪的豪宅始終籠罩在深沉陰影與蒙塵奢華之中,一架巨大的管風琴陰森地矗立其中,將整座宅邸拍成了某種「活埋」的意象;片中大量實地取景於派拉蒙片廠真實場景,並邀請真正的業界人物客串本色演出,使本片對好萊塢的自我諷刺格外尖銳,且帶有一種業界人士自曝其短的獨特力道。
  • 文化/歷史意義:普遍被視為史上探討好萊塢「自我吞噬」主題最傑出的電影之一;本片獲得 11 項奧斯卡提名,最終奪得三項獎項(最佳原創劇本、最佳配樂、最佳藝術指導);1989 年入選美國國家影片登記表首批入選作品之一。
  • 影響與傳承:諾瑪的台詞「我很偉大,是這些畫面變小了」("I am big. It's the pictures that got small.")與結尾那句「準備好拍我的特寫鏡頭了」,至今仍是影史上最常被引用的台詞之一;1993 年由安德魯·洛伊·韋伯(Andrew Lloyd Webber)改編為舞台音樂劇,曾獲東尼獎與奧利佛獎肯定;經常被視為後續無數部「好萊塢自我吞噬」題材電影的直接原型範本。
  • 現況/如何欣賞:已完成數位修復,並透過派拉蒙家庭影音及各大主流串流平台廣泛提供觀賞;長年是電影學院課程中探討黑色電影技法與好萊塢自我神話化主題的必修教材。

8)看片方法——傾斜鏡位/歪斜構圖 The Dutch Angle(第 32 課)

「傾斜鏡位」(Dutch angle,亦稱 canted frame)是刻意讓攝影機偏離正常的水平軸線,使畫面中的水平線呈現對角傾斜,而非端正水平,藉此在任何台詞或情節明確說明之前,就先向觀眾傳達出畫面中某種失衡、不穩定,或道德/心理層面遭到扭曲的訊號。這與第 8 天教過的「構圖與框景」(著重於水平畫面內的對稱/不對稱、單點透視、留白等元素)不同,也與第 3 天的「攝影與燈光」(著重於光線質感與色彩,而非攝影機本身的旋轉軸線)不同:傾斜鏡位的全部效果,單純來自一項機械性的選擇——讓攝影機繞著鏡頭軸心旋轉——而非畫面中打光、上色或陳設方式的改變。發現傾斜鏡位時,可以檢視三個重點:這個傾斜是僅在某個特定失衡瞬間作為點狀強調符號使用,還是貫穿整段場景乃至全片、成為代表某個腐化或失衡世界觀的持續性風格標記?傾斜的角度是輕微(帶出一絲不安)還是極端(近乎令人眩暈,暗示嚴重危機)?這個傾斜是由角色本身真實的生理/心理狀態所驅動(眩暈、酒醉、遭受重擊),還是純粹屬於創作者本人、角色自身無法「感受到」的敘事外部道德評論?

實例演練:《黑獄亡魂》(The Third Man,1949,導演:卡洛·李〔Carol Reed〕,攝影指導:羅伯特·克拉斯克〔Robert Krasker〕)——故事場景設定在盟軍佔領下、滿目瘡痍的戰後維也納,全片一再回歸傾斜鏡位的運用,從偵訊室場景到結局的下水道追逐戲皆然,藉由讓畫面本身傾斜,具象化呈現一座城市、乃至一段友誼,其道德與現實根基早已因戰爭與背叛而徹底失衡、傾斜。這些傾斜鏡位並非由任何角色的真實生理失衡所驅動,而是純粹作為創作者本人的敘事評論——一種視覺化的論述,宣告「這整個世界早已失去平衡」——全片如此堅持不懈地反覆運用這項手法,使其連同片中著名的齊特琴配樂,成為本片最令人一眼辨識的招牌風格之一;當年部分影評人曾認為這項手法用得過於誇張(據傳有製片人曾開玩笑說要送卡洛·李一個水平儀),但如今這部作品已成為電影學院課程中教授「傾斜鏡位」時的經典教科書範例。
