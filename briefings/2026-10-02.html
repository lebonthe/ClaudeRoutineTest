3) Spanish Lesson — Day 78 (10 min, Ir vs. Venir and Llevar vs. Traer)

Today covers the "perspective verbs": ir (go away from here) vs. venir (come toward the speaker or listener), and llevar (take away) vs. traer (bring here). Key trap: when you are going to the listener's place, Spanish says voy, not vengo.

  • ¿Vienes a la fiesta de Ana? Ya estamos aquí. — Are you coming to Ana's party? We're already here.
  • Sí, ya voy. ¿Qué llevo? — Yes, I'm on my way. What should I bring?
  • Trae algo de beber. — Bring something to drink.
  • Vale. Llevo también unas galletas. — OK. I'll also bring some cookies.

4) Zhuangzi — Essay 14: 天運 (Tiān Yùn) — "The Turning of Heaven"

The seventh chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇), following "The Way of Heaven." It opens with a string of unanswerable questions: does Heaven turn, does Earth rest, who pushes the sun and moon along, who lives at ease and sets it all in motion? The point is that the cosmos moves on its own without a manager. The chapter then argues, against the Confucian virtues praised elsewhere, that "tigers and wolves are benevolent" in the sense that they love their young, and that perfect benevolence has no partiality (至仁無親). It also contains the music of Xianchi, in which the Yellow Emperor's three movements move the listener from fear, to weariness, to confusion and finally a state beyond understanding. Confucius' meeting with Lao Dan gives the line that the Six Classics are only the footprints of the former kings, not the shoe that made them (夫跡,履之所出,而跡豈履哉). The best-known anecdote here is Xi Shi frowning in pain, and a plain-looking woman of the village copying the frown, which only frightened her neighbors (the origin of the idiom 東施效顰, "Dong Shi imitates the frown"): forms copied without the inner source go wrong. 5) Country / Region Spotlight — Austria (奧地利)

  • Location & territory size: Austria is a landlocked country in Central Europe bordering Germany, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Slovenia, Italy, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. At about 83,900 km², it is roughly 2.3 times the size of Taiwan (about 36,200 km²) and close to the size of South Carolina; about two-thirds of the country is Alpine.
  • Population: About 9.2 million (9,219,113 on Jan 1, 2026, per a Robert Schuman Foundation country sheet); roughly a fifth live in the capital, Vienna.
  • Religion: Roman Catholics are the largest group (a little over half of the population by recent estimates), with sizeable non-religious, Muslim and Orthodox Christian communities; secularization is ongoing.
  • Language: German is the official language, with Austrian-German dialects; Croatian, Slovene, Hungarian and others hold regional minority-language status.
  • Economy: A rich, export-oriented economy with GDP of about EUR 514 billion in 2025 and weak estimated growth of about +0.6% for 2026 (same source). Strengths include machinery, steel, tourism (Alpine skiing, Vienna, Salzburg), finance and a strong SME sector; it uses the euro.
  • History: A frontier march under the Babenbergs from 976, then the Habsburg heartland from 1278, ruling the Holy Roman Empire and later the Austrian Empire (1804) and Austria-Hungary (1867). The empire collapsed in 1918; Austria was annexed by Nazi Germany in 1938 (the Anschluss), occupied by the Allies from 1945, and regained sovereignty with the State Treaty of 1955, followed by a constitutional neutrality law. It joined the EU in 1995.
  • Current leader: President Alexander Van der Bellen (in office since 2017, re-elected 2022); Chancellor Christian Stocker (ÖVP), sworn in March 3, 2025, leads an ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS coalition (per the same source; verify for later changes).
  • Fun fact: Vienna has repeatedly topped global "most liveable city" rankings, and the country is the birthplace of Mozart (Salzburg) and Schubert and the home of the classical waltz tradition.

7) Film / Performing-Arts Spotlight — A Touch of Zen (俠女, 1971, King Hu)

  • Name and key figures: A Touch of Zen (俠女, Xiá Nǚ), directed and written by King Hu (胡金銓), starring Hsu Feng (徐楓) as Yang Huizhen, Shih Chun (石雋) as the scholar Gu Shengzhai, Bai Ying (白鷹) as General Shi, and Roy Chiao (喬宏) as the monk Hui Yuan. It is loosely adapted from the Qing-era story "Xia Nü" (俠女) in Pu Songling's Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio.
  • Era / background: Shot in Taiwan over about three years (c. 1968-1970) after King Hu's hit Dragon Inn (1967), with a story set in the Ming dynasty. It was a commercial disappointment on release, then reassessed.
  • Plot overview: Gu Shengzhai, a poor painter and scribe, lives beside a haunted-looking ruined fort. He befriends a mysterious woman, Yang, who is in hiding from the agents of the eunuch Wei Zhongxian, who destroyed her father. Together with General Shi's forces they stage ambushes. In the final part, the monk Hui Yuan, who has protected Yang, is badly wounded and shows a golden-red blood, a sign of enlightenment; Yang is left to grieve and seek her own Buddhist path.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Painterly landscape composition drawn from Chinese art, long contemplative takes, and ultra-quick cutting in the action. The bamboo-forest fight, with warriors seemingly floating between trees (done with trampolines and rapid editing), is a landmark of wuxia choreography.
  • Cultural / historical significance: It fuses the martial-arts genre with Chan (Zen) Buddhist philosophy and with classical aesthetics, raising wuxia to art cinema. It was a key work of Taiwanese cinema.
  • Influence and legacy: It won the Technical Grand Prize at the 1975 Cannes Film Festival and is often cited as the direct ancestor of the bamboo-forest scene in Ang Lee's Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000). It is regularly ranked among the best Chinese-language films.
  • Current status / where to experience it: Restored editions and home-video releases exist, and it screens at retrospectives; check local streaming availability.

Sources: general film-reference knowledge; dates and credits were not re-verified live, so confirm festival and restoration details locally.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Pacing & Average Shot Length (Lesson 64)

Earlier lessons taught how individual cuts, angles and sounds work. This lesson zooms out to the film's tempo: how fast or slow it cuts across a scene. Average Shot Length (ASL) is the total running time of a scene or film divided by its number of shots. A low ASL (a shot every second or two) reads as frenetic, anxious and energetic; a high ASL (shots of a minute or more) reads as contemplative, tense through patience, or realist. How to use it: (1) count shots in a one-minute stretch with a pencil, or use the Cinemetrics database; (2) compare against the film's own baseline rather than a universal "fast" number; (3) look for changes in rhythm, because the meaning is usually in the contrast; (4) ask which emotion the tempo manufactures and whether it matches what the story is doing.

Worked example: Psycho (1960, dir. Alfred Hitchcock), the shower scene. Marion's earlier scenes use long, patient takes (the drive, the quiet parlor conversation with Norman). When the attack starts, the film switches to a burst of extremely short shots, commonly cited as roughly 78 camera setups and 50-odd cuts across about 45 seconds, so that the average shot lasts well under a second. Each shot shows only a fragment (a hand, the knife's arc, a face, the shower head), so the violence is built in the viewer's head rather than shown in one view. After the murder, the cutting slows into a long, still shot of the draining water and the dead eye, so the contrast between burst and stillness is what makes the scene land. (Shot counts are widely repeated figures; treat as approximate.)

  • 時代/背景:在《龍門客棧》(1967)大賣之後,於台灣拍攝約三年(約 1968–1970),故事設定在明代。上映時票房不理想,後來才被重新評價。
  • 劇情概要:貧窮的畫師兼代筆書生顧省齋住在一座像鬧鬼的廢棄古堡旁,結識了神祕女子楊慧貞,她正躲避毀了她父親的宦官魏忠賢的爪牙。他們與石將軍的人馬設下埋伏。後段,一路保護楊的慧圓和尚身受重傷,流出金紅色的血,象徵悟道;楊獨自哀悼,尋找自己的佛道之路。
  • 藝術風格與技法:取自中國繪畫的山水構圖、冗長沉思的長鏡頭,以及動作戲中極快的剪接。竹林打鬥中武者彷彿在樹梢間飛行(以彈跳網與快速剪接完成),是武俠動作設計的里程碑。
  • 文化/歷史意義:它把武俠類型與禪宗哲學及古典美學融合,把武俠片提升為藝術電影,是台灣電影的重要作品。
  • 影響與傳承:獲 1975 年坎城影展技術大獎,常被視為李安《臥虎藏龍》(2000)竹林戲的直接源頭,並經常名列最佳華語片之列。
  • 現況/何處可看:已有修復版與家庭影音發行,也常見於影展回顧;串流平台請依所在地查詢。

來源:一般電影參考知識;日期與片單未即時重新查證,影展與修復細節請自行確認。

8)看片方法——節奏與平均鏡頭長度(第 64 課)

之前的課程教的是單一剪接、角度與聲音如何運作。這一課把鏡頭拉遠,看整部電影的節奏:一個場景剪得快還是慢。平均鏡頭長度(ASL)是某場戲或整部片的總時間除以鏡頭數。ASL 低(每一兩秒就換鏡頭)給人緊張、焦躁、有活力的感覺;ASL 高(一個鏡頭一分鐘以上)則偏向沉思、靠耐心製造張力,或寫實。使用方法:(1)拿筆數一分鐘內的鏡頭數,或查 Cinemetrics 資料庫;(2)跟這部片自己的基準比較,而不是拿一個通用的「快」標準;(3)留意節奏的變化,意義通常在對比裡;(4)問這個節奏製造了什麼情緒,是否與故事正在做的事一致。

範例:《驚魂記》(Psycho,1960,導演希區考克)的淋浴場景。Marion 先前的戲使用長而從容的鏡頭(開車、與 Norman 安靜的客廳對話)。攻擊開始後,影片切換成一連串極短的鏡頭,常被引用為約 45 秒內有約 78 個機位、五十多次剪接,平均每個鏡頭遠不到一秒。每個鏡頭只呈現碎片(一隻手、刀的弧線、一張臉、蓮蓬頭),所以暴力是在觀眾腦中拼出來,而不是在單一畫面中直接看到。謀殺之後,剪接放慢,變成一個長而靜止的鏡頭,拍下流走的水與死去的眼睛,爆發與靜止之間的對比讓這場戲發揮力量。(鏡頭數是廣為流傳的數字,請視為概略值。)
