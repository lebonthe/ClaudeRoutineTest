3) Spanish Lesson — Day 30 (10 min, builds on Day 29)

Day 27 covered wishing about someone else's action (querer/esperar que), Day 28 covered doubt and impersonal possibility, and Day 29 covered emotional reactions. Today adds a fourth trigger family: recommendations, requests, and necessity — sentences where the subject is actively trying to influence what another subject does, plus a genuinely tricky twist: the verb decir ("to say") can trigger either mood depending on what it means.

  • Te recomiendo que estudies más gramática. — I recommend that you study more grammar.
  • Mi jefe pide que lleguemos a las ocho. — My boss asks that we arrive at eight.
  • Es necesario que todos firmen el documento. — It's necessary that everyone sign the document.
  • Le digo que venga temprano, no que viene temprano. — I'm telling him to come early, not telling him [the fact] that he's coming early.

Grammar note: recommendation/request verbs (recomendar, sugerir, pedir, exigir) share the exact two-subject-plus-subjunctive structure taught on Day 27, but mark a stronger "actively trying to influence someone's behavior" meaning rather than a mere wish; impersonal necessity expressions (es necesario/importante que) extend Day 28's impersonal-expression category from possibility into necessity. The sharpest trap is decir: used to report a fact ("Le digo que viene temprano" — I'm telling him [the fact] that he's coming early), it takes the indicative; used to give an order ("Le digo que venga temprano" — I'm telling him to come early), it takes the subjunctive, because at that point decir is functionally doing the same job as pedir. With today's lesson, the WEIRDO mnemonic (Wishes, Emotions, Impersonal expressions, Recommendations, Doubt, Ojalá) has now covered five of its six categories — only Ojalá remains for a future lesson.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 30: 離 (Lí) — "The Clinging" / "Fire" (Fire above, Fire below — the second and final "doubled single trigram" hexagram of the Upper Canon besides Hexagram 29, and, together with Hexagrams 1 (乾) and 2 (坤), one of only four hexagrams in the entire 64 formed by stacking one trigram on itself). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary explains today's transition directly from yesterday's danger: "陷必有所麗,故受之以離;離者,麗也。" ("Once one has fallen into a pit, one must cling to something [to climb back out]; hence Kǎn is followed by Lí. Lí means 'to cling, to adhere.'") Where Kǎn (29) was about danger that recurs and must be endured through habitual sincerity, Lí answers it: survival in the pit requires finding something solid to cling to, and that clinging becomes today's governing image — brightness that only exists by attaching itself to fuel. The Judgment reads: "離,利貞,亨。畜牝牛,吉。" ("The Clinging: perseverance furthers, and brings success. Care for a cow brings good fortune.") The image of the docile, nurturing cow (a yin symbol) hints at the hexagram's core teaching: unlike water, which sustains its own nature by simply flowing, fire has no independent existence — it must cling to firewood, oil, or some other fuel to burn at all, so the hexagram counsels a corresponding gentleness and dependence rather than aggressive, unsupported action. The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) develops the image further: "離,麗也。日月麗乎天,百穀草木麗乎土,重明以麗乎正,乃化成天下。柔麗乎中正,故亨,是以畜牝牛吉也。" ("Lí means to cling. The sun and moon cling to heaven; the hundred grains, grasses and trees cling to the earth. A double brightness clinging to what is correct transforms and perfects all under heaven. The soft/yielding [line] clings to the central, correct position — hence success, and hence caring for a cow brings good fortune.") The Image commentary adds: "明兩作,離;大人以繼明照于四方。" ("Brightness arising twice over: this is Lí. Thus the great person, by continuing this brightness without interruption, illuminates the four quarters of the world.") Read together with Hexagram 29, the pair closes out the Upper Canon (hexagrams 1-30) on a deliberate elemental balance — water (danger, endurance, formlessness) answered by fire (clarity, dependence, illumination) — before the Lower Canon opens at Hexagram 31 (咸, Xián, "Influence") with the shift from cosmic/natural forces to human relationships.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Papua New Guinea (巴布亞紐幾內亞)

  • Location: An Oceanian country occupying the eastern half of the island of New Guinea (the western half, Papua, belongs to Indonesia), plus several hundred offshore islands including New Britain, New Ireland, and Bougainville; it sits just north of Australia across the Torres Strait, and is part of the wider Melanesian cultural region.
  • Territory: Approximately 462,840 km² — nearly 13 times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km²), and comparable in area to Sweden or the US state of California; the terrain is dominated by dense rainforest, rugged central highlands, and active volcanoes, which historically kept many communities isolated from one another.
  • Population: Approximately 10.3 million (2023 est.), the largest population of any Pacific Island nation by a wide margin, though still sparsely distributed given the country's size and terrain.
  • Religion: Overwhelmingly Christian (over 95%, split mainly among Roman Catholic, Lutheran, and various Evangelical/Pentecostal denominations introduced by 19th- and 20th-century missionaries), with traditional animist and ancestor-veneration beliefs still widely practiced alongside, rather than fully replaced by, Christian observance in many rural communities.
  • Economy: Resource-dependent, built on liquefied natural gas, gold, and copper exports plus oil, alongside a large subsistence-agriculture sector that still employs the majority of the population; despite substantial resource wealth, PNG remains a lower-middle-income country with severe infrastructure gaps, high rates of poverty, and one of the world's highest rates of gender-based violence, and it is a member of the Commonwealth of Nations.
  • History: Colonized in two halves — Germany administered the northeast (German New Guinea) while Britain (later transferring administration to Australia) controlled the southeast (the Territory of Papua); Australia took over the German portion after World War I and administered the combined Territory of Papua and New Guinea through World War II, when it became one of the war's bloodiest Pacific battlegrounds (the Kokoda Track campaign against Japan). Papua New Guinea gained full independence from Australia in 1975.
  • Current leader: Prime Minister James Marape, in office since May 2019 and returned to office after the 2022 general election; as a Commonwealth realm, King Charles III is head of state, represented locally by a Governor-General.
  • Language: The most linguistically diverse country on Earth — home to more than 840 living languages, roughly 12% of all languages spoken worldwide, a legacy of its fragmented highland geography. English, Tok Pisin (an English-based creole and the most widely understood lingua franca), and Hiri Motu are the three official languages.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, cia.gov/the-world-factbook

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — Harambe

  • Name: Harambe, a 17-year-old western lowland gorilla who lived at the Cincinnati Zoo.
  • Origin/history: On May 28, 2016, a three-year-old boy climbed into Harambe's enclosure and fell into the moat; zoo staff, judging the child to be in mortal danger, shot and killed Harambe rather than risk a tranquilizer's delayed effect. Footage of Harambe appearing to drag the child through the water went viral, and the killing immediately sparked a real, heated public debate over zoo safety, animal welfare, and parental responsibility, including a Change.org petition ("Justice for Harambe") that gathered hundreds of thousands of signatures.
  • How it became popular: Within weeks, the internet's response curdled from genuine outrage into deliberate absurdist irony: a fringe protester's sign reading "Dicks Out for Harambe" was seized on and repeated as a nonsensical rallying cry, and the gorilla was ironically "memorialized" with mock-solemn tributes, fake shrines, and jokes across Twitter and Reddit. The joke escalated further that November, when thousands of Americans cast write-in presidential votes for "Harambe" in the 2016 US election, cementing it as one of the year's largest and most-discussed meme events.
  • Meaning/usage: Functions less as a tribute to the actual animal and more as a satire of performative internet grief — invoking Harambe ironically mocks people who publicly overdramatize mourning for something the mocker considers undeserving of that level of attention, while also functioning as a nihilistic in-joke shared by people who know the reference is "supposed" to be dead by now.
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned an enormous volume of remix images and captions, "dicks out for Harambe" chants shouted at sporting events and other public gatherings, in-game memorials built by players in unrelated video games, and countless "RIP Harambe" jokes recycled every May 28 anniversary; it is frequently cited by internet-culture researchers and journalists as the definitive case study of a real tragedy being absorbed, flattened, and repurposed into pure meme material.
  • Current status: Largely dormant as an actively circulating joke by the early 2020s, but it remains one of the most-referenced "canonical" memes of the mid-2010s, regularly cited in retrospectives on "peak 2016 internet" and as the moment ironic-mourning memes entered the cultural mainstream.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, knowyourmeme.com

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — In the Mood for Love (花樣年華, 2000, dir. Wong Kar-wai)

  • Name/creator(s): In the Mood for Love (花樣年華), written and directed by Wong Kar-wai, shot by two cinematographers — Christopher Doyle and Mark Lee Ping-bin — and starring Tony Leung Chiu-wai and Maggie Cheung.
  • Era/background: A Hong Kong production (Jet Tone Films) set in 1962 Hong Kong, notorious for its improvisational, script-as-you-go production process that stretched shooting over roughly 15 months as Wong reshot and reconceived scenes; it premiered in competition at the 2000 Cannes Film Festival, where Tony Leung won Best Actor.
  • Plot/content overview: Two neighbors who move into adjoining Hong Kong apartments on the same day — Chow Mo-wan, a journalist, and Su Li-zhen, a secretary — gradually realize their respective spouses (who are seen only from behind or not at all) are having an affair with each other. Bonding first over that shared betrayal, Chow and Su begin meeting privately, rehearsing how their spouses might have started their affair, and develop their own deep, unconsummated emotional intimacy, all while consciously resisting becoming exactly what their spouses became.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Defined by a lush, saturated color palette anchored by Maggie Cheung's more than twenty distinct cheongsams (qipao); recurring slow-motion passages set to Shigeru Umebayashi's "Yumeji's Theme" and a rotation of Nat King Cole Spanish-language songs; extremely tight, doorway- and corridor-framed compositions that visually compress the characters inside cramped, watched spaces; and a deliberately elliptical narrative that withholds full scenes and even faces (the two unfaithful spouses are never shown), forcing the audience to read emotion through gesture, glance, and repetition rather than plot exposition.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely regarded as a defining work of Hong Kong art cinema and of Wong Kar-wai's international reputation, and celebrated for its meticulous, nostalgic recreation of a vanished 1960s Hong Kong dominated by Shanghainese émigré culture (Wong's own childhood milieu).
  • Influence and legacy: Topped the BBC's 2016 poll of the 21st century's greatest films (voted on by 177 critics worldwide); its score and visual grammar are explicitly cited as influences by directors including Barry Jenkins (Moonlight quotes "Yumeji's Theme" directly) and Sofia Coppola, and it triggered a lasting revival of interest in the cheongsam/qipao in fashion; Wong later revisited its world and characters in the loosely connected 2046 (2004).
  • Current status/where to experience: Restored in 4K and released by the Criterion Collection in 2021 (alongside a "Wong Kar-wai" box set), and frequently programmed at retrospectives and repertory cinemas worldwide; also streaming on major arthouse-focused platforms.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, criterion.com

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Depth of Field & Focus (景深與焦點控制) (Lesson 16)

Lesson 3 (Cinematography & Lighting) looked at the quality and direction of light, and Lesson 1 (Mise-en-scène) briefly used a deep-focus example in passing without naming the technique itself. Today's lens isolates that specific tool on its own terms: what the lens's focus is doing, independent of lighting, color, or composition.

The method: A shot's depth of field is the range of distance from the camera that stays acceptably sharp. In deep focus, that range is wide — foreground, middle ground, and background can all be sharp simultaneously (achieved with a small aperture, wide-angle lens, and strong lighting to compensate for the smaller aperture), and the effect is often described as "democratic": the viewer's eye is free to roam the frame and choose what to look at, rather than being told. In shallow focus, that range narrows to a single thin plane — everything nearer or farther blurs into bokeh — which isolates a subject from its surroundings and directs the eye with much less viewer choice; it's frequently used to convey psychological isolation, tunnel-vision, or to separate a character emotionally from a crowded environment they're physically standing inside. A rack focus (or "pulling focus") is a live shift of the lens's focus from one plane to another within a single continuous shot — no cut required — used either to redirect the audience's attention (revealing something that was there all along but unreadable while blurred) or to mark an emotional pivot (shifting focus onto a listener's reaction just as they process what's being said). When analyzing focus, ask: what is kept legible and what is deliberately obscured, is a focus shift functioning as a reveal or as an emotional beat, and does the depth of field's narrowness or width match how much control/clarity the scene is granting its characters.

Worked example — E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982, dir. Steven Spielberg, cinematography by Allen Daviau), the toolshed reveal: At night, young Elliott sits in his family's suburban backyard leaving out a trail of Reese's Pieces, with tall cornstalks and foliage placed heavily out of focus in the extreme foreground of the shot. As Elliott watches, the lens racks focus away from that blurred foreground vegetation and onto the previously illegible shape behind it, which sharpens into E.T.'s unmistakable silhouette and glowing eyes.

What to look for and why it works: (1) The reveal happens entirely through focus, with no cut and no camera movement — this keeps the audience locked into Elliott's exact optical experience in real time, so we don't just watch him discover E.T., we discover E.T. at the identical instant and rate he does. (2) The blurred foreground isn't wasted space — it's doing the withholding — shallow focus here is used specifically to create uncertainty (is that a shape, or just plants moving in the dark?) before the rack resolves the ambiguity, which is a very different job than the isolating/psychological use of shallow focus described in the method above; here shallow focus creates suspense through obscured information rather than isolating an already-clear subject. (3) The rack focus mirrors the film's whole thematic stance toward E.T. — vision here is gradual, earned, and childlike rather than immediate and authoritative (contrast this with how adult characters in the film are kept from ever clearly seeing E.T. at all), reinforcing that clarity in this story is something that has to swim up out of blur rather than being granted all at once.

3) 西班牙語課程 — 第三十天(10 分鐘,銜接第二十九天內容)

第二十七天學了「希望對方做某事」(querer/esperar que),第二十八天學了懷疑與非人稱的可能性表達,第二十九天學了情感反應。今天要加入第四組觸發情境:建議、請求與必要性——這是主詞主動想要「影響」另一個主詞行為的句型,同時還藏著一個真正棘手的陷阱:動詞 decir(說)本身,竟然會依語意不同而觸發不同語氣。

  • Te recomiendo que estudies más gramática. — 我建議你多讀一點文法。
  • Mi jefe pide que lleguemos a las ocho. — 我老闆要求我們八點到。
  • Es necesario que todos firmen el documento. — 大家都必須簽署這份文件。
  • Le digo que venga temprano, no que viene temprano. — 我是在「叫他」早點來,不是在「說他」早點來。

文法重點:建議/請求類動詞(recomendar、sugerir、pedir、exigir)沿用第二十七天教過「兩個不同主詞+虛擬式子句」的結構,但語意更進一步,標記「主動想影響對方行為」而非單純的心願;非人稱必要性表達(es necesario/importante que)則是把第二十八天教過的非人稱表達類別,從「可能性」延伸到「必要性」。今天最尖銳的陷阱是動詞 decir:當它用來轉述事實時(「Le digo que viene temprano.」——我告訴他[事實]他早點來),接直陳式;但當它用來下達命令時(「Le digo que venga temprano.」——我叫他早點來),則要接虛擬式,因為這時候的 decir,本質上就是在扮演今天學的 pedir(要求)的角色。學完今天的內容,WEIRDO 記憶口訣(Wishes 希望、Emotions 情感、Impersonal expressions 非人稱表達、Recommendations 建議、Doubt 懷疑、Ojalá 希望詞)六大類別已經教完五類,只剩下 Ojalá 尚待未來專門一課介紹。

4) 易經卦象

第三十卦:離卦(Lí)——「附麗光明」/「火」(上離下離,是繼第二十九卦之後,上經中第二個「同一單卦自我重疊」而成的卦象,也是六十四卦中——連同第一卦乾與第二卦坤——僅有的四個「純卦」之一)。〈序卦傳〉直接說明了從昨日危險到今日的轉折:「陷必有所麗,故受之以離;離者,麗也。」(一旦陷入坑洞險境,必須依附著某個東西才能脫困,因此坎卦之後便是離卦。離,意即「依附、附著」。)如果說坎卦(第二十九卦)談的是反覆出現、必須靠恆常誠信去承受的危險,離卦則給出了答案:要在坑洞中求生,就必須找到可以依附的實體——而這種「依附」正是今天的核心意象:光明本身並非獨立存在,而是必須依附燃料才能燃燒。卦辭寫道:「離,利貞,亨。畜牝牛,吉。」(附麗光明之卦:正固能帶來利益與亨通;畜養母牛則會帶來吉祥。)溫馴、滋養萬物的母牛(陰性的象徵)這個意象,正暗示了這一卦的核心教誨:水能靠自身流動便維持本性,火卻沒有獨立的存在——它必須依附柴薪、油脂或其他燃料才能燃燒,因此這一卦告誡的是相應的柔順與依附,而非躁進、無所憑藉的行動。〈彖傳〉進一步闡述這個意象:「離,麗也。日月麗乎天,百穀草木麗乎土,重明以麗乎正,乃化成天下。柔麗乎中正,故亨,是以畜牝牛吉也。」(離,意即依附。日月依附於天,百穀草木依附於土地,雙重的光明依附於正道,便能化育、成就天下萬物。柔順之爻依附於中正之位,因此得以亨通,這也是畜養母牛會帶來吉祥的原因。)〈象傳〉補充道:「明兩作,離;大人以繼明照于四方。」(光明接連兩次升起,這正是離卦的象徵;因此德行高尚的人,應當使這份光明持續不斷,照亮四方。)把第二十九卦與第三十卦合起來看,這一對卦象刻意以水火兩種元素,為《易經》「上經」(第一卦至第三十卦)畫下平衡的句點——水(危險、忍耐、無形)與火(光明、依附、照明)相互呼應——而下經則從第三十一卦(咸卦,Xián,「感應」)開始,主題也從宇宙自然力量,轉向人與人之間的感應與關係。

(第三十次介紹的卦象——也是「上經」(第一卦至第三十卦)的最後一卦——已記錄於 ROUTINE_LOG.md 避免未來重複。目前已介紹:第一卦 乾(Qián)、第二卦 坤(Kūn)、第三卦 屯(Zhūn)、第四卦 蒙(Méng)、第五卦 需(Xū)、第六卦 訟(Sòng)、第七卦 師(Shī)、第八卦 比(Bǐ)、第九卦 小畜(Xiǎo Chù)、第十卦 履(Lǚ)、第十一卦 泰(Tài)、第十二卦 否(Pǐ)、第十三卦 同人(Tóng Rén)、第十四卦 大有(Dà Yǒu)、第十五卦 謙(Qiān)、第十六卦 豫(Yù)、第十七卦 隨(Suí)、第十八卦 蠱(Gǔ)、第十九卦 臨(Lín)、第二十卦 觀(Guān)、第二十一卦 噬嗑(Shì Kè)、第二十二卦 賁(Bì)、第二十三卦 剝(Bō)、第二十四卦 復(Fù)、第二十五卦 无妄(Wú Wàng)、第二十六卦 大畜(Dà Chù)、第二十七卦 頤(Yí)、第二十八卦 大過(Dà Guò)、第二十九卦 坎(Kǎn)、第三十卦 離(Lí)。)

5) 國家/地區/政治實體介紹 — 巴布亞紐幾內亞(Papua New Guinea)

  • 位置: 位於大洋洲,占據紐幾內亞島東半部(西半部「巴布亞」屬印尼領土),另外還擁有數百個外島,包括新不列顛島、新愛爾蘭島與布干維爾島;隔托雷斯海峽與澳洲北方相望,屬於廣義的美拉尼西亞文化區。
  • 領土面積: 約 462,840 平方公里——約為台灣(約 3.62 萬平方公里)的 13 倍大,面積與瑞典或美國加州相當;地形以茂密雨林、崎嶇的中央高地與活火山為主,歷史上使許多部落社群彼此長期隔絕。
  • 人口: 約 1,030 萬人(2023 年估計),是太平洋島國中人口最多的國家,差距頗大,但相對於國土面積與地形,人口分布仍相當稀疏。
  • 宗教: 絕大多數人口信仰基督教(超過 95%,主要分為天主教、路德教會,以及 19、20 世紀傳教士引入的各福音派/五旬節派教會),但傳統的萬物有靈信仰與祖先崇拜,在許多鄉村社群中仍與基督教信仰並存,並未被完全取代。
  • 經濟: 高度依賴天然資源出口,包括液化天然氣、黃金與銅礦,以及石油,同時仍有大量人口從事自給自足的農業;儘管資源豐富,巴布亞紐幾內亞仍屬中低所得國家,基礎建設嚴重不足、貧窮率偏高,性別暴力發生率也位居全球前列;該國為大英國協會員國。
  • 歷史: 曾一分為二遭殖民統治——德國統治東北部(德屬紐幾內亞),英國(後轉交澳洲管理)統治東南部(巴布亞領地);第一次世界大戰後,澳洲接管德屬部分,並在第二次世界大戰前統一管理「巴布亞與紐幾內亞領地」,二戰期間該地更成為太平洋戰場上死傷最慘烈的戰役之一(對日本的科科達小徑戰役)的發生地。巴布亞紐幾內亞於 1975 年正式脫離澳洲獨立。
  • 現任領導人: 總理詹姆斯・馬拉佩(James Marape),自 2019 年 5 月上任,並於 2022 年大選後連任;作為大英國協王國成員,現任國家元首為英王查爾斯三世,並由總督在當地代表王室行使職權。
  • 語言: 全世界語言最多元的國家——境內使用中的語言超過 840 種,占全球現存語言總數約 12%,這是其高地地形長期分割隔絕所形成的歷史遺產。官方語言有三種:英語、以英語為基礎的克里奧爾語「托克皮辛語」(Tok Pisin,通行最廣的共通語),以及希里摩圖語(Hiri Motu)。
    資料來源:en.wikipedia.org、cia.gov/the-world-factbook

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 哈蘭貝(Harambe)

  • 名稱: 哈蘭貝(Harambe),一隻曾生活在美國辛辛那提動物園、當時 17 歲的西部低地大猩猩。
  • 起源/歷史: 2016 年 5 月 28 日,一名三歲男童攀爬進入哈蘭貝的猩猩欄舍,跌落護城河中;動物園人員判斷男童生命垂危,決定直接射殺哈蘭貝,而非冒險使用可能延遲生效的麻醉槍。哈蘭貝疑似拖行男童穿越水中的畫面在網路上瘋傳,這起獵殺事件立刻引發真實而激烈的公眾論戰,議題涵蓋動物園安全、動物福利與家長責任,一份名為「Justice for Harambe」(為哈蘭貝討公道)的 Change.org 連署更累積了數十萬人簽署。
  • 如何爆紅: 短短數週內,網路上的反應便從真實的憤怒,轉變為刻意營造的荒謬反諷:一名邊緣抗議者舉著「Dicks Out for Harambe」(字面直譯、實則毫無意義的口號)標語的畫面被大量轉貼、重複傳誦,哈蘭貝也被以裝模作樣的「哀悼」方式在推特與 Reddit 上「紀念」,包括虛構的追思祭壇與各種玩笑。這股玩笑熱潮在同年 11 月進一步升溫——當年美國總統大選中,數千名選民以「Harambe」作為候選人進行「寫入投票」(write-in vote),讓這起事件正式成為當年討論度最高的迷因事件之一。
  • 意義與使用情境: 這個哏與其說是紀念這隻真實存在的動物,不如說更接近諷刺「表演式的網路哀悼」——刻意提起哈蘭貝,是在嘲諷那些公開誇張表達哀傷、但在嘲諷者眼中根本不值得如此大做文章的人,同時也成為一群「明知這個哏早該過氣、卻仍樂在其中」的網友之間帶點虛無感的內部笑話。
  • 變體與演變: 衍生出大量的改圖與配文迷因、在運動賽事等公開場合高喊的「Dicks Out for Harambe」口號、玩家在各種不相干的電玩遊戲中自發建造的紀念裝置,以及每年 5 月 28 日忌日必定重新流傳的「RIP Harambe」玩笑;網路文化研究者與記者也經常將此事件,視為一起真實悲劇如何被網路文化吸收、扁平化,並徹底轉化為純迷因素材的經典案例。
  • 目前現況: 到了 2020 年代初期,這個哏作為「主動流傳中」的笑話已大致沉寂,但它仍是 2010 年代中期最常被引用的「經典級」迷因之一,經常出現在回顧「2016 年網路文化巔峰期」的文章中,並被視為「反諷式哀悼」迷因正式進入主流文化的關鍵時刻。
    資料來源:en.wikipedia.org、knowyourmeme.com

7) 電影/表演藝術介紹 — 《花樣年華》(In the Mood for Love,2000,導演王家衛)

  • 名稱/創作者: 《花樣年華》,編劇與導演皆為王家衛,由杜可風(Christopher Doyle)與李屏賓兩位攝影指導共同掌鏡,梁朝偉與張曼玉主演。

8) 看片方法 — 景深與焦點控制 Depth of Field & Focus(第十六課)

第三課「攝影與燈光」談的是光線的品質與方向,第一課「場面調度」則曾順帶用過一個深焦攝影的例子,卻沒有把這項技巧本身獨立命名、深入拆解。今天的分析視角,要把「鏡頭的焦點在做什麼」單獨拉出來討論,和燈光、色彩、構圖分開處理。

方法說明: 一顆鏡頭的「景深」,指的是從攝影機出發、畫面仍維持清晰銳利的距離範圍。深焦攝影(deep focus)的景深範圍很廣——前景、中景、背景可以同時清晰銳利(通常透過縮小光圈、使用廣角鏡頭,並加強燈光以彌補小光圈造成的進光量不足來達成),這種效果常被形容為「民主式」的:觀眾的視線可以自由在畫面中游走、自行選擇要看哪裡,而不是被鏡頭指定該看哪裡。淺焦攝影(shallow focus)則把清晰範圍縮到極窄的單一平面——前後距離的一切都糊化成散景(bokeh)——藉此把某個主體從周遭環境中「隔離」出來,觀眾幾乎沒有自行選擇要看哪裡的空間;這種手法經常用來表現心理上的孤立、隧道視野般的專注,或是把角色從他實際身處的、擁擠的環境中,在情感上抽離出來。拉焦(rack focus / pulling focus)則是在同一個連續鏡頭之中,不經剪接、即時把焦點從一個平面轉移到另一個平面——可以用來重新導引觀眾的注意力(揭露一個原本存在畫面中、卻因失焦而無法辨識的東西),也可以用來標記情緒的轉折點(把焦點轉移到聆聽者臉上,恰好捕捉到對方聽懂某句話那一瞬間的反應)。分析焦點時,可以問:這個鏡頭刻意讓什麼保持清晰可辨、又刻意讓什麼模糊不清?一次拉焦,功能上是在「揭露訊息」,還是在標記「情緒轉折」?畫面景深的寬窄,是否呼應了這場戲中角色所擁有的掌控感或清晰度的多寡?

實例示範——《E.T. 外星人》(E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial,1982,導演史蒂芬・史匹柏 Steven Spielberg,攝影指導艾倫・戴維奧 Allen Daviau)的工具間初遇場景: 夜裡,年幼的艾略特坐在自家後院,沿路灑下瑞氏花生醬巧克力豆想引誘外星人現身,鏡頭前景刻意安排高聳的玉米稈與植叢、以嚴重失焦的狀態擋在畫面最前方。就在艾略特凝視之際,鏡頭將焦點從那片模糊的前景植物上拉開,轉向原本完全無法辨識的背景形體——畫面漸漸清晰,顯現出 E.T. 那具標誌性的剪影與發光的雙眼。

該注意什麼、為什麼這樣的手法有效:(1)整個「揭露」完全靠焦點的變化完成,既沒有剪接,也沒有運鏡——這讓觀眾被牢牢鎖定在艾略特當下的視覺經驗之中、與他同步進行,我們不是「看著」他發現 E.T.,而是與他在完全相同的時間點、以完全相同的速度,一起「發現」了 E.T.。(2)前景的模糊植叢絕非閒筆,它正在執行「隱藏資訊」的任務——這裡的淺焦是刻意用來製造不確定感(那到底是個形體,還是只是夜裡晃動的植物?),直到拉焦解除這份模糊為止,這和前述方法說明中「隔離既已清晰主體」的淺焦用法截然不同——這裡的淺焦是透過遮蔽資訊來製造懸念,而非隔離一個早已清楚可見的主體。(3)這次拉焦,呼應了整部電影對待「看見 E.T.」這件事的整體立場——這裡的「看見」是漸進的、需要努力掙得的、帶有童稚視角的,而非一步到位、權威式的(對照片中的成年角色,始終被安排成完全看不清 E.T. 的存在),進一步強化了這則故事的核心立場:清晰,是必須從模糊中一點一滴浮現出來的,而不是一次就能被賦予的。
