  • ¿Qué día es hoy? — What day is it today?
  • Hoy es miércoles. — Today is Wednesday.

Grammar note: most day names come from Roman gods/celestial bodies (martes=Mars, miércoles=Mercury, jueves=Jupiter, viernes=Venus), while sábado and domingo have religious roots (Sabbath, "the Lord's day") — a different pattern from English Saturday/Sunday.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 7: 師 (Shī) — "The Army" (Earth over Water). Water gathered within the earth — the image of masses of people held together, mobilized and organized like troops under discipline. The classical judgment: "The Army. Perseverance. A mature/experienced leader (丈人). Good fortune. No blame." Success in any large collective undertaking — a military campaign, but by extension any project requiring many hands — depends on firm, disciplined, and righteous leadership; a leader who commands without genuine authority or moral standing brings danger, while one who mobilizes people justly and keeps discipline strict earns good fortune without going astray. The hexagram immediately follows 訟 (Conflict): when disputes are not resolved by mediation, they can escalate into the organized contention of armies — a caution about where unresolved conflict leads, and about the responsibility of whoever leads the masses that result.

5) Country/Region Spotlight: Uruguay

  • Location & size: A small country in southeastern South America, on the Atlantic coast between Argentina and Brazil, with the Río de la Plata estuary to its south; about 176,215 km² — roughly the size of the US state of Washington, about 4.9× the size of Taiwan (~36,197 km²), and a little smaller than Cambodia.
  • Population: About 3.4-3.5 million, with nearly half concentrated in and around the capital, Montevideo — one of the least densely populated countries in South America outside Patagonia.
  • Religion: Uniquely secular for Latin America — church and state were formally separated in the early 20th century; Uruguay has the region's highest share of people identifying as non-religious/agnostic/atheist (over 35-40%), alongside a Roman Catholic plurality and smaller Protestant and Afro-Brazilian-derived (Umbanda) communities.
  • Economy: A high-income economy by regional standards, built on agriculture and agro-exports (beef, wool, soybeans, rice) plus a growing tech/services sector; nearly all of its electricity comes from renewables (wind, hydro, biomass, solar); known for strong institutions, low corruption, and an extensive social welfare state, earning it a reputation as "the Switzerland of South America."
  • History: Colonized by Spain, contested with Portuguese/Brazilian forces; national hero José Gervasio Artigas led the independence movement in the early 1800s; created in 1828 as an independent buffer state between Argentina and Brazil under British mediation (the Treaty of Montevideo); a 1973-1985 military dictatorship was followed by a stable return to democracy that has held since.
  • Current leadership: President Yamandú Orsi, of the left-wing Frente Amplio (Broad Front) coalition, took office March 1, 2025, after winning the November 2024 runoff — continuing Uruguay's long record of peaceful, competitive transfers of power.
  • Language: Spanish is the sole official language, spoken in the distinctive Rioplatense dialect (shared with neighboring Argentina), which uses "vos" instead of "tú" for informal "you."

6) Internet Meme Spotlight: "8+9" (Taiwan)

  • Name/origin: "8+9" (八加九, often written literally as the equation "8+9") is Taiwanese internet slang that phonetically mimics 八家將 (Bā-jiā-jiàng, "Eight Generals") in Taiwanese Hokkien — a troupe of ritual performers who portray guardian deities in temple parades.
  • How it became popular: During the 2010s, online mockery of the rowdy, aggressive behavior of some (mostly young, male) parade participants and their subculture — flashy scooters, tattoos, betel nut, brash fashion — spread through PTT's Gossiping board and viral Facebook videos; by 2017 "8+9" was a common, if derogatory, catch-all label across Taiwanese social media.
  • Meaning/usage: Used to stereotype a young man as uneducated, rough-mannered, gang-adjacent, or low-brow — roughly analogous to "chav" (UK) or "guido" (US) as a class-coded insult, though it is also sometimes used self-deprecatingly or ironically among friends.
  • Variations: Spun off related terms like "9-native" (9原) fashion parody brands and merchandise that knowingly lean into the aesthetic, plus lighter, less pejorative usages ("8+9 style," "8+9 car mods") describing the associated look rather than the person.
  • Current status: Still in everyday use in Taiwan as of 2026, though its edge has softened somewhat as some of the associated fashion and scooter/car-modification culture has been reclaimed and commercialized rather than purely mocked.

5) 國家/地區介紹:烏拉圭

  • 位置與面積: 位於南美洲東南部大西洋沿岸,介於阿根廷與巴西之間,南側緊鄰拉布拉他河口;面積約 176,215 平方公里——大約與美國華盛頓州相當,約為台灣(約 36,197 平方公里)的 4.9 倍,略小於柬埔寨。
  • 人口: 約 340-350 萬人,其中近半數集中於首都蒙特維多(Montevideo)及其周邊,是南美洲(巴塔哥尼亞地區以外)人口密度最低的國家之一。
  • 宗教: 在拉丁美洲極為罕見地採政教分離——20 世紀初即正式將教會與國家分離;烏拉圭是該地區無宗教信仰/不可知論/無神論人口比例最高的國家(超過 35-40%),同時仍以天主教為多數,並有較小規模的新教與源自非裔巴西傳統的溫班達教(Umbanda)社群。
  • 經濟: 以區域標準而言屬於高所得經濟體,以農業與農產品出口(牛肉、羊毛、大豆、稻米)為基礎,科技與服務業比重也持續成長;電力幾乎全數來自再生能源(風力、水力、生質能、太陽能);以健全的制度、低貪腐程度與完善的社會福利體系聞名,因此有「南美洲的瑞士」之稱。
  • 歷史: 曾為西班牙殖民地,並與葡萄牙/巴西勢力多次爭奪;民族英雄何塞·赫瓦西奧·阿蒂加斯(José Gervasio Artigas)於 19 世紀初領導獨立運動;1828 年在英國調停下,以阿根廷與巴西之間緩衝獨立國家的身分建國(《蒙特維多條約》);1973 年至 1985 年經歷軍事獨裁統治,其後穩定回歸民主體制並延續至今。
  • 現任領導人: 總統亞曼杜·奧爾西(Yamandú Orsi),來自左翼「廣泛陣線」(Frente Amplio)聯盟,於贏得 2024 年 11 月第二輪選舉後,在 2025 年 3 月 1 日就任——延續烏拉圭長期以來和平、具競爭性的政權交接傳統。

6) 網路迷因介紹:「8+9」(台灣)

  • 名稱/起源: 「8+9」(八加九,常直接寫成算式「8+9」)是台灣網路用語,發音上模仿台語「八家將」(Bā-jiā-jiàng)——一種在廟會遶境中扮演護法神將的陣頭表演團體。
  • 如何爆紅: 2010 年代,部分陣頭參與者(多為年輕男性)行為粗魯、招搖的次文化——改裝機車、刺青、嚼檳榔、誇張穿著——在網路上遭到嘲諷,透過 PTT 八卦板與 Facebook 瘋傳影片擴散;到了 2017 年,「8+9」已成為台灣社群媒體上常見(但帶有貶意)的統稱標籤。
  • 意義與使用情境: 用來刻板化形容一個年輕男性沒教養、粗魯、疑似與幫派掛勾或品味低俗——大致類似英式英語的「chav」或美式英語的「guido」這類帶有階級意味的貶稱,但有時朋友之間也會自嘲或戲謔性地使用。
  • 變體: 衍生出如「9 原」(諧擬「8+9」的服飾/周邊品牌)等刻意擁抱該美學的次文化商品,也出現較無貶意的用法(例如「8+9 風格」「8+9 改裝車」),單純形容相關風格而非指涉特定的人。
  • 現況: 截至 2026 年,這個詞在台灣日常生活中仍相當常見,不過隨著相關服飾與機車/汽車改裝文化逐漸被「收編」商業化、而非單純被嘲諷,這個詞的攻擊性已略有淡化。
