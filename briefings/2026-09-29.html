4) Zhuangzi — Essay 11: 在宥 (Zài Yòu) — "Letting Be and Leaving Alone"

The fourth chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇), continuing directly from Essay 10 ("Rifling Trunks"). It opens with its thesis: "I have heard of letting the world be, of leaving it alone (在宥天下); I have never heard of governing the world (治天下)." Zhuangzi worries that governing, and even rewarding and punishing, pushes people's natures out of true; the rewards of virtue and the penalties of vice both warp them. The chapter's set pieces are the Yellow Emperor's audience with the sage Guang Chengzi (廣成子) on Mount Kongtong, who tells him the essence of the Dao is dark and silent: "see nothing, hear nothing, embrace the spirit in stillness, and the body will right itself; guard what is within, shut out what is without, for much knowing is ruin." Guang Chengzi says he has held to the One and rested in harmony for 1,200 years without his body declining. The second is the encounter between Cloud General (雲將) and Great Nebulous (鴻蒙), who is slapping his thighs and hopping about like a bird; Cloud General asks how to nurture all things, and Hongmeng answers with "the fasting of the heart" (心養): discard your body, spit out your cleverness, forget things and merge with the boundless. A line often quoted from the chapter: "That which treats things as things is not itself a thing (物而不物), and so it can be the master of things." The lesson: the ruler who does least best preserves what people already are.

5) Country / Region Spotlight — Brazil (巴西)

  • Location & territory size: Brazil covers roughly half of South America, bordering every South American country except Chile and Ecuador, with a long Atlantic coastline. At about 8.5 million km² it is the world's fifth-largest country, slightly larger than the contiguous United States and roughly 235 times the size of Taiwan.
  • Population: About 212-213 million, the largest in Latin America and the seventh or so in the world; highly urbanized, with São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro among the region's biggest metropolitan areas. The capital is Brasília, purpose-built and inaugurated in 1960.
  • Religion: Historically Roman Catholic (about 56-57% in the 2022 census, down sharply from past decades), with a fast-growing evangelical/Protestant share (roughly a quarter), plus Afro-Brazilian religions such as Candomblé and Umbanda and a growing non-religious group. The state is secular.
  • Economy: The largest economy in Latin America, usually ranked around 9th-10th in the world by nominal GDP (roughly US$2+ trillion), a major exporter of soybeans, iron ore, coffee, beef, sugar, and oil, with a substantial industrial and services base (Embraer aircraft, Petrobras). Persistent challenges include inequality, high interest rates, and fiscal pressure; exact current figures should be checked against a live source.
  • History: Home to Indigenous peoples for millennia before Pedro Álvares Cabral's Portuguese landing in 1500. Colonial rule built a sugar and gold economy on enslaved African labor; Brazil received more enslaved Africans than any other country and abolished slavery last in the Americas (1888). The Portuguese court fled to Rio in 1808, Brazil declared independence in 1822 under Pedro I, the monarchy ended in 1889, a military dictatorship ruled 1964-1985, and the current democratic Constitution dates from 1988.
  • Current leadership: President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers' Party (PT), serving his third (non-consecutive) term since January 2023. A general election is scheduled for early October 2026; check current news for candidates and polling.
  • Language: Portuguese is the official and near-universal language (Brazilian Portuguese differs notably from European Portuguese in pronunciation and vocabulary); more than 200 Indigenous languages survive, plus immigrant languages such as German and Japanese dialects in some communities. Brazil hosts the world's largest Japanese diaspora outside Japan.

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — Keyboard Cat

  • Name & origin: Keyboard Cat is a short video of a cat named Fatso appearing to play a keyboard, filmed by his owner Charlie Schmidt in the 1980s (Schmidt moved the cat's paws to the beat). Schmidt later digitized the tape and uploaded it to YouTube in 2007.
  • How it became popular: In 2009, Brad O'Farrell edited the clip onto the end of a video of someone failing at something (a "fail" clip), with the caption "Play him off, Keyboard Cat" — the cat "plays off" the failing person the way a stage musician would cover a bad act. The pairing spread rapidly through YouTube and forums as a meme format.
  • Meaning & usage context: Adding Keyboard Cat over the end of a mishap turns it into a comic exit — a "walk-off" for a blunder, gaffe, or failed attempt. It works because it recasts an embarrassing moment as a vaudeville hook-pulling.
  • Variations & evolution: The format inspired an endless run of "play him off" edits, live re-creations, and remixes; Schmidt later launched an official second cat, Bento, and licensed the character for commercials and events. The idea also echoes the older "sad trombone" sound gag.
  • Current status: A classic from the earliest era of YouTube meme culture; it is rarely a new trend today but remains a widely recognized reference and is still used for fail edits.

Sources: general reference knowledge (Know Your Meme entry on Keyboard Cat); details such as exact dates were not re-verified live for this run.

7) Film Spotlight — Spirited Away (千と千尋の神隠し, 2001, dir. Hayao Miyazaki)

  • Name & key background: Spirited Away (Japanese: Sen to Chihiro no Kamikakushi, "Sen and Chihiro's Spiriting Away") is an animated fantasy written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki for Studio Ghibli, released in Japan in July 2001. Miyazaki has said he built it around the 10-year-old daughter of a friend, wanting to make a film for girls of that age.
  • Content & overview: Ten-year-old Chihiro, sulking on the way to a new home, wanders with her parents into an abandoned-looking theme park that is actually a spirit world; her parents are transformed into pigs for gluttony. To rescue them she takes a job at a bathhouse for gods run by the witch Yubaba, who takes her name and renames her "Sen." With the help of the river spirit Haku, the boiler man Kamaji, and the lonely masked spirit No-Face, Chihiro grows brave and resourceful and reclaims her name and family.
  • Artistic style & technique: Hand-drawn animation with a limited amount of digital work, rich in detailed backgrounds and fluid character movement; Joe Hisaishi's score supports the quiet, wondering tone. Miyazaki uses the Japanese concept of "ma" — pauses and stillness — such as the silent train ride across the water.
  • Cultural & historical significance: It draws on Shinto ideas of kami (gods) and purification, and on the bathhouse (onsen) as a place of cleansing; its themes include consumerism (the parents' greed, No-Face's hunger), the importance of names and identity, and growing up. It became the highest-grossing film in Japanese history at release and won the Golden Bear at Berlin (2002) and the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature (2003), the first hand-drawn, non-English-language film to win the category (Miyazaki's The Boy and the Heron won it again in 2024).
  • Influence & legacy: It brought Studio Ghibli and Japanese anime to wider global audiences, and is routinely listed among the greatest animated films. The story has been adapted to the stage, and its imagery has shaped popular perceptions of Japanese folklore.
  • Where to experience it today: Widely available on home video and on streaming services in many regions (availability varies by country); the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo, and Ghibli Park in Aichi feature related exhibits; the film still appears in repertory screenings.

Sources: general film-history reference knowledge (Studio Ghibli / Academy Awards records); no live source consulted, so verify streaming availability locally.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Subtext in Dialogue (Lesson 61)

Previous lessons in this track have taught how to read images, movement, editing, and sound; none has taught how to read what characters say. Subtext is the gap between what a line literally says and what the character means or feels. To analyze it, ask four questions of any exchange: (1) What does the character want in this scene? (2) What are they actually saying? (3) What are they avoiding saying, and why (fear, propriety, power, love)? (4) What in the image, pause, or performance betrays the gap — a glance, a held silence, an object, a changed subject? When a scene is about one thing on the surface (ordering lunch, the weather, a rehearsal) but a different thing underneath, the meaning sits in the subtext.

Worked example: In the Mood for Love (2000, dir. Wong Kar-wai). In the restaurant scene, neighbors Mr. Chow (Tony Leung) and Mrs. Chan (Maggie Cheung), who each suspect their spouses of having an affair with each other, decide to rehearse how she should confront her husband. Chow plays the husband and Chan plays herself. On the surface the dialogue is a practice run; underneath, both are discovering their own pain. When Chan slaps "the husband" and then flinches and apologizes as herself, it shows the rehearsal has stopped being pretend. Wong lets the subtext work through the frame: tight two-shots in a booth, the lingering pauses, and the way each of them looks away from the other's eyes. What to look for: the characters never say they are hurt or drawn to each other; the fiction of role-play is the only permitted way to say it. It works because the constraint (propriety, and the fear of becoming what their spouses are) forces feeling into indirect speech, and the audience feels the pressure of everything unsaid.

4)莊子——第 11 篇:在宥(Zài Yòu)——「讓天下自在」

《外篇》第四篇,直接承接第 10 篇〈胠篋〉。開篇即立論:「聞在宥天下,不聞治天下也。」莊子擔心「治理」——乃至賞善罰惡——會讓人的本性走樣,獎賞德行與懲罰惡行都會扭曲人。本篇兩個名場面:一是黃帝到崆峒山拜見廣成子問至道,廣成子說道的精髓幽深寂靜:「無視無聽,抱神以靜,形將自正……慎汝內,閉汝外,多知為敗」;他自稱守住「一」、處於和諧,修身一千二百年而形體未曾衰老。二是雲將與鴻蒙的相遇:雲將遊東方,遇見鴻蒙拍著大腿像鳥一樣蹦跳,雲將問怎麼養育萬物,鴻蒙答以「心養」:墮爾形體,吐爾聰明,忘卻外物,與無邊的渾沌合一。本篇常被引用的一句是「物而不物,故能物物」——不把自己當成物的,才能主宰萬物。啟示:最少干預的統治者,反而最能保全人民原本的樣子。

6)網路迷因介紹——鍵盤貓(Keyboard Cat)

  • 名稱與起源:鍵盤貓是一段名叫 Fatso 的貓似乎在彈奏電子琴的短片,由飼主 Charlie Schmidt 於 1980 年代拍攝(他握著貓爪隨節拍移動)。後來 Schmidt 把錄影帶數位化,於 2007 年上傳到 YouTube。
  • 如何爆紅:2009 年,Brad O'Farrell 把這段影片接在一段有人搞砸事情的「失敗影片」結尾,並加上標語「Play him off, Keyboard Cat」(讓鍵盤貓送他下台)——就像舞台樂手用音樂把出糗的表演者請下台。這個組合迅速在 YouTube 與論壇擴散成迷因格式。
  • 意義與使用情境:在失誤、出糗或失敗的片段結尾接上鍵盤貓,就變成一種喜劇式的「謝幕」:把尷尬時刻包裝成雜耍劇場的下台橋段。
  • 變體與演變:此格式衍生出無數「play him off」剪輯、現場重現與混音版本;Schmidt 後來推出第二隻官方貓 Bento,並把角色授權給廣告與活動使用。這個概念也呼應更早的「悲傷長號」音效梗。

7)電影介紹——《神隱少女》(千と千尋の神隠し,2001,導演:宮崎駿)

  • 名稱與背景:《神隱少女》(日文原名《千與千尋的神隱》)是宮崎駿為吉卜力工作室編導的奇幻動畫,2001 年 7 月在日本上映。宮崎駿曾表示,他是以一位朋友十歲的女兒為對象,想為這個年紀的女孩做一部電影。
  • 劇情概要:十歲的千尋在搬家途中悶悶不樂,和父母誤入一座看似廢棄的主題樂園,其實是靈界;父母因貪吃變成豬。為了救他們,她到女巫湯婆婆經營的神明澡堂工作,名字被奪走並改名「千」。在河神少年白龍、鍋爐爺爺與孤獨戴面具的無臉男幫助下,千尋變得勇敢、機智,最後找回名字與家人。
  • 藝術風格與技法:以手繪動畫為主、少量數位輔助,背景細緻、角色動作流暢;久石讓的配樂襯托安靜而充滿驚奇的氣氛。宮崎駿運用日本的「間」(ma)——停頓與靜止,例如那段安靜地穿越水面的電車場景。
  • 文化與歷史意義:電影取材於神道教的神明與淨化觀念,澡堂正是洗滌之處;主題包括消費主義(父母的貪婪、無臉男的飢渴)、名字與身分的重要,以及成長。上映時成為日本影史票房冠軍,並獲得柏林影展金熊獎(2002)與奧斯卡最佳動畫長片獎(2003),是首部贏得該獎的手繪、非英語電影(宮崎駿的《蒼鷺與少年》於 2024 年再度獲獎)。
  • 影響與傳承:讓吉卜力工作室與日本動畫被全球更廣大的觀眾認識,常被列入影史最偉大動畫片之列。故事已被改編為舞台劇,片中影像也影響了大眾對日本民間信仰的想像。

8)看片方法——對白中的潛台詞(第 61 課)

此系列先前教過如何讀影像、運鏡、剪接與聲音,但還沒教如何讀角色「說出口的話」。潛台詞(subtext)是一句台詞字面意思與角色真正想法或感受之間的落差。分析時對任何一段對話問四個問題:(1)這個角色在這場戲想要什麼?(2)他實際說了什麼?(3)他迴避不說的是什麼,為什麼(恐懼、禮教、權力、愛)?(4)畫面、停頓或演技中有什麼洩漏了這個落差——一個眼神、一段沉默、一件物品、突然轉換的話題?當一場戲表面上談論的是一件事(點餐、天氣、排練),底下卻是另一件事時,意義就在潛台詞裡。

示範:《花樣年華》(2000,導演:王家衛)。在餐廳那場戲中,鄰居周慕雲(梁朝偉)與陳太太(張曼玉)各自懷疑配偶彼此有染,決定演練陳太太該如何面對自己的丈夫。周扮演丈夫,陳扮演自己。表面上這是排練;底下兩人正在發現自己的痛苦。當陳太太打了「丈夫」一巴掌,隨後以自己的身分縮了一下並道歉,顯示這場排練已經不再是假裝。王家衛讓潛台詞透過畫面運作:包廂裡緊湊的雙人鏡頭、拉長的停頓,以及兩人彼此避開對方目光的方式。要觀察的重點:兩人從未說出自己受傷或被對方吸引;角色扮演的虛構是唯一被允許的說法。它之所以有效,是因為限制(禮教,以及害怕變成配偶那樣的人)迫使情感只能以間接的言語出現,觀眾因此感受到所有未說出口的東西所帶來的壓力。
