ENEnglish version

Topic: "look for", "find" and "run into" — buscar, encontrar and encontrarse con. Chinese speakers often mix up 找 (looking) and 找到 (finding).

🎯 Learning Goal: say that you are looking for something or someone (Busco mis llaves), that you found it or can't find it (¡Lo encontré! / No lo encuentro), that you bumped into someone by chance (Me encontré con Ana), and that you will pick someone up (Te paso a buscar a las ocho). The key test: buscar = the process of looking; encontrar = the result of finding. Chinese 找不到 = no encuentro, never no busco.

Vocabulary: buscar (regular, but past busqué, c→qu; negative command no busques) · encontrar (o→ue: encuentro, encuentras, encuentra, encontramos; past encontré) · encontrarse con alguien (run into someone) · encontrarse bien / mal / mejor (feel well / unwell / better) · se encuentra en… (is located in…, formal) · perder (e→ie, to lose: He perdido el móvil) · buscar trabajo / piso (look for a job / a flat; piso in Spain, departamento/apartamento in Latin America) · pasar a buscar / ir a buscar (go and pick someone or something up; also recoger) · objetos perdidos (lost and found).

Grammar Point: (1) buscar already contains the idea of English "for", so no preposition follows: ✗ busco por mis llaves → ✔ Busco mis llaves. For a specific person, add the personal a: Busco a mi hermano. For an unknown person who must meet a condition, there is usually no a, and the relative clause takes the subjunctive: Busco un profesor que hable chino. (2) encontrar is the result: ¡Lo encontré!, ¿Encontraste trabajo?; "I can't find it" is simply No lo encuentro (no poder needed). Both verbs often appear together: Busqué por todas partes y no lo encontré ("I looked everywhere and didn't find it" — here por todas partes is a place, not "for"). (3) encontrarse con = run into by chance; compare quedar con = meet by arrangement (Day 80) and conocer = meet for the first time: Me encontré con mi profesora en el súper / He quedado con Marta a las seis / Conocí a Marta en Madrid. (4) encontrarse also means "to feel" (Hoy me encuentro mucho mejor) and, formally, "to be located" (La estación se encuentra a cinco minutos). (5) pasar a buscar / ir a buscar = go and fetch: Te paso a buscar a las ocho, Voy a buscar a los niños al colegio; the pronoun can follow the infinitive or go in front (paso a buscarlas / las paso a buscar, Day 45).

Example Sentences:

  • ¿Qué buscas? — What are you looking for?
  • Busco mis llaves. No las encuentro. — I'm looking for my keys. I can't find them.
  • ¿No te encontraste con Pablo ayer? Él las tenía. — Didn't you run into Pablo yesterday? He had them.
  • ¡Es verdad! Paso a buscarlas esta tarde. — That's right! I'll go and get them this afternoon.
  • Busqué por todas partes y no lo encontré. — I looked everywhere and didn't find it.
  • Estoy buscando la estación. — I'm looking for the station (a polite way to ask for directions).
  • El que busca, encuentra. — He who seeks, finds (proverb; also Quien busca, halla).

Culture and usage tip: when you lose something while travelling, ask ¿Dónde está la oficina de objetos perdidos? ("Where is the lost-and-found office?"). Starting a question with Estoy buscando… sounds softer and more natural than a bare ¿Dónde está…?

Chapter 31 — 夫佳兵者 (Fū jiā bīng zhě) — "Fine Weapons Are Instruments of Ill Omen"

Original text (received Wang Bi edition):

夫佳兵者,不祥之器,物或惡之,故有道者不處。君子居則貴左,用兵則貴右。兵者不祥之器,非君子之器,不得已而用之,恬淡為上。勝而不美,而美之者,是樂殺人。夫樂殺人者,則不可以得志於天下矣。吉事尚左,凶事尚右。偏將軍居左,上將軍居右,言以喪禮處之。殺人之眾,以哀悲泣之,戰勝以喪禮處之。

Translation: "Fine weapons are instruments of ill omen; things seem to loathe them, so one who has the Way does not dwell with them. In ordinary life the noble person honours the left; when using arms, the right is honoured. Weapons are instruments of ill omen, not the noble person's instruments; when there is no choice but to use them, calm restraint is best. Victory is not something beautiful; to find it beautiful is to delight in killing people. One who delights in killing cannot have his way in the world. In auspicious affairs the left is honoured; in inauspicious affairs, the right. The lieutenant general stands on the left, the supreme general on the right — that is, war is treated as a funeral. When many people have been killed, mourn them with grief and sorrow; a victory in war should be handled with the rites of mourning."

Key words and textual notes:

  • 佳兵: literally "fine weapons". The Qing philologist Wang Niansun (王念孫) argued that 佳 is a miscopying of 隹 (= 唯), giving "夫唯兵者" — "weapons, indeed, are…". The Mawangdui silk manuscripts simply read 夫兵者. Either way, the subject is weapons and war in general, not only "good" weapons.
  • 物或惡之,故有道者不處 repeats a line from Chapter 24 (about tiptoeing and boasting). In both places, the Daodejing treats something as unnatural excess that the sage avoids.
  • Left and right (左/右): in Zhou ritual the left belonged to yang, life and good fortune; the right to yin, death and mourning. Placing the supreme general on the right — the mourners' side — means the army is organised like a funeral procession.
  • Textual history: received Wang Bi editions carry no commentary for this chapter, and since the Song dynasty some scholars suspected that parts of it were old commentary that had slipped into the main text. But the Guodian bamboo slips (c. 300 BCE, bundle C) contain the chapter's second half, from 君子居則貴左, which shows that this material is early. The final 泣之 ("weep over them") appears in the excavated texts as 立/莅 ("attend to them"), so some modern editors read "attend to it with grief" rather than "weep".

Historical and philosophical background: the chapter belongs to the Warring States world, when states mobilised whole populations and battles killed tens of thousands. It continues yesterday's Chapter 30 (以道佐人主者), which warned that "where armies camp, thorns and brambles grow" and that a good commander stops once the result (果) is achieved. Chapter 31 moves from strategy to attitude: force may sometimes be unavoidable (不得已, the same phrase as in Chapters 29 and 30), but it must be used with 恬淡 — calm, without excitement or appetite. This is close to, but not the same as, pacifism: the text accepts that defence may be necessary, yet refuses to glorify it. Later thinkers drew on this: Han-dynasty Huang–Lao thought and military writers who quoted it often stressed restraint, and the idea that victory should be followed by mourning rituals has echoes in ancient Chinese practice of honouring the dead of both sides.

Philosophical implications:

  1. The danger is in the delight. The text does not say "never fight"; it says "whoever enjoys killing cannot win the world". The moral test is the emotion with which force is used.
  2. Victory is a loss to be mourned. Treating triumph as a funeral reverses ordinary values: the parade becomes a procession of mourners.
  3. Ritual shapes feeling. By assigning war to the "inauspicious" side, ritual itself teaches people how to feel about it.

Modern reflection: we use military language everywhere — "price war", "talent war", "crushing the competition", "killing it". Chapter 31 suggests a quiet test: when you have to compete hard — a layoff decision, a lawsuit, a negotiation that leaves the other side worse off — do it with 恬淡, not triumph. Win if you must, but don't make the other side's loss your celebration. And at the level of history, it asks us to remember that every war memorial is, in Laozi's terms, the correct ritual for a "victory": a funeral.

白話翻譯:「精良的兵器是不吉祥的東西,萬物似乎都厭惡它,所以有道的人不靠近它。君子平時以左邊為尊,用兵時則以右邊為尊。兵器是不吉祥的東西,不是君子該用的器物;不得已而使用時,最好保持淡然克制。打了勝仗也不認為是美事;把勝利當成美事的人,就是喜歡殺人。喜歡殺人的人,不可能在天下實現他的志向。吉慶的事以左為上,凶喪的事以右為上。偏將軍站在左邊,上將軍站在右邊,意思是用辦喪事的禮儀來對待戰爭。殺了很多人,要用哀痛悲傷的心情去面對;打了勝仗,也要用喪禮來處理。」

關鍵字與版本說明:

  • 佳兵:字面是「精良的兵器」。清代學者王念孫認為「佳」是「隹」(即「唯」)的誤寫,原文應是「夫唯兵者」——「兵器這種東西……」。馬王堆帛書直接寫作「夫兵者」。不論哪種讀法,主題都是兵器與戰爭本身,不只是「好的」兵器。
  • 「物或惡之,故有道者不處」重複了第 24 章(講踮腳、自誇)的句子。在兩處,《道德經》都把某件事視為違反自然的過度,聖人會避開。
  • 左與右:在周代禮制中,左屬陽,代表生與吉;右屬陰,代表死與喪。把上將軍放在右邊——也就是送葬者的位置——意思是軍隊的編排就像一支送葬隊伍。
  • 版本流傳:王弼通行本這一章沒有注文,從宋代起就有學者懷疑部分內容是古注混入正文。但郭店楚簡(約西元前 300 年,丙組)收有本章後半(從「君子居則貴左」開始),顯示這些文字相當早就存在。結尾「泣之」在出土文本中作「立/莅」(親臨、面對),所以有些現代學者讀成「以悲哀的心情去面對」,而不是「哭泣」。

歷史與思想背景:這一章屬於戰國時代的世界:國家動員全體人民,一場戰役可能死傷數萬人。它延續了昨天的第 30 章(以道佐人主者)——那一章警告「師之所處,荊棘生焉」,並說善於用兵的人達成結果(果)就停止。第 31 章則從戰略轉向態度:武力有時無法避免(「不得已」,和第 29、30 章同一個詞),但使用時必須「恬淡」——平靜、沒有興奮與貪欲。這接近反戰,但不完全等於反戰:文本接受防衛可能必要,卻拒絕美化戰爭。後來漢代的黃老思想以及引用這一章的兵家作者,多半強調克制;而「打勝仗要用喪禮處理」的想法,也和中國古代祭悼雙方陣亡者的做法有所呼應。

哲學意涵:

  1. 危險在於「樂在其中」。文本沒有說「永遠不要打仗」,而是說「樂殺人者,不可以得志於天下」。道德的檢驗在於使用武力時的心情。
  2. 勝利是一種應該哀悼的損失。把凱旋當成喪禮,翻轉了一般的價值:慶功遊行變成了送葬行列。
  3. 禮儀塑造情感。把戰爭歸到「凶事」那一邊,禮儀本身就在教人們該用什麼心情看待戰爭。

現代反思:我們到處都在用軍事語言——「價格戰」「人才爭奪戰」「把對手殺得片甲不留」。第 31 章提供一個安靜的檢驗:當你必須激烈競爭時——做裁員決定、打官司、一場讓對方吃虧的談判——用「恬淡」的態度去做,而不是得意洋洋。必須贏就贏,但不要把對方的損失當成你的慶典。從歷史的層次來看,它也提醒我們:每一座戰爭紀念碑,用老子的話來說,就是對「勝利」正確的禮儀——一場喪禮。

第 4 段 — 國家:維德角(Cabo Verde)

ENEnglish version

Name and location: the Republic of Cabo Verde (República de Cabo Verde; formerly known in English as Cape Verde — in 2013 the government asked the UN to use "Cabo Verde" in all languages). It is an Atlantic archipelago about 570 km west of Senegal, named after Cap-Vert, the "green cape" near Dakar. It has ten islands (nine inhabited; Santa Luzia is uninhabited), grouped as the windward Barlavento islands (Santo Antão, São Vicente, Santa Luzia, São Nicolau, Sal, Boa Vista) and the leeward Sotavento islands (Maio, Santiago, Fogo, Brava). The capital, Praia, is on Santiago. The highest point is the active volcano Pico do Fogo (about 2,829 m), which last erupted in 2014–15.

Size: about 4,033 km² — roughly 11% of Taiwan, a little smaller than Hualien County (about 4,629 km²), and about 1.6 times the size of Luxembourg. The land is spread across islands hundreds of kilometres apart, which makes transport expensive.

Population: about 491,000 in the 2021 census; recent international estimates are around 500,000–525,000 (UNFPA). Most people are of mixed African and Portuguese descent. The diaspora is often said to be larger than the population at home, with large communities in the United States (especially Massachusetts and Rhode Island), Portugal, the Netherlands (Rotterdam) and France.

Religion: mainly Roman Catholic (roughly three-quarters of the population), with Protestant churches (including the Church of the Nazarene and Adventists), a small Muslim community and a few people with no religion.

Languages: Portuguese is the official language, used in government and schools. Almost everyone speaks Cape Verdean Creole (Kriolu or Kabuverdianu) at home, with different varieties on different islands; there has long been debate about making Creole a co-official language.

Economy: the islands have few natural resources, little rain and little farmland, so the country imports most of its food and fuel. Tourism — beach resorts on Sal and Boa Vista — is the largest sector, roughly a quarter of GDP; the World Bank reports real growth of 6.3% in 2025 thanks to record arrivals (World Bank). Remittances from the diaspora, fishing, and services (including the port of Mindelo) also matter. The Cape Verdean escudo is pegged to the euro. Cabo Verde graduated from the UN's "least developed country" category in 2007, and in January 2024 the WHO certified it as malaria-free.

History:

  • The islands were uninhabited when Portuguese navigators reached them around 1456–1462. Ribeira Grande (today Cidade Velha) on Santiago became the first European colonial town in the tropics and a major hub of the Atlantic slave trade; it is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2009).
  • Repeated droughts caused deadly famines, the last major one in the 1940s, and drove centuries of emigration — including on American whaling ships.
  • Amílcar Cabral (born in Guinea-Bissau to Cape Verdean parents) founded the PAIGC, which fought an armed independence war mainly in Guinea-Bissau. Cabral was assassinated in Conakry in January 1973. After Portugal's Carnation Revolution (1974), Cabo Verde became independent on 5 July 1975, with Aristides Pereira as first president.
  • Plans for union with Guinea-Bissau ended after a 1980 coup in Bissau; the Cape Verdean branch became the PAICV, which ruled as a single party until 1990. The first multi-party elections in 1991 were won by the opposition MpD, and power has changed hands peacefully several times since.

Current leaders and politics: Cabo Verde is a semi-presidential republic and one of Africa's most stable democracies. President José Maria Neves (PAICV, in office since 2021) won re-election on 15 November 2026 with about 64% of the vote in provisional results, but turnout was only about 31.7% (Africanews; AP via ABC News). Prime Minister Francisco Carvalho (PAICV, former mayor of Praia) has led the government since 19 June 2026, after his party won an absolute majority (37 of 72 seats) in the 17 May 2026 parliamentary election, ending ten years of MpD government under Ulisses Correia e Silva (Rio Times; Wikipedia). The president's role is mostly ceremonial but includes a veto.

International relations: close ties with Portugal and the Lusophone world (CPLP); a "special partnership" with the EU since 2007; ECOWAS membership; two US Millennium Challenge Corporation compacts. It has diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China (since 1976), not with Taiwan, and China has funded several public buildings in Praia. Its location makes it a stopover for Atlantic shipping and a front line against drug trafficking routes from South America.

Culture: music is central. Morna — slow, melancholic songs about longing (sodade) and emigration — was added to UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2019. Its most famous voice was Cesária Évora (1941–2011), the "barefoot diva" from Mindelo. Other styles include energetic funaná (accordion and the scraped iron ferrinho), which was discouraged in colonial times, and batuku, traditionally performed by women. The national dish is cachupa, a slow-cooked stew of maize and beans. Mindelo's Carnival and the Baía das Gatas music festival are well known. In football, Cabo Verde qualified for the 2026 FIFA World Cup — its first ever — making it one of the smallest countries ever to qualify.

Current challenges: water scarcity (much drinking water comes from desalination) and drought made worse by climate change; dependence on imported food and energy; high public debt; a tourism economy exposed to shocks such as the COVID-19 pandemic; youth emigration; and, as the 2026 election shows, low voter turnout.

ENEnglish version

Name and variations: "Pointing Rick Dalton", also called "Leonardo DiCaprio Pointing", "Leo Pointing at the TV" or simply "That's me!" / "Hey, I know that!" in captions.

Origin: the image comes from Quentin Tarantino's Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (released 26 July 2019). Leonardo DiCaprio plays Rick Dalton, a fading star of 1950s–60s TV westerns. In one scene, Rick sits on a couch with his stunt double and friend Cliff Booth (Brad Pitt) and watches his own guest appearance on the real TV series The F.B.I.. When he appears on the screen, Rick leans forward and points at the TV with an excited, slightly drunk, proud expression (Know Your Meme).

How it spread: the film was a hit in 2019, but the screenshot became a meme only months later. Meme.com traces an early meme use to a Twitter post on 7 February 2020, and The Mary Sue and the Daily Dot noted that fans began using it widely in early 2020 (Meme.com; Daily Dot). It worked because the pose is instantly readable even without knowing the film: a grown man thrilled to recognise something on a screen.

Meaning and usage: the meme expresses excited recognition — the feeling when you spot a reference, a cameo, an in-joke, a place you know, or something from your own niche interest in a film, game, or news story. Typical caption: "Me when the movie mentions my hometown", "Anime fans when a character does the pose from episode 3", or "Taiwanese viewers when a Hollywood film shows a 7-Eleven in Taipei". It is affectionate and slightly self-mocking: you are laughing at how excited you get about recognising something small.

Common variations and evolution:

  • Two-panel format: the thing being recognised (pasted onto the TV or placed in a second panel) plus Rick pointing.
  • Chain references: Rick pointing at another meme, used when one meme references another — a joke about meme literacy.
  • Contrast with "Spider-Man Pointing at Spider-Man": that meme (introduced earlier in this series) is about two things being the same or blaming each other; Rick Dalton is about one viewer recognising something.
  • Related DiCaprio images, such as the raised-glass toast from The Great Gatsby (2013) and "Strutting Leo", are separate memes and not part of this one.

Irony and cultural context: there is an extra layer for film fans. In the movie, Rick is pointing at himself — an insecure actor briefly proud of his own work. Using the image for "I recognise this!" quietly keeps that vanity: the recogniser feels smart for knowing. The film's title also echoes Sergio Leone's Once Upon a Time in the West (today's Section 8 example), and Rick's whole storyline is about a star's screen image — a nice link to today's film-analysis lesson on star personas.

Current status: it peaked around 2020–2021 and is now an evergreen reaction image — widely understood and still used, especially in fan communities and when trailers reveal cameos, but not a currently trending meme. There is no evidence of a new revival in 2026.

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 8: for / since / during / while (Stage 2, time).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese says 我住在台北五年了、從 2015 年開始、在德國的時候、開會的時候 — with words like 了, 從, 的時候 that do not map one-to-one onto English. Learners often say "since five years", "during I was in Germany" or "I studied English during three years".
  • Core concept — two questions and two shapes: for answers "How long?" — it measures a length of time (for five years, for two hours). since answers "Since when?" — it marks a starting point that continues up to now (since 2015, since Monday, since I moved here), so it normally goes with the present perfect (I've lived here since 2019). during answers "When?" — something happens inside a named period, and it is followed by a noun (during the meeting, during the typhoon). while means the same "at the same time as", but it is followed by a clause (subject + verb: while I was in Berlin) or an -ing form (while waiting).
  • Comparison: for + length (for ten days) · since + starting point (since 2015) · during + noun (during the holidays) · while + clause (while we were on holiday). Changing the word changes the question: "I was sick for the trip" sounds wrong; "I was sick during the trip" = at some time inside the trip; "I've been sick since the trip" = it started then and continues now.
  • Examples: I've lived in Taipei for eight years. / I've lived in Taipei since 2018. / I lived in Munich for two years, then moved back. (finished, so past simple) / My phone rang during the meeting. / My phone rang while I was giving a presentation. / Please don't talk while you're eating. / The MRT is very crowded during rush hour.
  • Common mistakes: (1) "I have lived here since three years" → for three years (length, not starting point). (2) "During I was in Germany, I…" → While I was in Germany… / During my time in Germany… (during needs a noun). (3) "I studied English during three years" → for three years (during says when, not how long). Also: "since" can mean "because" (Since it's raining, let's stay in).

Unit 1 — 介系詞第 8 課:for / since / during / while(第二階段:時間)

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:「多久」用 for,「從什麼時候開始」用 since,「在某段期間裡」用 during 或 while。中文說「我住在台北五年了」「從 2015 年開始」「在德國的時候」「開會的時候」,用的是「了」「從」「的時候」這些字,和英文並不是一對一對應。所以常聽到 ✗ since five years、✗ during I was in Germany、✗ I studied English during three years 這類錯誤。

2. 核心概念:兩個問題、兩種形狀

  • for = 回答「多久?」(How long?)——它量的是一段長度,像一把尺:for five years、for two hours、for a long time。
  • since = 回答「從什麼時候開始?」(Since when?)——它標出一個起點,而且一直延續到現在,像一面插在時間軸上的旗子:since 2015、since Monday、since I moved here。因為「一直到現在」,通常搭配現在完成式:I've lived here since 2019.
  • during = 回答「什麼時候發生?」(When?)——某件事發生在一段有名稱的期間裡面,後面接名詞:during the meeting、during the typhoon、during my trip。
  • while = 「在……的同時」,意思和 during 接近,但後面接子句(主詞 + 動詞):while I was in Berlin,或接 -ing:while waiting。
  • 畫面:for 是一把量長度的尺;since 是插在起點的旗子,從那裡一直拉到「現在」;during / while 是一個框框,事情發生在框框裡面的某個時候。

3. 比較與例句

介系詞/連接詞回答的問題後面接什麼例子
for多久?一段長度for ten days、for an hour
since從什麼時候開始?起點(時間點或事件)since 2015、since last summer、since I was a kid
during在什麼時候?名詞(一段期間)during the holidays、during the film
while在……的同時子句或 -ingwhile we were on holiday、while cooking

    我住在台北八年了。(長度:八年)
  2. I've lived in Taipei since 2018.
    我從 2018 年開始住在台北。(起點:2018,一直到現在)
  3. I lived in Munich for two years, then moved back to Taiwan.
    我在慕尼黑住了兩年,然後搬回台灣。(已經結束的一段時間 → for + 過去簡單式;這裡不能用 since)
  4. My phone rang during the meeting.
    開會的時候我的手機響了。(during + 名詞 the meeting)
  5. My phone rang while I was giving a presentation.
    我在做簡報的時候手機響了。(while + 子句 I was giving…)
  6. Please don't look at your phone while you're driving.
    開車的時候請不要看手機。
  7. The MRT is really crowded during rush hour.
    尖峰時段捷運非常擠。

換一個字,意思就變了:

  • I was sick during the trip. = 旅行期間的某個時候我生病了(病可能只有兩天)。
  • I was sick for the whole trip. = 整趟旅行我都在生病(強調長度)。
  • I've been sick since the trip. = 從那趟旅行開始一直病到現在。

4. 常見錯誤

  1. ✗ I have lived here since three years. → ✔ I have lived here for three years.
    原因:中文「住三年了」的「了」讓人想到「從……以來」,所以選了 since。但「三年」是一段長度,不是起點。檢查方法:能不能在前面加「從」?「從三年」不通,所以用 for;「從 2022 年」通,才用 since。
  2. ✗ During I was in Germany, I learned to ski. → ✔ While I was in Germany, … / ✔ During my time in Germany, …
    原因:中文「在德國的時候」後面什麼都能接。但 during 是介系詞,後面只能接名詞;要接「主詞 + 動詞」就要用 while。
  3. ✗ I studied English during three years. → ✔ I studied English for three years.
    原因:中文的「期間」「之間」容易讓人選 during;而且學過西班牙文的人要特別小心——西班牙文 durante tres años 正是「三年之久」的意思,英文卻不能這樣用。但 during 回答的是「什麼時候」,不是「多久」;「三年」是長度,所以用 for。

Section 7 — Film & Performing Arts: Man with a Movie Camera (1929, Dziga Vertov)
第 7 段 — 電影與表演藝術:《持攝影機的人》(1929,吉加・維多夫)

ENEnglish version

The work: Man with a Movie Camera (Russian Chelovek s kino-apparatom; Ukrainian Lyudyna z kinoaparatom), a silent, feature-length documentary released in 1929 and produced by VUFKU, the Ukrainian state film organisation. Running time is about 68 minutes, depending on projection speed.

Key figures: director Dziga Vertov (born David Kaufman, 1896–1954; "Dziga Vertov" is a chosen name suggesting a spinning top); cinematographer Mikhail Kaufman, his younger brother, who also appears on screen as the cameraman; and editor Elizaveta Svilova, Vertov's wife, who is shown at the editing table. The three called themselves the "Council of Three" of the Kinoks ("cinema-eye" group).

Historical and cultural background: the film comes from the experimental decade of Soviet cinema after the 1917 revolution, the same period as Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin (1925). Vertov had made newsreels during the Civil War and the Kino-Pravda series (1922–25). In manifestos such as "We: Variant of a Manifesto" (1922) he attacked fiction film — stories, actors and studio sets — as a kind of drug borrowed from literature and theatre, and argued for "Kino-Eye": the camera as a mechanical eye, more perfect than the human eye, catching "life unawares". Around 1929 the Soviet state was turning towards Stalin's Five-Year Plans and, soon after, Socialist Realism, which would make Vertov's formal experiments politically suspect.

Content: there is no plot, no actors and — as the opening titles proudly announce — no intertitles, no script and no theatre; it calls itself "an experiment in the cinematic communication of visible events". The film follows a single "day" in a composite Soviet city, assembled from footage shot in Moscow, Kyiv, Kharkiv and Odesa: a city waking up, factories and trams starting, births, weddings, divorces, a funeral, people working, exercising and relaxing on the beach, and finally evening entertainment. It opens and closes in a cinema: an audience takes its seats and watches the very film we are watching.

Artistic style and distinctive techniques:

  • Self-reflexivity: we see the cameraman filming, the editor cutting the film strip, and the audience watching — the process of film-making is the subject. A frozen frame of a woman or a child turns into a strip of film on Svilova's editing bench, then comes back to life.
  • A dictionary of effects: split screen (the Bolshoi Theatre appears to fold in half), superimposition (a giant cameraman standing over a crowd, a cameraman inside a beer glass), slow and fast motion, reverse motion, freeze frames, extreme angles, and stop-motion animation (the camera on its tripod "walks" and bows by itself).
  • Rhythmic montage: the editing builds like music. In the final section, shots last only a few frames each, producing a dizzying acceleration. Vertov left notes for musical accompaniment, but the film was released silent.
  • Graphic matching: shapes and motions rhyme across cuts — a blinking eye and a camera shutter, spinning machines and human limbs — suggesting that people and machines share one modern rhythm.

Reception, significance and interpretation: at release, the film confused many viewers and was criticised in the Soviet press as "formalist". Sergei Eisenstein, a rival theorist, reportedly dismissed it as "formalist jackstraws and unmotivated camera mischief". Critical interpretation today (not fact) sees it as both a celebration of the modern Soviet city and a theory of cinema made in film form: it argues that the camera does not just record reality but constructs it through editing. Historically it belongs to the "city symphony" genre with Walter Ruttmann's Berlin: Symphony of a Metropolis (1927), but it is more radical in showing its own construction.

Influence and legacy: the film was rediscovered in the 1960s; in 1968 Jean-Luc Godard and Jean-Pierre Gorin named their political film collective the "Dziga Vertov Group", and documentary movements from cinéma vérité onwards drew on Vertov's ideas. Its rapid montage anticipates the music video and essay films. In the 2012 Sight and Sound critics' poll it ranked 8th among the greatest films of all time; in the magazine's 2014 documentary poll it ranked 1st; and in the 2022 critics' poll it ranked 9th. Ukrainian institutions such as the Dovzhenko Centre now emphasise it as a landmark of Ukrainian film production.

Where to experience it today: it is widely available on home video and streaming, and public-domain copies circulate online. It is often screened with live or new scores — notably the Alloy Orchestra's 1995 score based on Vertov's notes, Michael Nyman's 2002 score, and The Cinematic Orchestra's version from the early 2000s. Try watching it twice with different scores: the same images feel very different, which is itself a lesson about image and sound.

作品:《持攝影機的人》(俄文 Chelovek s kino-apparatom;烏克蘭文 Lyudyna z kinoaparatom),1929 年上映的無聲長片紀錄片,由烏克蘭國營電影機構 VUFKU 製作。片長約 68 分鐘,依放映速度而有差異。

藝術風格與獨特技巧:

  • 自我指涉:我們看到攝影師在拍攝、剪接師在剪膠卷、觀眾在觀看——電影製作的過程本身就是主題。一個女人或孩子的定格畫面,變成斯維洛娃剪接台上的一段膠卷,接著又重新動起來。
  • 一本特效字典:分割畫面(莫斯科大劇院看起來像被對折)、疊印(巨大的攝影師站在人群上方、攝影師在啤酒杯裡)、慢動作與快動作、倒轉、定格、極端角度,以及停格動畫(攝影機和三腳架自己「走路」、鞠躬)。
  • 節奏蒙太奇:剪接像音樂一樣層層推進。最後一段每個鏡頭只有幾格,造成令人暈眩的加速感。維多夫留下了配樂說明,但影片上映時是無聲的。
  • 圖形匹配:形狀和動作在剪接之間互相呼應——眨眼和快門、轉動的機器和人的四肢——暗示人與機器共享同一種現代節奏。

評價、意義與詮釋:上映時,許多觀眾看不懂,蘇聯媒體批評它是「形式主義」。另一位理論家艾森斯坦據說嘲諷它是「形式主義的挑竿遊戲和沒有動機的攝影機惡作劇」。今天的評論詮釋(不是事實)認為它既是對現代蘇聯城市的禮讚,也是一部用電影形式寫成的電影理論:它主張攝影機不只是記錄現實,而是透過剪接建構現實。在歷史上,它和華特・魯特曼的《柏林:城市交響曲》(1927)同屬「城市交響曲」類型,但它更激進,因為它把自己的建構過程攤開來給觀眾看。

影響與傳承:這部片在 1960 年代被重新發掘;1968 年,高達與尚-皮耶・高漢把他們的政治電影團體命名為「吉加・維多夫小組」,從真實電影(cinéma vérité)以降的紀錄片運動也都吸收了維多夫的想法。它的快速剪接預告了音樂錄影帶與散文電影。在 2012 年《視與聽》(Sight and Sound)影評人票選中,它名列史上最佳電影第 8 名;在該刊 2014 年紀錄片票選中排名第 1;在 2022 年影評人票選中排名第 9。烏克蘭的杜甫仁科中心等機構,如今也強調它是烏克蘭電影製作的里程碑。

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 114: Star Studies — Reading the Star Persona and Casting Against Type
第 8 段 — 電影欣賞與評論方法第 114 課:明星研究——解讀明星形象與「反類型選角」

ENEnglish version

1. Today's Analytical Method

Star studies treats a film star not just as an actor playing a role, but as a meaning that the audience brings into the cinema. The key text is Richard Dyer's Stars (1979), followed by Heavenly Bodies (1986). Dyer argued that a star image is built from many sources: the films themselves, studio promotion, publicity (interviews, gossip, private life as reported), and criticism and commentary. When a star appears on screen, all of that comes along. It matters because filmmakers use these expectations — to reassure us, to make a character instantly likeable, or to shock us by breaking them.

2. Detailed Explanation

  • Step 1 — Describe the persona before the film starts. What roles is this star known for? What values, body type, voice and off-screen reputation are attached? Write three adjectives. (Example: Tom Hanks — decent, ordinary, trustworthy.)
  • Step 2 — Compare persona and character. Dyer describes three relations: a perfect fit (the role matches the image, so the character feels "true" immediately), a selective use (the film uses only some aspects of the image), and a problematic fit (the role contradicts the image). Casting against type is the deliberate form of the problematic fit.
  • Step 3 — Look at how the film introduces the star. Is the face delayed? Is there a close-up "reveal"? Introductions are where films play most openly with star expectations.
  • Step 4 — Ask what work the persona does. Does it make a villain more frightening, a weak script more convincing, or a political message more acceptable? Does the film comment on the star's image (as in films about ageing stars)?
  • Step 5 — Separate evidence levels. The persona is an observable public record (earlier roles, publicity); how a particular audience felt is interpretation. Avoid claims about the star's "real" personality — star studies analyses the public image, not the private person.

How it affects the viewer: a star persona works like a shortcut — we trust, fear or forgive a character before the script has earned it. When the film breaks the persona, the shock is double: we are surprised by the character and by our own misplaced trust.

Common misconceptions: (1) "It's just good or bad acting." — Star studies is about the image the audience brings, not only performance skill. (2) "Only Hollywood has stars." — Bollywood, Hong Kong, Japanese and Taiwanese cinema all built strong star systems. (3) "Casting against type is a gimmick." — Used well, it is a precise tool for making the audience question its assumptions.

3. Concrete Film Example — Once Upon a Time in the West (C'era una volta il West, 1968), dir. Sergio Leone

The scene: the massacre of the McBain family at Sweetwater, early in the film (cinematography Tonino Delli Colli, music Ennio Morricone).

  1. The persona before the film (observable record): by 1968 Henry Fonda was one of Hollywood's great symbols of American decency — young Abraham Lincoln in Young Mr. Lincoln (1939), Tom Joad in The Grapes of Wrath (1940), Wyatt Earp in My Darling Clementine (1946), and the patient juror who saves an innocent man in 12 Angry Men (1957). His face meant honesty and justice.
  2. What to look for — the setup: the McBain family prepares an outdoor feast. The cicadas suddenly stop. Shots ring out; the father, daughter and son are killed. Only the youngest boy, Timmy, runs out of the house and finds the bodies.
  3. The delayed reveal: long-coated gunmen walk out of the brush. Leone keeps the leader's face hidden, then moves the camera slowly around him until we see — in close-up — Henry Fonda's famous blue eyes, while Morricone's music rises. At this moment the audience's persona knowledge does its work: many first viewers would expect Fonda to be the rescuer.
  4. The violation: a gunman asks what to do with the boy, calling the leader "Frank". Frank answers that since the boy has heard his name, he can't let him live — and, smiling, shoots the child. Leone cuts on the gunshot to the whistle of an arriving train, so the act is not shown in detail; the shock comes from who does it, not from graphic violence.
  5. Why it works: the scene turns the audience's trust in Fonda into a weapon. Everything that made his face reassuring for thirty years now makes Frank more frightening: evil wears the face of American virtue.
  6. Deeper interpretation (subjective): many critics read the casting as Leone's comment on the Hollywood western itself — the genre's heroic myth of the American West (Fonda had been its moral centre) is exposed as a story built on killing for land and railways. A frequently retold production anecdote says Fonda arrived with dark contact lenses and a beard to look like a villain, and Leone asked him to remove them because he wanted the familiar face; treat this as anecdote rather than documented fact.

4. Practical Viewing Exercise

Watch the first 50 minutes of Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho (1960). Janet Leigh was the film's top-billed star. Before watching, write down what an audience in 1960 would expect from a top-billed female star (how long she stays, whether she will survive, whether we should sympathise with her). Then note: (a) how the film encourages us to follow her even after she steals the money; (b) what happens to our expectations at the motel; (c) how the star system helps the shock work. Separate what you observe on screen from what you infer about the 1960 audience.

5. Key Takeaways

  • A star is an image built from films, promotion, publicity and criticism — and it enters every film the star appears in.
  • Compare persona and role: perfect fit, selective use, or problematic fit (casting against type).
  • Watch the star's introduction, and ask what the persona does for the film's meaning — analyse the public image, not the private person.

1. 今日分析方法

明星研究(star studies)不只把電影明星看成扮演角色的演員,而是看成觀眾帶進電影院的一種意義。關鍵著作是理查・戴爾(Richard Dyer)的《明星》(Stars,1979),之後還有《天體》(Heavenly Bodies,1986)。戴爾主張,明星形象由許多來源組成:電影本身、片廠的宣傳、媒體曝光(訪問、八卦、被報導的私生活),以及評論與討論。明星一出現在銀幕上,這一切就跟著一起進來。這很重要,因為電影創作者會利用這些期待——讓我們安心、讓角色一出場就討人喜歡,或是打破期待來震撼我們。

2. 詳細說明

  • 步驟 1——在電影開始前,先描述明星形象。這位明星以什麼角色著名?他身上附帶了什麼價值觀、體型、聲音與銀幕外的名聲?寫下三個形容詞。(例:湯姆・漢克斯——正直、平凡、值得信賴。)
  • 步驟 2——比較明星形象與角色。戴爾描述了三種關係:完美契合(角色符合形象,角色一出場就讓人覺得「真」)、選擇性使用(電影只用到形象的某些面向)、有問題的契合(角色和形象互相矛盾)。「反類型選角」就是刻意製造的「有問題的契合」。
  • 步驟 3——看電影如何介紹這位明星出場。臉是不是被延遲才露出?有沒有特寫的「揭露」?出場方式是電影最明顯玩弄明星期待的地方。
  • 步驟 4——問:明星形象在這部片裡發揮了什麼作用?它讓反派更可怕、讓薄弱的劇本更有說服力,還是讓某個政治訊息更容易被接受?電影有沒有評論明星本身的形象(例如講明星老去的電影)?
  • 步驟 5——區分證據層次。明星形象是可觀察的公開紀錄(過去的角色、媒體報導);某一群觀眾當時的感受則屬於詮釋。不要宣稱明星「真正」的個性是什麼——明星研究分析的是公眾形象,不是私人本人。

它如何影響觀眾:明星形象就像一條捷徑——在劇本還沒說服我們之前,我們就已經信任、害怕或原諒某個角色。當電影打破這個形象時,衝擊是雙重的:我們既被角色嚇到,也被自己錯放的信任嚇到。

常見誤解:(1)「這只是演技好壞的問題。」——明星研究談的是觀眾帶進來的形象,不只是表演技巧。(2)「只有好萊塢才有明星。」——寶萊塢、香港、日本與台灣電影都建立過強大的明星制度。(3)「反類型選角只是噱頭。」——用得好,它是讓觀眾質疑自身預設的精準工具。

3. 具體電影範例——《狂沙十萬里》(Once Upon a Time in the West / C'era una volta il West,1968),導演塞吉歐・李昂尼

場景:電影前段,麥克貝恩一家在甜水農場被屠殺的段落(攝影東尼諾・德利・科利,配樂顏尼歐・莫利克奈)。

  1. 電影之前的明星形象(可觀察的紀錄):到 1968 年,亨利・方達已經是好萊塢「美國正直精神」的代表人物之一——《少年林肯》(1939)裡年輕的林肯、《憤怒的葡萄》(1940)裡的湯姆・約德、《俠骨柔情》(1946)裡的懷亞特・厄普,以及《十二怒漢》(1957)裡耐心救了一個無辜者的陪審員。他的臉代表誠實與正義。
  2. 要注意什麼——鋪陳:麥克貝恩一家正在準備戶外宴席。蟬聲突然停止。槍聲響起,父親、女兒和兒子被殺。只有最小的男孩提米從屋裡跑出來,看見了屍體。
  3. 延遲揭露:穿著長大衣的槍手從灌木叢中走出來。李昂尼一直不讓我們看到首領的臉,然後讓攝影機慢慢繞到他身前,直到我們在特寫中看見亨利・方達那雙著名的藍眼睛,莫利克奈的音樂同時升起。這一刻,觀眾對明星形象的認知開始發揮作用:很多第一次看的觀眾會以為方達是來救人的。
  4. 打破期待:一名槍手問要怎麼處理這個男孩,並叫首領「法蘭克」。法蘭克回答:既然男孩已經聽到他的名字,就不能讓他活著——然後微笑著對孩子開槍。李昂尼在槍響的瞬間剪到一列進站火車的汽笛聲,所以並沒有詳細呈現這個動作;震撼來自「是誰」做了這件事,而不是血腥畫面。
  5. 為什麼有效:這場戲把觀眾對方達的信任變成了武器。三十年來讓他的臉令人安心的一切,現在讓法蘭克更加可怕:邪惡戴上了美國美德的面孔。
  6. 更深的詮釋(主觀):許多評論者認為,這個選角是李昂尼對好萊塢西部片本身的評論——這個類型關於美國西部的英雄神話(方達曾是它的道德中心),被揭露為建立在為土地和鐵路殺人之上的故事。一則經常被轉述的拍片軼事說,方達到片場時戴了深色隱形眼鏡、留了鬍子,想看起來像反派,李昂尼卻要他拿掉,因為他要的正是那張熟悉的臉;請把這當成軼事,而不是有文件證明的事實。

4. 實際觀影練習

看希區考克《驚魂記》(Psycho,1960)的前 50 分鐘。珍妮・李是這部片掛頭牌的明星。觀看前,先寫下 1960 年的觀眾會對一位掛頭牌的女明星有什麼期待(她會出現多久、會不會活下來、我們該不該同情她)。然後記錄:(a)即使她偷了錢,電影如何讓我們繼續跟著她;(b)到了汽車旅館,我們的期待發生了什麼事;(c)明星制度如何幫助這個震撼發揮作用。把你在銀幕上觀察到的,和你對 1960 年觀眾的推論分開寫。

5. 重點整理

  • 明星是一種形象,由電影、宣傳、媒體曝光與評論共同建構——而且會跟著明星進入他演出的每一部電影。
  • 比較明星形象與角色:完美契合、選擇性使用,或有問題的契合(反類型選角)。
  • 注意明星的出場方式,並問明星形象為電影的意義做了什麼——分析公眾形象,而不是私人本人。

6. 延伸觀賞推薦

《鳥人》(Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance),2014,導演阿利安卓・崗札雷・伊納利圖)——曾在 1989 年與 1992 年飾演蝙蝠俠而成名的米高・基頓,在片中飾演一個被讓他成名的超級英雄角色糾纏的演員。想一想:這部片的意義有多少取決於觀眾知道基頓自己的明星歷史?(也和今天第 5 段的迷因有關:《從前,有個好萊塢》裡的瑞克・道爾頓,就是一個和自己的明星形象拉扯的虛構明星。)
