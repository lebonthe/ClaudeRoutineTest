3) Spanish Lesson — Day 59 (10 min, Expressing Obligation: Tener que / Deber / Hay que)

Steps outside the tense/mood/pronoun/se-family tracks (Days 46-57) and the personal "a" (Day 58) into a foundational everyday-usage gap: Spanish's three core ways to express obligation, which English/Chinese speakers often collapse into a single "have to/must/should." Tener que + infinitive (conjugated for the specific subject, e.g. tengo que, tienes que) marks a strong, personal, often externally-imposed obligation ("I have to..."); deber + infinitive (also subject-conjugated) marks a softer moral "should/ought to," closer to advice or personal responsibility than hard necessity; and hay que + infinitive reuses Day 56's invariable existential hay to state a general, impersonal obligation with no named subject at all ("one must...", "it's necessary to..."), never conjugating for person or number no matter how many people the rule applies to. The three sit on a clear cline from most personal/urgent (tener que) to most general/detached (hay que), with deber occupying the softer, advisory middle ground.

  • Tengo que terminar este informe hoy. — I have to finish this report today.
  • Deberías descansar un poco antes de seguir. — You should rest a little before continuing.
  • Hay que revisar el informe antes de enviarlo. — One must review the report before sending it.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 59: 渙 (Huàn) — "Dispersion" (Wind over Water) (Xùn 巽/Wind above, Kǎn 坎/Water below — 風水渙). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary draws a direct line from the previous hexagram: "說而後散之,故受之以渙;渙者,離也。" ("After joy [Duì, Hexagram 58], one then disperses it — therefore Duì is followed by Huàn; Huàn means to scatter, to separate.") Where Hexagram 58 showed the reward of shared, well-grounded joy, Hexagram 59 turns to what happens when a group, a bond, or a state of mind begins to fragment — and, more importantly, what can be done about it. The Judgment reads: "渙,亨。王假有廟,利涉大川,利貞。" ("Huàn. Success. The king approaches his ancestral temple. It furthers one to cross the great water. Perseverance furthers.") Despite naming a condition of scattering, the judgment is unexpectedly positive: dispersion, correctly handled, is not pure loss but an opportunity to reunite people around something larger than themselves. The king's approach to the ancestral temple is the classical image for this: a shared ritual center strong enough to draw a fragmenting community back together under one common devotion, even when no single person's authority alone could compel their unity. The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) elaborates: "渙,亨,剛來而不窮,柔得位乎外而上同。王假有廟,王乃在中也。利涉大川,乘木有功也。" ("Huàn brings success because the firm line has come [into the lower trigram] without being exhausted, while the yielding line has attained its place outside and harmonizes upward with those above. The king's approach to the temple means the king occupies the center. It furthers one to cross the great water, because riding on wood [the boat, i.e. the image of Wind over Water] achieves its work.") The image of a wooden vessel carried safely over water by the wind above it captures the hexagram's core insight: dispersion itself can become the very vehicle of reunification, provided the crossing is undertaken with correct purpose rather than avoided out of fear of the scattering already underway. The Image commentary reads: "風行水上,渙;先王以享于帝,立廟。" ("Wind moves over water: this is the image of Huàn. Thus the ancient kings made offerings to the Supreme Ancestor and established ancestral temples.") Wind blowing across a water's surface visibly scatters and dissolves any fixed pattern on it — waves, reflections, ripples — yet this same wind-driven motion is also what keeps water from stagnating; the ancient kings' response to social dispersion was neither to suppress difference by force nor to let a community drift apart, but to build a shared center of reverence capacious enough to hold a scattered people together. Read against Hexagram 58, the pair traces a natural cycle: joy that is not renewed inevitably disperses (as the Xugua states outright), and Hexagram 59 answers that dispersion not with despair but with the constructive, patient work of building something communal and durable enough to draw the scattered parts back into relation.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Peru (秘魯)

  • Location: Western South America, on the Pacific coast, bordered by Ecuador and Colombia to the north, Brazil to the east, Bolivia to the southeast, and Chile to the south. Geographically split into three dramatically different zones running north-south: the arid Pacific coastal strip (home to Lima and most major cities), the towering Andes mountain range through the center (including the world's largest tropical ice-capped range), and the vast Amazon rainforest basin to the east, which makes up roughly 60% of the country's territory.
  • Territory: About 1,285,216 km² — the 20th-largest country in the world, roughly 35 times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km²), a little over three times the size of Germany (~357,600 km²), and larger than France and Spain combined.
  • Population: Approximately 34.4 million as of 2026 — roughly 1.5 times Taiwan's population — making Peru the fifth most populous country in South America.
  • Religion: Predominantly Roman Catholic (roughly 76%), with a fast-growing Evangelical/Protestant minority (roughly 14%) and smaller shares of other Christian denominations, no religion, and indigenous belief systems that in practice often blend syncretically with Catholic devotion (most visibly in Andean festivals honoring both Catholic saints and pre-Columbian earth deities like Pachamama).
  • Economy: One of the world's top producers and exporters of copper, gold, silver, and zinc, with mining as the dominant export sector; also a major global exporter of fishmeal/fish oil (anchoveta) and increasingly of high-value agricultural exports (asparagus, blueberries, avocados, grapes, coffee). Tourism, anchored by Machu Picchu and Cusco's Inca heritage, is a major and fast-recovering revenue source. The economy has historically been vulnerable to commodity-price swings and, in recent years, to sustained political instability.
  • History: Home to some of the Americas' oldest civilizations (the Norte Chico/Caral culture, among the earliest known complex societies in the Americas, dating back roughly 5,000 years), later the Moche and Nazca cultures, and ultimately the Inca Empire, which by the early 16th century was the largest empire in pre-Columbian America, centered on the capital Cusco. Spanish conquistador Francisco Pizarro captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa in 1532 and effectively ended Inca imperial rule, incorporating Peru into the Viceroyalty of Peru, the wealthiest and most important Spanish colonial administrative center in South America for over two centuries. Peru declared independence in 1821 (proclaimed by José de San Martín) though Spanish royalist forces were not fully defeated until Simón Bolívar and Antonio José de Sucre's victory at the Battle of Ayacucho in 1824. The 20th and early 21st centuries brought recurring cycles of military rule and civilian democracy, a brutal internal conflict with the Shining Path insurgency (1980s-early 2000s), and — most recently — an unusually turbulent stretch of political instability: President Pedro Castillo was removed and arrested after attempting to unconstitutionally dissolve Congress in December 2022, his successor Dina Boluarte was herself removed by Congress in October 2025 amid a security crisis, and two further interim presidents (José Jerí, then José María Balcázar) held office only months apart before Peru's 2026 general election.
  • Current leader: President Keiko Fujimori, sworn in July 28, 2026 after winning a razor-thin runoff victory in Peru's 2026 presidential election — Peru's first female president, and the daughter of former President Alberto Fujimori (in office 1990-2000).
  • Language: Spanish is the official language spoken by the large majority; Quechua and Aymara hold co-official status in the regions where they are predominant (Quechua, the language of the Inca Empire, is still spoken by several million Peruvians, concentrated in Andean highland regions), and dozens of indigenous Amazonian languages are spoken by smaller communities in the eastern rainforest.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, en.wikipedia.org (Keiko Fujimori), as-coa.org

  • Name/origin: "It's Corn," also known as the "Corn Kid" meme, originates from an August 4, 2022 episode of the YouTube/Instagram/TikTok interview series Recess Therapy, hosted by Julian Shapiro-Barnum, titled "The CEO of Corn." In it, a seven-year-old boy named Tariq, interviewed in Prospect Park, Brooklyn, delivers an infectiously enthusiastic, rambling monologue about his love of corn ("It's corn! ... I can't imagine a more beautiful thing").
  • How it became popular: The Gregory Brothers (known for their "Auto-Tune the News" and "Songify" video series) remixed portions of the original interview into a full song, released on YouTube on August 28, 2022.   • Meaning/usage: The meme is used to express unfiltered, over-the-top enthusiasm for something mundane or unremarkable — the humor comes from the gap between the modest subject (corn) and the operatic, almost spiritual intensity of the child's delivery ("a big lump with knobs... it has the juice"). It's commonly deployed as a reaction clip/song snippet whenever someone expresses disproportionate joy over something ordinary.

  • Current status: Considered one of the defining wholesome/"core memory" memes of the 2022-era short-form video boom; still widely referenced in "best memes" retrospectives and periodically resurfaces as a nostalgic touchstone, distinct from the more cynical or ironic meme formats that followed it.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, npr.org

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Come and See (Иди и смотри, 1985, dir. Elem Klimov)

  • Name/key figures: Come and See (Russian: Иди и смотри, "Idi i smotri"), a 1985 Soviet film directed by Elem Klimov, co-written by Klimov and Ales Adamovich, based on Adamovich's own wartime testimony collection I Am from the Fiery Village and the 1971 novel Khatyn. It stars a then-14-year-old Aleksei Kravchenko in his screen debut as Flyora and Olga Mironova as Glasha.
  • Era/background: Klimov spent roughly eight years fighting Soviet state censorship before he was permitted to make the film exactly as conceived, finally released in 1985 to mark the 40th anniversary of the Soviet victory over Nazi Germany. It would prove to be the final film Klimov directed before his death in 2003, reportedly because he felt he had said everything he needed to say.
  • Plot overview: Set in Nazi-occupied Byelorussia (now Belarus) in 1943, the film follows teenage Flyora, who eagerly digs up a rifle to join the Soviet partisan resistance, only to be left behind by the retreating fighters and forced to witness — and survive — escalating atrocities, culminating in the real historical massacre-by-fire of an entire village's population inside a barn, modeled directly on the actual 1943 Khatyn massacre and hundreds of similar wartime atrocities across Byelorussia.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Shot largely in unbroken long takes with a subjective, disorienting handheld/Steadicam camera that frequently breaks the conventional distance between character and viewer, forcing the audience to occupy Flyora's own traumatized perspective. Klimov used a specially-developed Steadicam-based "Rakurs" system and, notoriously, deployed real, closely-passing live ammunition during several battle sequences to elicit Kravchenko's genuine terror on camera — Kravchenko reportedly went partially grey during the shoot and required hypnosis sessions afterward to recover psychologically. The sound design foregrounds a persistent high-pitched ringing tinnitus effect after nearby explosions, immersing the audience directly in Flyora's damaged hearing.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely regarded as one of the most unflinching anti-war films ever made, distinguishing itself from conventional war-film heroics by refusing any triumphant or redemptive framing of violence; it is frequently cited alongside Klimov's home region's own devastating World War II losses (Byelorussia lost an estimated quarter of its population during the Nazi occupation).
  • Influence and legacy: Ranked 41st in the 2022 Sight & Sound directors' poll of the greatest films ever made (104th in the critics' poll), and consistently cited by later filmmakers (including Guillermo del Toro and other prominent directors) as one of the most harrowing and technically influential war films in cinema history, particularly for its use of unbroken subjective camera work to convey psychological trauma rather than battlefield spectacle.
  • Current status/where to experience it: Available via the Criterion Collection (Blu-ray and Criterion Channel streaming) in a restored transfer, and widely taught in film studies courses on both war cinema and Soviet-era filmmaking under censorship.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, criterion.com

8) Film-Appreciation Method — The Split-Diopter Shot (Lesson 45)

A split diopter is a half-convex supplementary lens element attached in front of a camera's main lens, making one half of the frame's optical path "nearsighted" relative to the other. The result is a single shot that keeps both a very close foreground element and a much more distant background element in simultaneous sharp focus at once — something no ordinary lens (regardless of aperture) can achieve, since depth of field always falls off continuously with distance from the lens. This is distinct from Day 8's Depth of Field & Focus (which covers deep focus, shallow focus, and rack focus — all still governed by one continuous focal plane) and from Day 41's Split Screen (which physically divides the frame into separate panels via editing/compositing rather than optically fusing two focal distances within one continuous, undivided shot). Because a split diopter leaves a visible soft seam where the two lens halves meet, its use is often deliberately foregrounded rather than hidden — check whether the shot is used to hold two characters (or a character and an object) in a single sustained visual relationship the director wants the audience to read together without cutting away, whether the seam is disguised behind a natural vertical edge in the composition (a doorframe, a pole) to minimize its visibility, and whether it's a single punctuation shot or a director's recurring stylistic signature.

Worked example: Blow Out (1981, dir. Brian De Palma, cinematography by Vilmos Zsigmond) — De Palma, the technique's most closely associated practitioner (documented across 15 separate split-diopter shots in this film alone), opens the story with sound recordist Jack Terry capturing nighttime ambient noise for a cheap horror film near a bridge; De Palma's split-diopter shots throughout the film repeatedly hold Jack's recording equipment or face in extreme close-up in one half of the frame while keeping the surrounding location — the road, the crowd, evidence relevant to the conspiracy he's uncovering — in equally sharp focus in the other half, visually insisting that both the foreground technology of surveillance/recording and the wider unfolding conspiracy demand the audience's simultaneous, undivided attention.

3)西班牙文課程——第 59 天(10 分鐘,表達義務:Tener que / Deber / Hay que)

今天跳出第 46-57 天的時態/語氣/代名詞/se 家族脈絡,以及第 58 天的個人受詞 a,補上一個日常使用頻率極高、卻常被英文/中文使用者簡化成同一個「必須/應該」的文法缺口:西班牙語表達義務的三種核心方式。Tener que + 原形動詞(依主詞變化,如 tengo que、tienes que)表達一種強烈、針對特定個人、且通常帶有外在強迫性質的義務(「我必須……」);deber + 原形動詞(同樣依主詞變化)則表達較為溫和的道德性「應該」,語氣更接近建議或個人責任感,而非硬性必要;hay que + 原形動詞則沿用第 56 天教過、不隨人稱數量變化的無人稱存在句 hay,表達一種完全不指名主詞的普遍性、非人稱義務(「必須……」「有必要……」),無論這條規則適用於多少人,永遠不需為人稱或數量變化。這三者恰好構成一條從「最個人、最急迫」(tener que)到「最普遍、最抽離」(hay que)的光譜,deber 則落在語氣較溫和、偏建議性質的中段。

  • Tengo que terminar este informe hoy. —— 我今天必須完成這份報告。
  • Deberías descansar un poco antes de seguir. —— 你在繼續之前應該稍微休息一下。
  • Hay que revisar el informe antes de enviarlo. —— 送出報告前一定要先檢查過。

4)易經卦象

第 59 卦:渙 (Huàn) ——「渙」(風水渙、渙散)(風水渙——上巽下坎:上卦為巽〔風〕,下卦為坎〔水〕。)《序卦傳》承接前一卦而來:「說而後散之,故受之以渙;渙者,離也。」(心生喜悅之後,終究會漸漸消散、渙散,因此兌卦之後,接的是渙卦;渙,意指離散、渙散。)第 58 卦呈現的是共同分享、根基穩固的喜悅所帶來的回報;第 59 卦則轉而處理:當一個群體、一段情感連結,或一種心境,開始渙散分裂時,會發生什麼事——更重要的是,此時該如何應對。卦辭寫道:「渙,亨。王假有廟,利涉大川,利貞。」(渙卦,亨通。君王親臨宗廟(祭祀祖先),適宜渡越大河,守持正固為有利。)儘管卦名點出的是「渙散」的狀態,卦辭的態度卻出人意料地正向:渙散若處理得當,並非純粹的損失,反而是一個能讓人們重新團結、凝聚於比個人更宏大之事物周圍的契機。君王親臨宗廟,正是這種契機的經典意象:一個足夠強大的共同儀式核心,即使單憑任何一人的權威都無法強迫眾人團結,卻能將已然渙散的群體重新凝聚回來,共同信仰同一件事。《彖傳》進一步闡述:「渙,亨,剛來而不窮,柔得位乎外而上同。王假有廟,王乃在中也。利涉大川,乘木有功也。」(渙卦之所以亨通,是因為剛健的爻進入下卦後並未耗竭窮盡,而柔順的爻則在外卦取得適當位置,並與居上位者同心協力。所謂「君王親臨宗廟」,意指君王身處於核心、中正的位置。「適宜渡越大河」,則是因為乘著木舟(即風行於水上之象)能夠成就此番渡越之功。)木舟能安然承載、渡越水面,正是靠著上方的風力推送——這個意象精準捕捉了本卦的核心洞見:渙散本身,只要以正確的目的去承擔、渡越,而非因恐懼眼前已然發生的離散而迴避不前,反而能成為促成重新團結的載具本身。《象傳》寫道:「風行水上,渙;先王以享于帝,立廟。」(風吹拂過水面,這就是渙卦的卦象。因此古代先王藉此領悟,應當向天帝獻祭,並建立宗廟。)風吹過水面,會明顯使水面上原本固定的圖案——波紋、倒影、漣漪——渙散消失;然而正是這股由風帶動的流動,同時也是讓水體不致停滯腐敗的原因。古代先王面對社會渙散分裂時的應對之道,既不是靠強力壓制差異,也不是放任群體漸行漸遠,而是建立一個足夠包容、能夠承載已然渙散的人心,重新凝聚在一起的共同敬奉核心。若與第 58 卦對照,兩卦恰好勾勒出一段自然循環:未能持續更新滋養的喜悅,終究難免渙散(正如《序卦傳》直言不諱地指出);而第 59 卦回應這種渙散的方式,不是絕望放棄,而是耐心、務實地建構出某種足夠共通、足夠持久的事物,將已然渙散的各方,重新凝聚回彼此的關係之中。

5)國家/地區/政體介紹——秘魯(Peru)

  • 位置:位於南美洲西部太平洋沿岸,北鄰厄瓜多與哥倫比亞,東接巴西,東南接玻利維亞,南鄰智利。地形由北至南可明顯分為三大截然不同的區域:乾燥的太平洋沿岸地帶(首都利馬與大多數主要城市皆位於此)、貫穿全國中部、高聳的安地斯山脈(境內擁有全球最大的熱帶冰川山脈),以及東側廣袤的亞馬遜雨林盆地,約占全國國土面積的 60%。
  • 領土:總面積約 128 萬 5,216 平方公里——為全球面積第 20 大的國家,約為台灣(約 36,193 平方公里)的 35 倍,約為德國(約 357,600 平方公里)的三倍多,也大於法國與西班牙面積的總和。
  • 人口:截至 2026 年,人口約 3,440 萬人——約為台灣人口的 1.5 倍,使秘魯成為南美洲人口第五多的國家。
  • 宗教:以羅馬天主教為主(約占 76%),福音派/新教少數族群比例快速成長(約占 14%),另有較小比例信仰其他基督教派、無宗教信仰,以及原住民信仰體系——這些原住民信仰在實務上經常與天主教信仰彼此融合並存,最明顯的例子是安地斯山區的節慶,同時祭祀天主教聖人與前哥倫布時期的大地女神帕查瑪瑪(Pachamama)。
  • 經濟:秘魯是全球數一數二的銅、金、銀、鋅生產與出口大國,礦業是最主要的出口部門;同時也是全球主要的魚粉/魚油(鯷魚)出口國,並逐漸發展出高附加價值的農產品出口(蘆筍、藍莓、酪梨、葡萄、咖啡)。以馬丘比丘與庫斯科印加文化遺產為核心的觀光業,是重要且正快速復甦的收入來源。這個經濟體長年容易受到大宗商品價格波動影響,近年來也持續受到政局不穩的衝擊。
  • 歷史:秘魯是美洲最古老文明的發源地之一(諾特奇科/卡拉爾文化〔Norte Chico/Caral〕,是美洲已知最早的複雜社會之一,歷史可追溯至約 5,000 年前),其後歷經莫切文化(Moche)與納斯卡文化(Nazca),最終發展出印加帝國——至 16 世紀初,印加帝國已是前哥倫布時期美洲最大的帝國,以首都庫斯科為中心。西班牙征服者法蘭西斯克·皮薩羅(Francisco Pizarro)於 1532 年俘虜印加皇帝阿塔瓦爾帕(Atahualpa),實質終結了印加帝國的統治,秘魯隨後被納入秘魯總督轄區(Viceroyalty of Peru)——這是西班牙在南美洲殖民地中,長達兩個多世紀以來最富裕、也最重要的行政中心。秘魯於 1821 年由何塞·德·聖馬丁(José de San Martín)宣告獨立,但西班牙保皇派勢力,一直要到 1824 年西蒙·玻利瓦(Simón Bolívar)與安東尼奧·何塞·德·蘇克雷(Antonio José de Sucre)於阿亞庫喬戰役(Battle of Ayacucho)獲勝後,才算徹底被擊敗。20 世紀至 21 世紀初,秘魯歷經軍事統治與文人民主政府反覆更迭,並曾與「光明之路」(Shining Path)叛亂組織爆發長達數十年(1980 年代至 2000 年代初)的殘酷內部衝突;最近一段時期,秘魯政局更經歷了異常動盪的階段:總統佩德羅·卡斯蒂約(Pedro Castillo)於 2022 年 12 月企圖非法解散國會後遭罷免並逮捕;其繼任者迪娜·博盧阿爾特(Dina Boluarte)本人也於 2025 年 10 月、在一場治安危機中遭國會罷免;此後又有兩位任期僅相隔數月的臨時總統(何塞·赫里〔José Jerí〕,其後為何塞·瑪麗亞·巴爾卡薩爾〔José María Balcázar〕)相繼執政,直到 2026 年秘魯大選舉行為止。
  • 現任領導人:總統藤森惠子(Keiko Fujimori),於 2026 年 7 月 28 日宣誓就職——她在秘魯 2026 年總統大選決選中,以極其些微的差距險勝當選,成為秘魯史上首位女性總統,也是前總統阿爾韋托·藤森(Alberto Fujimori,1990 至 2000 年在位)之女。
  • 語言:西班牙語為官方語言,絕大多數人口使用;蓋丘亞語(Quechua)與艾馬拉語(Aymara)在其主要使用地區具有官方共同語言地位(蓋丘亞語為印加帝國時期使用的語言,如今仍有數百萬秘魯人使用,主要集中於安地斯高原地區);此外,東部雨林地區的較小社群,仍使用數十種原住民亞馬遜語言。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、en.wikipedia.org(藤森惠子)、as-coa.org

6)網路迷因介紹——「這是玉米!」(It's Corn / Corn Kid)

  • 名稱/起源:「It's Corn」(這是玉米!),又稱「玉米男孩」(Corn Kid)迷因,源自 2022 年 8 月 4 日,YouTube/Instagram/TikTok 訪談系列節目《Recess Therapy》(由主持人 Julian Shapiro-Barnum 主持)的一集,標題為「玉米執行長」("The CEO of Corn")。片中,一名七歲男孩塔里克(Tariq)在布魯克林展望公園接受訪問,以極具感染力、滔滔不絕的語氣,發表一段關於自己有多熱愛玉米的獨白(「這是玉米!……我無法想像還有比這更美的東西了。」)。
  • 如何走紅:以「Auto-Tune the News」與「Songify」系列影片聞名的 Gregory Brothers(格雷戈里兄弟),將原始訪談片段混音改編成一首完整歌曲,於 2022 年 8 月 28 日在 YouTube 發布。這首混音歌曲在 TikTok、Instagram 與 YouTube Shorts 上迅速爆紅,其中麥可·格雷戈里(Michael Gregory)製作的版本,單在 TikTok 上就累積超過 7,300 萬次播放,並被使用於超過一百萬支網友自製影片中。
  • 意義/使用情境:這則迷因常被用來表達一種毫不掩飾、誇張到近乎失控的熱情,而熱情的對象卻是再平凡不過的事物——其幽默感正來自於「玉米」這種平淡無奇的主題,與這名孩子近乎歌劇式、近乎虔誠的激動語氣之間的巨大落差(「一大坨帶著小疙瘩的東西……它有汁!」)。這則迷因常被用作反應片段或歌曲片段,用來搭配「有人對一件稀鬆平常的事物,展現出不成比例的興奮之情」的情境。
  • 變體/演化:此後衍生出無數混音版本、配音版本與「玉米化」剪輯改編;包括聯合航空、Dunkin' Donuts 與 Chips Ahoy 在內的多個品牌,都曾在自家行銷活動中引用這支影片;訪談爆紅後不久,美國南達科他州更授予塔里克「玉米大使」("Corn-bassador")的榮譽頭銜。《Recess Therapy》這個節目系列本身,也因這支影片的爆紅而收穫大批新粉絲,此後持續訪問更多孩子、探討其他各種主題。
  • 目前狀態:被視為 2022 年前後短影音爆炸性成長時期,最具代表性的「溫馨/核心回憶」型迷因之一;至今仍常見於各類「史上最佳迷因」回顧盤點之中,並不時作為一種懷舊符號重新浮上檯面,與其後崛起、風格更偏諷刺或嘲弄的迷因類型形成鮮明對比。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、npr.org

7)電影/表演藝術介紹——《見證》(Иди и смотри / Come and See,1985,導演:埃倫·克里莫夫)

  • 名稱/關鍵人物:《見證》(俄語:Иди и смотри,音譯「Idi i smotri」,英語片名 Come and See)是一部 1985 年蘇聯電影,由埃倫·克里莫夫(Elem Klimov)執導,並與阿列斯·亞達莫維奇(Ales Adamovich)共同編劇,劇本改編自亞達莫維奇本人蒐集的戰時證言集《我來自烈焰村莊》,以及 1971 年小說《哈滕》(Khatyn)。片中由當時年僅 14 歲、首次登上大銀幕的阿列克謝·克拉夫琴科(Aleksei Kravchenko)飾演男主角弗廖拉(Flyora),奧爾嘉·米羅諾娃(Olga Mironova)飾演格拉莎(Glasha)。
  • 時代/背景:克里莫夫耗費約八年時間對抗蘇聯官方的審查制度,才終於獲准依照自己原本的構想完整拍攝此片,最終於 1985 年上映,恰逢蘇聯戰勝納粹德國四十週年紀念。這部作品後來成為克里莫夫執導的最後一部電影,他於 2003 年辭世前再未執導任何作品——據稱是因為他認為自己已經把想說的話都說完了。
  • 劇情概述:故事背景設定於 1943 年遭納粹佔領的白俄羅斯(當時稱為 Byelorussia),描述青少年弗廖拉滿懷熱情地挖出一把步槍、加入蘇聯游擊隊抵抗運動,卻在撤退的游擊隊員拋下他之後,被迫親眼目睹——並倖存於——一連串不斷升級的暴行,最終演變為整座村莊居民被關進穀倉、活活燒死的真實歷史慘案,直接取材自 1943 年真實發生的哈滕大屠殺(Khatyn massacre),以及白俄羅斯境內數百起類似的戰時暴行。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:全片大量採用不間斷的長鏡頭拍攝,搭配主觀、令人暈眩不安的手持/穩定器攝影運鏡,經常打破角色與觀眾之間的慣常距離感,迫使觀眾直接進入弗廖拉本人受創的心理視角。克里莫夫使用一套特別開發、名為「Rakurs」的穩定器攝影系統,更惡名昭彰地是,在多場戰鬥場景中實際使用近距離掃射的真實實彈,以誘發克拉夫琴科在鏡頭前的真實恐懼反應——據稱克拉夫琴科在拍攝期間頭髮一度部分變灰,拍攝結束後更需要接受催眠治療才逐漸恢復心理狀態。音效設計則著重呈現爆炸後持續不散的高頻耳鳴效果,讓觀眾直接沉浸於弗廖拉受損的聽覺體驗之中。
  • 文化/歷史意義:普遍被視為史上最不留情面、最直視戰爭真相的反戰電影之一,拒絕採用傳統戰爭片慣用的英雄主義敘事,也拒絕給予暴力任何勝利式或救贖式的美化包裝;本片也經常與克里莫夫家鄉白俄羅斯在二戰中蒙受的慘重損失並提(白俄羅斯在納粹佔領期間,估計約有四分之一人口喪生)。
  • 影響與傳承:在 2022 年《視與聽》(Sight & Sound)雜誌影史最偉大電影導演票選中排名第 41 位(在影評人票選中排名第 104 位),並持續被後世電影導演(包括吉勒摩·戴托羅〔Guillermo del Toro〕等知名導演)引用,視為影史上最令人心痛、技術上也最具影響力的戰爭電影之一,尤其是其運用不間斷的主觀攝影運鏡,傳達心理創傷、而非戰場奇觀場面的獨特手法。
  • 現況/如何觀賞:目前可透過標準收藏(Criterion Collection)藍光光碟與 Criterion Channel 串流平台,觀賞經過修復的版本;本片至今仍是電影研究課程中,探討戰爭電影與蘇聯審查制度下電影創作的常見教材。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、criterion.com

8)看片方法——分割光圈鏡頭 The Split-Diopter Shot(第 45 課)

分割光圈鏡頭(split diopter)是一種附加在攝影機主鏡頭前方的半凸透鏡配件,能讓畫面光學路徑的其中一半,相對於另一半呈現「近視」的聚焦特性。這樣拍出的單一鏡頭,能同時讓一個極靠近鏡頭的前景元素、與一個距離遙遠的背景元素,在同一畫面中維持同步清晰對焦——這是任何一般鏡頭(無論光圈大小)都無法達成的效果,因為景深原本就會隨著與鏡頭的距離連續遞減。這不同於第 8 天教過的「景深與焦點控制」(涵蓋深焦、淺焦與變焦拉焦,但這些技巧終究仍受單一連續對焦平面所支配),也不同於第 41 天教過的「分割畫面」(那是透過剪輯/合成的方式,將畫面實體切割為多個獨立面板,而非在同一個連續、未經切割的鏡頭之中,以光學方式融合兩個不同焦距)。由於分割光圈鏡頭會在兩片鏡片交界處,留下一道明顯柔焦的接縫,這項技法的使用往往是刻意被凸顯、而非刻意被隱藏的——判讀時可以檢視:這個鏡頭是否是為了讓兩個角色(或一位角色與一件物品)同時維持在同一段持續的視覺關係中,讓導演希望觀眾將兩者放在一起「不經剪接地共同解讀」;接縫是否被巧妙藏在畫面構圖中天然存在的垂直邊線之後(例如門框、柱子),藉此降低其可見度;以及這是一次性的點綴性鏡頭,還是導演反覆出現的個人風格標記。

實例演練:《凶線》(Blow Out,1981,導演:布萊恩·狄帕瑪〔Brian De Palma〕,攝影:維爾莫斯·齊格蒙德〔Vilmos Zsigmond〕)——狄帕瑪是這項技法最具代表性的實踐者(單單這部片,就有紀錄在案的 15 個分割光圈鏡頭),電影開場便是音效錄音師傑克·泰瑞(Jack Terry)在一座橋邊,為一部廉價恐怖片錄製夜間環境音效;狄帕瑪貫穿全片的分割光圈鏡頭,反覆讓傑克的錄音設備或面部特寫,占據畫面的一半、維持極近距離的清晰對焦,同時讓周遭場景——道路、人群,以及與他正逐漸揭發的陰謀有關的線索——在畫面的另一半同樣維持清晰對焦,以視覺手法明確主張:前景中「監聽/錄音」這項科技裝置,與正在展開的整起陰謀事件,兩者同樣值得、也同時要求觀眾不分心地全神貫注。
