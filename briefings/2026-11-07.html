4) Daodejing (道德經) — Chapter 17: 太上,下知有之 (Tài shàng, xià zhī yǒu zhī) — "The Best Rulers Are Barely Known"

  • Context: The I Ching and Zhuangzi sequences are used up, so this section continues through the Daodejing in order. Yesterday was Chapter 16.
  • Text: 太上,下知有之;其次,親而譽之;其次,畏之;其次,侮之。信不足焉,有不信焉。悠兮其貴言。功成事遂,百姓皆謂「我自然」。
  • Meaning: In the best age, the people merely know that a ruler exists. Next come rulers the people love and praise; next, rulers they fear; worst, rulers they despise. When trust is lacking, distrust follows. The best ruler is unhurried and sparing with words; when the work is done and affairs are settled, the people all say, "We did it ourselves" (自然, naturally, of itself). (Wording varies between editions, e.g. 下知有之 versus 不知有之.)
  • Link to earlier chapters: Chapter 16 taught returning to stillness and constancy; Chapter 17 applies it to leadership: the ruler who acts least, by wu wei (無為), earns the most natural result. It echoes Chapters 2, 3 and 10 on governing without forcing. (Interpretation from general knowledge.)

5) Country Spotlight — Tanzania (坦尚尼亞)

  • Location & size: East Africa, on the Indian Ocean, bordering Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, DR Congo, Zambia, Malawi and Mozambique. Area about 945,000 km², roughly 26 times Taiwan (about 36,200 km²) and about 1.4 times Texas; it includes the islands of Zanzibar and Pemba. It contains Africa's highest peak, Mount Kilimanjaro (5,895 m), and parts of Lake Victoria, Lake Tanganyika and Lake Malawi.
  • Population: about 65-68 million (the 2022 census counted about 61.7 million), one of Africa's fastest-growing populations; the largest city is Dar es Salaam, while Dodoma is the official capital.
  • Religion: roughly 60% Christian and 35% Muslim (approximate), with Zanzibar overwhelmingly Muslim; small traditional-belief minorities.
  • Language: Swahili (national language; Swahili and English are the official languages), plus more than 100 other languages.
  • Economy: GDP on the order of US$80 billion (approximate). Agriculture employs most people (coffee, cashews, tea, cloves from Zanzibar); gold is the leading export; tourism (Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Kilimanjaro, Zanzibar) and natural gas are important. Currency: the Tanzanian shilling.
  • History: Early Swahili coast trading towns (Kilwa) and the Omani sultanate's Zanzibar slave and spice trade; German East Africa from the 1880s; British mandate over Tanganyika after World War I. Tanganyika became independent on 9 December 1961. After the Zanzibar Revolution (January 1964), Tanganyika and Zanzibar merged on 26 April 1964 to form Tanzania. Julius Nyerere led from independence to 1985, pursuing Ujamaa ("familyhood") socialism after the 1967 Arusha Declaration; multiparty politics returned in 1992. Later presidents: Mwinyi, Mkapa, Kikwete and Magufuli (who died in office in March 2021).
  • Current leader: President Samia Suluhu Hassan, the country's first female president, in office since 19 March 2021. From general knowledge, I recall that she was declared winner of the October 2025 election amid protests and a deadly crackdown; I did not verify this or any later events in this session, so treat it as unconfirmed.

6) Internet Meme — "Charlie Bit My Finger" (2007)

  • Name: "Charlie Bit My Finger — again!" (often "Charlie Bit My Finger").
  • Origin: A 55-second home video uploaded to YouTube on May 22, 2007 by Howard Davies-Carr in England. It shows his sons Harry (about 3) and baby Charlie (about 1) sitting together; Charlie bites Harry's finger, Harry yelps "Ow, Charlie!", and then Charlie smiles. Davies-Carr said he uploaded it to share with the boys' godfather in the US.
  • How it spread: It was shared through email and blogs and then by YouTube's own recommendations, and became one of the first videos to reach hundreds of millions of views; it was widely described as the most-watched YouTube video of its time (around 2009-2010). Its popularity was word of mouth, not a campaign.
  • Meaning & usage: the phrase "Charlie bit my finger" and Harry's "Ow, Charlie, that really hurt!" became shorthand for a small, funny betrayal; it is quoted in reaction posts, remixed with auto-tune and parodied (including by other families and TV shows).
  • Variations & evolution: Parody and remix videos (such as autotuned versions and re-enactments) multiplied; the family made follow-up videos and used ad revenue to help fund the boys' future; in 2021 the original was sold as an NFT (reported at about US$760,000) and removed from YouTube.
  • Current status: a classic of early viral video, kept alive by re-uploads and nostalgia; the original is no longer on the family's YouTube channel (as I recall; not re-checked). From general knowledge; figures are approximate and no live source was checked today.

7) Film / Performing Arts — Mother India (1957, dir. Mehboob Khan)

  • Name & creators: Mother India (Hindi, Mehboob Khan's "Bharat Mata"), produced and directed by Mehboob Khan; starring Nargis (Radha), Sunil Dutt (Birju), Rajendra Kumar (Ramu) and Raaj Kumar; music by Naushad; cinematography by Faredoon Irani. It is a color remake of Mehboob's own 1940 film Aurat ("Woman").
  • Era & background: Made ten years after Indian independence, during the "Golden Age" of Hindi cinema, as the new nation was building its self-image through film. It was shot in Gujarat and Mumbai.
  • Plot overview: A poor village woman, Radha, is left to raise her sons after her husband loses his arms in a farming accident and disappears. Struggling against a predatory moneylender (Sukhilala), she holds to her principles through floods and famine. Her younger son Birju grows into an angry outlaw; at the end she must choose between her son and her moral code (dharma) for the village, and shoots him.
  • Style & techniques: big epic melodrama with folk and devotional songs (Naushad drew on Indian folk and classical music), richly colored rural imagery, and Nargis's performance, which turns a farm woman into a national symbol (the title casts her as "Mother India", Bharat Mata).
  • Significance & legacy: One of the biggest hits in Indian film history and a template for the "suffering mother" and "angry young man" figures in later Hindi cinema (including Deewaar and Sholay-era films). It was India's first submission nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film (1958), reportedly losing to Fellini's Nights of Cabiria by a single vote. Sunil Dutt and Nargis married after filming (the fire rescue on set is often retold).
  • Where to experience it today: a restored version was released in 2000s home video and is widely available on DVD; streaming availability varies by region. (From general knowledge; details approximate; no live source was checked today.)

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 100: Proxemics (Distance Between Characters as Meaning)

  • What it is: Proxemics is the study of how people use physical distance to signal relationships; the anthropologist Edward T. Hall described four zones: intimate (touching to about 45 cm), personal (to about 1.2 m), social (to about 3.5 m) and public (beyond that). On film, directors place characters in these zones, and change them, to show closeness, conflict and status, often with no dialogue at all. This differs from Lesson 86 (negative space) and Lesson 96 (shot/reverse shot), since the question here is how far apart the bodies are, in the story, not how the frame is cut.
  • How to analyze: (1) Measure: which of Hall's zones are two characters in at the start of the scene? (2) Track change: who steps closer, who retreats, and when? (3) Check the frame: does the camera show the distance (wide shot with space between) or flatten it (long lens)? (4) Find barriers: tables, doors, desks, windows, newspapers. (5) Compare scenes: does the film repeat a place (a dinner table) with different distances? (6) Ask what it does: intimacy, coldness, status, control or fear.
  • Worked example — Citizen Kane (1941, dir. Orson Welles; cinematography Gregg Toland; score Bernard Herrmann), the breakfast-table sequence: In a few minutes of linked scenes, Charles Foster Kane and his first wife Emily sit at the same breakfast table over the years of their marriage. (a) Early: they sit close, in the intimate and personal zones, talking over each other with affection. (b) Later: each scene places them farther apart; the table stretches, the dialogue shrinks to curt remarks, and the pauses lengthen. (c) End: they sit far apart at a long table, each behind a newspaper (Emily reading Kane's rival's paper), silent. (d) Why it works: the table, set, costume and the camera angle remain nearly the same, so the only thing that changes is the distance, and that carries the whole story of the marriage, as the "social" and then "public" zone replace the "intimate" one. (This is also an ellipsis device, but here the lens is distance.)
  • Practice: pick one two-person scene from any film and sketch a top-down diagram of the two characters at the beginning, middle and end; label each with one of Hall's four zones and write a sentence about what the change means.

4) 道德經 — 第 17 章:太上,下知有之 (Tài shàng, xià zhī yǒu zhī) —「最好的統治者,人民只知道有他」

  • 原文:太上,下知有之;其次,親而譽之;其次,畏之;其次,侮之。信不足焉,有不信焉。悠兮其貴言。功成事遂,百姓皆謂「我自然」。

6) 網路迷因 —「Charlie Bit My Finger」(查理咬我手指,2007)

  • 名稱:「Charlie Bit My Finger — again!」(常簡稱「Charlie Bit My Finger」)。
  • 起源:一支 55 秒的家庭影片,2007 年 5 月 22 日由英國的 Howard Davies-Carr 上傳到 YouTube。影片裡他的兒子哈利(約 3 歲)與嬰兒查理(約 1 歲)並排坐著,查理咬了哈利的手指,哈利大叫「Ow, Charlie!」,然後查理笑了。Davies-Carr 說他上傳是為了讓住在美國的孩子教父也能看到。
  • 如何爆紅:先靠電子郵件與部落格分享,再由 YouTube 的推薦帶動,成為最早達到數億觀看次數的影片之一;被廣泛描述為當時 YouTube 觀看次數最多的影片(約 2009-2010 年)。它靠的是口耳相傳,而非宣傳活動。
  • 意思與用法:「Charlie bit my finger」與哈利那句「Ow, Charlie, that really hurt!」成為「小小但好笑的背叛」的代名詞;常出現在回應貼文、被加上自動調音混音,也被其他家庭與電視節目模仿。
  • 變化與演進:惡搞與混音影片(如自動調音版、重演版)大量出現;這個家庭後來拍了續集,並將廣告收入用於孩子們的未來;2021 年原版影片被當作 NFT 賣出(報導約 76 萬美元),並從 YouTube 下架。
  • 目前狀態:早期病毒影片的經典,靠重新上傳與懷舊維持生命力;原始影片已不在這個家庭的 YouTube 頻道上(依我的記憶,沒有重新查證)。內容來自一般常識;數字為概數,今天沒有查證即時來源。

7) 電影/表演藝術 —《印度母親》(Mother India,1957,導演 Mehboob Khan)

  • 名稱與創作者:《印度母親》(Mother India,印地語片),由 Mehboob Khan 製作並導演;主演有 Nargis(飾 Radha)、Sunil Dutt(飾 Birju)、Rajendra Kumar(飾 Ramu)與 Raaj Kumar;音樂 Naushad;攝影 Faredoon Irani。這是 Mehboob 自己 1940 年作品《Aurat》(女人)的彩色重拍。
  • 時代背景:拍攝於印度獨立十年後、印地語電影「黃金時代」,新國家正透過電影建立自我形象。在古吉拉特與孟買取景。
  • 劇情概要:貧窮的村婦 Radha 在丈夫因農事意外失去雙臂並離家後,獨力撫養兒子。她對抗放高利貸的 Sukhilala,在洪水與饑荒中堅守原則。小兒子 Birju 長成憤怒的亡命之徒;片尾她必須在兒子與替全村堅持的道德準則(dharma)之間選擇,最後開槍打死了他。
  • 風格與技法:大型史詩式的通俗劇,搭配民謠與宗教歌曲(Naushad 取材自印度民間與古典音樂)、色彩豐富的鄉村畫面,以及 Nargis 的演出,把一位農婦升格為民族象徵(片名把她塑造成「印度母親」、Bharat Mata)。
  • 意義與影響:印度電影史上最賣座的作品之一,為後來印地語電影中的「受苦的母親」與「憤怒青年」形象立下範本(包括《牆》與《火焰》時期的電影)。它是印度第一部入圍奧斯卡最佳外語片的作品(1958 年),據說以一票之差輸給費里尼的《卡比莉亞之夜》。Sunil Dutt 與 Nargis 在拍攝後結婚(片場救火的故事常被轉述)。
  • 現在哪裡看得到:修復版在 2000 年代發行家庭影片,DVD 流通廣;串流平台各地區不同。(來自一般常識;細節為概略;今天沒有查證即時來源。)

8) 看片方法 — 第 100 課:鄰近學/人際距離(Proxemics:人物之間的距離就是意義)

  • 是什麼:Proxemics(人際空間學)研究人如何用身體距離傳達關係;人類學家 Edward T. Hall 提出四個區域:親密區(接觸到約 45 公分)、個人區(到約 1.2 公尺)、社交區(到約 3.5 公尺)與公共區(更遠)。在電影裡,導演把角色放在這些區域,並改變距離,來呈現親近、衝突與地位,常常完全不需要對白。這與第 86 課(負空間)、第 96 課(正反打)不同:這裡問的是故事裡的身體相隔多遠,而不是畫面怎麼剪。
  • 如何分析:(1) 量距離:場景開始時兩人在 Hall 的哪個區域?(2) 追蹤變化:誰靠近、誰退後、什麼時候?(3) 看鏡頭:鏡頭是展現距離(廣角、中間有空間),還是壓平距離(長鏡頭)?(4) 找屏障:桌子、門、辦公桌、窗戶、報紙。(5) 比較場景:影片是否重複同一地點(餐桌)但距離不同?(6) 問它的作用:親密、冷淡、地位、控制或恐懼。
  • 示範案例——《大國民》(Citizen Kane,1941,導演奧森·威爾斯;攝影 Gregg Toland;配樂 Bernard Herrmann),早餐桌段落:在幾分鐘連續的場景中,查爾斯·福斯特·凱恩與第一任妻子艾蜜莉在婚姻的多年間坐在同一張早餐桌前。(a) 早期:兩人坐得很近,在親密與個人區內,帶著愛意搶著說話。(b) 後來:每個場景都讓兩人離得更遠;桌子變長,對白縮成簡短的幾句,停頓變長。(c) 結尾:兩人坐在長桌兩端,各自躲在一份報紙後(艾蜜莉讀的是凱恩對手的報紙),沉默無語。(d) 為什麼有效:桌子、佈景、服裝與機位幾乎沒變,所以唯一改變的就是距離,而距離承載了整段婚姻的故事:「社交」再「公共」距離取代了「親密」距離。(這也是一種省略時間的手法,但這裡的分析重點是距離。)
  • 練習:選任何一部電影中的雙人場景,畫出開頭、中段、結尾兩個角色的俯視圖;各標上 Hall 的四個區域之一,並寫一句話說明這個變化的意義。
  • 附註:第 5-8 節來自一般常識,今天沒有查證即時來源;日期、距離與出處為概略。
