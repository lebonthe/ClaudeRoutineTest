3) Spanish Lesson — Day 92: Muy vs. Mucho, and Tan / Tanto (Degree and Equality)

Muy ("very") goes before an adjective or adverb and never changes; mucho ("a lot / much") goes with a verb or noun, agrees with the noun (mucho, mucha, muchos, muchas) and is invariable after a verb. Never say muy mucho. In comparisons of equality, tan + adjective/adverb + como ("as ... as") and tanto/a/os/as + noun + como ("as much/many ... as") follow the same split: tan before adjectives and adverbs, tanto before nouns and after verbs.

  • Ella es muy simpática. — She is very nice.
  • Trabajo mucho y tengo mucha tarea. — I work a lot and I have a lot of homework.
  • Mi hermano es tan alto como mi papá. — My brother is as tall as my dad.
  • No tengo tantos amigos como tú. — I don't have as many friends as you.

4) Zhuangzi — Essay 28: 讓王 (Ràng Wáng) — "Yielding the Throne"

The sixth of the Miscellaneous Chapters (雜篇, Ch. 28). It is a string of stories about people who decline power, wealth or honor, and the argument underneath them is 重生: value your life above the things that life is used to obtain. It begins with Yao offering the empire to Xu You and Shun offering it to Shanjuan; both refuse. Shanjuan's answer is the chapter's mood: 冬日衣皮毛,夏日衣葛絺,春耕種,秋收斂,日出而作,日入而息,逍遙於天地之間而心意自得,吾何以天下為哉 — "In winter I wear furs, in summer fine linen; I plow in spring and harvest in autumn; I work when the sun rises and rest when it sets; I wander between heaven and earth and my heart is content. What would I do with the empire?" He then goes off into the mountains and is never seen again.

Several other stories sharpen the point. King Tai (Gugong Danfu), pressed by the Di tribes, leaves Bin rather than fight, saying he will not live with someone's elder brother and kill the younger, and adding 不以所用養害所養, "I will not harm what I am nourishing for the sake of what I nourish it with" (land is a means; people are the end). Zihuazi asks Marquis Zhao of Han whether he would grab the empire if it cost him an arm; the Marquis says no, so Zihuazi concludes that the body outweighs the arms and the arms outweigh the empire, and that the Marquis is wasting his life fretting over a small border dispute. Prince Mou of Zhongshan says "my body is by the rivers and seas, but my heart is under the palace towers of Wei" (身在江海之上,心居乎魏闕之下), and Zhanzi tells him to 重生: when life is valued, profit weighs lightly. Elsewhere Confucius, trapped between Chen and Cai, goes on playing the zither and singing while his disciples complain, and says a gentleman keeps his virtue whether things go through or are blocked. The chapter also contains the sharpest line of its political theory: 道之真以治身,其緒餘以為國家,其土苴以治天下 — "The true essence of the Way is for tending one's own person; its leftover is for the state; its dregs are for governing the world."

Takeaway: Ask what a job, a title or a fortune is for. If keeping it costs the life it was meant to serve, the trade has gone wrong. (Chapter details are from general knowledge and labeled as such; not checked against a text today.)

  • Language: German (official, de facto national language), with recognized minority languages including Danish, Low German, North Frisian, Sorbian and Romani. English is widely understood among younger people.

Typical captions: "me, when my group project partner says they 'forgot'" or "when the teacher says 'one more thing'." It also works for tension generally: restraint, not the explosion.
  • Variations and evolution: The image is typically paired with a caption, and sometimes edited into other panels or posted next to other Arthur frames. It is a typical example of how a children's show becomes adult irony. (I did not find documented regional variants, so none are claimed.)

7) Film / Performing-Arts Spotlight — Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles (1975, dir. Chantal Akerman)

  • Name and creators: Jeanne Dielman, 23, quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles, a Belgian-French production directed and written by Chantal Akerman (1950-2015) when she was 25. Delphine Seyrig plays Jeanne; the cinematographer was Babette Mangolte. It runs about 201 minutes. Akerman reportedly put women in most of the key crew positions, which was unusual in 1975.
  • Era and background: Made in Brussels in the mid-1970s, in the wake of the French New Wave, the American structural and experimental film scene Akerman had seen in New York (she cited Michael Snow and Jonas Mekas), and the second-wave feminist movement. It is both a feminist film and a minimalist one.
  • Plot overview: Over three days, Jeanne, a widowed housewife, goes through an exact routine in her Brussels apartment: cooking, peeling potatoes, bathing, polishing her son Sylvain's shoes, making the bed, and receiving a man in the afternoon who pays her (she is a part-time sex worker; the film shows this plainly and without sensation). On the second day the routine begins to slip: a potato overcooks, a button is left undone, her hair is out of place. By the third day the cracks grow, and the film ends with a long final shot of her sitting at the table after a violent act (shown off-screen and in a single glimpse).
  • Artistic style and techniques: Fixed, frontal, waist-high camera, long takes with no cuts inside a scene and almost no close-ups, so that time is felt in real duration. A chore such as peeling potatoes or making meatballs is shown in full. The sparse dialogue and the absence of a score make small deviations register like disasters. Akerman treated the hours of a woman's domestic labor as worthy of the same patient attention that cinema usually gives to action and romance.
  • Cultural and historical significance: It made domestic labor visible as cinema's subject, and it is one of the most discussed works of feminist film theory. In the 2022 Sight and Sound critics' poll it was voted the greatest film of all time, ahead of Vertigo and Citizen Kane.
  • Influence and legacy: Directors of "slow cinema" and observational realism (Lucrecia Martel, Kelly Reichardt, Céline Sciamma, Tsai Ming-liang with his long takes) are often discussed in its light. The film changed what critics count as dramatic.
  • Current status / where to experience it: A restored version circulates in festivals and revival screenings (Cinematek in Brussels maintains Akerman's legacy), and it has been released by the Criterion Collection; streaming availability changes, so check current listings. Allow an afternoon, and watch it in one sitting if you can. (Details from general knowledge; not checked against a source today.)

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 78: The Character Introduction (How a Film Presents a Person for the First Time)

The method: The first time we see a character is the most efficient moment in a film for shaping our expectations. A good introduction is a short scene that tells us who this person is, what they want or control, and how we should feel about them, before the plot needs them. When you meet a major character, pause and ask five questions: (1) What do we see first? A hand, a back, a shadow, a face, an object? Withholding the face can build mystery or power. (2) From whose point of view? Is the camera with the character or with someone reacting to them? (3) What are they doing? A simple action, such as how they handle a drink, a cat or a signature, can show habits and status. (4) How does the space and light frame them? Bright or dark, central or edge, large or small in the frame. (5) What do others do in response? People's fear, deference or relief is characterization. Then ask whether the film later pays off or contradicts the first impression.

How to analyze it: Write down your first-impression words (for example "calm, dangerous, patient") within a minute of the character appearing. Then trace which shot, sound or reaction caused each word. Compare to a later scene. Whether the later behavior confirms or reverses the impression is usually the point of the introduction.

Worked example — The Godfather (1972, dir. Francis Ford Coppola, cinematography Gordon Willis), the opening scene in Don Corleone's study: The film starts in black, then on a close-up of the undertaker Bonasera saying "I believe in America." For several minutes we only hear and see a man pleading for justice. The camera slowly pulls back to reveal, in the foreground, the back of a head and a shoulder: Vito Corleone, who has been listening the whole time. (a) What we see first: not the Godfather's face but his silhouette from behind, with Bonasera as the figure addressing him. Power is shown by being the one who is listened to, not the one who speaks. (b) Point of view: the scene begins on the supplicant, so we enter as a petitioner and experience the Don as a figure to be approached. (c) Action: he strokes a cat, speaks quietly and answers slowly, with a formal rebuke ("Why did you go to the police? Why didn't you come to me first?") and then a conditional offer, treating the favor as a binding obligation. (d) Space and light: Willis lights from above so that the Don's eye sockets fall into shadow (his nickname was "the Prince of Darkness"). The dark, shuttered study contrasts with the bright wedding in the garden outside, so we sense a private world of power behind the public celebration. (e) Response of others: Bonasera lowers his voice, kisses the Don's hand and calls him "Godfather"; the deference tells us the rules of this world before the plot explains them. Why it works: everything we need to know about Vito, his authority, courtesy, menace and the code he enforces, arrives before he moves his head to face us. Contrast: in Casablanca (1942, dir. Michael Curtiz) Rick is first seen only as a hand signing a check and then playing chess alone; he is introduced as a man who keeps his distance, which the film then complicates. Try it: next time a major character appears, pause the film and list the five questions above before the next shot.

3) 西班牙文課程 — 第 92 天:Muy 與 Mucho,以及 Tan / Tanto(程度與同等比較)

Muy(很)放在形容詞或副詞前面,永遠不變;mucho(很多)搭配動詞或名詞,修飾名詞時要性數配合(mucho、mucha、muchos、muchas),接在動詞後則不變。不可以說 muy mucho。同等比較也是同樣的分工:tan + 形容詞/副詞 + como(「像……一樣」),tanto/a/os/as + 名詞 + como(「和……一樣多」);tan 用在形容詞和副詞前,tanto 用在名詞前或動詞後。

  • Mi hermano es tan alto como mi papá. — 我哥哥跟我爸爸一樣高。

4) 莊子 — 第 28 篇:讓王

《雜篇》第六篇(第 28 篇)。這一篇是一連串婉拒權力、財富與榮譽的人物故事,底下的主張是重生:生命比用來換取的外物更值得珍惜。開頭是堯要把天下讓給許由、舜要讓給善卷,兩人都拒絕。善卷的回答是這一篇的基調:冬日衣皮毛,夏日衣葛絺,春耕種,秋收斂,日出而作,日入而息,逍遙於天地之間而心意自得,吾何以天下為哉,於是他走進深山,再也沒人見過他。

其他故事讓這個主張更鋒利。太王亶父被狄人逼迫,寧可離開邠地也不打仗,他說不忍心和別人的兄長同居卻殺其弟,並說不以所用養害所養:不因用來養人的土地,而傷害所要養的人(土地是手段,人才是目的)。子華子問韓昭僖侯:如果搶天下要斷一隻手臂,你要不要?昭僖侯說不要,子華子便結論:身體比手臂重,手臂比天下重,而昭僖侯為一小塊邊界爭執憂愁,是在傷害生命。中山公子牟說「身在江海之上,心居乎魏闕之下」,瞻子勸他重生:重視生命,利益就會顯得輕。另有孔子被困於陳蔡之間,仍然彈琴唱歌,學生抱怨時他說君子無論通達或窮困,德行不變。這一篇也有最尖銳的一句政治論:道之真以治身,其緒餘以為國家,其土苴以治天下,意思是道的精華用來修養自身,剩餘的拿來治國,糟粕才用來治天下。

重點:要問一份工作、一個頭銜或一筆財富是為了什麼;如果保住它要付出它原本要服務的那條生命,這筆交易就錯了。(篇章細節來自一般知識,並標示如上;今天未對照原文。)

5) 國家/地區介紹 — 德國

  • 位置與面積: 位於中歐,與丹麥、波蘭、捷克、奧地利、瑞士、法國、盧森堡、比利時及荷蘭接壤,北臨北海與波羅的海。面積約 357,600 平方公里(一般知識):約為台灣的 10 倍、略小於美國蒙大拿州,約為法國本土(約 55 萬平方公里)的 65%。首都為柏林。
  • 人口: 約 8,370 萬(依某國家簡介的 2026 年估計,未對照德國聯邦統計局),是歐盟人口最多的國家,約為台灣(2,300 萬)的 3.6 倍。自然增加為負,靠移民維持人口不減。最大城市:柏林(約 370 萬)、漢堡、慕尼黑、科隆、法蘭克福。
  • 宗教: 約 45% 為基督徒(天主教與新教教會成員大致各半,兩者都在下降),約 6-7% 為穆斯林(多為土耳其裔,加上近年的敘利亞等地新移民),約 40% 無宗教信仰(近似值,一般知識)。1517 年的宗教改革由馬丁·路德在這裡發起。
  • 經濟: 歐洲最大、世界第三大經濟體(名目 GDP 約 4.5-5 兆美元,一般知識)。強項是汽車(福斯、BMW、賓士)、機械、化學、製藥,以及由中型出口冠軍組成的「Mittelstand」。自 2023 年以來長期停滯,受高能源價格(有報導稱今年中東戰爭使能源價格再度上升)與中國需求疲弱拖累。2026 年成長預測依來源與時間不同,約在 0.5% 到 1.3% 之間。2025 年柏林放寬憲法中的債務煞車,並設立為期 12 年、規模 5,000 億歐元的基礎建設基金。
  • 歷史: 日耳曼部落在萊茵河與多瑙河沿線與羅馬相遇;接著是查理曼的法蘭克帝國(800 年加冕)與神聖羅馬帝國(962-1806 年),由數百個邦國拼成。古騰堡印刷術(約 1450 年)與路德的宗教改革(1517 年)改變了歐洲。1871 年俾斯麥在普魯士領導下統一德國。一戰戰敗後是威瑪共和(1919-33),接著是納粹獨裁(1933-45)、二次大戰與大屠殺。1949 年分裂為西德與東德;柏林圍牆從 1961 年矗立到 1989 年,1990 年 10 月 3 日兩德統一。德國是歐盟創始成員,使用歐元。
  • 現任領導人: 總理弗里德里希·梅爾茨(Friedrich Merz,基民盟 CDU),2025 年 2 月大選後,自 2025 年 5 月起領導基民盟/基社盟與社民黨的聯合政府。國家元首:聯邦總統法蘭克-瓦爾特·史坦邁爾(2022 年連任)。聯邦共和國,共 16 個邦。
  • 語言: 德語(官方語言與事實上的國語),並承認丹麥語、低地德語、北弗里西語、索布語與羅姆語等少數語言。年輕人普遍聽得懂英語。
  • 來源與說明: 人口與領導人資料取自 舒曼基金會國家簡介;成長預測背景來自 美聯社/ABC News。其餘數字為一般知識,屬近似值。

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 亞瑟的拳頭(Arthur's Fist)

  • 名稱:「Arthur's Fist」(亞瑟的拳頭,也叫「Arthur Clenched Fist」「Arthur's Angry Fist」)。這是 PBS 兒童卡通《Arthur》(1996-2022)主角土豚亞瑟的反應圖,畫面是他低頭看著自己緊握的拳頭。
  • 起源與歷史(一般知識,已標示): 這張圖是卡通中的單一畫面,約 2016 年起以裁切過的截圖在 Tumblr 與 Twitter 流傳,2016-2017 年廣為擴散。我沒有核實它出自哪一集,所以這裡不指名。兒童節目的特寫之所以有效,是因為畫風簡單、情緒卻明確:一個小角色明顯在忍住怒氣。
  • 如何流行起來: 它被當作「壓抑怒氣」的反應圖:遇到令人火大的事,卻不能說話時使用。可愛天真的卡通畫風與用力顫抖的拳頭形成反差,就是笑點。它屬於更大的《Arthur》截圖幽默風潮:看這部卡通長大的人,把節目畫面重新當成反諷用的反應圖。
  • 意義與使用情境: 貼在令人煩躁的情境下,例如無禮的同事、被劇透、排隊太慢或糟糕的言論,意思是「我很火大,但我忍著不說」。典型文字:「當小組報告的組員說他『忘了』的時候」或「當老師說『還有最後一件事』的時候」。它表達的是克制而不是爆發。
  • 變體與演變: 這個畫面通常搭配文字說明,有時被嵌入其他格子,或與其他《Arthur》畫面並列使用。它是兒童節目變成成人反諷素材的典型例子。(我沒有找到有記載的地區性變體,所以不宣稱有。)
  • 目前狀態: 是 2010 年代後期廣為人知、如今已算經典的反應圖。它仍會出現在留言串裡、容易辨識,但缺乏新格式那樣的流行動能。(迷因歷史細節來自一般知識,今天未查證。)

7) 電影/表演藝術介紹 — 《珍妮·迪爾曼》(Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles,1975,導演香妲·艾克曼)

  • 名稱與創作者: 《珍妮·迪爾曼,布魯塞爾商業碼頭 23 號》,比利時與法國合製,由香妲·艾克曼(Chantal Akerman,1950-2015)在 25 歲時編導。由黛芬·賽麗格飾演珍妮,攝影師為芭貝特·曼戈特,片長約 201 分鐘。據稱艾克曼讓女性擔任大部分重要的工作人員職位,這在 1975 年相當罕見。
  • 時代與背景: 1970 年代中期在布魯塞爾拍攝,承接法國新浪潮、艾克曼在紐約看過的美國結構主義與實驗電影(她提過麥可·斯諾與喬納斯·梅卡斯),以及第二波女性主義運動。它既是女性主義電影,也是極簡主義電影。
  • 劇情概述: 在三天裡,寡婦兼家庭主婦珍妮在布魯塞爾的公寓中精準地過日子:煮飯、削馬鈴薯、洗澡、擦兒子西爾萬的鞋、整理床鋪,下午接待一位付錢的男人(她兼職性工作;電影平淡直接地呈現,不加渲染)。第二天,日常開始出現小失誤:馬鈴薯煮過頭、鈕釦沒扣好、頭髮亂了。到第三天裂縫變大,電影以她在一場暴力行為(畫面外、僅一瞥)之後坐在桌前的長鏡頭結束。
  • 藝術風格與技法: 固定、正面、腰部高度的攝影機,場景內長鏡頭不剪接、幾乎沒有特寫,讓時間以真實的長度被感受。像削馬鈴薯或做肉丸這樣的家務會完整呈現。稀少的對白與沒有配樂,使得小小的偏差感覺像災難。艾克曼把女性家務勞動的時數,當作值得電影給予動作與愛情那種耐心注視的題材。
  • 文化與歷史意義: 它讓家務勞動成為電影的主題,也是女性主義電影理論討論最多的作品之一。在 2022 年《Sight and Sound》影評人票選中,它被選為史上最偉大的電影,排在《迷魂記》與《大國民》之前。
  • 影響與遺產: 「慢電影」與觀察寫實的導演(盧奎西亞·馬泰爾、凱莉·雷卡特、瑟琳·席安瑪,以及以長鏡頭著稱的蔡明亮)常被放在它的脈絡下討論。這部電影改變了影評人眼中「戲劇性」的定義。
  • 現狀/哪裡可以欣賞: 修復版在影展與重映中流傳(布魯塞爾的 Cinematek 維護艾克曼的遺產),也有 Criterion Collection 發行;串流平台上架狀況常變動,請查詢最新片單。請留出一個下午,可能的話一口氣看完。(細節來自一般知識;今天未對照來源。)

8) 看片方法 — 第 78 課:角色登場(電影如何第一次呈現一個人)

方法: 角色第一次出現,是電影塑造觀眾期待最有效率的時刻。好的登場是一段短場景,在劇情還沒需要他之前,就告訴我們這個人是誰、想要或掌控什麼,以及我們該對他有什麼感覺。看到重要角色登場時,暫停並問五個問題:(1) 我們先看到什麼? 一隻手、一個背影、一道影子、一張臉,還是一件物品?不給臉,可以營造神祕或權力感。(2) 從誰的視角? 鏡頭是跟角色在一起,還是跟反應他的人在一起?(3) 他在做什麼? 一個簡單的動作,例如怎麼拿酒杯、貓或簽名,能顯示習慣與地位。(4) 空間與光線如何框住他? 明或暗、在中央或邊緣、在畫面中大或小。(5) 別人怎麼回應? 人們的恐懼、恭敬或寬慰,本身就是角色塑造。然後再問:電影後來是兌現還是推翻這個第一印象?

如何分析: 在角色出現的一分鐘內,寫下你的第一印象詞(例如「冷靜、危險、有耐心」),再追溯是哪個鏡頭、聲音或反應造成每個詞。再與後面的場景對照。後來的行為是證實還是翻轉這個印象,通常就是這場登場戲的重點。

實例分析 — 《教父》(The Godfather,1972,導演法蘭西斯·福特·柯波拉,攝影戈登·威利斯)唐·柯里昂書房的開場: 影片從黑畫面開始,接著是殯葬業者波納賽拉的特寫,他說「我相信美國」。好幾分鐘我們只聽到、看到一個人在懇求正義。鏡頭緩慢拉遠,在前景露出一個後腦勺與肩膀:維多·柯里昂,他一直在聽。(a) 先看到什麼: 不是教父的臉,而是他背對鏡頭的剪影,波納賽拉則是向他說話的人。權力是以「被傾聽的人」而不是「說話的人」來呈現。(b) 視角: 場景從請願者開始,所以我們以請求者的身分進入,把唐視為需要上前拜見的人物。(c) 動作: 他撫摸一隻貓,說話輕聲、回答緩慢,先是正式的責備(「你為什麼去找警察?為什麼不先來找我?」),然後提出有條件的幫忙,把人情當成有約束力的義務。(d) 空間與光線: 威利斯從上方打光,讓唐的眼窩落入陰影(他的綽號是「黑暗王子」)。昏暗、關著百葉窗的書房,與外面花園明亮的婚禮形成對比,讓我們感覺到公開慶典背後的私人權力世界。(e) 他人的回應: 波納賽拉壓低聲音、親吻唐的手並稱他「教父」;這份恭敬在劇情解釋之前,就告訴我們這個世界的規矩。為什麼有效: 關於維多的一切,他的權威、禮貌、威脅與他所執行的規矩,在他轉頭面對我們之前就已經傳達。對照: 《北非諜影》(1942,導演麥可·寇蒂茲)中,瑞克第一次出現只是一隻簽支票的手,接著是獨自下棋;他被介紹成一個與人保持距離的人,電影之後再讓這個印象變得複雜。試試看: 下次有重要角色登場時,先暫停,在下一個鏡頭之前列出上面五個問題。
