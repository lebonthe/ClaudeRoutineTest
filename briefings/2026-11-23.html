Topic: four everyday "putting" verbs — poner (put, place), meter (put into), sacar (take out) and guardar (put away, keep, save).

🎯 Learning Goal: say where you put things (Pon las llaves en la mesa), put things inside something (Mete la leche en la nevera), take things out (Saca el móvil del bolso), and put things away or save them (Guarda el archivo) — plus the common fixed expressions poner la mesa, sacar fotos, sacar la basura and guardar un secreto.

Vocabulary: poner (to put; irregular: pongo, pones, pone…; past: puse, pusiste, puso) · meter (to put in, insert; regular) · sacar (to take out; past: saqué) · guardar (to put away, keep, save) · el cajón (the drawer) · el bolso / la mochila (the bag / backpack) · la nevera / el refrigerador (the fridge — Spain / Latin America) · el armario (the wardrobe, cupboard) · el bolsillo (the pocket) · el archivo (the file) · sacar fotos (to take photos) · sacar dinero (to withdraw money) · sacar buena nota (to get a good mark) · sacar la basura (to take out the rubbish) · poner la mesa (to set the table) · poner la tele / la radio (to turn on the TV / radio).

Grammar Point: (1) poner = general "put" (on, at, somewhere). Pon el vaso en la mesa. The preposition is almost always en, which covers both "on" and "in" — the verb itself carries no idea of "inside". (2) meter = put inside something closed or narrow (a bag, a drawer, the fridge, a pocket, a slot): Mete la ropa en la lavadora. If you would say "put into" in English, Spanish usually prefers meter. Using poner here is understood, but meter sounds more natural. (3) sacar = the opposite of meter, "take out", with de for the place it comes out of: Saca el pan del horno. (de + el = del). Spelling: before e, c becomes qu to keep the "k" sound: saqué, and in the subjunctive/negative command no saques. (4) guardar = put back in its place / keep safely / save: Guarda los platos. (put the dishes away), Guárdame un sitio. (save me a seat), Guarda el documento. (save the file), guardar un secreto (keep a secret). (5) Pronouns attach to affirmative commands (as in Day 25 and Day 45): Ponlo aquí. (put it here), Mételo en la caja., Sácalo., Guárdalo. — note the written accent once the stress moves back: mételo, sácalo, guárdalo. Negative commands put the pronoun before the verb: No lo pongas ahí. (6) Reflexive versions: ponerse (put on clothes, Day 120) and meterse (get into: meterse en la cama; and figuratively No te metas en mis asuntos — stay out of my business).

Example Sentences:

  • ¿Dónde pongo las llaves? —Ponlas en el cajón. — "Where do I put the keys?" "Put them in the drawer."
  • Mete la leche en la nevera, que hace calor. — Put the milk in the fridge, it's hot.
  • Saqué el móvil del bolsillo para sacar una foto. — I took my phone out of my pocket to take a photo.
  • ¿Puedes poner la mesa? Yo saco la basura. — Can you set the table? I'll take out the rubbish.
  • Guarda el archivo antes de cerrar el programa. — Save the file before you close the program.
  • Guárdame un sitio, llego en cinco minutos. — Save me a seat, I'll be there in five minutes.
  • No te metas en mis cosas. — Don't stick your nose into my things.

Chapter 29 — 將欲取天下而為之 (Jiāng yù qǔ tiānxià ér wéi zhī) — "Wanting to Take the World and Work On It"

Text (received Wang Bi edition, with common variants noted):

將欲取天下而為之,吾見其不得已。天下神器,不可為也。為者敗之,執者失之。故物或行或隨,或歔或吹,或強或羸,或挫或隳。是以聖人去甚,去奢,去泰。

Translation: "Those who want to take hold of the world and work on it — I see that they will not succeed. The world is a sacred vessel; it cannot be worked on. Whoever works on it ruins it; whoever grasps it loses it. For among things, some lead and some follow; some breathe gently and some blow hard; some are strong and some are weak; some are broken down and some are destroyed. Therefore the sage gets rid of the extreme, gets rid of the extravagant, gets rid of the excessive."

Key words:

  • 取天下 — "take the world": to win or rule the empire. 為之 — to act on it deliberately, to shape it by force or plan. The criticism is not of ruling as such but of ruling by imposed design.
  • 不得已 — here read as "will not attain it / will not succeed" (不得 + 已 as a final particle). Note that in modern Chinese 不得已 means "having no choice"; this is a common misreading of the line.
  • 神器 — "sacred / numinous vessel". Wang Bi explains: 神,無形無方也;器,合成也。無形以合,故謂之神器也 — "spirit" means having no fixed shape or direction; "vessel" means something put together; because it is joined together without a visible form, it is called a sacred vessel. The world is not a pot you can shape on a wheel.
  • 為者敗之,執者失之 — this exact couplet reappears in Chapter 64, followed there by 是以聖人無為故無敗,無執故無失. Some modern editions add 不可執也 after 不可為也 to match it; the Wang Bi and Mawangdui texts do not have it, which is why it is left out above.
  • 或行或隨,或歔或吹,或強或羸,或挫或隳 — four pairs of opposites. 歔 is a soft, warming breath; 吹 a strong, cooling blow. For 挫 ("broken down") the Heshang Gong text reads 載 ("carried, kept safe"), giving "some are kept safe, some are destroyed". The point: things differ by nature and move in alternating rhythms. A single plan imposed on all of them must fail.
  • 去甚,去奢,去泰 — "remove the extreme, the extravagant, the excessive". Heshang Gong gives concrete readings: 甚 = indulgence in music and sex, 奢 = luxury in clothes, food and drink, 泰 = grand palaces and terraces. Philosophically the three words mean almost the same thing: too much.

Background: the chapter belongs to a run of political chapters (Ch. 26–32) about how a ruler should hold power. Chapter 28 ended with 大制不割 — "the great carving does not cut". Chapter 29 explains why: the world is a 神器 that resists cutting. It also prepares Chapter 30 (以道佐人主者,不以兵強天下 — one who helps a ruler with the Way does not use weapons to dominate the world). Wang Bi's general comment states the method clearly: 萬物以自然為性,故可因而不可為也,可通而不可執也 — "all things take being-so-of-themselves as their nature; therefore you can follow them but not work on them, you can let them flow but not grasp them."

Philosophical implications:

  • Complex systems resist control. The world contains leaders and followers, strong and weak, rising and falling. A ruler who tries to force them into one shape creates the opposite reaction. This is an early statement of what we would call "unintended consequences".
  • Not passivity. The sage does act — by removing excess. The work is subtraction, not addition: take away extremes so that things can find their own balance. This links to Chapter 48's 為道日損 ("in pursuing the Way, one loses a little each day").
  • Grasping as the root of loss. 執者失之 applies beyond politics: holding on too tightly to anything — a position, a person, an outcome — makes it slip away.

Modern reflection: after a week like the one the stock markets just had, the chapter reads almost like market commentary: some assets lead and some follow, some rise and some collapse, and no one can "work on" the whole system. For a software team, it is a warning against the grand rewrite — the plan to redesign a large, living codebase in one go. The Daodejing's alternative is modest and practical: remove what is extreme (the riskiest dependency), extravagant (the feature nobody uses) and excessive (the meeting that could be a message), and let the system settle. In personal life, 去甚去奢去泰 is a good check before a big decision: am I trying to grab and shape something that will only work if I loosen my grip?

第 29 章 將欲取天下而為之(Jiāng yù qǔ tiānxià ér wéi zhī)——「想要奪取天下並加以作為」

原文(通行王弼本,並註明常見異文):

將欲取天下而為之,吾見其不得已。天下神器,不可為也。為者敗之,執者失之。故物或行或隨,或歔或吹,或強或羸,或挫或隳。是以聖人去甚,去奢,去泰。

白話翻譯:「想要奪取天下並刻意去改造它的人,我看他是達不到目的的。天下是神聖的器物,不能刻意改造。刻意改造的人會把它弄壞,緊抓不放的人會失去它。因為萬物有的走在前、有的跟在後,有的輕輕呵氣、有的用力吹氣,有的強壯、有的瘦弱,有的受挫、有的毀壞。所以聖人去除極端,去除奢侈,去除過度。」

關鍵字詞:

  • 取天下——奪取、治理天下。為之——刻意對它施加作為,用強力或計畫去塑造它。這裡批評的不是「治理」本身,而是用強加的設計去治理。
  • 不得已——此處解為「達不到、得不到」(不得 + 句末語氣詞「已」)。要注意現代中文的「不得已」是「沒辦法、被迫」的意思;這是這一句常見的誤讀。
  • 神器——「神聖、神妙的器物」。王弼注:「神,無形無方也;器,合成也。無形以合,故謂之神器也。」——「神」是沒有固定形狀與方向;「器」是組合而成的東西;因為它沒有可見形體卻組合在一起,所以叫神器。天下不是一個可以在轆轤上隨意塑形的陶罐。
  • 為者敗之,執者失之——這一聯在第 64 章原句重現,後面接「是以聖人無為故無敗,無執故無失」。有些現代版本為了對應,在「不可為也」後加上「不可執也」;王弼本與馬王堆帛書都沒有這一句,所以上面的原文不收。
  • 或行或隨,或歔或吹,或強或羸,或挫或隳——四組相反的狀態。「歔」是輕柔溫暖的呵氣;「吹」是強勁、使之變涼的吹氣。「挫」字,河上公本作「載」(承載、安穩),意思變成「有的安穩、有的毀壞」。重點是:萬物本性各不相同,而且在交替的節奏中運動。用單一計畫強加在所有事物上,一定會失敗。
  • 去甚,去奢,去泰——「去除極端、奢侈、過度」。河上公給了具體解釋:甚 = 貪淫聲色,奢 = 服飾飲食的奢華,泰 = 宮室臺榭的壯麗。從哲學上看,三個字的意思幾乎一樣:太多了。

背景:本章屬於一連串談政治的章節(第 26–32 章),討論君主應如何掌握權力。第 28 章以「大制不割」作結——「最好的裁製是不切割」。第 29 章說明原因:天下是一個抗拒被切割的「神器」。它也為第 30 章(「以道佐人主者,不以兵強天下」——用道輔佐君主的人,不靠武力稱霸天下)鋪路。王弼的總注把方法說得很清楚:「萬物以自然為性,故可因而不可為也,可通而不可執也。」——萬物以自然為本性,所以只能順著它,不能刻意改造;只能讓它流通,不能緊抓不放。

哲學意涵:

  • 複雜的系統抗拒控制。天下有領頭的、有跟隨的,有強有弱,有興有衰。想把它們硬塞進同一個模子的君主,只會引發相反的反應。這是我們今天所說「非預期後果」的早期表述。
  • 不是消極。聖人確實有行動——但方式是去除過度。功夫在減法,不在加法:拿掉極端,讓事物自己找到平衡。這和第 48 章的「為道日損」相呼應。
  • 緊抓是失去的根源。「執者失之」不只適用於政治:對任何東西抓得太緊——職位、一個人、一個結果——它反而會溜走。

現代反思:在股市剛經歷這樣一週之後,這一章讀起來幾乎像市場評論:有的資產領漲、有的跟隨,有的上升、有的崩跌,沒有人能「改造」整個系統。對軟體團隊來說,它是對「大改寫」的警告——想一口氣重新設計一個龐大、仍在運作中的程式碼庫。道德經提供的替代方案樸實又實用:去除極端的(風險最高的相依套件)、奢侈的(沒人用的功能)、過度的(其實傳個訊息就好的會議),然後讓系統自己穩定下來。在個人生活中,「去甚去奢去泰」是做重大決定前很好的自我檢查:我是不是想抓住並塑造某樣東西,而它其實只有在我放手一點時才會順利?

In 2021 Belize completed a "blue bond" debt-for-nature deal arranged with The Nature Conservancy, cutting its public debt by roughly 12% of GDP in return for marine-conservation commitments — often cited as a model for small, indebted, nature-rich countries.

  • 加利富納人的到來:加利富納人是西非人與加勒比原住民的後代,1797 年被英國從聖文森島驅逐,後來定居在南部海岸。他們抵達的日子被定為加利富納定居日(11 月 19 日)——也就是上週四。加利富納的語言、舞蹈和音樂已被聯合國教科文組織列為人類非物質文化遺產。
  • 英屬宏都拉斯:1862 年正式成為英國殖民地(1871 年成為直轄殖民地)。19 世紀有逃離墨西哥「種姓戰爭」的梅斯蒂索難民移入,1950 年代起又有門諾教徒移入。
  • 獨立:1964 年自治,1973 年改名貝里斯,1981 年 9 月 21 日在喬治·普萊斯(「國父」)領導下獨立。由於瓜地馬拉不承認其獨立,英國軍隊繼續駐守協防;瓜地馬拉在 1991 年承認貝里斯為國家,但仍維持領土主張。

Those are a separate meme family but grew from the same image of a kind, slightly melancholy man.

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 6: opposite / across from / in front of / behind (Stage 1, basic spatial concepts).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese 對面 ("the opposite side") and 前面 ("in front") are often both translated as "in front of". But in English, in front of means on the same side, directly before the front of something, while opposite / across from means on the other side of a space — usually a street, a table or a room — facing it.
  • Core concept — the street test: imagine you stand at the door of a 7-Eleven. Something in front of the 7-Eleven is on your side of the street, between you and the shop door (a scooter parked in front of it). Something opposite or across from the 7-Eleven is on the other side of the street, facing it. behind is the back side, hidden from the front. opposite is more British; across from (or across the street from) is more American; both are fine.
  • Examples: The bus stop is right in front of the bank. / There's a pharmacy opposite the hospital. / The café is across the street from my office. / She sat opposite me at dinner, so we talked all night. / The car park is behind the supermarket. / Don't stand in front of the TV! / The sun went down behind the mountains.
  • Meaning change: "Meet me in front of the station" = at the entrance, on the station's side. "Meet me opposite the station" = on the other side of the road, looking at it. Mixing them up can send your friend to the wrong side of a busy road. Also: in front of the bus (outside, before its front — dangerous!) vs. in the front of the bus (inside, in the front seats).
  • Common mistakes: (1) "My home is in front of the park" when it is across the road → opposite / across from the park. (2) "I sat in front of him at dinner" for a face-to-face seat → I sat opposite him / across from him (in front of would mean in the row before him, like in a cinema). (3) "opposite of the bank" / "behind of the house" → opposite the bank, behind the house (no "of"; but the opposite of = the reverse idea: "Hot is the opposite of cold"). Also "in front of" for time → use before: "before 6 p.m.", not "in front of 6 p.m."

Unit 1 — 介系詞第 6 課:opposite / across from / in front of / behind

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:「對面」不是「前面」。中文的「對面」和「前面」常常都被翻成 in front of。但在英文裡,in front of 是「在同一邊、就在某物正面的前方」;opposite / across from 則是「隔著一個空間(通常是一條馬路、一張桌子、一個房間)、在另一邊,面對著它」。

2. 核心概念:馬路測試

  • 想像你站在一家 7-Eleven 門口。in front of the 7-Eleven 的東西在你這一側的馬路上,就在店門前(例如停在店門口的機車)。
  • opposite / across from the 7-Eleven 的東西在馬路的另一邊,正對著這家店。
  • behind 是背面、從正面看不到的那一側。
  • opposite 比較英式;across from(或 across the street from)比較美式;兩者都正確。

英文母語者的邏輯:in front of 回答「在它正面的哪裡?」;opposite 回答「隔著空間,誰面對著它?」中文的「前面」和「對面」都帶著「看得到正面」的意思,所以容易混在一起;英文則看中間有沒有隔著一條路或一張桌子。

4. 常見錯誤

  1. ✗ My home is in front of the park.(其實隔著一條馬路)
    原因:中文說「我家在公園對面/前面」時,常直接想到 in front of。但如果中間隔著馬路,英文要用 opposite 或 across from。
    ✔ My home is opposite the park. / My home is across from the park.
  2. ✗ At dinner I sat in front of him.(意思是面對面坐)
    原因:中文「坐在他前面」和「坐在他對面」容易混。in front of him 會讓人以為你坐在他前一排(像電影院)。
    ✔ At dinner I sat opposite him / across from him.
  3. ✗ The ATM is opposite of the bank. / ✗ behind of the house
    原因:受到「……的對面」「……的後面」中「的」的影響,想加 of。opposite 和 behind 本身就是介系詞,後面直接接名詞。

Section 7 — Film & Performing Arts: The 400 Blows (1959)
第 7 段 — 電影與表演藝術:《四百擊》(Les Quatre Cents Coups,1959)

ENEnglish version

  • Work: Les Quatre Cents Coups (The 400 Blows), France, 1959, black-and-white, widescreen (Dyaliscope), about 99 minutes. The title comes from the French idiom faire les quatre cents coups, "to raise hell / to get up to all sorts of mischief" — not literally 400 beatings.
  • Key figures: director François Truffaut (his first feature, made at 26 and largely drawn from his own childhood); co-writer Marcel Moussy; cinematographer Henri Decaë; music by Jean Constantin. Cast: Jean-Pierre Léaud (14) as Antoine Doinel; Claire Maurier and Albert Rémy as his mother and stepfather; Patrick Auffay as his friend René; Guy Decomble as the teacher. Produced by Truffaut's own company, Les Films du Carrosse.
  • Historical background: Truffaut was the most aggressive young critic at Cahiers du cinéma; his 1954 essay "A Certain Tendency of French Cinema" attacked the polished, literary "tradition of quality". His attacks were so fierce that he was not invited to the 1958 Cannes Festival as a critic. A year later, in 1959, The 400 Blows won him the Best Director prize at Cannes — the moment usually treated as the public birth of the French New Wave, together with Godard's Breathless (1960). Shooting began in November 1958; Truffaut's mentor and father figure, the critic André Bazin, died that same week, and the film is dedicated to him. The screenplay was nominated for an Academy Award.
  • Content: Antoine, about 12–13, lives in a cramped Paris flat with a mother who neglects him and a stepfather who is friendly but distant. At school he is punished, skips class with René, sees his mother kissing another man, lies that his mother has died, is caught when a candle shrine to Balzac sets the curtain on fire, and finally steals a typewriter. His parents hand him to the police; he spends a night in a cell and is sent to an observation centre for young offenders by the sea. He escapes and runs until he reaches the shore.
  • Style and distinctive techniques:
    • Real locations and light, lightweight equipment: Paris streets, Pigalle, a real fairground. Decaë's mobile camera follows Antoine through the city; the opening tracking shots around the Eiffel Tower present Paris from a child's height, without the postcard view.
    • Non-judgemental observation: Truffaut shows small details — Antoine taking milk from a doorstep, setting the dinner table, the rubbish bin he must empty — and lets the audience understand his situation without speeches.
    • The Rotor scene: in a spinning fairground drum, centrifugal force pins Antoine to the wall; the brief joy of losing control expresses his life better than dialogue. Truffaut himself appears among the riders.
    • The psychologist interview: Antoine answers questions from a psychologist who is never seen. The camera stays on Léaud's face in a long, simple take; his answers were partly improvised, which gives the scene a documentary honesty. Many critics consider it the emotional centre of the film.
    • The ending: a very long tracking shot follows Antoine running across fields to the sea, which he has never seen. He reaches the water, turns, and looks straight into the camera; the image freezes and the camera zooms in. The freeze-frame refuses to tell us what happens next — freedom, or a dead end — and turns the question to the viewer.
  • Significance: one of the defining films of the French New Wave and of personal, autobiographical cinema; a model for low-budget filmmaking outside the studio system; one of the most influential films about childhood ever made. Critics often point out how it treats a child's point of view seriously, without sentimentality.
  • Legacy: Truffaut and Léaud continued Antoine Doinel's story for twenty years — Antoine and Colette (1962, a short in Love at Twenty), Stolen Kisses (1968), Bed and Board (1970) and Love on the Run (1979) — a rare case of an actor and a character growing up together on screen. The final freeze-frame has been copied and referenced countless times. In Taiwan, director Tsai Ming-liang is a devoted admirer: Léaud appears in Tsai's What Time Is It There? (2001), where a character watches The 400 Blows, and in Face (2009).
  • Interpretation vs. fact: it is a fact that the story draws on Truffaut's childhood (truancy, a stolen typewriter, a stay in a reform centre). Whether the ending is hopeful or despairing is interpretation; Truffaut deliberately left it open.
  • Where to see it today: it is widely available in restored editions (for example the Criterion Collection on disc and streaming in some regions) and often screens at retrospectives and film festivals. The Truffaut catalogue is managed by the French company MK2.

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 112: The Cinema of Attractions — Spectacle vs. Story
第 8 段 — 看電影的方法第 112 課:「吸引力電影」——奇觀 vs. 敘事

Section 7 introduced a work (The 400 Blows); this section teaches a transferable lens. Viewing-exercise answers from earlier lessons have not been received yet.

ENEnglish version

1. Today's analytical method

The cinema of attractions. The term comes from film historian Tom Gunning, who developed it with André Gaudreault; his essay "The Cinema of Attraction(s): Early Film, Its Spectator and the Avant-Garde" appeared in the journal Wide Angle in 1986. Gunning argued that early films (roughly before 1906–1908) were not mainly about telling stories. They were about showing — presenting a thrilling sight directly to the audience: a train arriving, a magic trick, a fight, a kiss in close-up. He contrasted this with the later "cinema of narrative integration" (associated with D. W. Griffith from 1908 onward), where every image serves the story. The word "attraction" deliberately echoes Sergei Eisenstein's 1923 "montage of attractions" and the fairground. Why it matters: Gunning argued that attractions never disappeared. They went "underground" — into the avant-garde, musical numbers, slapstick and, above all, the special-effects blockbuster. This lens helps you notice when a film stops telling and starts showing off, and to ask whether that is a weakness or the very point.

2. Detailed explanation — how to apply it

  • Find the moments of display: where does the story pause so that you can simply look — an explosion, a dance number, a stunt, a landscape, a technical trick?
  • Look for direct address: attractions often acknowledge the viewer: actors look into the lens, objects fly at the camera, 3D effects come "out of" the screen. Narrative cinema usually hides the camera; attractions confront you.
  • Check whether the moment can be moved or removed: if a scene could be cut, or put in a different place, without changing the plot, it is working as an attraction rather than as narrative.
  • Notice the time structure: attractions are built for a burst of surprise and shock ("now you see it"); narrative is built for suspense over time ("what will happen next?").
  • Ask about integration: in many good films the spectacle also moves the story or reveals character. Measure the balance rather than labelling a film "all spectacle".
  • Common misconception: "attraction" is not an insult. Gunning's point was historical and descriptive: early audiences came for astonishment, and so do modern audiences at an IMAX screening. A great attraction is a real cinematic achievement.

3. Concrete film example — The Great Train Robbery (1903), dir. Edwin S. Porter

Background: made by the Edison Manufacturing Company, filmed in November 1903 in Edison's New York studio and on location in New Jersey, released in December 1903; about 12 minutes and 14 shots. It is often called one of the first American narrative films (and an early western): bandits rob a train, escape, and are chased and killed by a posse. That makes it an ideal case — a film that is already telling a story, but still contains a pure attraction.

The scene, step by step:

  1. The story shots: for most of the film the camera is far away, showing whole actions in long shots: the telegraph office, the robbery in the mail car, the passengers lined up beside the train, the escape, the dance hall, the final shoot-out. Notice how the film already uses cross-cutting between places — narrative tools are emerging.
  2. The extra shot: then comes a single medium close-up of an outlaw (usually credited to Justus D. Barnes) against a plain background. He looks straight at the audience, raises his revolver and fires directly into the camera, several times. Some prints were hand-coloured, so the gun smoke and flashes stood out.
  3. What to notice — it doesn't belong to the story: this bandit has no place in the plot timeline, and the setting is not any of the film's locations. Edison's own catalogue said the scene could be used to begin or end the picture — the exhibitor decided. That is the clearest possible sign of an attraction: it can move because it is not part of the story's chain of cause and effect.
  4. Which choices are made: the only close framing in the film, the only direct look into the lens, the only action aimed at the viewer instead of at other characters.
  5. Why it works: after watching the robbery from a safe distance, the audience is suddenly targeted. The shock is physical — a gun pointed at you — not dramatic. Early audiences reportedly reacted strongly, and the shot became the film's most famous image.
  6. Deeper meaning: the film shows two kinds of cinema living side by side. The story invites you to follow events "through a window"; the gunman breaks the window. Gunning used exactly this kind of moment to show that "narrative cinema" did not simply replace "attractions" overnight.

Objective vs. subjective: the shot's framing, the direct look and the catalogue note are observable facts. That the shot "breaks the window" or "targets" the viewer is an interpretation, though a widely shared one among film historians.

Modern link: Martin Scorsese's Goodfellas (1990) ends with Joe Pesci's character firing directly at the camera — an explicit homage to this shot.

4. Practical viewing exercise

Watch the Burj Khalifa climb in Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol (2011, dir. Brad Bird). Answer: (1) Which moments exist mainly to make you gasp at height, speed or the stunt itself? (2) Does the sequence also move the plot or reveal character — and how much? (3) Could any shot be removed without changing the story? (4) Where does the film acknowledge you as a viewer (point-of-view shots looking down, sound, framing)? Write three or four sentences and send them to be discussed.

5. Key takeaways

  • Attractions show; narrative tells. Many films do both.
  • Test: can the moment be moved or removed without changing the plot? Does it address the viewer directly?
  • Spectacle is not automatically weak — ask whether it is integrated, and whether astonishment is the point.

1. 今日分析方法

「吸引力電影」(the cinema of attractions)。這個詞來自電影史學者湯姆·岡寧(Tom Gunning),他與安德烈·高德侯(André Gaudreault)共同發展出這個概念;他的論文〈吸引力電影:早期電影、其觀眾與前衛藝術〉於 1986 年發表在期刊《Wide Angle》。岡寧主張,早期電影(大約 1906–1908 年以前)主要不是在說故事,而是在展示——把一個驚人的畫面直接呈現給觀眾:火車進站、魔術、打鬥、特寫的親吻。他把它和後來的「敘事整合電影」(與 1908 年起的 D. W. 葛里菲斯有關)對比,後者的每一個畫面都為故事服務。「吸引力」一詞刻意呼應愛森斯坦 1923 年的「吸引力蒙太奇」,也呼應遊樂場。為什麼重要:岡寧認為吸引力從來沒有消失,而是轉入「地下」——進入前衛電影、歌舞片段、鬧劇,尤其是特效大片。這個方法幫助你察覺電影什麼時候停止「說」、開始「秀」,並思考那是缺點,還是正是重點。

2. 詳細說明——如何應用

  • 找出「展示」的時刻:故事在哪裡暫停,讓你單純觀看——爆炸、歌舞段落、特技、風景、技術奇招?
  • 注意直接對觀眾說話:吸引力常常承認觀眾的存在:演員直視鏡頭、物體朝攝影機飛來、3D 效果「衝出」銀幕。敘事電影通常隱藏攝影機;吸引力則正面迎向你。
  • 檢查這個片段能不能被移動或刪除:如果一場戲刪掉或換到別的位置都不會改變情節,它就是以「吸引力」而非「敘事」在運作。
  • 注意時間結構:吸引力是為了瞬間的驚奇與衝擊而設計(「你看!」);敘事是為了隨時間累積的懸念(「接下來會怎樣?」)。
  • 思考整合程度:在許多好電影裡,奇觀同時推動故事或揭露角色。要衡量兩者的平衡,而不是直接把一部片貼上「只有特效」的標籤。
  • 常見誤解:「吸引力」不是貶義詞。岡寧的觀點是歷史性、描述性的:早期觀眾是為了驚奇而來,今天在 IMAX 影廳的觀眾也是。一個出色的吸引力,本身就是真正的電影成就。

3. 具體範例——《火車大劫案》(The Great Train Robbery,1903),導演艾德溫·S·波特(Edwin S. Porter)

背景:由愛迪生製造公司出品,1903 年 11 月在愛迪生的紐約攝影棚與紐澤西外景地拍攝,同年 12 月發行;長約 12 分鐘、14 個鏡頭。它常被稱為最早的美國敘事電影之一(也是早期西部片):盜匪搶劫火車、逃走,最後被追捕隊追上擊斃。這讓它成為理想的範例——一部已經在說故事的電影,卻仍包含一個純粹的吸引力。

逐步拆解這場戲:

  1. 說故事的鏡頭:全片大部分時間攝影機都離得很遠,用遠景呈現完整動作:電報室、郵件車廂裡的搶劫、乘客在火車旁排成一列、逃亡、舞廳、最後的槍戰。注意電影已經會在不同地點之間交叉剪接——敘事工具正在成形。
  2. 多出來的那一個鏡頭:接著出現一個單獨的中近景:一名盜匪(通常認為由賈斯特斯·D·巴恩斯飾演)站在素色背景前。他直視觀眾,舉起左輪手槍,朝攝影機連開好幾槍。有些拷貝經過手工上色,讓槍口的煙和火光特別醒目。
  3. 要注意的是——它不屬於故事:這名盜匪在情節時間線上沒有位置,背景也不是片中任何地點。愛迪生公司自己的片目寫道,這個鏡頭可以放在片頭或片尾——由放映商決定。這就是吸引力最明確的證據:它可以被移動,因為它不在故事的因果鏈中。
  4. 做了哪些選擇:全片唯一的近距離構圖、唯一直視鏡頭的眼神、唯一朝向觀眾而不是朝向其他角色的動作。
  5. 為什麼有效:觀眾從安全距離看完搶劫之後,突然變成了目標。衝擊是身體上的——一把槍指著你——而不是戲劇上的。據說當時觀眾反應強烈,這個鏡頭也成為全片最有名的畫面。
  6. 更深層的意義:這部片呈現了兩種電影並存的狀態。故事邀請你「透過一扇窗」看事件發生;槍手卻打破了這扇窗。岡寧正是用這類時刻說明:「敘事電影」並不是一夜之間取代了「吸引力」。

客觀與主觀:鏡頭的構圖、直視鏡頭的眼神、片目上的說明,都是可觀察的事實。說這個鏡頭「打破了窗」或「把觀眾當成目標」,則是詮釋——儘管是電影史學者普遍認同的詮釋。

現代連結:馬丁·史柯西斯的《四海好傢伙》(1990)結尾,喬·派西飾演的角色直接朝攝影機開槍——明確向這個鏡頭致敬。

4. 實作觀影練習

看《不可能的任務:鬼影行動》(2011,導演布萊德·博德)中攀爬杜拜哈里發塔的段落。回答:(1)哪些時刻主要是為了讓你對高度、速度或特技本身倒抽一口氣?(2)這個段落是否也推動了情節或揭露角色——比例有多少?(3)有沒有哪個鏡頭刪掉也不會改變故事?(4)電影在哪裡把你當成觀眾來對待(往下看的主觀鏡頭、聲音、構圖)?寫三、四句話傳給我,我們再一起討論。

5. 重點整理

  • 吸引力在展示;敘事在訴說。許多電影兩者兼具。
  • 檢驗方法:這個片段能不能被移動或刪除而不影響情節?它是否直接面向觀眾?
  • 奇觀不一定是弱點——要問它是否與故事整合,以及「驚奇」是不是本來的目的。

6. 延伸觀賞建議

《月球之旅》(Le Voyage dans la Lune,1902,喬治·梅里愛)——約 15 分鐘的魔術、跳舞的星星和手繪布景,「故事」大多只是串起一個又一個奇景的藉口。拿它和《火車大劫案》的敘事段落比較看看。
