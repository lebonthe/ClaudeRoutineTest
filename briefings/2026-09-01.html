3) Spanish Lesson — Day 52 (10 min, Relative Pronouns: Que, Quien, Donde, Cuyo)

Today steps away from the tense/mood and preposition tracks to cover Spanish's four core relative pronouns, used to join two sentences into one without repeating the same noun. Que ("that/which/who") is the universal default for both people and things, and — unlike English, which often drops "that/who" in casual speech — Spanish's que can never be omitted. Quien/quienes ("who") is reserved for people only, most commonly right after a preposition (con quien, "with whom") or in a comma-set-off descriptive clause. Donde ("where") replaces a location. Cuyo/cuya/cuyos/cuyas ("whose") marks possession, but — the sharpest trap in today's lesson — it agrees in gender/number with the noun being possessed, not with the possessor, the reverse of what English or Chinese intuition would predict.

  • La chica que vive al lado es la profesora de quien te hablé ayer. — The girl who lives next door is the teacher I told you about yesterday.
  • ¿La casa donde vive es esa con el jardín grande? — Is the house where she lives the one with the big garden?
  • Sí, esa cuyo jardín tiene rosas es su casa. — Yes, the one whose garden has roses is her house.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 52: 艮 (Gèn) — "Keeping Still" (Mountain) (Mountain doubled: Gèn over Gèn — the trigram for mountain repeated both above and below, the fourth hexagram in the Sequence built from a single trigram doubled twice in succession, following Hexagram 1's doubled Heaven, Hexagram 2's doubled Earth, and Hexagram 51's doubled Thunder). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary states the transition directly: "震者,動也。物不可以終動,止之,故受之以艮。艮者,止也。" ("Zhèn means to move. But things cannot move forever — they must be brought to rest; therefore it is followed by Gèn. Gèn means to keep still.") Having spent Hexagram 51 teaching how to meet sudden shock without losing composure, the Sequence now turns to the opposite discipline: knowing when and how to stop. The Judgment reads: "艮其背,不獲其身;行其庭,不見其人,無咎。" ("Keeping his back still, so that he no longer feels his own body. He goes into his courtyard and does not see his people. No blame.") The image is deliberately paradoxical: true stillness is not merely physical immobility but a state in which the self becomes so quiet that it stops even fixating on itself — one can move through one's own courtyard, surrounded by people, and yet remain undisturbed by them, neither craving their attention nor being agitated by their presence. The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) elaborates: "艮,止也。時止則止,時行則行,動靜不失其時,其道光明。艮其止,止其所也。上下敵應,不相與也,是以不獲其身,行其庭不見其人,無咎也。" ("Gèn means to stop. When it is time to stop, one stops; when it is time to act, one acts. Movement and stillness each occurring at their proper time — this is the way that shines bright. 'Keeping his stillness' means stopping in one's proper place. [In this hexagram] the upper and lower trigrams face each other as opposing correspondents, without mutual involvement — this is why he does not feel his own body, and moving through his courtyard, he does not see his people; there is no blame.") The key teaching is that stillness is not the absence of action but action's proper timing and placement — knowing one's "proper place" (止其所) and stopping there, neither pushing forward out of restlessness nor being distracted by what surrounds one. The Image commentary draws the practical application: "兼山,艮;君子以思不出其位。" ("Mountains joined together: the image of Keeping Still. Thus the superior man does not let his thoughts go beyond his own position.") Paired directly against Hexagram 51's lesson on composure amid sudden disruption, Hexagram 52 completes the pair by teaching the deliberate, disciplined stillness that must follow — restraining one's thinking and action to one's proper sphere, so that neither shock nor stillness, when each arrives in its proper turn, throws the superior man off balance.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Botswana (波札那)

  • Location: A landlocked country in Southern Africa, bordered by Namibia to the west and north, Zambia and Zimbabwe to the northeast, and South Africa to the south and east; the Zambezi River region gives it a brief four-way border touchpoint near Kazungula. Capital: Gaborone, in the country's southeast.
  • Territory: About 581,730 km² — roughly the size of France (551,695 km²) or slightly larger than Ukraine's pre-2014 territory, and about 16 times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km²), despite having a much smaller population than either comparison. Roughly 70% of the country's land area is covered by the Kalahari Desert, and much of the remainder in the north forms the vast Okavango Delta, one of the world's largest inland river deltas, which floods seasonally and never reaches the sea.
  • Population: Approximately 2.6 million (2026 estimate), giving Botswana one of the lowest population densities of any country on Earth — fewer than 5 people per km², versus roughly 650 per km² in Taiwan.
  • Religion: Predominantly Christian (roughly 75-80%, spanning Protestant, independent African-initiated, and Catholic denominations), with a meaningful minority practicing traditional Tswana/Sotho-Tswana belief systems (often blended with Christian practice) and small Muslim, Baháʼí, and Hindu communities.
  • Economy: One of Africa's most consistent post-independence success stories, transformed by diamonds — discovered shortly after independence and, through the state's roughly 15%-plus equity stake in De Beers-linked joint venture Debswana, turned into one of the highest GDP-per-capita economies in Sub-Saharan Africa (nominal GDP per capita around $8,000-9,000 in 2026, among the continent's highest outside oil-rich or city-state economies). Botswana has consistently ranked as one of Africa's least corrupt countries by Transparency International, though heavy diamond dependence and a need to diversify into tourism, beef, and financial services remain long-standing policy priorities.
  • History: Home for millennia to San (Bushmen) hunter-gatherers, later settled by Tswana-speaking Bantu peoples who established several powerful chiefdoms. In 1885, local Tswana chiefs successfully petitioned the British Crown for protection against Boer and Rhodesian expansion, becoming the Bechuanaland Protectorate rather than a full colony — a lighter-touch arrangement credited with preserving more indigenous political institutions than most of colonial Africa. Botswana gained independence peacefully in 1966 under Seretse Khama, whose 1948 marriage to English woman Ruth Williams had earlier caused a major international diplomatic controversy under apartheid-era Southern African politics; Khama went on to found the Botswana Democratic Party, which has governed continuously (via multi-party elections) since independence, making Botswana one of Africa's longest continuously-running multiparty democracies.
  • Current leader: President Duma Boko, leader of the Umbrella for Democratic Change coalition, who won the October 2024 election — ending the Botswana Democratic Party's uninterrupted 58-year rule in what was widely praised internationally as a peaceful, orderly democratic transfer of power.
  • Language: English is the official language of government, courts, and higher education; Setswana (Tswana) is the widely spoken national language used in Parliament, primary schooling, and daily life across most of the population, alongside several smaller minority languages including Kalanga and San/Khoisan click languages.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, worldometers.info (population), en.wikipedia.org (economy)

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — Wojak

  • Name/origin: Wojak (from the Polish word "wojak," meaning "soldier" or "warrior") is a crudely drawn, bald, doughy-faced cartoon head originally posted on the German imageboard Krautchan around 2009-2010 under the title "Der Wojak," accompanying a rambling, self-pitying confessional post — giving the character its earliest alternate name, "Feels Guy," tied to expressing raw, often melancholic emotional states ("I know that feel, bro").
  • How it became popular: The image spread from Krautchan to 4chan's /r9k/ and /b/ boards in the early-to-mid 2010s, where its blank, minimally-detailed face made it an easy template for endless recoloring and re-drawing to fit new emotional or social archetypes, cementing it as one of imageboard culture's most durable and endlessly forkable base templates rather than a single fixed joke.
  • Meaning/usage: Functions less as one specific joke and more as a flexible visual shorthand for a raw, often uncomfortably sincere emotional or social state — awkwardness, resignation, ironic self-deprecation, or a caricatured social "type" — used across both earnest confessional posts and heavily ironic political/cultural commentary, frequently in side-by-side comparison formats contrasting two or more Wojak variants.
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned an entire taxonomy of derivative characters, most notably "NPC Wojak" (a flat-grey-faced variant satirizing unthinking conformist opinion, 2018 onward), "Doomer" (a black-hoodie, cigarette-smoking variant embodying nihilistic Gen-Z/Millennial despair), "Soyjak" (an exaggerated, wide-mouthed, screaming variant mocking performative enthusiasm or "soy-boy" affect), and numerous political, national, and subculture-specific Wojak "types," often deployed in tiered comparison-chart formats ranking or contrasting different social archetypes against each other.
  • Current status: Still highly active in 2026, particularly across politically-charged and ironic/post-ironic online discourse, where new Wojak variants continue to be generated to represent emerging social types or viewpoints almost as quickly as internet subcultures themselves evolve; dedicated Wojak-editing communities and wikis continue cataloguing new derivatives, making it one of the longest-running and most prolifically forked meme templates in internet history.
    Sources: knowyourmeme.com

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Persona (1966, dir. Ingmar Bergman)

  • Name/creator: Persona, written and directed by Swedish filmmaker Ingmar Bergman, shot by longtime collaborator cinematographer Sven Nykvist, and starring Bibi Andersson and Liv Ullmann in the two lead roles — a pairing (and, off-screen, a real-life relationship between Ullmann and Bergman that began during filming) that became one of the most studied actor collaborations in arthouse cinema.
  • Era/background: Made in 1966 at a low point in Bergman's health (he wrote much of the script while hospitalized) and at a moment when he had grown dissatisfied with conventional narrative filmmaking, Persona marked a deliberate turn toward radical formal experimentation, produced quickly and cheaply on the island of Fårö and at a nearby beach location that would become closely associated with Bergman's later work and personal life.
  • Plot/content overview: A stage actress, Elisabet Vogler (Ullmann), suddenly stops speaking mid-performance and is placed under the care of a young nurse, Alma (Andersson), at a remote seaside cottage. As the silent, watchful Elisabet listens, the talkative Alma gradually confesses increasingly intimate personal secrets, and the film steadily blurs which woman's identity, memories, and even physical face belong to whom — climaxing in an unresolved, dreamlike merging of the two characters that resists any single stable interpretation.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Famous for an audaciously self-reflexive opening montage (a rapid flicker of unrelated, disturbing images — a spider, a nail being driven into a hand, a boy reaching toward a screen — that appears to precede the "start" of the film itself, foregrounding cinema's own mechanical apparatus, distinct from the "title sequence as thesis statement" convention covered in an earlier lesson), extreme, unusually prolonged close-ups isolating the two actresses' faces, a shot literally splitting the two women's faces down the center into one composite visage, and moments where the film stock itself appears to burn and break mid-scene, deliberately rupturing the illusion of a smoothly continuous narrative.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely regarded as a landmark of modernist, psychologically interior European arthouse cinema and one of the most exhaustively analyzed films in academic film studies, frequently cited alongside Fellini's 8½ (already featured in this series) as a defining work of 1960s cinema's turn toward reflexive, form-breaking psychological narrative over conventional plot.
  • Influence and legacy: Its identity-merging premise and self-interrogating formal techniques have been cited as a direct influence on directors including David Lynch, Robert Altman (3 Women), and countless subsequent arthouse explorations of doubled or dissolving identity; Bergman himself later called it one of the two or three films in his career he considered essential to his own artistic survival.
  • Current status/where to experience it: Preserved and widely distributed via the Criterion Collection (Blu-ray/streaming) and regularly revived at repertory cinemas and film festivals worldwide as a canonical text in film-school curricula; considered part of the permanent core of the international arthouse canon.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, criterion.com

8) Film-Appreciation Method — The Freeze Frame (Arrested Motion) (Lesson 38)

A freeze frame halts the moving image on a single still frame, held on screen for a deliberate duration rather than continuing to play — most commonly deployed as a punctuation device at the very end of a film or scene, though it can also appear mid-sequence to isolate one instant within an otherwise continuous montage. This is a distinct technical lever from every prior lesson: distinct from Day 3's Long Take (an unbroken shot that keeps playing in real time, the opposite gesture of arresting motion entirely), Day 15's Camera Movement (physical camera travel, irrelevant once the image itself has stopped moving), and Day 30's Slow Motion & Speed Ramping (which stretches motion via frame-rate manipulation but never fully halts it into a single still image). When you spot a freeze frame, ask: is it used as a final punctuation mark closing the film on a deliberately unresolved or ambiguous note, or as a mid-scene emphasis device isolating one instant for the audience to study; whether the moment frozen is one of triumph, defeat, epiphany, or — as in the classic case below — irreducible ambiguity; and whether the freeze is total (the entire frame stops) or partial (one element frozen against continuing motion elsewhere in the frame).

Worked example: The 400 Blows (Les Quatre Cents Coups, 1959, dir. François Truffaut) — the celebrated final shot, in which young runaway Antoine Doinel, having escaped a juvenile detention center, sprints along a beach toward the sea (a place he has never seen before) in one continuous tracking shot; as he reaches the water's edge, turns, and looks directly into the camera, the image abruptly freezes on his face in an extended, unbroken close-up that becomes the film's very last image before the credits. The freeze deliberately refuses to resolve Antoine's fate — is he escaping toward freedom, trapped by the sea itself as a dead end, or simply suspended in an unanswerable, open present — and its refusal to let the moving image continue was, at the time, a startling formal gesture that became one of the most influential single shots of the French New Wave, imitated and referenced in countless subsequent films precisely because it demonstrated how arresting motion at exactly the right instant could make a film's ending feel permanently, productively unresolved rather than simply cut short.

3)西班牙文課程——第 52 天(10 分鐘,關係代名詞 Que, Quien, Donde, Cuyo)

今天暫時離開動詞時態/語態與介係詞的主線,學習西語文法中另一塊重要拼圖:用來把兩個句子接在一起、避免重複同一個名詞的四個核心關係代名詞。Que(那個、那位、……的)是人與物皆可用的萬用預設選項,而且——與英語口語中經常省略 that/who 不同——西語的 que 絕對不能省略。Quien/quienes(那位)僅限指人,最常出現在介係詞之後(con quien,和……的那位),或用逗號隔開的補充說明子句中。Donde(……的地方)用來代替地點。Cuyo/cuya/cuyos/cuyas(……的)表示所有關係——今天最容易誤踩的陷阱在於,它的陰陽性/單複數要配合「被擁有的名詞」變化,而非配合「擁有者」本身,恰好與中文或英文的直覺相反。

  • La chica que vive al lado es la profesora de quien te hablé ayer. —— 住在隔壁的那個女孩,就是我昨天跟你提過的那位老師。
  • ¿La casa donde vive es esa con el jardín grande? —— 她住的那間房子,是那棟有大花園的嗎?
  • Sí, esa cuyo jardín tiene rosas es su casa. —— 對,那棟花園裡種著玫瑰的房子,就是她家。

4)易經卦象

第 52 卦:艮 (Gèn) ——「艮」(止、山)(山上山下——艮上艮下:山之卦象上下重疊,是《序卦傳》系列中,第四個由單一卦象連續重疊兩次所構成的卦,繼第 1 卦乾〔天疊天〕、第 2 卦坤〔地疊地〕、第 51 卦震〔雷疊雷〕之後)。《序卦傳》直接說明兩卦之間的轉折:「震者,動也。物不可以終動,止之,故受之以艮。艮者,止也。」(震,就是動、驚動的意思。然而萬事萬物不可能永遠處於動盪之中,終究必須有所止息,因此震卦之後,接的是艮卦。艮,就是止息、靜止的意思。)在第 51 卦教導了如何在突如其來的驚動中,依然保持定力之後,《序卦傳》此時轉向探討恰恰相反的功課:懂得何時、以及如何停下來。卦辭寫道:「艮其背,不獲其身;行其庭,不見其人,無咎。」(讓自己的後背保持靜止,以至於不再感覺到自己身體的存在;即便行走於自家庭院之中,也彷彿看不見院裡的人;如此便沒有過失。)這段文字刻意呈現出一種弔詭的意象:真正的靜止,並非單純肉體上的不動,而是一種連對自身的執著都一併平息下來的狀態——即便置身於庭院之中、周遭人來人往,也依然不為所動,既不刻意渴求他人的關注,也不因他人的存在而心生波動。《彖傳》進一步闡述:「艮,止也。時止則止,時行則行,動靜不失其時,其道光明。艮其止,止其所也。上下敵應,不相與也,是以不獲其身,行其庭不見其人,無咎也。」(艮,就是停止的意思。應當停止的時候就停止,應當行動的時候就行動,動與靜各自不失其應有的時機,這樣的處世之道才能光明磊落。「保持靜止」,指的是停留在自己應處的本位。〔此卦上下兩個卦象〕彼此相對而不相應、互不涉入牽扯,這正是為何〔行止之人〕感覺不到自身的存在,行走於庭院之中也彷彿看不見院裡的人,如此便沒有過失。)這段文字點出的核心教誨在於:真正的「止」,並非行動的闕如,而是行動能否恰如其分地掌握時機與本位——懂得自己應處的「本位」(止其所)並安於其中,既不因躁動而躁進,也不因周遭紛擾而分心。《象傳》則進一步引申出務實的教訓:「兼山,艮;君子以思不出其位。」(兩山相疊,這就是艮卦的卦象。因此君子藉此領悟,應當使自己的思慮,不逾越出自己應處的本位之外。)與第 51 卦教導面對驟然驚動時應保持的定力互為對照,第 52 卦則補上了另一半的功課:懂得刻意、有紀律地讓自己靜止下來——將自己的思慮與行動,克制在應處的本位範圍之內,如此一來,無論是驚動、或是靜止,只要各自在恰當的時機到來,君子都不致因此而失去分寸。

5)國家/地區/政體介紹——波札那(Botswana)

  • 位置:位於非洲南部的內陸國家,西側與北側與納米比亞接壤,東北與尚比亞、辛巴威為鄰,南側與東側則與南非相接;尚比西河流域一帶,更形成了一個罕見的「四國交界」地帶,靠近卡祖恩古拉(Kazungula)。首都:嘉柏隆里(Gaborone),位於該國東南部。
  • 領土:總面積約 581,730 平方公里——大致與法國(551,695 平方公里)相當,略大於 2014 年前的烏克蘭國土面積,約為台灣(約 36,193 平方公里)的 16 倍,儘管人口卻遠遠少於這兩個比較對象。該國約 70% 的國土,被喀拉哈里沙漠所覆蓋;北部其餘大部分地區,則構成了全球規模數一數二的內陸河流三角洲——奧卡萬戈三角洲(Okavango Delta),每年隨季節性氾濫而形成大片濕地,河水卻始終未能真正流入海洋。
  • 人口:約 260 萬人(2026 年估計值),使波札那成為全球人口密度最低的國家之一——每平方公里不到 5 人,相較之下台灣每平方公里約有 650 人。
  • 宗教:以基督教為主(約佔 75% 至 80%,涵蓋新教、非洲本土教派與天主教等不同宗派),另有相當比例人口信奉茨瓦納/索托-茨瓦納傳統信仰體系(常與基督教信仰混合實踐),此外也有少數穆斯林、巴哈伊信徒與印度教徒社群。
  • 經濟:是非洲獨立後經濟發展最為穩健的成功案例之一,轉型的關鍵在於鑽石產業——獨立後不久即發現鑽石礦藏,透過政府在戴比爾斯(De Beers)關聯合資企業「戴布斯瓦納」(Debswana)中持有的逾 15% 股權,將鑽石收益轉化為撒哈拉以南非洲人均 GDP 數一數二的經濟體(2026 年名目人均 GDP 約落在 8,000 至 9,000 美元之間,在非洲大陸上,僅次於少數石油富國或城市型經濟體)。波札那長年被「國際透明組織」列為非洲貪腐程度最低的國家之一,不過過度依賴鑽石產業、亟需朝觀光、牛肉出口與金融服務業多元化發展,始終是該國長期的政策優先課題。
  • 歷史:數千年來一直是桑族(布希曼人)狩獵採集者的家園,後有講茨瓦納語的班圖族群遷入定居,建立起數個勢力強大的酋邦。1885 年,當地茨瓦納各部酋長成功向英國王室請願,尋求保護以抵禦波耳人與羅德西亞的擴張勢力,因而成為「貝專納保護國」,而非徹底淪為殖民地——這種相對寬鬆的治理安排,一般認為使該地得以保留比大多數殖民地非洲地區更多的原住民政治體制。波札那於 1966 年在塞雷茨·卡馬(Seretse Khama)領導下和平獨立;卡馬 1948 年與英國女子露絲·威廉斯(Ruth Williams)的跨種族婚姻,曾在種族隔離時代的南部非洲政治氛圍中,引發一場重大的國際外交爭議。卡馬其後創立波札那民主黨,該黨透過多黨選舉持續執政直至獨立後多年,使波札那成為非洲歷史最悠久、不曾中斷的多黨民主國家之一。
  • 現任領導人:總統杜馬·博科(Duma Boko),為「變革保護傘聯盟」(Umbrella for Democratic Change)領袖,於 2024 年 10 月大選中勝出,終結了波札那民主黨長達 58 年不曾間斷的執政地位,這場政權和平、有序的移轉,在國際間普遍獲得高度讚譽。
  • 語言:英語為政府、司法體系與高等教育所使用的官方語言;塞茨瓦納語(茨瓦納語)則是國會議事、國小教育與多數人口日常生活中廣泛使用的國語,此外還有卡蘭加語(Kalanga)與桑族/科伊桑語系(以搭嘴音著稱)等數種少數族群語言並存。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、worldometers.info(人口)、en.wikipedia.org(經濟)

6)網路迷因介紹——Wojak

  • 名稱/起源:「Wojak」(源自波蘭語 wojak,意為「戰士」)是一個畫風粗糙、光頭、面容鬆垮的卡通人頭圖像,最早於 2009 至 2010 年間,張貼於德國圖片論壇 Krautchan 上,標題為「Der Wojak」,搭配一篇語無倫次、充滿自憐情緒的告白文章——這也是這個角色最早的別名「Feels Guy」(感受哥)的由來,用以表達一種原始、往往帶有憂鬱色彩的情緒狀態(「我懂那種感覺,兄弟」)。
  • 如何走紅:這張圖像從 Krautchan 論壇,擴散至 2010 年代初中期的 4chan /r9k/ 與 /b/ 看板,由於其面容空白、細節極簡,使其極易被反覆重新上色、重新繪製,以套用到各種新的情緒或社會人設身上,也讓它成為圖片論壇文化中,最持久、最能無限衍生分支的基礎模板之一,而非僅止於單一固定的笑點。
  • 意義/使用情境:與其說是特定的單一笑點,不如說它更像是一種靈活的視覺速記符號,用來表達某種原始、往往帶有令人不自在的真誠情緒或社會狀態——尷尬、認命、帶著諷刺意味的自嘲,或是被誇張化的某種社會「類型」——廣泛應用於真摯的告白式貼文,以及高度諷刺性的政治/文化評論之中,經常以並列比較不同 Wojak 變體的形式呈現。
  • 變體/演化:衍生出一整套龐大的衍生角色譜系,其中最具代表性的包括:「NPC Wojak」(自 2018 年起流行,一張面色死灰的變體,用來諷刺不加思考、人云亦云的從眾意見)、「Doomer」(身穿黑色連帽衫、叼著菸的變體,象徵 Z 世代/千禧世代的虛無主義式絕望)、「Soyjak」(誇張化、張大嘴巴尖叫的變體,用來嘲諷做作的過度熱情或所謂「豆奶男孩」〔soy-boy〕氣質),以及數不清的政治立場、國族、次文化專屬 Wojak「類型」,經常以分級比較圖表的形式呈現,用來排序或對比不同的社會人設類型。
  • 目前狀態:截至 2026 年,在充滿政治色彩與諷刺/後諷刺網路論述中依然十分活躍,新的 Wojak 變體幾乎與網路次文化本身的演變同步、持續被創造出來,用以代表各種新興的社會類型或立場觀點;專門的 Wojak 編輯創作社群與 wiki 網站,至今仍持續收錄、彙整新的衍生版本,使其成為網路迷因史上,持續時間最長、衍生分支最為龐雜的模板迷因之一。
    來源:knowyourmeme.com

7)電影/表演藝術介紹——《假面》Persona(1966,導演:英格瑪·柏格曼)

  • 名稱/創作者:《假面》(Persona)由瑞典導演英格瑪·柏格曼(Ingmar Bergman)親自編劇並執導,由長年合作的攝影師史文·尼克維斯特(Sven Nykvist)掌鏡,並由碧比·安德森(Bibi Andersson)與麗芙·烏曼(Liv Ullmann)分飾兩位女主角——這對演員組合(以及拍攝期間展開、烏曼與柏格曼兩人現實生活中的一段感情關係),日後成為藝術電影領域中,最受學界深入研究的演員合作案例之一。
  • 時代/背景:本片拍攝於 1966 年,正值柏格曼健康狀況陷入低潮之際(劇本大部分是他住院期間所寫成),也正值他對傳統敘事電影日漸感到不滿足的時期。《假面》標誌著他刻意轉向激進形式實驗的分水嶺之作,以低成本、快節奏在法羅島(Fårö)及附近一處海灘地點拍攝完成,而這處海灘,日後也與柏格曼晚期作品及其個人生活產生了緊密的關聯。
  • 劇情/內容概述:一位舞台女演員伊麗莎白·沃格勒(烏曼飾)在演出途中突然停止說話,被安排由一名年輕護士艾瑪(安德森飾)在一處偏遠的海邊小屋中照顧。當沉默、專注凝視的伊麗莎白靜靜聆聽時,健談的艾瑪逐漸向她坦承愈發私密的個人秘密,電影也隨之逐步模糊了兩位女性的身分、記憶,甚至連兩人的容貌本身,究竟屬於誰,最終在一場如夢似幻、懸而未決的兩人融合意象中攀上高潮,拒絕給出任何單一、穩定的詮釋。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:本片以一段大膽自我指涉的開場蒙太奇而聞名——一連串迅速閃現、彼此毫無關聯且令人不安的影像(一隻蜘蛛、一根釘子被釘入手掌、一名男孩伸手觸向銀幕),彷彿發生在電影本身「正式開始」之前,刻意將電影自身的機械裝置暴露在觀眾眼前,有別於前一課所介紹、將片頭字幕作為主題宣言的慣例手法;此外,片中大量運用極端且異常持久的特寫鏡頭,將兩位女演員的臉孔孤立呈現,甚至有一個鏡頭將兩位女性的臉孔,從正中央一分為二、拼接成一張複合的臉;另有幾個時刻,膠捲本身彷彿在場景進行到一半時燒毀、斷裂,刻意打破敘事流暢連貫的幻覺。
  • 文化/歷史意義:普遍被視為現代主義、深入探索心理內在世界的歐洲藝術電影中的里程碑之作,也是電影學術研究領域中,被剖析得最為透徹的作品之一,經常與費里尼的《八又二分之一》(本系列先前已介紹過)相提並論,被視為 1960 年代電影從傳統情節導向,轉向自我反身、打破形式常規之心理敘事的代表作。
  • 影響與傳承:其身分融合的核心命題,以及自我質問式的形式技巧,被公認直接影響了包括大衛·林區(David Lynch)、勞勃·阿特曼(Robert Altman,《三女性》)等後世導演,以及無數後續藝術電影對於雙重或消融身分議題的探索;柏格曼本人日後也將本片稱為自己創作生涯中,少數幾部他認為對其藝術生命至關重要、不可或缺的作品之一。
  • 現況/如何觀賞:目前由標準收藏(Criterion Collection)完整保存並廣泛發行(藍光光碟/線上串流),並經常於全球各地的經典影院與影展重新放映,是電影學校課程中公認的核心教材;被視為國際藝術電影正典中,長久不衰的核心構成之一。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、criterion.com

8)看片方法——凝結畫面/靜止幀 The Freeze Frame(第 38 課)

凝結畫面(freeze frame)是將原本流動的影像,定格於單一靜止的畫格上,並刻意持續停留一段時間,而非繼續播放下去——最常見的用法,是作為整部電影或整個場景結尾處的標點符號,不過偶爾也會出現在一段連續蒙太奇的中段,用來孤立呈現其中某一個瞬間。這是一個完全獨立於先前所有課程的技術面向:有別於第 3 天的「長鏡頭」(以真實時間持續播放、不曾中斷的鏡頭,恰恰是「讓動作徹底靜止」的相反手勢)、第 15 天的「運鏡」(攝影機本身的實體移動,一旦畫面本身已經靜止不動,運鏡與否便不再相關),以及第 30 天的「慢動作與變速攝影」(透過格率操縱來拉長動作,但從未讓畫面徹底凝結為單一靜止的影像)。發現凝結畫面手法時,可以檢視:它究竟是作為結束整部電影的最終標點,刻意停留在一個懸而未決、模糊不清的時刻;還是作為場景中段的強調手法,孤立出某個瞬間供觀眾細細品味?被凝結下來的那一刻,究竟是勝利、挫敗、頓悟,還是——如下方經典案例所示——一種無從化約的曖昧不明?這個凝結,究竟是整個畫面全部靜止,還是僅有畫面中某個元素被凍結、其餘部分仍持續運動的局部凝結?

實例演練:《四百擊》(Les Quatre Cents Coups,1959,導演:法蘭索瓦·楚浮〔François Truffaut〕)——本片著名的最後一個鏡頭:少年安端·達諾(Antoine Doinel)逃出少年感化院後,在一鏡到底的跟拍長鏡頭中,沿著海灘一路狂奔,朝著他此生從未見過的大海奔去;當他抵達海水邊緣、轉身直視鏡頭的那一瞬間,畫面驟然凝結在他的臉上,定格成一個延長、不曾中斷的特寫鏡頭,並就此成為片尾字幕出現前,整部電影的最後一個畫面。這個凝結畫面,刻意拒絕交代安端最終的命運究竟為何——他究竟是正奔向自由,還是被眼前這片大海本身困於絕境,抑或僅僅是懸置於一個無解、開放的當下——這種讓流動的影像戛然而止、拒絕再繼續播放下去的手法,在當時是一項令人驚豔的形式創舉,日後更成為法國新浪潮最具影響力的單一鏡頭之一,被無數後世電影模仿、致敬,正因為這個鏡頭示範了:只要在恰到好處的那一瞬間讓動作徹底靜止,就能讓一部電影的結局,顯得永久地、且具有生產性地懸而未決,而非僅僅是被硬生生截斷。
