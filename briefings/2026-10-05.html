3) Spanish Lesson — Day 81 (10 min, Weather: hace / está / hay / llover)

A light everyday topic: how to talk about the weather. Weather uses hace + noun (hace calor, hace frío, hace sol, hace viento), está + adjective (está nublado), hay + noun (hay niebla) and impersonal verbs (llueve, nieva). Because calor and frío are nouns you say mucho (hace mucho calor), not muy. Trap: weather is hace calor, but a person feels hot with tengo calor.

  • ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy? — What's the weather like today?
  • Hace mucho calor, pero esta tarde va a llover. — It's very hot, but it's going to rain this afternoon.
  • Ayer hacía frío y estaba nublado. — Yesterday it was cold and cloudy.
  • Tengo frío; ¿puedes cerrar la ventana? — I'm cold; can you close the window?

4) Zhuangzi — Essay 17: 秋水 (Qiū Shuǐ) — "Autumn Floods"

The tenth chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇) and one of the most beloved in the book. In the autumn floods the Lord of the Yellow River (河伯) is delighted that the whole world's beauty seems gathered in his own waters. Following the current to the sea, he sees the North Sea's vastness and is humbled, and Ruo of the North Sea (北海若) tells him: a frog in a well cannot discuss the sea, a summer insect cannot discuss ice, and a narrow-minded scholar cannot discuss the Way. Ruo then argues that size, value and right/wrong are all relative to one's standpoint: in the great scheme the sea is itself small, and one should not be proud of knowing "so much" out of what can never be known. The chapter closes with famous stories: the one-legged kui who envies the centipede, the Chu tortoise who would rather drag its tail in the mud than be honored as a sacred bone in a temple (Zhuangzi declining a ministership), the owl clutching a rotten rat who thinks the phoenix is after it (Huizi vs. Zhuangzi), and the debate on the Hao River bridge: "You are not a fish — how do you know the fish are happy?" Core idea: widen your horizon, hold knowledge humbly, and follow nature instead of fame.

Under the Kalmar Union from 1397 and Danish rule for about four centuries; ceded to Sweden in 1814, when the Eidsvoll assembly adopted a constitution on 17 May 1814 (Norway's national day). 6) Internet Meme Spotlight — "Keep Calm and Carry On"

  • Origin: A British government poster designed in 1939 by the Ministry of Information, one of three morale posters prepared in case of German bombing. About 2.5 million copies were printed but they were never officially put on public display and most were pulped. Its red background, white crown and bold sans-serif lettering made it instantly recognizable.
  • How it went viral: In 2000 Stuart and Mary Manley found a copy in a box of old books at Barter Books in Alnwick, Northumberland, and framed it by the till. Customer demand led the shop to sell reprints, and the poster spread through design blogs and home-decor stores in the mid-2000s. The 2008 financial crisis and the 2012 London Olympics era gave it a second wave as a symbol of stoic, understated resilience.
  • Meaning and usage: Originally sincere advice to stay composed under wartime stress; online it became a template of the form "Keep Calm and ___". Used as a joke or as a reassuring message on mugs, shirts, social-media captions and workplace posters.
  • Variations: Endless parodies ("Keep Calm and Call Batman", "Keep Calm and Drink Coffee", "Now Panic and Freak Out"), online generator sites, and imitations in many languages. Trademark disputes over the phrase also arose in the 2010s.
  • Current status: Ubiquitous but now often seen as a cliché or nostalgic joke; still a go-to template and a rare case of a century-old propaganda poster becoming a meme. (Written from general knowledge; some details are approximate.)

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Takarazuka Revue (寶塚歌劇團, Japan)

  • What it is: A Japanese musical-theatre company in which every performer is a woman. Women playing male roles are otokoyaku and those playing female roles are musumeyaku. It is based in Takarazuka, Hyogo Prefecture, near Osaka.
  • Founder & background: Founded in 1913 by Ichizō Kobayashi, founder of the Hankyu railway, as a way to attract passengers to the hot-spring resort at the end of his rail line; first public performance in 1914. A training school (Takarazuka Music and Dance School) supplies the cast, and performers are known by their stage names and by troupe.
  • Structure: Five troupes, Flower, Moon, Snow, Star and Cosmos (Cosmos was added in 1998), plus a group of veterans called Senka. Each troupe has a top otokoyaku and a top musumeyaku. The Takarazuka Grand Theater (rebuilt in 1993) and the Tokyo Takarazuka Theater are the main homes. The motto is "Kiyoku, tadashiku, utsukushiku" (Pure, correct, beautiful).
  • Style & techniques: Lavish Broadway-meets-Paris revue spectacle: a grand staircase finale, line dances, sequins and giant feather fans worn by the top star, sweeping romance and heightened stylized gender performance. The first full Western-style revue in Japan, Mon Paris (1927), set the template. Repertoire ranges from original Japanese works to adaptations of manga and Western musicals.
  • Landmark works: The Rose of Versailles (1974), from Riyoko Ikeda's manga, set off a nationwide boom; Takarazuka also staged the first Japanese production of the musical Elisabeth (1996), which became a signature hit.
  • Significance & legacy: Over 100 years of one of Japan's most successful theatre businesses, with devoted fan clubs and a strong influence on shōjo manga and anime culture and on how Japan discusses gender performance. It has also faced criticism and scrutiny over rigid hierarchy and working conditions, including the 2023 death of a young performer that prompted investigation and public debate.
  • Where to experience: Takarazuka Grand Theater (Hyogo) and Tokyo Takarazuka Theater; shows rotate roughly monthly (for example the Cosmos Troupe's Black Lizard / Diamond IMPULSE was scheduled for May 2026), with broadcasts and recordings on Japanese TV and streaming. (Details are from general knowledge plus a search summary; check the official site for the current schedule.)

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 67: Silence and the Absence of Score

The method: Most viewers think about what they hear; a trained viewer also listens for what is not there. Silence is a choice, just like a camera angle. Ask: (1) Is there a music score at all, and where does it enter or leave? (2) What does the room tone (the quiet background hum of a space) tell you about the place? (3) When music is withheld, which small sounds become foreground (breathing, footsteps, a rustle of packaging, a clock)? (4) Does silence arrive before a threat or a violent act, making the audience lean in and do the work the score would normally do for them? (5) Whose point of view does the silence put you in? Practice: watch a scene once with the sound muted, then again with sound, and note what changed in your feelings; or close your eyes and list every sound you can identify. This builds on Lesson 4's sound design, the acousmatic-sound lesson and Foley, but focuses on subtraction.

Worked example: No Country for Old Men (2007, dir. Joel and Ethan Coen), the gas-station scene. Anton Chigurh enters a remote gas station and, while the elderly proprietor chats, he slowly turns a casual question about the weather and the owner's life into a coin-toss that decides whether the man lives. The film famously has almost no conventional score (composer Carter Burwell used only sparse, near-inaudible tones), so there is no music telling you how to feel. Instead the sound design (Skip Lievsay) lets the tiny sounds carry the tension: the rustle of a snack package, the register, Chigurh's flat voice, the proprietor's nervous rambling. Watch how the shot-reverse-shot is held patiently, how the quiet makes every pause long, and why this works: with no score to cue the audience, the threat feels unmanaged and real, matching Chigurh's role as an impersonal force of fate. Compare with the opposite choice, in which a swelling score would have told you "be afraid" and relieved you of doing it yourself.

3) 西班牙文課 — 第 81 天(10 分鐘,天氣表達:hace / está / hay / llover)

輕鬆的日常主題:怎麼聊天氣。天氣用 hace + 名詞(hace calor、hace frío、hace sol、hace viento)、está + 形容詞(está nublado)、hay + 名詞(hay niebla),以及無人稱動詞(llueve、nieva)。因為 calor、frío 是名詞,加強語氣要用 mucho(hace mucho calor),而不是 muy。陷阱:天氣熱是 hace calor,人覺得熱則是 tengo calor。

  • ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy? — 今天天氣怎麼樣?

  • Ayer hacía frío y estaba nublado. — 昨天很冷,而且是陰天。
  • Tengo frío; ¿puedes cerrar la ventana? — 我覺得冷,可以把窗戶關上嗎?

6) 網路迷因介紹 — "Keep Calm and Carry On"(保持冷靜,繼續前進)

  • 起源:1939 年英國資訊部設計的政府海報,是為了德軍轟炸時備用的三張士氣海報之一。約印製 250 萬張,但從未正式公開張貼,大多被回收做成紙漿。紅色底、白色皇冠與粗體無襯線字體,辨識度極高。
  • 走紅過程:2000 年,Stuart 與 Mary Manley 在北安柏蘭郡 Alnwick 的 Barter Books 書店一箱舊書中發現一張,裱框放在櫃檯旁。顧客紛紛詢問,書店開始賣複製品,2000 年代中期透過設計部落格與家飾店擴散。2008 年金融海嘯與 2012 年倫敦奧運時期再度掀起第二波熱潮,成為「沉著堅忍」的象徵。
  • 意義與用法:原本是戰時勸人保持鎮定的真誠建議;網路上變成「Keep Calm and ___」的填空模板,用在馬克杯、T 恤、社群貼文與辦公室海報,既可當玩笑也可當安慰。
  • 變體:無數惡搞版(「Keep Calm and Call Batman」「Keep Calm and Drink Coffee」「Now Panic and Freak Out」)、線上產生器網站與各語言的仿作;2010 年代也出現過關於這句話的商標爭議。
  • 現況:隨處可見,但如今常被視為老梗或懷舊笑話;仍是常用模板,也是少見由近百年前宣傳海報變成迷因的案例。(依一般知識撰寫,部分細節為大致描述。)

7) 電影/表演藝術介紹 — 寶塚歌劇團(Takarazuka Revue,日本)

  • 是什麼:由女性擔任全部演員的日本音樂劇團。扮演男角的稱為「男役」(otokoyaku),扮演女角的稱為「娘役」(musumeyaku)。總部位於兵庫縣寶塚市,鄰近大阪。
  • 創辦人與背景:1913 年由阪急電鐵創辦人小林一三創立,原意是吸引乘客到鐵路終點的溫泉度假區;1914 年首度公演。團員都來自附設的寶塚音樂舞蹈學校,以藝名與所屬組別演出。
  • 組織:花、月、雪、星、宙五個組(宙組於 1998 年新增),另有資深團員組成的「專科」。每組各有一位男役頂級明星(Top star)與一位娘役頂級明星。主要劇場為寶塚大劇場(1993 年重建)與東京寶塚劇場。團訓是「清く正しく美しく」(清純、正直、美麗)。
  • 風格與技法:融合百老匯與巴黎歌舞秀的華麗舞台:大階梯終場、群舞、亮片服裝與頂級明星背負的巨大羽毛扇,以及帶有高度風格化的性別表演與浪漫敘事。1927 年的《Mon Paris》是日本第一齣完整的西式歌舞秀,奠定了範本。劇目從日本原創到漫畫與西方音樂劇改編皆有。
  • 代表作:1974 年改編自池田理代子漫畫的《凡爾賽玫瑰》掀起全國熱潮;寶塚也在 1996 年推出日本首演的音樂劇《伊莉莎白》,成為招牌劇目。
  • 意義與影響:超過百年,是日本最成功的劇場事業之一,擁有死忠的後援會,對少女漫畫與動漫文化、以及日本社會討論性別扮演的方式影響深遠。同時也因階級嚴格與工作條件受到批評與關注,包括 2023 年一名年輕團員過世引發的調查與公共討論。
  • 哪裡可以體驗:寶塚大劇場(兵庫)與東京寶塚劇場;公演大約每月輪替(例如宙組的《黑蜥蜴》/《Diamond IMPULSE》原定 2026 年 5 月演出),也有日本電視與串流平台的轉播與影像。(內容依一般知識與搜尋摘要整理,最新檔期請查官方網站。)

8) 看片方法課 — 第 67 課:沉默與無配樂

方法:多數觀眾只注意聽見了什麼;受過訓練的觀眾也會聽「沒有什麼」。沉默和鏡頭角度一樣,是導演的選擇。可以問:(1) 有沒有配樂?配樂從何時進入、何時離開?(2) 空間的背景底噪(room tone)透露了這個地方的什麼?(3) 當音樂被拿掉,哪些小聲音變成前景(呼吸、腳步、包裝紙的沙沙聲、時鐘)?(4) 沉默是否出現在威脅或暴力之前,讓觀眾自己靠近、替配樂做原本該做的情緒工作?(5) 沉默把你放在誰的視角?練習:同一場戲先靜音看一次,再開聲音看一次,記下感受的差別;或閉上眼睛,列出你能辨認的每一種聲音。這是第 4 課「聲音設計」、畫外音與擬音課程的延伸,但重點放在「減法」。

實例:《險路勿近》(No Country for Old Men,2007,柯恩兄弟導演)的加油站場景。殺手安東·奇哥走進偏僻的加油站,老闆閒聊之際,他把一個關於天氣與老闆生活的隨口問題,慢慢變成決定老闆生死的擲硬幣。這部片幾乎沒有傳統配樂(作曲卡特·伯威爾只用了極少、近乎聽不見的低鳴),沒有音樂告訴你該有什麼感覺。取而代之的是音效設計(Skip Lievsay)讓微小的聲音撐起緊張感:零食包裝的沙沙聲、收銀機、奇哥平板的語調、老闆緊張的碎念。注意鏡頭正反打耐心地停留、安靜如何讓每個停頓都變得很長,以及為什麼有效:沒有配樂暗示觀眾,威脅感就顯得不受控而真實,正好呼應奇哥如同命運般無情的角色。對照相反的做法:若加上漸強的配樂,就等於告訴你「要害怕」,讓你不必自己去感受。
