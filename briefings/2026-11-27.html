ENEnglish version

Original text (Wang Bi received text)

知人者智,自知者明。勝人者有力,自勝者強。知足者富。強行者有志。不失其所者久。死而不亡者壽。

Translation: "One who knows others is clever; one who knows himself is clear-sighted. One who defeats others has strength; one who defeats himself is strong. One who knows what is enough is rich. One who acts with determination has will. One who does not lose his place endures. One who dies but does not perish is long-lived."

Structure and key words

  • Four pairs, then four single lines. The chapter opens with two balanced contrasts (others vs. self) and then gives four short sayings. It is one of the most quoted chapters because it reads like a set of proverbs.
  • 智 vs. 明. 智 (zhì) is cleverness turned outward — reading people, calculating. 明 (míng) is "clarity, illumination", a word the Daodejing uses for deep insight: 知常曰明 ("knowing the constant is called clarity", Ch. 16) and 見小曰明 (Ch. 52). Knowing yourself ranks higher than knowing others. Wang Bi comments that knowing others is merely cleverness, which is not as good as knowing oneself, which goes beyond cleverness.
  • 有力 vs. 強. Beating others shows force (力); beating yourself — your desires and habits — is true strength (強). This sits uneasily with other chapters where 強 is negative (強梁者不得其死, Ch. 42; 堅強者死之徒, Ch. 76), so commentators read 自勝者強 as a special, inward kind of strength, close to 守柔曰強 ("holding to softness is called strength", Ch. 52).
  • 知足者富. Wealth defined as knowing when you have enough — a theme repeated in Ch. 44 (知足不辱) and Ch. 46 (禍莫大於不知足).
  • 強行者有志. Read two ways: positively ("one who perseveres has will") or with Daoist irony ("one who forces things merely has ambition"). Most modern translators take the positive reading because the line sits among positive sayings, but the ironic reading fits the Daodejing's general suspicion of forcing. Both are defensible.
  • 不失其所者久. "Not losing one's place" — staying rooted in what one is, like the root in Ch. 16 (歸根曰靜).
  • 死而不亡者壽 — a textual crux. The Mawangdui silk manuscripts are commonly cited as reading 忘 ("forgotten") instead of 亡 ("perish"): "one who dies but is not forgotten is long-lived" — a much more Confucian idea of fame after death (cf. the "three imperishables" 三不朽 in the Zuozhuan). With 亡, the line points to the Dao itself, which does not perish. Heshang Gong reads it as a matter of self-cultivation and long life. I am reporting the variant as it is commonly cited; I did not check the manuscript images.

Historical and philosophical background

The chapter echoes the Greek maxim "Know thyself" (γνῶθι σεαυτόν) at Delphi — a parallel often drawn by comparative philosophers, though there is no historical link. Within Chinese thought, it contrasts with Confucian learning, which focuses on knowing others and rites (知人 is a Confucian virtue in Analects 12.22: 樊遲問知,子曰「知人」). The Daodejing does not reject knowing others but places it below self-knowledge.

Modern reflection

In a workplace, "knowing others" is reading the room, negotiating, managing up. "Knowing yourself" is knowing when you are tired, when you are reacting from ego, and what you actually want. Market days like this week test 自勝: the hardest opponent is your own urge to chase a rebound or panic-sell. And 知足者富 is a practical rule: deciding in advance what "enough" means keeps you from being driven by every headline.

結構與關鍵字

  • 前面兩組對比,後面四句短語。本章先用兩組平衡的對比(別人 vs. 自己),再接四句短句。因為讀起來像一組格言,是最常被引用的章節之一。
  • 智 vs. 明。「智」是向外的聰明——看懂人、會算計。「明」是「清明、照見」,是《道德經》用來指深刻洞察的字:「知常曰明」(第 16 章)、「見小曰明」(第 52 章)。認識自己高於認識別人。王弼注的意思是:了解別人只是聰明而已,不如了解自己,那已超越了聰明。
  • 有力 vs. 強。打敗別人顯示的是力氣(力);打敗自己——自己的慾望與習慣——才是真正的強(強)。這和其他章節把「強」當負面的用法有張力(「強梁者不得其死」第 42 章、「堅強者死之徒」第 76 章),所以注家把「自勝者強」讀成一種特殊、向內的強,接近「守柔曰強」(第 52 章)。
  • 知足者富。把財富定義成「知道夠了」——第 44 章(知足不辱)、第 46 章(禍莫大於不知足)都重複這個主題。
  • 強行者有志。有兩種讀法:正面(「堅持力行的人有志氣」),或帶道家式的反諷(「勉強去做的人只是有野心」)。多數現代譯者採正面讀法,因為這句夾在一串正面格言中間;但反諷讀法也符合《道德經》對「強為」的普遍懷疑。兩者都說得通。
  • 不失其所者久。「不失其所」——守住自己的根本,就像第 16 章的「歸根曰靜」。
  • 死而不亡者壽——文本上的關鍵爭議。一般引述馬王堆帛書本作「忘」而不是「亡」:「死而不忘者壽」——死後仍不被遺忘才是長壽,這更接近儒家「死後留名」的想法(參見《左傳》的「三不朽」)。若作「亡」,則指向不會消亡的道本身。河上公則從修養、長生的角度解讀。這裡是依常見引述報告此異文,我沒有親自核對帛書圖版。

歷史與哲學背景

本章常被比較哲學學者拿來對照希臘德爾菲神廟的格言「認識你自己」(γνῶθι σεαυτόν)——兩者並無歷史關聯。在中國思想內部,它和儒家的學習取向形成對照:儒家重視認識他人與禮(《論語·顏淵》12.22:「樊遲問知,子曰:『知人。』」)。《道德經》並不否定認識別人,而是把它放在認識自己之下。

現代反思

在職場上,「知人」是察言觀色、談判、向上管理;「自知」是知道自己什麼時候累了、什麼時候是出於自尊在反應、自己真正想要什麼。這週的股市就是在考驗「自勝」:最難對付的對手,是自己想追反彈或恐慌殺出的衝動。而「知足者富」是很實用的規則:事先想清楚什麼叫「夠了」,就不會被每一則新聞牽著走。

History

  • Settled by Polynesians, probably around 2,000 years ago or later (dates are debated). European ships passed from the 16th century; Peruvian "blackbirding" slave raids in 1863 took hundreds of people and devastated some islands.
  • Became part of the British Gilbert and Ellice Islands protectorate (1892), later colony. In a 1974 referendum, the mostly Polynesian Ellice Islanders voted to separate from the Micronesian Gilbertese; Tuvalu became a separate colony in 1975/76 and independent on 1 October 1978.
  • In the Second World War, US forces built airfields on Funafuti, Nanumea and Nukufetau for the campaign in the Gilbert Islands (Tarawa, 1943). The Funafuti runway is still the country's airport.
  • Taiwan: Tuvalu has recognised the Republic of China (Taiwan) since 1979 and remains one of its Pacific allies; Taiwan funds scholarships, medical and agriculture projects.

International relations and current developments

  • Climate change is the defining issue. Most land is less than 2–3 m above sea level, and king tides already flood parts of Funafuti. In 2021, Foreign Minister Simon Kofe recorded a COP26 speech standing knee-deep in the sea. Tuvalu has started land reclamation on Funafuti (the Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project) and a "digital nation" plan to preserve its culture and statehood online.
  • Falepili Union with Australia: a treaty signed in November 2023 and in force since 28 August 2024. Australia commits to help Tuvalu respond to natural disasters, health pandemics and military aggression, and offers a special migration pathway — up to 280 Tuvaluans per year, chosen by ballot. The 2026–27 ballot ran from 1 May to 1 June 2026. In return, Tuvalu must agree with Australia on security partnerships with other countries — a clause some Tuvaluans criticised as limiting sovereignty. Media often call it a "climate visa"; that is a nickname, not the official name.
  • Statehood: Tuvalu has amended its constitution to say its statehood and maritime boundaries remain even if the land is lost — part of a wider Pacific effort to keep sea zones fixed.

Culture

  • Fatele: a fast group dance with singing and wooden-box drumming that speeds up until it ends with a shout; performed at community celebrations.
  • Maneapa: the island meeting house where the falekaupule (traditional assembly of elders) decides local matters.
  • Traditional navigation, outrigger canoes, pandanus and coconut weaving, and pulaka (swamp taro) grown in pits dug into the coral — now threatened by saltwater.
  • The Funafuti airstrip doubles as a public space: when there is no flight, people play football, volleyball and ride scooters on it.

第 5 段 — 網路迷因:「Hello there」/「General Kenobi!」

ENEnglish version

  • Name and variations: "Hello there"; the call-and-response "Hello there!" — "General Kenobi!"; "You are a bold one"; image macros of Obi-Wan dropping in with a smile; spelled-out versions such as "Hello there 🙂".
  • Origin: Star Wars: Episode III – Revenge of the Sith (dir. George Lucas, US release 19 May 2005). On the planet Utapau, Obi-Wan Kenobi (Ewan McGregor) jumps down from a high walkway into a hangar full of droids, lands in front of the cyborg commander General Grievous and says calmly, "Hello there." Grievous answers, "General Kenobi! You are a bold one," and orders his droids to attack.
  • How it spread: according to Know Your Meme and the Daily Dot, the GIF circulated on Tumblr around 2014, moved to Imgur and then became a signature joke of the subreddit r/PrequelMemes (from 2016). That subreddit turned the once-mocked prequel trilogy's dialogue into an ironic, affectionate language of its own — "I don't like sand", "It's over Anakin, I have the high ground", "This is where the fun begins" — and "Hello there" became its greeting. TikTok users began re-enacting the scene around late 2019.
  • Why it works: the contrast is funny — a polite, almost British-butler greeting delivered in the middle of an army of killer robots. It is also a call-and-response: if you say "Hello there" in a fan space, someone is expected to reply "General Kenobi!" Getting the answer proves you are "in the know", which builds community.
  • Meaning and usage: a playful greeting, especially when you appear suddenly in a chat, enter a meeting late, or reply to a long-silent thread. It can also signal "I'm a Star Wars fan".
  • Status: peak popularity was roughly 2017–2020, helped by the 2017 announcement of a Kenobi series. In a May 2022 interview promoting the Disney+ series Obi-Wan Kenobi, McGregor said he was aware of the meme. Today it is an evergreen classic, widely understood by English-speaking internet users, but not currently trending.
  • Example: a colleague who has been silent for a week posts in the team chat: "Hello there 👋". Another replies: "General Kenobi!" — and everyone knows it is a friendly joke.
  • Cultural note for Taiwanese readers: the literal meaning (「哈囉」) is not the joke. The humour comes from the specific scene and the expected reply. Outside fan circles, "Hello there" is also normal, friendly English (often to a child or a stranger), so not every "Hello there" is a meme.

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 10: to / toward(s) / from (Stage 3, direction and movement).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese 往、到、去、向、從 do not map one-to-one onto English. Learners say "go to home", "arrive to Taipei", or use "toward" when they mean they actually reached a place.
  • Core concept — an arrow with or without a target: to = an arrow that reaches its destination (I went to Tainan = I got there). toward(s) = an arrow that only points in a direction; you may not reach it (We walked toward the station = in that direction). from = the arrow's starting point (The train from Hualien). "Toward" is more common in American English, "towards" in British English; both are correct.
  • Comparison: "The typhoon is moving toward Taiwan" (direction, may change course) vs. "The typhoon moved to Taiwan" (unusual — sounds like it arrived and stayed). "She ran to me" (she reached me) vs. "She ran toward me" (in my direction; maybe she stopped). "From Taipei to Kaohsiung" = start and end points.
  • Examples: I take the train to work. / The HSR goes from Nangang to Zuoying. / Walk towards the mountain and you'll see the trail. / I'm from Taichung, but I live in Taipei. / We're heading toward the coast. / She's always kind to her neighbours.
  • Common mistakes: (1) "go to home" → go home (home, here, there, downtown, abroad, upstairs are adverbs — no "to"). (2) "arrive to Taipei" → arrive in Taipei (city/country) / arrive at the station (point); "get to" and "reach" (no preposition) are also natural. (3) "I come from Taipei yesterday" when you mean you travelled → I came back from Taipei yesterday / I got back from Taipei; "I'm from Taipei" = my hometown.
  • Quiz: 5 questions in the Chinese block (questions in English). Answers will be given after you reply.
  • Key takeaways: to = reach the target; toward(s) = point in a direction; from = starting point. No "to" before home/here/there. Arrive in/at, not arrive to.

Content

May is trying to sell an empty luxury apartment in Taipei. Hsiao-kang steals a key from the door and secretly uses the flat — at one point he attempts suicide in the bathtub. May brings Ah-jung there for a one-night stand, and he also starts using the flat secretly. The three move through the same rooms, often unaware of each other; there is very little dialogue. The film ends with one of the most famous shots in Taiwanese cinema: at dawn, May walks alone through the newly built, still unfinished Daan Forest Park (大安森林公園), sits on a bench, lights a cigarette and cries — a single long take of several minutes (sources give roughly six to seven minutes for the closing sequence) with no music and no cut away.

Artistic style

  • Long, static takes and minimal dialogue. Meaning comes from bodies, silence, waiting and small actions (eating, washing, hiding under a bed) rather than from plot or speech.
  • No score. The soundscape is traffic, air-conditioners, running water — the sound of the city pressing into private space.
  • Empty urban space. The unsold apartment, the half-built park and the columbarium (homes for the dead, sold like flats) form a coherent image of a booming 1990s Taipei where property is plentiful but intimacy is scarce.
  • Hsiao-kang as recurring figure. Lee Kang-sheng plays a character called Hsiao-kang in almost all of Tsai's films, so his films read like chapters of one long portrait — comparable to Truffaut's Antoine Doinel cycle (introduced in this briefing on 23 November).

Cultural and historical significance

Vive L'Amour is a key work of what critics call the second wave of Taiwan New Cinema, after Hou Hsiao-hsien and Edward Yang (both introduced earlier in this series). Where the first wave often looked at history and family, Tsai turned to urban loneliness, the body and desire, including queer desire, at a time when Taiwan had just lifted martial law (1987) and was becoming rich and democratic. The ironic title — "long live love" in a film about people who cannot connect — is now part of Taiwanese cultural vocabulary. The ending is widely read as a release of everything the film has held back; that is an interpretation, but a very common one.

Influence and legacy

  • Established Tsai's international reputation; later films include The River (河流, 1997), The Hole (洞, 1998), What Time Is It There? (你那邊幾點, 2001), Goodbye, Dragon Inn (不散, 2003), Stray Dogs (郊遊, 2013, Grand Jury Prize at Venice) and the slow-walking Walker series with Lee Kang-sheng as a monk.
  • Influenced "slow cinema" across Asia and Europe, and helped make the long take a festival-world signature for Taiwanese film.
  • Daan Forest Park, which opened in 1994 and appears unfinished in the film, has since become one of Taipei's most loved green spaces — the ending is now a piece of the city's own history.

Where to experience it today

A restored version was selected for Venice Classics in 2025. The Taipei Times reported a New Year's Eve gathering and screening in Daan Forest Park in which fans, Tsai, Yang Kuei-mei and Lee Kang-sheng took part. The film appears in repertory programmes and on specialist streaming services such as the Criterion Channel; availability varies by region.

ENEnglish version

1. Today's analytical method

Added value and synchresis come from the French composer and theorist Michel Chion, in L'Audio-vision (1990; English translation Audio-Vision: Sound on Screen, Columbia University Press, 1994). Added value is the expressive and informative value that sound gives to an image — so naturally that we believe the meaning was already "in" the image. Synchresis (synchronism + synthesis) is the spontaneous, irresistible mental fusion of a sound and an image that occur at exactly the same moment, even when the sound has nothing to do with the real object. Together they describe what Chion calls the audiovisual contract: we agree, without noticing, to treat sound and image as one thing.

Why it matters: most viewers think they "watch" a film and the sound "accompanies" it. Chion argues the opposite: sound changes what we see — how fast, how heavy, how painful, how real.

2. Detailed explanation — how to identify and apply it

  • The mute test. Watch a short scene with the sound off, then on. Write down what changed: speed, weight, emotion, where you looked, what you thought happened. Whatever changed is the added value.
  • Ask what the sound "is" vs. what it "says". A punch in a film is almost never the sound of a real punch (which is quiet and dull). It is a designed sound that tells you how hard the hit felt. Synchresis makes you accept it as the punch.
  • Look for points of synchronisation. Chion's term for strong moments where a sound and an image hit together (an impact, a door slam, a cut on a beat). They act like punctuation; notice where a film places them and where it avoids them.
  • Notice temporal shaping. Sound can make an image feel faster or slower, continuous or broken. A steady sound across a series of cuts makes them feel like one moment; a sudden silence makes time stretch.
  • Common misconceptions: (1) "Good sound is realistic sound" — film sound is usually exaggerated or invented, and audiences accept it as more real than real recordings. (2) "Sound only adds emotion" — it also adds information: material, weight, distance, off-screen space. (3) "Added value is the same as music" — it covers all sound, including effects, voices and silence.

3. Concrete film example — Raging Bull (1980, dir. Martin Scorsese)

Credits relevant here: sound effects by Frank Warner; editing by Thelma Schoonmaker (who won the Academy Award for it); Robert De Niro as Jake LaMotta (Best Actor). Scene: the final fight against Sugar Ray Robinson (staged as the 14 February 1951 bout), in black-and-white.

  1. What to look for (image): slow motion, haze in the ring, Robinson raising his arm before the final barrage, LaMotta leaning on the ropes, blood spraying onto spectators, flashbulbs going off.
  2. What to listen for (sound): punches that sound far heavier than any real glove hitting a body; flashbulbs that pop like small explosions or gunshots; strange non-human textures under the blows. Reports on the production say Warner built these from sources such as smashed melons and tomatoes, rifle shots and animal sounds (elephants, horses), sometimes slowed down or reversed, and that he destroyed the recordings afterwards so they would not be reused. (Accounts differ on the exact materials and on his reason.)
  3. The key moment: before Robinson's final flurry, the crowd and the arena noise seem to drop away and the image slows — then the blows land with exaggerated, almost animal force. Observation: the sound changes sharply at that point. Interpretation: the near-silence feels like the moment before an execution, and the violent sound makes each hit feel like punishment rather than sport.
  4. Why it works (synchresis): we never doubt that those sounds "are" the punches, even though they are melons and gunshots. Because image and sound strike at the same frame, the brain fuses them, and the designed sound defines what the punch is.
  5. Added value: try the mute test. Without sound, the images are stylised and almost beautiful — slow motion, smoke, light. With sound, they become brutal. The meaning "this is a man being destroyed, and perhaps wanting it" comes largely from sound, yet we feel it is "in" the picture.
  6. Deeper reading: many critics read the film as being about LaMotta's self-destructiveness and guilt. The animal sounds support the title — the "raging bull" — and the flashbulbs-as-gunshots link the press and the crowd's gaze to violence. These are interpretations built on observable sound choices.

4. Practical viewing exercise

Pick any fight, chase or horror scene you know. Watch 60 seconds muted and write three adjectives. Watch again with sound and write three more. Then answer: (a) Which sounds are probably not "real" recordings of what you see? (b) Where are the strongest points of synchronisation? (c) Is there a moment where sound drops out — and what does that do to time?

5. Key takeaways

  • Sound does not decorate the image; it changes what we believe we see (added value).
  • When a sound and an image happen at the same moment, we fuse them automatically, even if the sound has a different source (synchresis).
  • Use the mute test to separate what the image shows from what the sound adds; label observations vs. interpretations.

6. Suggested further viewing

Monsieur Hulot's Holiday (Les Vacances de M. Hulot, 1953, dir. Jacques Tati) — listen to the hotel dining-room door that makes a distinctive twanging sound every time it swings. Chion wrote about Tati's sound; the film shows synchresis used for comedy instead of violence.

中繁體中文版

1. 今日分析方法

附加價值(added value)與同步融合(synchresis)這兩個概念,來自法國作曲家與理論家米歇爾·希翁(Michel Chion)的《L'Audio-vision》(1990;英譯本《Audio-Vision: Sound on Screen》,哥倫比亞大學出版社,1994)。附加價值是指聲音賦予影像的表達與資訊價值——它自然到讓我們以為那個意義本來就「在」影像裡。同步融合(synchronism 同步 + synthesis 合成)是指:一個聲音和一個影像在同一瞬間出現時,大腦會自發、無法抗拒地把它們融合在一起,即使那個聲音跟畫面上的真實物體毫無關係。兩者合起來,就是希翁所說的視聽契約:我們不知不覺同意把聲音和影像當成同一件事。

為什麼重要:大多數觀眾以為自己在「看」電影,聲音只是「伴奏」。希翁主張正好相反:聲音會改變我們看到的東西——快慢、輕重、痛不痛、真不真實。

2. 詳細說明——怎麼辨識與應用

  • 靜音測試。把一小段場景先關掉聲音看,再打開聲音看。寫下改變了什麼:速度、重量、情緒、你看哪裡、你以為發生了什麼。改變的部分,就是附加價值。
  • 問:這個聲音「是什麼」vs.「說了什麼」。電影裡的拳頭聲幾乎從來不是真的打拳聲(真的很小聲、很悶)。那是設計出來的聲音,告訴你這一拳「感覺」有多重。同步融合讓你接受它就是那一拳。
  • 找出同步點(points of synchronisation)。這是希翁的用語,指聲音和影像一起「打下去」的強烈時刻(撞擊、甩門、剪接點落在節拍上)。它們像標點符號;留意電影把它們放在哪裡、又刻意避開哪裡。
  • 留意時間的塑形。聲音可以讓影像感覺更快或更慢、連續或斷裂。一段持續的聲音跨過好幾個剪接,會讓它們感覺是同一個瞬間;突然的安靜則會讓時間被拉長。
  • 常見誤解:(1)「好的聲音就是寫實的聲音」——電影聲音通常是誇張或創造出來的,觀眾反而覺得它比真實錄音更「真」。(2)「聲音只增加情緒」——它也增加資訊:材質、重量、距離、畫外空間。(3)「附加價值就是配樂」——它涵蓋所有聲音,包括音效、人聲與靜默。

3. 具體電影範例——《蠻牛》(Raging Bull,1980,馬丁·史柯西斯導演)

相關幕後:音效由法蘭克·華納(Frank Warner)負責;剪接是瑟瑪·斯昆梅克(Thelma Schoonmaker,以本片獲奧斯卡最佳剪輯);勞勃·狄尼洛飾演傑克·拉莫塔(獲最佳男主角)。場景:與舒格·雷·羅賓森的最後一戰(重現 1951 年 2 月 14 日那場比賽),黑白畫面。

  1. 看什麼(影像):慢動作、擂台上的煙霧、羅賓森在最後一輪猛攻前舉起手臂、拉莫塔靠在圍繩上、血噴到觀眾身上、閃光燈此起彼落。
  2. 聽什麼(聲音):拳頭聲比任何真實拳套打在身上都重得多;閃光燈像小型爆炸或槍聲一樣「砰」地響;拳聲底下有奇怪、不像人類的質感。關於製作的報導指出,華納用砸爛的西瓜和番茄、步槍槍聲、動物聲(大象、馬)等素材組合出這些聲音,有時放慢或倒放,而且事後把錄音毀掉,不讓它們被重複使用。(各來源對確切素材和他的理由說法不一。)
  3. 關鍵時刻:在羅賓森最後一輪連續猛攻之前,觀眾和場館的聲音彷彿突然消失,畫面變慢——接著拳頭以誇張、近乎野獸般的力道落下。觀察:聲音在那個點出現劇烈變化。詮釋:那片近乎靜默的時刻像是行刑前的一瞬,暴烈的聲音讓每一拳感覺像懲罰而不是運動。
  4. 為什麼有效(同步融合):我們從來不會懷疑那些聲音「就是」拳頭聲,儘管它們其實是西瓜和槍聲。因為影像和聲音在同一格畫面打下去,大腦就把它們融合,設計出來的聲音決定了那一拳「是什麼」。
  5. 附加價值:做一次靜音測試。沒有聲音時,畫面是風格化的,甚至有點美——慢動作、煙霧、光線。有了聲音,畫面變得殘暴。「這是一個人正在被摧毀,甚至可能是他自己想要的」這層意義,大部分來自聲音,但我們卻覺得它「在」畫面裡。
  6. 更深的解讀:許多評論者把這部片讀成拉莫塔的自我毀滅與罪惡感。動物聲呼應片名「蠻牛」,閃光燈像槍聲,則把媒體與觀眾的注視和暴力連在一起。這些是建立在可觀察的聲音選擇上的詮釋。

4. 實作練習

挑一段你熟悉的打鬥、追逐或恐怖場景。先靜音看 60 秒,寫下三個形容詞;再開聲音看一次,再寫三個。然後回答:(a)哪些聲音大概不是畫面中東西的「真實」錄音?(b)最強的同步點在哪裡?(c)有沒有聲音突然消失的時刻——它對時間感造成什麼影響?

5. 重點整理

  • 聲音不是在裝飾影像;它改變我們以為自己看到的東西(附加價值)。
  • 聲音和影像同時出現時,我們會自動把它們融合,即使聲音來源完全不同(同步融合)。
  • 用靜音測試區分「影像呈現了什麼」和「聲音加了什麼」;並標明哪些是觀察、哪些是詮釋。

6. 延伸觀看

《胡洛先生的假期》(Les Vacances de M. Hulot,1953,賈克·大地導演)——注意旅館餐廳那扇每次擺動都發出獨特「咚~」彈響的門。希翁寫過關於大地電影聲音的專論;這部片示範同步融合可以用在喜劇,而不是暴力。
