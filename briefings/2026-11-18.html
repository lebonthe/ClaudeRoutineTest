Topic: four ways to say "change" — cambiar, cambiar de, cambiarse and mudarse.

🎯 Learning Goal: say that something changes or that you change it (La ciudad ha cambiado mucho), switch one thing for another of the same kind (cambiar de trabajo), change clothes (cambiarse de ropa), move house (mudarse), and exchange money or a purchase (cambiar dinero, cambiar una camiseta).

Vocabulary: cambiar (to change; to exchange) · el cambio (change; small change/coins; exchange rate) · cambiar de trabajo / de casa / de idea / de opinión (change job / home / mind / opinion) · cambiarse (de ropa) (to get changed) · mudarse (to move house) · la mudanza (the house move) · el piso / el apartamento (flat) · cambiar dinero (exchange money) · cambiar algo por algo (swap A for B) · el tique / el recibo (receipt) · la talla (size).

Grammar Point: (1) cambiar + direct object = you change something, transforming it or replacing it with another: Cambié la contraseña (I changed the password). Without an object, the subject itself changes: Tú no has cambiado nada (You haven't changed at all). (2) cambiar de + noun with NO article = switch to another one of the same category: cambiar de trabajo, de móvil, de tema, de idea. Compare: Cambié el móvil (I changed/replaced the phone — focus on the object) vs. Cambié de móvil (I switched to a different phone — focus on switching). Never say cambiar de mi trabajo. (3) cambiarse (reflexive) = change yourself: get changed (Voy a cambiarme), or cambiarse de ropa / de zapatos. (4) mudarse = move to a new home (reflexive): Nos mudamos a Taichung en marzo. Mexico and Spain both use it; cambiarse de casa is also common. Don't use moverse for moving house — it means "to move (your body)". (5) cambiar A por B = exchange A for B: ¿Puedo cambiar esta camiseta por una talla más grande? Preterite: regular -ar (cambié, cambiaste, cambió; me mudé, se mudó).

Example Sentences:

  • Me mudé a Taipéi hace tres años y el barrio ha cambiado mucho. — I moved to Taipei three years ago, and the neighbourhood has changed a lot.
  • Mi hermano quiere cambiar de trabajo porque gana poco. — My brother wants to change jobs because he earns little.
  • Espera, me cambio de ropa y salimos. — Wait, I'll get changed and we'll go out.
  • ¿Puedo cambiar estos zapatos por un número más pequeño? Aquí tiene el tique. — Can I exchange these shoes for a smaller size? Here's the receipt.
  • Iba a ir en tren, pero cambié de idea. — I was going to take the train, but I changed my mind.
  • ¿Dónde puedo cambiar dólares a euros? — Where can I change dollars into euros?

Chapter 26 — 重為輕根 (Zhòng wéi qīng gēn), "Heaviness Is the Root of Lightness"

Original text (Wang Bi received version): 重為輕根,靜為躁君。是以聖人終日行不離輜重。雖有榮觀,燕處超然。奈何萬乘之主,而以身輕天下?輕則失本,躁則失君。

Translation: The heavy is the root of the light; stillness is the master of restlessness. Therefore the sage travels all day without leaving the baggage wagons. Though there are splendid sights, he stays at ease, calm and detached. How can the lord of ten thousand chariots treat himself lightly before the world? Be light and you lose the root; be restless and you lose the master.

Key terms and interpretations:

  • 重 / 輕 and 靜 / 躁: two pairs of opposites, as in Chapter 2, but here they are not equal: the heavy grounds the light, and stillness governs agitation. A tree's root is unseen and heavy; leaves are light and move with every wind.
  • 輜重 (zīzhòng): the covered supply wagons of an army or travelling party. A commander who rides ahead of his supplies is exposed. Wang Bi's reading: the heavy and the still are the "root" that keeps the light and the moving from being lost. The Mawangdui silk manuscripts read 君子 ("the noble person") instead of 聖人 ("the sage").
  • 榮觀 / 燕處超然: 榮觀 can mean splendid sights or a magnificent palace-tower; 燕處 means resting at ease (燕 = 宴, leisure). Even surrounded by glamour, the grounded person is not carried away.
  • 萬乘之主: a ruler of a state able to field ten thousand chariots — a great power in the Warring States period. "以身輕天下" is read in two ways: he treats his own person lightly (recklessness) in governing the world, or he values the world less than his own pleasures. Both point to a ruler who has lost weight and composure.
  • 輕則失本 / 失臣: Wang Bi has 失本 ("lose the root"); some editions, including the Heshang Gong text, read 失臣 ("lose one's ministers"), making it a warning about political support.

Historical and philosophical background: the chapter speaks the language of Warring States statecraft — chariots, armies, baggage trains — and turns it into a principle of character. Han Feizi's "Explaining Laozi" (解老) and "Illustrating Laozi" (喻老) chapters apply it to rulers who lost their states through rash moves. It continues Chapter 16's stillness (歸根曰靜) and follows Chapter 25's grand cosmology with a practical, grounded lesson.

Philosophical implications: lightness and speed are not bad in themselves, but they need an anchor. Without a "heavy" centre — values, routines, resources held in reserve — quick reactions become drift. Leadership in particular depends on composure: a restless leader loses the authority (君) that stillness would have given.

Modern reflection: on a day like Tuesday — outage headlines, a fourth market decline, a big earnings release after the bell — the "baggage wagons" are your plan, your cash buffer, your tested backups. A developer who ships every hot new framework without tests, or an investor who trades every headline, is travelling ahead of the supply train. Ask: what is my 輜重 today, and am I still walking beside it?

關鍵字與傳統詮釋:

  • 重/輕、靜/躁:兩組對立概念,和第 2 章一樣,但這裡兩端並不對等:重的是輕的基礎,靜能統御躁。樹根看不見、又沉又重;葉子輕盈,隨風搖擺。
  • 輜重(zīzhòng):軍隊或旅行隊伍中載運糧草物資、有篷的車。指揮官若騎馬跑在補給車隊前面,就會暴露在危險中。王弼的解讀:重與靜是「根」,讓輕與動不會迷失。馬王堆帛書本寫作「君子」而不是「聖人」。

  • 萬乘之主:能出動一萬輛兵車的國君——戰國時代的大國。「以身輕天下」有兩種讀法:以輕率的態度對待自己來治理天下,或是把天下看得比自己的享樂還輕。兩者都指向一個失去穩重與沉著的君主。

哲學意涵:輕快與速度本身不是壞事,但需要錨。沒有「重」的核心——價值觀、生活紀律、保留的資源——快速反應就會變成隨波逐流。領導尤其仰賴沉著:一個浮躁的領導者,會失去原本靠「靜」得來的權威(君)。

現代省思:像週二這樣的日子——當機新聞、股市連四跌、收盤後的重量級財報——你的「輜重」就是你的計畫、你的現金緩衝、你測試過的備份。一個不寫測試就搶用每個熱門新框架的開發者,或是每則新聞都要進出的投資人,就是跑在補給車隊的前面。問問自己:今天我的輜重是什麼?我還走在它旁邊嗎?

Monarchy under King Zog (1928–1939), Italian invasion (1939), then one of Europe's harshest communist regimes under Enver Hoxha (1944–1985): it broke with Yugoslavia (1948), the USSR (1961) and China (1978), and built hundreds of thousands of concrete bunkers (estimates around 170,000). Communism fell in 1990–1991; the collapse of pyramid investment schemes caused near-civil-war in 1997. Albania joined NATO in 2009.

Strongly pro-US; close to Kosovo; a 2023 migration agreement lets Italy run asylum centres on Albanian soil (Shëngjin and Gjadër).

Mother Teresa was an ethnic Albanian; Tirana's airport is named after her.
  • Current developments and challenges: finishing EU accession (target around 2030), stopping population decline, rule-of-law reform, and managing a tourism boom that is pushing up housing costs along the coast.

Unit 1 — Preposition Lesson 3: inside / outside vs. in / out (of) (Stage 1, basic spatial concepts).

  • Today's topic and the usual confusion: Chinese says 在……裡面 / 在……外面 for everything, so learners either overuse "inside" ("I'm inside Taipei") or forget "of" ("He went out the room" in formal writing, "outside of" vs. "outside").
  • Core concept: in is the neutral, everyday word for being within a space (Lesson 1: in = container/area). inside adds focus on the boundary: walls, a lid, a door — "within the walls, not outside them". It is most natural with buildings, rooms, boxes, cars and bodies, and less natural with big areas like cities or countries. outside = beyond the boundary, often "just outside", near the wall or door. out of = movement from inside to outside (He walked out of the room), or "no longer having" (We're out of milk).
  • Examples: Please take your shoes off before you go inside. / It's 35 degrees outside, but it's cool inside the MRT station. / I left my umbrella in the car. / Keep the scooter keys inside your bag, not in the outside pocket. / She ran out of the house when the earthquake started. / We live just outside Taichung. / We're out of rice.

(2) "He walked out the room" → out of the room in standard English (US speech sometimes drops "of" with doors/windows: "look out the window" is fine). (3) "Wait me at outside" → Wait for me outside (outside is already an adverb; no "at").

Unit 1 — 介系詞第 3 課:inside / outside 與 in / out (of)

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:「裡面/外面」什麼時候用 in/out of,什麼時候用 inside/outside。中文一律說「在……裡面」「在……外面」,所以常見兩種錯:一是過度使用 inside(I'm inside Taipei),二是漏掉或亂加小字(wait me at outside、walk out the room)。

2. 核心概念

  • in = 預設的「在裡面」。第 1 課學過:in 是容器/區域。in the car, in Taipei, in my bag。日常大部分時候用 in 就對了。
  • inside = 強調「牆、蓋子、門」這個邊界。意思是「在圍起來的東西的內側,不在外面」。最適合建築物、房間、箱子、車子、身體;用在城市、國家這種大區域就不自然。想像一個有牆的盒子:你特別想說「在牆的這一邊」時才用 inside。
  • outside = 在邊界之外,常常是「就在外面、靠近牆或門」。Wait outside the door(在門外等)。它也可以當副詞單獨用:Let's eat outside.
  • out of = 從裡面「移動」到外面(walk out of the room),或「用完、沒有了」(We're out of milk)。

重點:in 和 inside 很多時候都可以,差別在強調。The kids are playing in the house(孩子在家裡玩,單純描述)vs. The kids are playing inside because it's raining(孩子待在室內玩,強調「不在外面」)。

第 7 段 — 電影與表演藝術:《相見恨晚》(Brief Encounter,1945)

ENEnglish version

  • Work and key figures: Brief Encounter (UK, 1945, black and white, about 86 minutes). Director: David Lean. Screenplay: Noël Coward, with Lean, Anthony Havelock-Allan and Ronald Neame, adapted from Coward's one-act play Still Life (1936, part of the cycle Tonight at 8.30). Producer: Coward's company Cineguild. Cinematography: Robert Krasker. Stars: Celia Johnson (Laura Jesson) and Trevor Howard (Dr Alec Harvey), with Cyril Raymond as Laura's husband Fred and Stanley Holloway and Joyce Carey as the station's ticket collector and refreshment-room manageress. Music: Rachmaninoff's Piano Concerto No. 2 (pianist Eileen Joyce, conducted by Muir Mathieson).
  • Period and background: made at the end of the Second World War and shot largely at Carnforth railway station in Lancashire in early 1945 — reportedly chosen because it was far enough from London to be lit at night with less risk under wartime restrictions. Britain was full of separated couples, rationing and moral restraint; the film's middle-class respectability reflects that world.
  • Content overview: Laura, a suburban housewife who goes to the town of Milford every Thursday to shop and see a film, gets grit in her eye at the station; Alec, a married doctor, removes it. Their chance meetings become a love affair that is never physically consummated. Guilt, fear of exposure and loyalty to their families end it: Alec takes a job in South Africa. The film opens with their final meeting — interrupted by Laura's chattering acquaintance Dolly — and then replays the story as Laura's silent confession to Fred, told in voice-over while he does a crossword.
  • Style and techniques: (1) a framed flashback with first-person voice-over, so we know the ending from the start and watch for how it happens; (2) repetition: the opening scene is shown twice, and the second time we understand the hand Alec places on Laura's shoulder; (3) expressive lighting and a tilted camera when Laura, in despair, runs to the platform and considers throwing herself in front of an express train; (4) sound: train whistles and rushing expresses as pressure and danger, and Rachmaninoff as the voice of feelings she cannot say aloud; (5) a comic counterpoint: the flirtation between the ticket collector and the café manageress is loud and free, the opposite of the lovers' whispers.
  • Significance (fact vs. interpretation): facts — it shared the Grand Prix at the first Cannes Film Festival (1946), received Oscar nominations for Best Director, Best Actress and Best Screenplay, and was voted second in the BFI's 1999 list of the best British films of the 20th century. Interpretation — critics see it as the definitive screen portrait of English emotional restraint; some later critics have read it as an encoded story of forbidden love more broadly, given Coward's own life, though this is a reading, not a documented intention.
  • Influence and legacy: it established Lean (later Lawrence of Arabia) as a major director; its station-café setting, voice-over confession and use of a classical piano concerto became shorthand for doomed romance (and are often parodied). Echoes appear in Wong Kar-wai's In the Mood for Love (2000) — another restrained, unconsummated affair between two married people, though there is no documented direct link. A TV remake starred Richard Burton and Sophia Loren (1974); Emma Rice's stage version with Kneehigh (2008) mixed theatre, film and music.
  • Where to experience it today: available on Blu-ray/DVD (Criterion in the US, a 4K restoration in the UK) and on some streaming services, depending on country. Carnforth Station Heritage Centre preserves the clock and a recreated refreshment room and is open to visitors.

  • 年代與背景:拍攝於二次世界大戰尾聲,主要在 1945 年初於蘭開夏郡的卡恩福斯(Carnforth)火車站取景——據說選在這裡,是因為離倫敦夠遠,在戰時燈火管制下夜間打燈的風險較低。當時英國到處是被戰爭分隔的夫妻、配給制度與道德上的自制;片中中產階級的體面,正反映了那個世界。
  • 內容概述:蘿拉是一位郊區家庭主婦,每週四到米爾福德鎮購物、看電影。某天她在車站眼睛跑進沙子,已婚的醫生亞歷克幫她取出。幾次偶遇演變成一段始終沒有發生肉體關係的戀情。罪惡感、怕被發現,以及對家庭的忠誠,讓這段感情結束:亞歷克接受了南非的工作。電影一開場就是他們最後一次見面——被蘿拉一位喋喋不休的熟人朵莉打斷——接著整段故事以蘿拉在心裡向丈夫弗雷德「無聲告白」的旁白重新播放,而弗雷德正在一旁玩填字遊戲。
  • 風格與技法:(1)框架式倒敘加上第一人稱旁白,我們一開始就知道結局,於是專注看「事情如何發生」;(2)重複:開場那一幕出現兩次,第二次我們才懂亞歷克放在蘿拉肩上的那隻手的意義;(3)表現性的打光與傾斜鏡頭:蘿拉絕望地跑到月台,想撲向疾駛而過的快車時;(4)聲音:火車汽笛與呼嘯而過的快車代表壓力與危險,拉赫曼尼諾夫則是她說不出口的感情之聲;(5)喜劇對位:剪票員和餐室女經理之間的調情又大聲又自在,和這對戀人的低聲細語形成強烈對比。
  • 重要性(事實與詮釋):事實——它在第一屆坎城影展(1946)共同獲得大獎,獲奧斯卡最佳導演、最佳女主角、最佳劇本提名,並在英國電影協會(BFI)1999 年「20 世紀最佳英國電影」票選中名列第二。詮釋——評論家視它為英國式情感壓抑最經典的銀幕寫照;也有後來的評論者,考量寇威爾本人的生平,把它解讀為更廣義的「禁忌之愛」的隱喻,但這是一種解讀,並非有文獻記載的創作意圖。
  • 影響與傳承:它奠定了大衛·連(後來拍《阿拉伯的勞倫斯》)的大導演地位;車站餐室、旁白告白和古典鋼琴協奏曲,成為「注定沒有結果的愛情」的代名詞(也常被模仿惡搞)。王家衛的《花樣年華》(2000)——同樣是兩位已婚者克制、未越界的情感——常被拿來相比,但兩者之間沒有文獻記載的直接關聯。1974 年有李察·波頓與蘇菲亞·羅蘭主演的電視重拍版;艾瑪·萊斯與 Kneehigh 劇團 2008 年的舞台版,則結合了劇場、電影與音樂。
  • 今日哪裡可以欣賞:有藍光/DVD(美國 Criterion 版、英國 4K 修復版),部分國家的串流平台也看得到,視地區而定。卡恩福斯車站文物中心保存了片中的時鐘和重建的車站餐室,開放參觀。

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 109: Postcolonial Criticism
第 8 段 — 看片方法第 109 課:後殖民批評

This continues the "critical lenses" strand (Lesson 102 ideological, 107 psychoanalytic, 108 structuralist). Not a repeat: earlier lessons did not cover colonialism, the colonised gaze or who gets to speak. 這延續「批評視角」系列(第 102 課意識形態、第 107 課精神分析、第 108 課結構主義);先前沒有教過殖民、被殖民者的觀看與「誰能發聲」。

ENEnglish version

1. Today's Analytical Method

Postcolonial criticism asks how a film represents the relationship between colonisers and the colonised — and its after-effects in the present: who is shown as the norm and who as "exotic"; who has a voice, a name, an inner life; whose land, labour and objects are taken; and how the colonised look back. It matters because cinema itself grew up during the age of empire, and many classic films see the non-Western world through Western eyes without saying so.

2. Detailed Explanation

  • Key thinkers (short): Frantz Fanon, Black Skin, White Masks (1952) — how colonised people are made to see themselves through the coloniser's eyes; Edward Said, Orientalism (1978) — how the West invents "the East" as exotic, irrational and timeless; Gayatri Spivak, "Can the Subaltern Speak?" (1988) — whether the most marginalised can ever be heard in the dominant language; Homi Bhabha — hybridity and mimicry. In film studies: Ella Shohat and Robert Stam, Unthinking Eurocentrism (1994); and the "Third Cinema" manifesto of Solanas and Getino (1969).
  • How to apply it — five questions: (1) Point of view: through whose eyes is the colonised place shown? Who narrates? (2) Voice and language: who speaks, in which language, and who is silent or subtitled — or not subtitled? (3) Space: who owns the space, who serves in it, who is confined to the kitchen or the margins of the frame? (4) Objects: what things (masks, art, food, land) move from one culture to the other, and do they become decoration? (5) Looking back: does the film let the colonised character return the gaze, resist, or tell the story?
  • What to watch for on screen: the "exotic establishing shot" (drums, markets, sunsets); unnamed local extras; a white protagonist who "discovers" or "saves"; the colonised character's interior life — is it shown (voice-over, close-ups) or only their usefulness?
  • Common misconceptions: it is not "only for films about Africa or India" — it applies to Westerns, adventure films, travel romances and science fiction (alien "natives"). It is not a verdict that a film is evil; a film can be beautiful and still Eurocentric. And it is not only about Western films: it also studies how formerly colonised filmmakers answer back.
  • Objective vs. subjective: objective — who has lines, names and screen time; which language is used; who is placed where in the frame. Interpretive — what the film "endorses" and whether an image is critique or repetition of stereotype.

3. Concrete Film Example — Black Girl (La Noire de…, 1966), dir. Ousmane Sembène

Senegal/France, black and white, about an hour long; often cited as the first feature film by a sub-Saharan African director to gain international recognition (Prix Jean Vigo, 1966). Diouana (Mbissine Thérèse Diop), a young Senegalese woman hired in Dakar as a nanny by a French couple, follows them to Antibes on the Riviera, where she is made a live-in maid, isolated in their flat.

  1. The voice-over (voice and language): Diouana barely speaks aloud to her employers; her thoughts are heard in voice-over. Look for: the gap between her rich inner voice and her silence in the room. Why it works: Sembène gives her the narration a colonial film would deny her — the subaltern does speak, but only the audience can hear her.
  2. The flat (space): Diouana is framed in the kitchen, behind doorways, at the edge of shots, while the couple sit at the table with guests. At a lunch, a guest kisses her "because he has never kissed a Black woman" — she is treated as an object of curiosity. Look for: where she stands while others sit; how often doors close her in.
  3. The mask (objects): in a flashback in Dakar, Diouana gives Madame a traditional mask as a gift. In Antibes it hangs on the wall among other African objects as décor. Near the end she takes it back; Madame struggles to keep it. Why it matters: the mask moves from a personal gift to a colonial "souvenir" and back — the fight over it is a fight over who owns African culture and Diouana's dignity. (Many critics connect it to Fanon's title — an interpretation.)
  4. The ending (looking back): after Diouana's death, Monsieur travels to Dakar to return her suitcase and wages to her mother, who refuses the money. A small boy puts on the mask and silently follows Monsieur through the streets; he walks faster and faster, unsettled. Interpretation: the mask now looks back at the coloniser; guilt cannot be paid off, and the colonised gaze follows him.

4. Practical Viewing Exercise

Choose Lawrence of Arabia (1962, featured in Section 7 earlier) or any adventure film set outside Europe. Watch one 10-minute scene and note: (a) who narrates or whose point of view we share; (b) which local characters have names and lines; (c) one object or landscape presented as "exotic". Then write two sentences: what the scene takes for granted, and how a filmmaker from that region might shoot the same moment differently.

5. Key Takeaways

  • Ask: whose eyes, whose voice, whose space, whose objects, and who looks back.
  • Count objectively first (names, lines, language, framing), then interpret.
  • Postcolonial cinema doesn't just complain — it answers back with new forms (voice-over, return of objects, the gaze reversed).

6. Suggested Further Viewing

Touki Bouki (1973, dir. Djibril Diop Mambéty, Senegal): two young Dakar lovers dream of escaping to Paris; watch how the film mixes the song "Paris, Paris" with images of Senegal to question the dream of the former colonial capital.

中繁體中文版

1. 今日分析方法

後殖民批評探討一部電影如何呈現殖民者與被殖民者之間的關係——以及這段關係在今日留下的影響:誰被當成「正常」、誰被當成「異國情調」;誰有聲音、有名字、有內心世界;誰的土地、勞動和物品被拿走;以及被殖民者如何「回看」。它之所以重要,是因為電影本身誕生於帝國時代,許多經典電影都不自覺地用西方的眼光去看非西方世界。

2. 詳細說明

  • 關鍵思想家(簡要):法農(Frantz Fanon)《黑皮膚,白面具》(1952)——被殖民者如何被迫透過殖民者的眼光看自己;薩依德(Edward Said)《東方主義》(1978)——西方如何把「東方」虛構成充滿異國情調、非理性、永恆不變的他者;史碧瓦克(Gayatri Spivak)〈底層人能說話嗎?〉(1988)——最邊緣的人能否在主流語言中被聽見;巴巴(Homi Bhabha)——混雜性與模仿。電影研究方面:修哈特與史坦(Ella Shohat & Robert Stam)《不思索的歐洲中心主義》(1994);以及索拉納斯與赫蒂諾的「第三電影」宣言(1969)。
  • 怎麼應用——五個問題:(1)觀點:被殖民的地方是透過誰的眼睛呈現的?誰在敘述?(2)聲音與語言:誰說話、用什麼語言?誰沉默,或被上字幕——甚至沒有字幕?(3)空間:誰擁有這個空間、誰在裡面服務?誰被侷限在廚房或畫面邊緣?(4)物品:哪些東西(面具、藝術品、食物、土地)從一個文化流到另一個文化?它們是否變成了裝飾品?(5)回看:電影是否讓被殖民的角色回望、反抗,或自己說故事?
  • 畫面上要注意什麼:「異國情調的定場鏡頭」(鼓聲、市集、夕陽);沒有名字的當地臨演;「發現」或「拯救」當地的白人主角;被殖民角色的內心世界——有被呈現(旁白、特寫)嗎?還是只呈現他們有什麼用處?
  • 常見誤解:它不是「只適用於非洲或印度題材的電影」——西部片、冒險片、異國戀愛片、科幻片(外星「原住民」)都可以用。它也不是判定一部電影邪惡;一部電影可以很美,同時仍是歐洲中心的。而且它不只研究西方電影,也研究曾被殖民地區的電影人如何「回嘴」。
  • 客觀與主觀:客觀——誰有台詞、名字和銀幕時間;用哪種語言;誰被放在畫面哪裡。詮釋——電影「認可」什麼,以及某個畫面是在批判刻板印象,還是在重複它。

3. 具體電影範例——《黑女孩》(La Noire de…,1966),導演烏斯曼·森貝內(Ousmane Sembène)

塞內加爾/法國,黑白片,約一小時;常被稱為第一部獲得國際肯定、由撒哈拉以南非洲導演拍攝的劇情長片(1966 年尚·維果獎)。迪奧娜(Mbissine Thérèse Diop 飾)是一位塞內加爾年輕女性,在達卡被一對法國夫婦雇為保母,跟著他們來到蔚藍海岸的安提布,卻被當成住家女傭,孤立在他們的公寓裡。

  1. 旁白(聲音與語言):迪奧娜幾乎不對雇主開口;她的想法是透過旁白讓我們聽見。注意:她豐富的內心聲音,和她在房間裡的沉默之間的落差。為什麼有效:森貝內把殖民電影不會給她的「敘述權」交給她——底層人確實在說話,只是只有觀眾聽得見。
  2. 公寓(空間):迪奧娜被框在廚房、門框後面、畫面邊緣,而夫婦和客人坐在餐桌旁。一次午餐時,一位客人親了她,「因為他從沒親過黑人女性」——她被當成好奇的對象。注意:別人坐著的時候她站在哪裡;門有多常把她關在裡面。
  3. 面具(物品):在達卡的倒敘中,迪奧娜送給夫人一個傳統面具當禮物。到了安提布,它和其他非洲物品一起掛在牆上當裝飾。接近結尾時,她把面具拿回來,夫人和她搶奪。為什麼重要:面具從個人的禮物變成殖民者的「紀念品」,又被拿回——這場搶奪,是在爭誰擁有非洲文化、誰擁有迪奧娜的尊嚴。(許多評論家把它和法農的書名連結——這是一種詮釋。)
  4. 結尾(回看):迪奧娜死後,先生到達卡把她的行李和工資還給她母親,母親拒收那筆錢。一個小男孩戴上面具,一言不發地跟著先生穿過街道;先生越走越快,心神不寧。詮釋:面具現在回望著殖民者;罪惡感無法用錢償清,被殖民者的目光一路跟著他。

4. 實作觀影練習

選《阿拉伯的勞倫斯》(1962,先前在第 7 段介紹過),或任何一部以歐洲以外地區為背景的冒險片。看其中一段 10 分鐘的場景並記下:(a)誰在敘述,或我們跟著誰的觀點;(b)哪些當地角色有名字和台詞;(c)一個被呈現為「異國情調」的物品或風景。然後寫兩句話:這場戲把什麼視為理所當然?如果是那個地區的電影人,可能會怎麼拍同一個時刻?

5. 重點整理

  • 問:誰的眼睛、誰的聲音、誰的空間、誰的物品,以及誰在回看。
  • 先客觀計數(名字、台詞、語言、構圖),再做詮釋。
  • 後殖民電影不只是抱怨——它用新的形式回應(旁白、物品的歸還、目光的反轉)。

6. 延伸觀賞

《土狼之旅》(Touki Bouki,1973,導演吉布里爾·迪奧普·曼貝提,塞內加爾):一對達卡的年輕戀人夢想逃到巴黎;注意電影如何把歌曲〈Paris, Paris〉和塞內加爾的畫面剪在一起,質疑「前殖民首都」這個夢。
