4) Daodejing (道德經) — Chapter 24: 企者不立 (Qǐ zhě bú lì) — "On Tiptoe You Cannot Stand"

  • Context: The I Ching and Zhuangzi sequences are used up, so this section continues through the Daodejing in order. The last entry (Nov 13) was Chapter 23.
  • Text (received text): 企者不立,跨者不行。自見者不明,自是者不彰,自伐者無功,自矜者不長。其在道也,曰:餘食贅形。物或惡之,故有道者不處。
  • Meaning: Whoever stands on tiptoe cannot stand firm; whoever takes huge strides cannot keep walking. Whoever shows himself off is not clearly seen; whoever insists he is right is not recognised; whoever boasts gets no credit; whoever is proud of himself does not last. From the point of view of the Way, these are "leftover food and a growth on the body." People find them distasteful, so whoever has the Way does not stay in them.
  • Key terms: 企: to raise oneself on the toes, straining to look taller. 跨: to straddle or overstride. The four 自 phrases (自見, 自是, 自伐, 自矜) repeat Chapter 22's four 不自 phrases in the negative: there, the sage does not show off and therefore shines; here, the person who does show off is not seen. 餘食贅形: surplus food and a superfluous growth (a wart or tumour): useless extras that the body would be better without.
  • Reading it: the opening image is physical. You can stand on tiptoe for a moment to look taller, and you can overstride for a few steps to go faster, but neither can be kept up; both end in losing your footing. Self-display works the same way: it costs effort and makes people turn away. The chapter follows the storm image of Chapter 23 (violent force does not last) and turns it toward personal behaviour. In the Mawangdui silk manuscripts this chapter is placed before Chapter 22, and the first character is written 炊 (read as 企), which makes the pairing with Chapter 22's 不自 list even clearer.

5) Country Spotlight — Cyprus (賽普勒斯)

  • Location: the third-largest island in the Mediterranean (after Sicily and Sardinia), in the far eastern corner of the sea, about 70 km south of Turkey, about 100 km west of Syria and about 380 km north of Egypt. Geographically it is in Asia, but politically and culturally it is European and a member of the EU.
  • Size: about 9,251 km² for the whole island, roughly a quarter of Taiwan (about 36,000 km²), almost exactly the size of Puerto Rico and a little smaller than Lebanon. The island is divided: the Republic of Cyprus controls about 59%; the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (recognised only by Turkey) about 36%; a UN buffer zone ("Green Line") about 3%; and two British Sovereign Base Areas, Akrotiri and Dhekelia, about 3%.
  • Population: about 1.4 million on the whole island: roughly 0.95-1 million in the Republic-controlled south and an estimated 380,000-480,000 in the north (including settlers from Turkey). Capital Nicosia (Lefkosia / Lefkoşa), the last divided capital in Europe.
  • Language and religion: official languages Greek and Turkish; English is very widely used, a legacy of British rule. Greek Cypriots are mostly Eastern Orthodox (the autocephalous Church of Cyprus); Turkish Cypriots are mostly Sunni Muslim, generally secular in practice. There are small Maronite, Armenian and Latin Catholic communities.
  • History: Mycenaean Greeks settled from about the 12th century BC; the island was later ruled by Assyrians, Egyptians, Persians, Ptolemies and Rome, then was part of the Byzantine Empire. In 1191 Richard the Lionheart seized it during the Third Crusade; the French Lusignan dynasty ruled a crusader kingdom for three centuries, followed by Venice (1489) and the Ottomans (1571). Britain took over administration in 1878 and made it a crown colony in 1925. After the Greek Cypriot EOKA armed campaign (1955-59), Cyprus became independent on 16 August 1960, with Archbishop Makarios III as president. Intercommunal violence in 1963-64 brought UN peacekeepers (UNFICYP, still present). In July 1974 a coup backed by the Athens military junta was followed by a Turkish invasion, which divided the island; about 200,000 people were displaced. The north declared independence in 1983. In the 2004 referendum on the UN's Annan Plan, Turkish Cypriots voted about 65% yes and Greek Cypriots about 76% no; a week later, on 1 May 2004, Cyprus joined the EU as a divided island. It adopted the euro in 2008, and in 2013 a banking crisis led to an EU bailout in which large bank deposits were "bailed in."
  • Economy: services make up most of the economy: tourism (about four million visitors a year, many from the UK), shipping (Limassol is one of the world's biggest ship-management centres), financial and professional services, and real estate. Offshore natural gas was found in 2011 (the Aphrodite field) and later in the Glaucus and Cronos fields. GDP per person is roughly on a par with southern EU members. Halloumi cheese has had EU protected status since 2021.
  • Current leaders: President Nikos Christodoulides (since February 2023) is head of state and government of the Republic. Cyprus held the rotating presidency of the Council of the EU in January-June 2026. In the north, Tufan Erhürman, who favours a federal settlement, won the October 2025 Turkish Cypriot leadership election against Ersin Tatar. (Leaders are from general knowledge and not re-checked today.)
  • Did you know? Greek myth says Aphrodite rose from the sea at Petra tou Romiou on the southwest coast, which is why Cyprus is called "the island of Aphrodite." Cars drive on the left, as in Britain.

6) Internet Meme — "Blinking White Guy" (Drew Scanlon reaction)

  • What it is: a short reaction GIF of a man with glasses and a beard who turns his head toward the camera, raises his eyebrows slightly and blinks twice in disbelief. It is used to say "Wait, what did you just say?" without words.
  • Origin: the man is Drew Scanlon, then a video producer at the gaming website Giant Bomb. The clip comes from a 2013 episode of the site's livestream show Unprofessional Fridays, where he reacts to a remark from a colleague off camera.
  • How it became popular: the clip was turned into a GIF and spread widely on Twitter and Reddit in early 2017, quickly becoming one of the standard reaction GIFs in GIF keyboards and Slack. Many people who used it did not know who he was, and the meme was often called simply "white guy blinking."
  • Meaning and usage: a reply to a statement that is so surprising, absurd or badly judged that the only possible reaction is a double take: a friend's terrible idea, a company's tone-deaf announcement, a price that makes no sense. It is gentle, not angry; it suggests polite disbelief.
  • Variations: captioned versions ("me when..."), edits that slow the blink down, and versions combining it with other reaction images. Scanlon himself acknowledged the meme publicly and has used his "blinking guy" fame in good humour, including for charity appeals (details from memory, unverified). He left Giant Bomb in 2017 and started the travel video series Cloth Map.
  • Current status: one of the evergreen reaction GIFs, still in GIF libraries and used daily. It is often cited as an example of how ordinary people become memes without choosing to, and of a meme subject who handled it with grace.

7) Film / Performing Arts — The Red Shoes (紅菱豔, 1948)

  • Creators: written, produced and directed by Michael Powell and Emeric Pressburger, who worked as "The Archers" and shared the credit "Written, Produced and Directed by". Cinematography by Jack Cardiff in three-strip Technicolor, production design by Hein Heckroth, music by Brian Easdale.
  • Cast and story: Moira Shearer, a Sadler's Wells ballerina in her first film, plays Victoria Page, a young dancer who joins the ballet company of the impresario Boris Lermontov (Anton Walbrook). At the same time Lermontov hires the young composer Julian Craster (Marius Goring). Lermontov stages a new ballet, The Red Shoes, based on Hans Christian Andersen's tale of a girl whose enchanted shoes make her dance until she dies. Vicky becomes a star, falls in love with Julian, and is forced to choose between love and art. The ending, at Monte Carlo, mirrors the ballet's tragedy. Léonide Massine and Robert Helpmann (who also choreographed the ballet) play dancers in the company.
  • Style and techniques: the heart of the film is a 17-minute ballet sequence in the middle. It starts as a filmed stage performance and then leaves the theatre: painted sets turn into dreamlike landscapes, a newspaper becomes a dancing partner, and Vicky sees Lermontov and Julian in the roles of the dancers. The camera shows what the dancer feels, not what the audience in the theatre would see. Colour is used expressively: the saturated red of the shoes, Heckroth's painted backdrops and Cardiff's lighting make it one of the most admired colour films ever made.
  • Background: made in post-war, rationed Britain by the Rank Organisation, which reportedly disliked the result and gave it little support. It became a long-running success in the United States. It won Oscars for art direction (Heckroth, Arthur Lawson) and score (Easdale) and was nominated for Best Picture. Lermontov is often seen as partly based on Sergei Diaghilev of the Ballets Russes.
  • Influence: it inspired a generation of ballet dancers and filmmakers. Gene Kelly and Vincente Minnelli's ballet finale in An American in Paris (1951) followed its model. Martin Scorsese calls it one of his favourite films, and Darren Aronofsky's Black Swan (2010) is a darker descendant. Matthew Bourne adapted it for the stage in 2016.
  • Where to see it today: a major restoration by the UCLA Film & Television Archive with The Film Foundation premiered at Cannes in 2009; that version is available on home video (e.g. the Criterion Collection) and is shown at revival screenings.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 107: Psychoanalytic Criticism (Desire, Repetition & the Double)

  • The method: psychoanalytic criticism reads a film as if it were a dream: the plot on the surface hides a second story about desire, fear and loss that the characters cannot say directly. It borrows ideas from Sigmund Freud (repression, the "return of the repressed", the compulsion to repeat, the uncanny, the double) and Jacques Lacan (desire as a lack that can never be filled; the mirror stage, in which we build our identity from an image). Christian Metz (The Imaginary Signifier, 1977) argued that cinema itself works like a dream. Lesson 14 (the gaze) was one branch of this tradition; today's lesson is the broader toolkit.
  • What to look for: (1) What does the character want but never say? Look at what they keep returning to, not at what they claim. (2) Repetition: does the character try to stage the same situation again and again? A compulsion to repeat usually points to a loss they have not dealt with. (3) Doubles and substitutes: characters who resemble each other, or one person standing in for another. (4) Objects carrying desire: a piece of clothing, a hairstyle or a photograph treated with excessive intensity. (5) The uncanny: moments when something familiar becomes strange, often marked by changes in light, sound or camera movement. (6) Caution: read the evidence on screen; do not try to diagnose the director, and do not treat every object as a symbol. A good psychoanalytic reading explains details that a plot summary cannot.
  • Worked example: Vertigo (1958, dir. Alfred Hitchcock), the makeover and the green-light scene at the Empire Hotel. After "Madeleine" (Kim Novak) dies, the detective Scottie (James Stewart) meets Judy, a shop girl who looks like her. He takes her to buy the same grey suit, has her hair dyed blonde, and insists she pin it up in Madeleine's spiral bun. In her hotel room Judy steps out of the bathroom bathed in the green glow of the neon sign outside, appearing out of a haze like a ghost. As they embrace, the camera circles around them 360 degrees, and behind them the room changes into the livery stable at Mission San Juan Bautista, where Scottie last kissed Madeleine.
  • What the scene shows, read this way: (a) Repetition compulsion: Scottie is not falling in love with Judy; he is trying to bring back a lost object by rebuilding it detail by detail, and he forces the repetition on her even when she begs him to love her as she is. (b) The double: Judy and Madeleine are the same woman (the audience already knows this from Judy's letter), so Scottie is unknowingly restoring a fiction that another man, Elster, built first. His desire was never for a real person. (c) Objects: the grey suit and especially the spiral bun, which echoes the spiral motifs of the opening titles and the staircase, carry the obsession. (d) The uncanny: the green light and the haze make a familiar woman look like a returning ghost, and the circling camera merges past and present into one space, the dream logic the scene is about.
  • Why it works: a plot summary says "a man remakes a woman to look like his dead lover". The psychoanalytic reading explains why the film shows it with such intensity, why the moment of "success" feels sickening rather than happy, and why the ending must repeat the loss at the bell tower. The method turns the visual details into evidence of the hero's desire.
  • Practice: in the next film you watch, list what the main character does more than once. For each repetition, ask what loss or fear it might be trying to master, and note where the film uses light, sound or camera movement to make something familiar feel strange.

4) 道德經 — 第 24 章:企者不立

  • 背景:易經六十四卦與莊子的篇章都已介紹完,本節依序介紹道德經。上一篇(11/13)是第 23 章。
  • 原文(通行本):企者不立,跨者不行。自見者不明,自是者不彰,自伐者無功,自矜者不長。其在道也,曰:餘食贅形。物或惡之,故有道者不處。
  • 白話:踮起腳尖的人站不穩;跨大步的人走不遠。愛表現自己的人反而看不清楚(也不被看清);自以為是的人反而不被肯定;自我誇耀的人反而沒有功勞;自高自大的人反而不能長久。從道的角度看,這些就像「吃剩的飯、身上多長的肉瘤」。大家都討厭它,所以有道的人不會停留在這種狀態。
  • 關鍵詞:企:踮腳,想讓自己看起來更高。跨:跨大步、超過自然步幅。四個「自」(自見、自是、自伐、自矜)正好是第 22 章四個「不自」的反面:第 22 章說聖人不自我表現,所以光明;這一章說自我表現的人反而看不見。餘食贅形:多餘的食物、多出來的肉瘤,都是對身體沒用、甚至有害的東西。
  • 怎麼讀:開頭的比喻很具體。踮腳可以讓你暫時高一點,跨大步可以讓你暫時快一點,但都撐不久,最後反而失去平衡。炫耀自己也一樣:費力,又讓人反感。這章接在第 23 章「飄風不終朝」之後,把「激烈的力量不能長久」從天地轉到個人的行為上。在馬王堆帛書中,這一章排在第 22 章之前,首字寫作「炊」(讀為「企」),和第 22 章「不自」的對照更明顯。

5) 國家介紹 — 賽普勒斯(Cyprus)

  • 位置:地中海第三大島(僅次於西西里島與薩丁尼亞島),位於地中海最東端,北距土耳其約 70 公里,東距敘利亞約 100 公里,南距埃及約 380 公里。地理上屬於亞洲,但政治與文化上屬於歐洲,是歐盟會員國。
  • 面積:全島約 9,251 平方公里,大約是台灣(約 3.6 萬平方公里)的四分之一,和波多黎各差不多大,比黎巴嫩稍小。島嶼處於分裂狀態:賽普勒斯共和國控制約 59%;自行宣布成立、只有土耳其承認的「北賽普勒斯土耳其共和國」約 36%;聯合國緩衝區(「綠線」)約 3%;英國的兩個主權基地區亞克羅提利與德凱利亞約 3%。
  • 人口:全島約 140 萬人:南部共和國控制區約 95 萬至 100 萬人,北部估計 38 萬至 48 萬人(包括來自土耳其的移民)。首都尼古西亞(希臘語 Lefkosia、土耳其語 Lefkoşa),是歐洲最後一個分裂的首都。
  • 語言與宗教:官方語言為希臘語和土耳其語;英國統治的影響讓英語非常普及。希臘裔居民多信東正教(自主的賽普勒斯教會);土耳其裔多為遜尼派穆斯林,日常生活大多世俗化。另有少數馬龍派、亞美尼亞人與拉丁天主教社群。
  • 歷史:約西元前 12 世紀起有邁錫尼希臘人定居,之後歷經亞述、埃及、波斯、托勒密王朝與羅馬統治,再成為拜占庭帝國的一部分。1191 年獅心王理查在第三次十字軍東征中奪下此島;法國呂西尼昂王朝統治十字軍王國約三百年,之後由威尼斯(1489)與鄂圖曼帝國(1571)接手。英國 1878 年取得管理權,1925 年改為直轄殖民地。希臘裔的 EOKA 武裝運動(1955-59)之後,賽普勒斯於 1960 年 8 月 16 日獨立,由馬卡里奧斯三世大主教任總統。1963-64 年族群衝突,聯合國維和部隊(UNFICYP)進駐至今。1974 年 7 月,雅典軍政府支持的政變之後,土耳其出兵,島嶼一分為二,約 20 萬人流離失所。北部在 1983 年宣布獨立。2004 年聯合國「安南計畫」公投,土耳其裔約 65% 贊成,希臘裔約 76% 反對;一週後的 5 月 1 日,賽普勒斯以分裂狀態加入歐盟。2008 年改用歐元;2013 年銀行危機,歐盟紓困方案要求大額存款「內部紓困」(存戶承擔損失)。
  • 經濟:以服務業為主:觀光(每年約四百萬旅客,很多來自英國)、航運(利馬索是全球最大的船舶管理中心之一)、金融與專業服務、房地產。2011 年在外海發現天然氣(阿芙蘿黛蒂氣田),之後又有 Glaucus 與 Cronos 氣田。人均 GDP 大致和南歐歐盟國家相當。哈羅米起司(halloumi)自 2021 年起受歐盟原產地保護。
  • 現任領導人:共和國總統尼科斯·赫里斯托祖利季斯(Nikos Christodoulides,2023 年 2 月就任),同時是國家元首與政府首長。賽普勒斯在 2026 年 1-6 月擔任歐盟理事會輪值主席國。北部方面,主張聯邦制解決方案的圖凡·埃爾許爾曼(Tufan Erhürman)在 2025 年 10 月的土耳其裔領導人選舉中擊敗艾爾辛·塔塔爾(Ersin Tatar)。(領導人資訊來自一般知識,今天沒有再查核。)
  • 你知道嗎?希臘神話說愛神阿芙蘿黛蒂從島嶼西南岸「羅密歐岩」(Petra tou Romiou)的海浪中誕生,所以賽普勒斯被稱為「愛神之島」。這裡和英國一樣靠左行駛。

6) 網路迷因 — 「眨眼白人男子」(Blinking White Guy,Drew Scanlon 反應圖)

  • 是什麼:一段短短的反應 GIF:一位戴眼鏡、留鬍子的男子把頭轉向鏡頭,眉毛微微上揚,不敢置信地眨了兩次眼。用來無聲地表達「等等,你剛剛說什麼?」
  • 來源:這位男子是 Drew Scanlon,當時是遊戲網站 Giant Bomb 的影片製作人。片段出自該網站 2013 年的直播節目 Unprofessional Fridays,他是在對鏡頭外同事的一句話做出反應。
  • 怎麼爆紅:這段畫面被做成 GIF,2017 年初在 Twitter 和 Reddit 上大量流傳,很快成為 GIF 鍵盤和 Slack 裡的標準反應圖之一。很多使用者並不知道他是誰,只稱它「眨眼的白人」。
  • 意思與用法:回應令人驚訝、荒謬或很不得體的話,讓人只能愣一下再看一次:朋友的爛點子、公司不食人間煙火的公告、毫無道理的價格。語氣溫和不帶怒氣,是「禮貌的不敢置信」。
  • 變化:加字幕的版本(「當我……的時候」)、放慢眨眼的版本,以及和其他反應圖拼接的版本。Scanlon 本人公開承認了這個迷因,也以幽默態度看待自己的「眨眼哥」身分,包括用在慈善募款上(細節來自記憶,未證實)。他在 2017 年離開 Giant Bomb,之後創立旅遊影片系列 Cloth Map。
  • 現況:是長青的反應 GIF 之一,至今仍在各 GIF 資料庫中天天被使用。常被拿來說明普通人如何在沒有選擇的情況下變成迷因,以及當事人如何優雅面對。

7) 電影/表演藝術 — 《紅菱豔》(The Red Shoes,1948)

  • 創作者:由麥可·鮑威爾(Michael Powell)與艾默利·皮斯伯格(Emeric Pressburger)共同編劇、製片與導演,兩人以「射手」(The Archers)名義合作,共享「編劇、製片、導演」的署名。攝影傑克·卡迪夫(Jack Cardiff,三色特藝彩色),美術設計海因·赫克羅思(Hein Heckroth),配樂布萊恩·伊斯戴爾(Brian Easdale)。
  • 演員與劇情:薩德勒之井芭蕾舞團的芭蕾舞者摩伊拉·席勒(Moira Shearer)首次演電影,飾演年輕舞者薇琪·佩吉(Victoria Page),她加入經紀人波里斯·萊蒙托夫(Anton Walbrook 飾)的芭蕾舞團。萊蒙托夫同時聘用年輕作曲家朱利安·克拉斯特(Marius Goring 飾)。萊蒙托夫推出新芭蕾《紅鞋》,改編自安徒生童話:女孩穿上魔法紅鞋,停不下來地跳舞直到死去。薇琪一舉成名,愛上朱利安,被迫在愛情和藝術之間選擇。結局發生在蒙地卡羅,和芭蕾的悲劇互相呼應。里奧尼德·馬辛(Léonide Massine)和羅伯特·赫爾普曼(Robert Helpmann,也是芭蕾段落的編舞)飾演舞團舞者。
  • 風格與技巧:全片核心是中段長達 17 分鐘的芭蕾段落。它從拍攝舞台演出開始,接著離開劇場:彩繪佈景變成夢境般的風景,一張報紙變成舞伴,薇琪看見萊蒙托夫與朱利安化身為舞者。攝影機呈現的是舞者的感受,而不是台下觀眾看到的畫面。色彩用來表達情感:紅鞋的濃烈紅色、赫克羅思的彩繪背景與卡迪夫的打光,讓本片成為影史上最受推崇的彩色電影之一。
  • 時代背景:在戰後物資配給的英國由蘭克公司出品,據說公司高層不喜歡成品,宣傳很少。後來在美國長期上映,大受歡迎。獲得奧斯卡最佳美術設計(赫克羅思、Arthur Lawson)與最佳配樂(伊斯戴爾),並入圍最佳影片。萊蒙托夫一角常被認為部分取材自俄羅斯芭蕾舞團的狄亞基列夫(Sergei Diaghilev)。
  • 影響:啟發了一整代芭蕾舞者與電影人。金·凱利與文森·明尼里的《花都艷舞》(An American in Paris,1951)結尾芭蕾段落就是仿效它。馬丁·史柯西斯說這是他最愛的電影之一,戴倫·艾洛諾夫斯基的《黑天鵝》(2010)則是更陰暗的後代。馬修·伯恩(Matthew Bourne)在 2016 年把它改編成舞台作品。
  • 今天在哪裡看:UCLA 電影電視資料館與電影基金會(The Film Foundation)合作的大規模修復版,2009 年在坎城首映;該版本有家用影音版(例如 Criterion 標準收藏),也常在經典影展放映。

8) 看片方法 — 第 107 課:精神分析批評(慾望、重複與替身)

  • 方法:精神分析批評把電影當成夢來讀:表面的劇情底下,藏著角色說不出口的第二個故事,關於慾望、恐懼與失去。它借用佛洛伊德的概念(壓抑、「被壓抑者的回返」、強迫性重複、詭異感 the uncanny、替身/分身),以及拉岡的概念(慾望是永遠填不滿的匱乏;鏡像階段:我們從影像建立自我)。克里斯蒂安·梅茲(Christian Metz)在《想像的能指》(1977)主張電影本身就像夢。第 14 課的「凝視」是這個傳統的其中一支;今天介紹的是更完整的工具箱。
  • 觀察重點:(1)角色想要什麼卻從不說出口?看他一再回到什麼,而不是他自己怎麼說。(2)重複:角色是否一次又一次重演同一個情境?強迫性重複通常指向一個沒有處理的失去。(3)替身與替代品:彼此相像的角色,或一個人被拿來代替另一個人。(4)承載慾望的物件:被過度在意的衣服、髮型或照片。(5)詭異感:熟悉的東西突然變得陌生的時刻,常用光線、聲音或運鏡的變化標示。(6)提醒:根據銀幕上的證據來讀,不要去「診斷」導演,也不要把每個物件都當象徵。好的精神分析解讀,能解釋劇情摘要解釋不了的細節。
  • 實例:希區考克的《迷魂記》(Vertigo,1958),帝國飯店的改造與綠光場景。「瑪德琳」(金·露華 飾)死後,警探史考提(詹姆斯·史都華 飾)遇到長得很像她的店員茱蒂。他帶她去買同一款灰色套裝,要她把頭髮染成金色,並堅持她把頭髮盤成瑪德琳的螺旋髮髻。在飯店房間裡,茱蒂從浴室走出來,全身籠罩在窗外霓虹招牌的綠光中,像鬼魂一樣從霧氣中浮現。兩人相擁時,攝影機繞著他們轉了 360 度,背景的房間變成聖胡安包蒂斯塔教會的馬廄,也就是史考提最後一次親吻瑪德琳的地方。
  • 用這個方法看:(a)強迫性重複:史考提不是愛上茱蒂,而是想一點一點重建失去的對象,即使茱蒂哀求他愛原本的她,他仍強迫她重演。(b)替身:茱蒂和瑪德琳是同一個人(觀眾已從茱蒂的信得知),所以史考提是在不知情下,重建另一個男人艾斯特先打造出來的虛構角色。他的慾望從來不是針對真實的人。(c)物件:灰色套裝,特別是螺旋髮髻,呼應片頭字幕和樓梯的螺旋圖案,承載了這份執念。(d)詭異感:綠光與霧氣讓熟悉的女人看起來像回來的鬼魂,環繞的運鏡把過去和現在融成同一個空間,這正是這場戲要呈現的夢的邏輯。
  • 為什麼有效:劇情摘要只會說「男人把女人改造成死去情人的樣子」。精神分析解讀則解釋了為什麼電影要拍得這麼強烈、為什麼「成功」的那一刻讓人反胃而不是開心,以及為什麼結局必須在鐘樓再一次失去。這個方法把視覺細節變成主角慾望的證據。
  • 練習:下次看電影時,列出主角做了不只一次的事。針對每一個重複,問問它可能在試圖掌控什麼失去或恐懼;再記下電影在哪裡用光線、聲音或運鏡讓熟悉的東西變得陌生。
