ENEnglish version

🎯 Learning Goal: talk about money in everyday life: earning and winning (ganar), spending (gastar), saving (ahorrar), paying (pagar) and charging or getting paid (cobrar), and handle the bill at a restaurant.

Vocabulary: ganar (earn; win) · perder (lose; miss) · gastar en (spend on) · ahorrar (save) · los ahorros (savings) · pagar (pay) · cobrar (charge; get paid) · costar o→ue (cost) · invitar (treat someone) · la tarjeta / el efectivo (card / cash) · el sueldo (salary) · la cuenta (bill; bank account) · la propina (tip).

Grammar Point: (1) pagar vs. cobrar depends on which side of the transaction you are on: the customer paga, the cashier cobra, so ¿Nos cobra? ("Will you charge us?") means "Can we have the bill?". An employee also cobra a salary. (2) ganar covers both "earn" and "win"; the object decides the meaning. (3) Hoy pago yo: putting the subject after the verb stresses it ("I'm paying"). (4) Spelling rule in the preterite: pagar → pagué (g becomes gu before é to keep the hard g sound). (5) Fixed prepositions: con tarjeta but en efectivo.

Example Sentences:

  • Este mes he gastado demasiado. Tengo que ahorrar. — I've spent too much this month. I have to save.
  • Tranquila, hoy pago yo. Acabo de cobrar. — Don't worry, I'm paying today. I just got paid.
  • Perdone, ¿nos cobra? ¿Se puede pagar con tarjeta? — Excuse me, can we pay? Can I pay by card?
  • Gana 2.000 euros al mes. — She earns 2,000 euros a month. / Ganamos el partido. — We won the match.
  • El taxista me cobró cinco euros de más. — The taxi driver overcharged me by five euros.

Practice Prompt: (a) Fill in pagar or cobrar in the preterite: El taxista me ____ cinco euros de más. / Ayer yo ____ la factura. (b) Translate: "I'm saving because I want to travel to Mexico. Are you treating today?" — Send your answers and I will grade them; the lesson file has an answer key below a divider.

ENEnglish version

Sequence status: all 64 hexagrams (King Wen order) and the Zhuangzi essays/poems in the log have already been introduced. Since 2026-10-22 this section continues with the Daodejing in chapter order (a default adopted when both lists ran out). Last entry before today: Chapter 23 (Nov 13). Today: Chapter 24.

Name and number: Daodejing, Chapter 24 — 企者不立 (Qǐ zhě bú lì), "On Tiptoe You Cannot Stand."

Original text (received text): 企者不立,跨者不行。自見者不明,自是者不彰,自伐者無功,自矜者不長。其在道也,曰:餘食贅形。物或惡之,故有道者不處。

Translation: Whoever stands on tiptoe cannot stand firm; whoever takes huge strides cannot keep walking. Whoever shows himself off is not clearly seen; whoever insists he is right is not recognised; whoever boasts gets no credit; whoever is proud of himself does not last. From the point of view of the Way, these are "leftover food and a growth on the body." People find them distasteful, so whoever has the Way does not dwell in them.

Key terms: 企 — to raise oneself on the toes, straining to look taller. 跨 — to overstride. The four 自 phrases (自見, 自是, 自伐, 自矜) are the negative mirror of Chapter 22's four 不自 phrases: there, the sage does not show off and therefore shines; here, whoever does show off is not seen. 餘食贅形 — surplus food and a superfluous growth (a wart or tumour): useless extras.

Traditional interpretations and background: Commentators such as Wang Bi read the chapter as a warning against forcing results: straining beyond one's natural capacity defeats itself. It continues Chapter 23's image of the storm that cannot last all day (violent force is short-lived) and applies it to personal conduct. In the Mawangdui silk manuscripts (2nd century BC) this chapter is placed before Chapter 22, and the first character is written 炊 (read as 企), which makes the pairing with Chapter 22 even clearer.

Philosophical implications: self-display is a kind of excess, like extra food the body cannot use. The Daoist claim is not that ability is bad, but that advertising it creates resistance in others and exhausts the person doing it.

Modern-life reflection: think of the colleague who answers every question first, or a product launch built on exaggerated claims: both stand "on tiptoe." Sustainable credibility comes from steady footing — work that speaks for itself, claims you can repeat tomorrow. A practical test: if you could not keep doing something for a year, it is probably tiptoe effort.

進度說明:六十四卦(依文王卦序)與記錄中的莊子篇章都已介紹完畢。自 2026-10-22 起,本段改為依章次介紹《道德經》(兩份清單用完後採用的預設做法)。今天之前的最後一篇是第 23 章(11/13)。今天:第 24 章。

名稱與章次:《道德經》第 24 章 —— 企者不立(Qǐ zhě bú lì),「踮著腳站不穩」。

原文(通行本):企者不立,跨者不行。自見者不明,自是者不彰,自伐者無功,自矜者不長。其在道也,曰:餘食贅形。物或惡之,故有道者不處。

白話翻譯:踮起腳尖的人站不穩;跨大步走的人走不遠。愛表現自己的人反而看不清楚(也不被看清);自以為是的人反而不被肯定;自我誇耀的人反而沒有功勞;自高自大的人反而不能長久。從「道」的角度來看,這些就像「吃剩的飯、身上多長的贅肉」。大家都厭惡它,所以有道的人不會停留在這種狀態。

關鍵字:企 —— 踮起腳跟,勉強讓自己看起來更高。跨 —— 跨大步。四個「自」(自見、自是、自伐、自矜)是第 22 章四個「不自」的反面:第 22 章說聖人不自我表現,所以光明;這一章說愛表現的人反而不被看見。餘食贅形 —— 多餘的食物、多長的瘤:沒有用的多餘之物。

傳統詮釋與背景:王弼等注家把這一章讀成對「強求」的警告:超出自然能力去硬撐,最後會自我挫敗。它延續第 23 章「飄風不終朝,驟雨不終日」(猛烈的力量不能持久)的意象,並把它用在個人行為上。在馬王堆帛書(西元前 2 世紀)中,這一章排在第 22 章之前,第一個字寫作「炊」(讀作「企」),與第 22 章的配對關係更加明顯。

哲學意涵:自我表現是一種「過多」,就像身體用不到的多餘食物。道家並不是說能力不好,而是說把能力拿出來炫耀,會在別人心中引起反感,也會把自己耗盡。

現代生活的反思:想想每個問題都搶第一個回答的同事,或建立在誇大宣傳上的產品發表會:兩者都在「踮腳尖」。可持續的信任來自站穩腳步——讓作品自己說話,說出明天還能再說一次的話。一個實用的檢驗:如果某件事你沒辦法持續做一年,它大概就是「踮腳」式的努力。

中繁體中文版(完整課程)

Unit 1 — 介系詞第 1 課:地點的 in / on / at

1. 今日主題

今天只學一個核心概念:描述「地點」時,in、on、at 怎麼選。中文一律說「在」,所以中文母語者最常見的困擾就是:「在台北」「在公車上」「在車站」到底要用哪一個?

2. 核心概念

英文母語者選介系詞時,其實是在腦中替地點「選一個形狀」:

  • at = 一個點。把地點想成地圖上的一顆圖釘,不在意它裡面長什麼樣子,只在意「人在那個位置/在那裡做那件事」:at the station(在車站那個點)、at work(在上班的地方)、at the front desk。
  • on = 一個面或一條線。東西貼在表面上、或沿著一條線:on the table、on the wall、on Zhongshan Road(路是一條線)、on the second floor(樓層是一個平面)、on an island(站在島的表面上)。
  • in = 一個容器或有邊界的區域。被包在裡面:in the box、in the room、in Taipei、in Taiwan(城市、國家都是有邊界的區域)。

關鍵:同一個地方可以有不同說法,取決於你怎麼「想像」它。I'm at the mall(我在商場——這個點,例如告訴朋友我在哪)vs. I'm in the mall(我在商場裡面——強調室內,例如外面下大雨)。兩個都對,意思的重點不同。

3. 比較與例句

at(點)on(面/線)in(容器/區域)
畫面地圖上的圖釘桌面、牆面、一條路盒子、房間、城市、國家
例子at the station, at home, at the dooron the table, on the bus, on Heping Roadin the kitchen, in the car, in Kaohsiung
  1. I'll meet you at Taipei Main Station. —— 我在台北車站跟你碰面。(車站是約定的「點」)
  2. My phone is on the table. —— 我的手機在桌上。(放在表面上)
  3. My parents live in Tainan. —— 我爸媽住在台南。(城市是區域)
  4. Our office is on Dunhua South Road. —— 我們辦公室在敦化南路上。(路是一條線)
  5. Our office is at 123 Dunhua South Road. —— 我們辦公室在敦化南路 123 號。(完整門牌是一個精確的「點」,所以改用 at)
  6. Green Island is a small island. People there live on the island and travel by scooter. —— 綠島是個小島。那裡的人住在島上,騎機車移動。(島是一個表面)
  7. Sorry, I can't talk — I'm in a meeting room. —— 抱歉不方便講話,我在會議室裡。(強調在室內空間裡)

換掉介系詞,意思怎麼變? He's at school(他在上學,是學生身分或正在上課)vs. He's in the school(他人在學校建築裡面,可能只是去開家長會)。on the bus(搭乘中的大型交通工具,可以在裡面站著走動)vs. in the car(小車子,只能坐著被「包」在裡面)。

4. 常見錯誤

  • ✗ I live at Taipei. → ✓ I live in Taipei. 原因:中文「住在台北」的「在」被直接翻成 at。城市是一個區域,住在區域「裡面」要用 in。(例外:說轉機地點時可以用 at,例如 We stopped at Taipei on the way to Tokyo,因為那時台北只是路線上的一個點。)
  • ✗ I'm in the bus. → ✓ I'm on the bus. 原因:中文說「在公車上」反而接近英文,但很多人以為「坐在裡面」就該用 in。公車、捷運、火車、飛機、船這類可以站起來走動的大型交通工具用 on;汽車、計程車用 in。
  • ✗ I found it at the internet. → ✓ I found it on the internet. 原因:網路、電視、電話、社群平台被想像成一個「平台/表面」,所以用 on:on the internet, on TV, on Instagram, on the phone。

5. 今日小測驗(請先作答,答案明天批改時才公布)

  1. Let's meet ___ the entrance of the museum at 3 p.m. (in / on / at)
  2. There's a beautiful painting ___ the wall of my grandmother's house. (in / on / at)
  3. Taroko Gorge is ___ Hualien County, ___ the east coast of Taiwan. (填兩個空格)
  4. Which sentence sounds natural? (a) I'm in the MRT now. (b) I'm on the MRT now. (c) I'm at the MRT now.
  5. Your friend calls you during a typhoon. You want to say you are safely inside the building, not outside. Which is better: "I'm at the office" or "I'm in the office"? 請說明理由。

6. 今日重點

  • at = 點、on = 面/線、in = 容器/區域。
  • 城市、國家用 in;路名用 on;完整門牌用 at。
  • 口訣:「點 at、面 on、裝在裡面 in」——先想地點的「形狀」,再選介系詞。

Section 7 — Film & Performing Arts: The Red Shoes (1948)
第 7 段 — 電影與表演藝術:《紅菱豔》(1948)

ENEnglish version

Creators: written, produced and directed by Michael Powell and Emeric Pressburger ("The Archers"); cinematography by Jack Cardiff in three-strip Technicolor; production design by Hein Heckroth; music by Brian Easdale.

Period and background: made in post-war, rationed Britain for the Rank Organisation, which reportedly disliked the result and gave it little support; it became a long-running success in the United States. The impresario Lermontov is often seen as partly based on Sergei Diaghilev of the Ballets Russes.

Content: Moira Shearer, a Sadler's Wells ballerina in her first film, plays Victoria Page, a young dancer who joins Boris Lermontov's (Anton Walbrook) company, alongside the young composer Julian Craster (Marius Goring). Lermontov stages a ballet based on Hans Christian Andersen's tale of a girl whose enchanted shoes make her dance until she dies. Vicky becomes a star, falls in love with Julian, and is forced to choose between love and art; the ending mirrors the ballet's tragedy. Léonide Massine and Robert Helpmann (who choreographed the ballet) appear as dancers.

Style and techniques: the centrepiece is a 17-minute ballet sequence that begins as a filmed stage performance and then leaves the theatre: painted sets become dream landscapes, a newspaper becomes a dancing partner, and Vicky sees Lermontov and Julian in the roles of the dancers. The camera shows what the dancer feels, not what a theatre audience would see. Colour is expressive — the saturated red of the shoes, Heckroth's painted backdrops and Cardiff's lighting.

Significance: it won Oscars for art direction (Heckroth, Arthur Lawson) and score (Easdale) and was nominated for Best Picture. Critics regard it as one of the great colour films and one of the most serious films about the cost of artistic devotion. (Historical facts vs. interpretation: the awards are facts; "greatest colour film" is a critical judgement.)

Influence: it inspired a generation of dancers and filmmakers; the ballet finale of An American in Paris (1951) followed its model; Martin Scorsese calls it a favourite; Darren Aronofsky's Black Swan (2010) is a darker descendant; Matthew Bourne adapted it for the stage in 2016.

Where to see it today: the UCLA Film & Television Archive / The Film Foundation restoration (premiered at Cannes 2009) is available on home video (e.g. the Criterion Collection) and in revival screenings.

Section 8 — Film Appreciation & Criticism Method, Lesson 107: Psychoanalytic Criticism
第 8 段 — 看片方法第 107 課:精神分析批評

ENEnglish version

1. Today's analytical method: psychoanalytic criticism — reading a film like a dream, in which the surface plot hides a second story about desire, fear and loss that the characters cannot say directly. It matters because it explains intensities and details that a plot summary cannot.

2. Detailed explanation: the method borrows from Sigmund Freud (repression, the return of the repressed, the compulsion to repeat, the uncanny, the double) and Jacques Lacan (desire as a lack that can never be filled; the mirror stage). Christian Metz (The Imaginary Signifier, 1977) argued that cinema itself works like a dream. Lesson 14 (the gaze) was one branch of this tradition; today is the broader toolkit. What to look for: (1) what the character wants but never says — watch what they keep returning to; (2) repetition — restaging the same situation usually points to an unprocessed loss; (3) doubles and substitutes; (4) objects charged with desire (clothing, hairstyles, photographs treated with excessive intensity); (5) the uncanny — the familiar made strange, often marked by changes in light, sound or camera movement. Common misconception: this is not diagnosing the director, and not every object is a symbol. Keep observation (what is on screen) separate from interpretation (what it may mean).

3. Concrete film example: Vertigo (1958), dir. Alfred Hitchcock — the makeover and the green-light scene at the Empire Hotel.

  • Observation: after "Madeleine" (Kim Novak) dies, Scottie (James Stewart) meets Judy, who resembles her. He buys her the same grey suit, has her hair dyed blonde and insists on Madeleine's spiral bun. Judy steps out of the bathroom bathed in the green neon light from outside, emerging from a haze. As they embrace, the camera circles 360 degrees and the room behind them turns into the livery stable at Mission San Juan Bautista, where Scottie last kissed Madeleine.
  • Choices: green light and haze; a circling camera; a background that shifts from present to past; costume and hair as obsessive details.
  • Interpretation: (a) repetition compulsion — Scottie is rebuilding a lost object, forcing the repetition on Judy even when she begs to be loved as herself; (b) the double — Judy and Madeleine are the same woman, so Scottie is restoring a fiction another man (Elster) built; his desire was never for a real person; (c) the spiral bun echoes the spirals of the titles and the staircase; (d) the uncanny — a familiar woman appears like a returning ghost, and the circling camera merges past and present.
  • Why it works: it explains why the moment of "success" feels sickening rather than romantic, and why the ending must repeat the loss at the bell tower.

4. Practical viewing exercise: in the next film you watch, list what the main character does more than once. For each repetition, ask what loss or fear it might be trying to master, and note where light, sound or camera movement makes something familiar feel strange. Write one sentence of observation and one of interpretation for each.

5. Key takeaways: read repetition as a clue; look for doubles and charged objects; mark uncanny moments through form (light, sound, camera); always ground interpretation in on-screen evidence.

6. Suggested further viewing: Black Swan (2010, dir. Darren Aronofsky) — the mirror scenes and Nina's double Lily — which also links to Section 7's The Red Shoes.

中繁體中文版

1. 今日分析方法:精神分析批評——把電影當成一場夢來讀:表面的劇情底下,藏著另一個關於慾望、恐懼與失去的故事,而角色無法直接說出口。它之所以重要,是因為它能解釋劇情摘要說明不了的強烈情緒與細節。

2. 詳細說明:這個方法借用佛洛伊德的概念(壓抑、被壓抑者的回歸、強迫性重複、詭異感、替身)與拉岡的概念(慾望是一種永遠填不滿的匱乏;鏡像階段)。克里斯蒂安·梅茲在《想像的能指》(1977)中主張,電影本身就像夢一樣運作。第 14 課(凝視)是這個傳統的一支;今天是更完整的工具箱。要注意:(1)角色想要卻從不說出口的東西——看他一再回到什麼地方;(2)重複——一再重演同一個情境,通常指向一個還沒處理的失去;(3)替身與替代品;(4)承載慾望的物件(被過度在意的衣服、髮型、照片);(5)詭異感——熟悉的東西變得陌生,常透過光線、聲音或攝影機運動的改變來標示。常見誤解:這不是在診斷導演,也不是每個物件都是象徵。要把「觀察」(畫面上有什麼)和「詮釋」(可能代表什麼)分開。

3. 具體電影範例:《迷魂記》(Vertigo,1958),希區考克導演——帝國飯店的改造與綠光場景。

  • 觀察:「瑪德琳」(金·露華飾)死後,史考提(詹姆斯·史都華飾)遇見長得像她的茱蒂。他帶她買一模一樣的灰色套裝、把頭髮染成金色,還堅持要她盤起瑪德琳的螺旋髮髻。茱蒂從浴室走出來,全身沐浴在窗外霓虹招牌的綠光中,從一片霧氣裡浮現。兩人擁抱時,攝影機繞著他們轉 360 度,背後的房間變成聖胡安包蒂斯塔修道院的馬廄——史考提最後一次親吻瑪德琳的地方。
  • 電影手法:綠光與霧氣;環繞的攝影機;從現在變成過去的背景;服裝與髮型成為執念的細節。
  • 詮釋:(a)強迫性重複——史考提是在重建一個失去的對象,即使茱蒂哀求他愛真實的她,他仍強迫她重演;(b)替身——茱蒂和瑪德琳是同一個人,所以史考提是在修復另一個男人(艾斯特)打造的虛構;他的慾望從來不是對一個真實的人;(c)螺旋髮髻呼應片頭與樓梯的螺旋圖案;(d)詭異感——熟悉的女人像歸來的鬼魂一樣出現,環繞的攝影機把過去與現在融成一體。
  • 為什麼有效:它解釋了為什麼這個「成功」的時刻讓人感到不舒服而不是浪漫,也解釋了為什麼結局必須在鐘樓重演那場失去。

4. 觀影練習:下一部看的電影,列出主角做了不只一次的事。針對每一次重複,問:它可能是在試圖掌控什麼失去或恐懼?並記下電影在哪裡用光線、聲音或攝影機運動,讓熟悉的東西變得陌生。每一項各寫一句觀察、一句詮釋。
