3) Spanish Lesson — Day 28 (10 min, builds on Day 27)

Day 27 named the present subjunctive and taught its first trigger: verbs of wish/desire (querer/esperar que) aimed at a different subject. Today stays in the subjunctive but switches to a second major trigger family — doubt, denial, and impersonal expressions of possibility — plus one common spoken-language exception that breaks the pattern learners just got comfortable with.

  • Dudo que él tenga razón. — I doubt (that) he is right.
  • No creo que sea buena idea. — I don't think it's a good idea.
  • Es posible que llueva mañana. — It's possible it will rain tomorrow.
  • No es cierto que ella lo sepa. — It isn't true/certain that she knows it.

Grammar note: the subjunctive isn't only for wishes — it marks anything the speaker isn't asserting as settled fact. That's why "creer que" (to believe) takes the indicative when affirmative (Creo que tiene razón — I believe he's right, asserted as fact) but flips to the subjunctive under negation (No creo que tenga razón — I don't believe he's right, now explicitly not asserted as fact); the same flip applies to "es cierto/verdad que" (indicative) vs. "no es cierto/verdad que" (subjunctive). "Dudar que" (to doubt) takes the subjunctive in both its affirmative and negative forms, since doubt itself is inherently about the speaker's uncertainty. Impersonal possibility expressions like "es posible/probable que" likewise take the subjunctive, since they describe something not yet known to happen. The trap: the everyday colloquial "a lo mejor" (maybe/perhaps) means almost the same thing as "es posible que" but conventionally takes the indicative instead ("A lo mejor llueve mañana," not "llueva") — a native-speaker habit textbook rules don't predict, and one of the most common "sounds wrong but isn't" moments for learners.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 28: 大過 (Dà Guò) — "Preponderance of the Great" / "Great Exceeding" (Lake above, Wind/Wood below). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary bridges directly from yesterday's Hexagram 27: "頤者,養也。不養則不可動,故受之以大過。" ("Yí means nourishing; without nourishment nothing can act or move, hence Yí is succeeded by Dà Guò, the Great Exceeding.") This continues yesterday's logic one step further: something properly nourished eventually has the strength to act — but that action sometimes must exceed ordinary measure to meet an extraordinary situation. The Judgment reads: "大過:棟橈。利有攸往,亨。" ("Great Exceeding: the ridgepole sags [under excess weight]. It is favorable to go forward; success is possible.") The hexagram's structure makes this image literal: four solid (yang) lines packed in the middle with a single broken (yin) line at each end — like a beam overloaded in its center and too weak at its two supporting ends, at risk of sagging or breaking under the very strength concentrated within it. The Image commentary reads: "澤滅木,大過;君子以獨立不懼,遯世無悶。" ("The lake rises over and submerges the trees: this is Great Exceeding. Thus the superior person, in such times, stands independently without fear, and can withdraw from the world without regret or gloom.") Read together, the hexagram teaches that moments demanding extraordinary, above-normal-measure action are necessary and can succeed — but they carry real structural risk, and require the inner steadiness to act (or deliberately not act) without being swayed by how isolated or unconventional that choice looks to others.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Bolivia (玻利維亞)

  • Location: A landlocked country in west-central South America, bordered by Brazil (north and east), Paraguay (southeast), Argentina (south), Chile (southwest), and Peru (west).
  • Territory: Approximately 1,098,581 km² — roughly the combined size of France and Spain, and about 30 times the size of Taiwan (~36,193 km²); terrain ranges from high Andean altiplano (home to La Paz, the world's highest administrative capital at ~3,640 m) to Amazon lowland rainforest, and includes the Salar de Uyuni, the world's largest salt flat.
  • Population: Approximately 12.4 million (2024 est.), with over 40 recognized Indigenous nations; roughly 40% of the population self-identifies as Indigenous, one of the highest shares in the Americas.
  • Religion: Predominantly Roman Catholic (roughly 70%), with a fast-growing Protestant/Evangelical minority (around 15-19%); Indigenous spiritual traditions, especially reverence for Pachamama ("Mother Earth"), remain widely practiced, often syncretized alongside Catholic observance rather than replaced by it.
  • Economy: Resource-dependent and one of South America's lower-income economies by GDP per capita, but rich in natural gas, tin, and — increasingly strategically important — an estimated one of the world's largest lithium reserves, concentrated under the Salar de Uyuni; agriculture (soy, and Bolivia is among the world's top quinoa exporters) is also significant. Uniquely, Bolivia has two capitals: Sucre is the constitutional/judicial capital, while La Paz is the seat of government and legislature.
  • History: Once part of the Inca Empire, then the Spanish Viceroyalty (as "Alto Perú"), where silver mined at Potosí's Cerro Rico helped bankroll the Spanish empire for centuries. Independence came in 1825, and the country is named for Simón Bolívar, with its first president, Antonio José de Sucre, giving his name to the constitutional capital. Bolivia lost its Pacific coastline to Chile in the War of the Pacific (1879-1884) and further territory to Paraguay in the Chaco War (1932-1935), and has long been noted for political instability — often cited as having had more changes of government than almost any other country since independence. Evo Morales, elected in 2005, became Bolivia's first Indigenous president and governed for nearly 14 years (2006-2019) under the Movement for Socialism (MAS) party.
  • Current leader: President Rodrigo Paz Pereira, the 68th president, took office on November 8, 2025, ending nearly two decades of MAS-party rule; his first year has been marked by a serious governance crisis, including an April-2026 protest wave and a June-2026 state of emergency declared amid blockades over living costs and fuel/food supply disruptions.
  • Language: Spanish is the official and dominant language, alongside 36 other constitutionally recognized official Indigenous languages, most widely Quechua and Aymara.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, csis.org

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — 傻眼貓咪 ("Blank/Stunned Cat")

  • Name: 傻眼貓咪 (shǎ yǎn māo mī), literally "dumbfounded/stunned cat" — a four-character Mandarin catchphrase, not originally an image macro.
  • Origin/history: The phrase's viral trigger was, unusually, a real criminal case rather than a joke: in April-May 2017, Kaohsiung police in Taiwan raided a drug party and seized packets of a synthetic drug cocktail ("毒咖啡," "drug coffee") whose packaging was printed with a blue cartoon cat and the large characters "傻眼" ("stunned/wtf"). Local media coverage of the bust spread the striking packaging image widely.
  • How it became popular: The phrase landed in a linguistic environment already primed for this kind of four-character reaction-slang, following 2016 hits like "藍瘦香菇" ("heartbroken, want to cry," a Guangxi-accent pun) and "黑人問號" ("Black guy question mark," from a meme'd NBA photo) and the older "無言薯條" ("wordless fries") image meme. After the second, higher-profile drug bust reported in May 2017, "傻眼貓咪" spread rapidly across Chinese-language social media; mainstream news outlets ran explainer pieces in August 2017 as the term hit its peak on Google Trends.
  • Meaning/usage: Used to express speechless disbelief, exasperation, or "I don't even know how to react to this" — functionally similar to English "I'm speechless" or "wtf," and by extension sometimes used to describe someone behaving erratically or "out of it" (echoing the drug-related origin, "很傻眼" / acting 茫/㎊ ).
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned a large family of matching cat-mascot sticker packs (widely circulated on LINE), meme-image templates pairing the phrase with reaction photos, and it is frequently paired in casual speech with its meme-era sibling "無言薯條" as a stock combo for "I have nothing to say to this."
  • Current status: Past its 2017 viral peak but fully absorbed into everyday Taiwanese/Chinese-language internet and spoken slang — still commonly used, especially via sticker, as a lightweight way to signal comic disbelief without further comment.
    Sources: zh.wikipedia.org, news.ltn.com.tw

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Swan Lake (Swan Lake / Лебединое озеро, 1877/1895; music by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, choreography by Marius Petipa & Lev Ivanov)

  • Name/creator(s): Swan Lake, a four-act classical ballet. Score composed by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1875-76); its now-canonical choreography is credited to Marius Petipa (acts I and III) and his assistant Lev Ivanov (acts II and IV), with the score revised by conductor Riccardo Drigo.
  • Era/background: Commissioned by Moscow's Bolshoi Theatre, Swan Lake premiered in 1877 with choreography by Julius Reisinger — a production widely considered a flop, criticized for weak staging. Only after Tchaikovsky's death in 1893 did the Imperial Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg stage the revised Petipa/Ivanov version (1895) that became the ballet's enduring standard.
  • Plot/content overview: Prince Siegfried falls in love with Odette, a princess transformed into a swan by the sorcerer Von Rothbart's curse, breakable only by a vow of true love never before given to another. At a court ball, Von Rothbart tricks Siegfried into declaring his love to Odile, Odette's dark double ("the Black Swan"), dooming the curse; the ending's exact resolution — tragic (the lovers unite only in death) or redemptive (love conquers the sorcerer) — varies by production and era, particularly between pre- and post-Soviet stagings.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Built on Petipa's grand-ballet structural template — formal pas de deux, extended corps de ballet ensemble sequences, and a Act III showcase variation — the ballet is best known for its corps de ballet's geometric, unison "flock of swans" formations in Acts II and IV, and for the dual Odette/Odile role's contrasting technical demands: Odette's lyrical, soft-armed "White Swan" adagio versus Odile's brilliant, sharp "Black Swan" variation, capped by the famously punishing sequence of 32 consecutive fouetté turns.
  • Cultural/historical significance: Tchaikovsky's densely symphonic, emotionally driven score was initially considered almost too complex and "unballetic" for dance, but its eventual success helped establish that ballet music could carry the same compositional weight as concert or operatic music, reshaping how later ballet scores (including Tchaikovsky's own Sleeping Beauty and The Nutcracker) were composed.
  • Influence and legacy: The White Swan's arched-back, wing-like arm port de bras is among the most globally recognizable images in all of dance; the Black Swan's 32 fouettés became a technical rite-of-passage benchmark for ballerinas worldwide; the ballet has inspired countless reinterpretations, from Matthew Bourne's all-male 1995 Swan Lake to its central role in the 2010 film Black Swan.
  • Current status/where to experience: One of the most frequently performed ballets on earth, staged every season by essentially every major company — the Bolshoi and Mariinsky in Russia, the Royal Ballet, Paris Opera Ballet, American Ballet Theatre, and others — in addition to touring productions and filmed recordings widely available for home viewing.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, roh.org.uk

8) Film-Appreciation Method — The Gaze / Spectatorship Theory (Lesson 14)

Lessons 1-13 gave you tools for reading what's inside the frame and how a film is built (mise-en-scène, editing, cinematography, sound, colour, narrative structure, blocking, framing, symbolism, auteur theory, the long take, performance, and genre convention). Today's lens turns the question around: it asks not what the camera shows, but whose look the camera is performing, and what that structures the audience into doing.

The method: Film theorist Laura Mulvey's 1975 essay "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" argued that classical Hollywood cinema is structured around an active, controlling "male gaze": the camera, the male characters within the story, and the audience are all aligned to look at a woman as visual spectacle, while she is denied an equivalent structuring look of her own. To apply this lens, ask: (1) Whose eyes does the camera borrow? Look for point-of-view shots, or shots that simply linger on a character's face as they watch — the film is telling you who the "bearer of the look" is, and who is positioned as the "object" being looked at. (2) Does the story punish, reward, or resolve the act of looking? Classical narrative often builds an entire plot around a male character's need to investigate, possess, or resolve the mystery of a woman he's watching — the gaze itself becomes the engine of the plot, not just a stylistic choice. (3) Is the audience's gaze aligned with a character's, or held separate from it? A film can invite you to share a character's voyeuristic or controlling look uncritically, or it can expose and complicate that look — showing you the watcher watching, so the mechanism itself becomes visible and open to critique rather than simply being consumed. (4) Whose gaze is withheld? Note which characters are never given a comparable point-of-view shot of their own — in classical Hollywood, Mulvey argued, this absence typically fell along gender lines, though the same question productively extends to race, class, and other structuring absences in any given film.

Worked example — Vertigo (1958, dir. Alfred Hitchcock), the hotel-room transformation scene: Vertigo is the single film most cited in gaze-theory scholarship, largely because of this one sequence. Retired detective Scottie has been obsessively trying to remake Judy, a shopgirl, into the image of Madeleine, the (apparently dead) woman he loved and failed to save. When Judy finally emerges from the bathroom in Madeleine's exact hair and dress, bathed entirely in green light from the hotel's neon sign outside, Hitchcock holds on Scottie's transfixed stare before the camera performs an unbroken 360-degree pan around the couple's kiss — and mid-turn, the hotel room dissolves into the memory of the Spanish mission stable where Scottie failed to stop Madeleine's apparent death, before turning back to the present. The entire scene is staged, shot, and paced around Scottie's look: Judy has no comparable point-of-view shot of her own in this moment, and the camera's swirling movement doesn't show us the room so much as it enacts Scottie's obsessive, controlling desire to merge the woman in front of him with the image in his memory.

What to look for and why it works: (1) The camera's movement is the argument, not just decoration — the 360-degree pan doesn't reveal new information about the space; its sole function is to visually enact Scottie's psychological absorption, which is exactly the kind of "form performing the gaze" this lens trains you to notice. (2) Judy's own subjectivity is visually withheld at the precise moment it matters most — we watch her being watched and remade, but the scene gives us no equivalent access to what she is thinking or wanting, which is the gendered asymmetry Mulvey's theory names. (3) The green neon lighting marks the gaze as unnatural/constructed — by bathing the "reveal" in artificial, otherworldly colour rather than naturalistic light, Hitchcock visually flags that what Scottie is looking at is his own fantasy projection rather than a real, autonomous person, which is precisely the critique gaze theory equips you to make explicit.

3) 西班牙語課程 — 第二十八天(10 分鐘,銜接第二十七天內容)

第二十七天正式命名了虛擬式現在式,並教了它的第一個觸發情境:表達願望的動詞(querer/esperar que)、且對象是另一個不同的主詞。今天同樣留在虛擬式的世界裡,但換成第二組主要的觸發情境——懷疑、否定,以及「可能性」的非人稱表達——外加一個常見的口語例外,會打破你剛剛才熟悉的規則。

  • Dudo que él tenga razón. — 我懷疑他是對的。
  • No creo que sea buena idea. — 我不認為那是個好主意。
  • Es posible que llueva mañana. — 明天可能會下雨。
  • No es cierto que ella lo sepa. — 她知道這件事並非事實/並不確定。

文法重點:虛擬式並不只用在表達願望上,而是用來標記「說話者並未主張為既定事實」的任何內容。這正是為什麼「creer que(相信⋯)」在肯定句時用直陳式(Creo que tiene razón——我相信他是對的,把這件事當成事實來陳述),但一加上否定就切換成虛擬式(No creo que tenga razón——我不相信他是對的,這時已經明確表示「不主張這是事實」);同樣的切換規則也適用於「es cierto/verdad que(確實如此)」(用直陳式)對比「no es cierto/verdad que(並非如此)」(用虛擬式)。「dudar que(懷疑)」則不論肯定或否定形式都固定搭配虛擬式,因為「懷疑」這個動作本身,關注的就是說話者心中的不確定感。「es posible/probable que」這類非人稱可能性表達,同樣因為描述的是「尚未確定會不會發生」的事,所以也要用虛擬式。今天的陷阱在於:日常口語中的「a lo mejor(可能、也許)」,意思幾乎和「es posible que」一樣,但依照母語者的慣用習慣,後面反而習慣搭配直陳式(「A lo mejor llueve mañana」,而不是「llueva」)——這是教科書規則不會預先告訴你的母語者習慣,也是學習者最容易覺得「聽起來怪怪的,但其實沒有錯」的地方之一。

4) 易經卦象

第二十八卦:大過卦(Dà Guò)——「過度之道」(上兌下巽,澤上風/木下)。〈序卦傳〉直接銜接了昨天的第二十七卦:「頤者,養也。不養則不可動,故受之以大過。」(頤,意即養育;若不經過養育,便無法有所行動,因此頤卦之後,便是大過卦。)這正是昨天邏輯的進一步延伸:凡是獲得充分養育的事物,終究會積蓄足夠的力量而有所作為——但這樣的行動,有時必須超越尋常的度量,才能應付非比尋常的處境。卦辭寫道:「大過:棟橈。利有攸往,亨。」(大過卦象徵:房屋的棟樑因負荷過重而彎曲下垂。此時宜勇於前行,方能有所成就。)這一卦的卦象,恰好把這個意象具體呈現出來:中間四條陽爻密集堆疊,上下兩端卻各只有一條陰爻支撐——就像一根中段負荷過重、兩端支撐力卻相對薄弱的棟樑,承受著自身內部集中的巨大力量,隨時可能彎曲甚至斷裂。〈象傳〉補充道:「澤滅木,大過;君子以獨立不懼,遯世無悶。」(澤水高漲、淹沒了樹木,這正是大過卦的象徵;因此君子在此時應當獨立而行、無所畏懼,即便選擇退隱於世,也能坦然自處,毫無怨懟或鬱悶。)綜合來看,這一卦教導的是:面對需要超越尋常度量、採取非常手段的時刻,這樣的行動不僅必要、也確實可能成功——但其中蘊藏著真實的結構性風險,因此更需要內在的沉穩與定力,才能在行動(或刻意選擇不行動)時,不因這個選擇在旁人眼中顯得孤立或不合常規,而動搖了自己的判斷。

5) 國家/地區/政治實體介紹 — 玻利維亞(Bolivia)

  • 位置: 位於南美洲中西部的內陸國,北側與東側鄰接巴西、東南側鄰接巴拉圭、南側鄰接阿根廷、西南側鄰接智利、西側鄰接秘魯。
  • 領土面積: 約 1,098,581 平方公里——大致相當於法國與西班牙兩國面積的總和,約為台灣(約 3.62 萬平方公里)的 30 倍;地形跨越安地斯山區的高原(首都拉巴斯即位於此,海拔約 3,640 公尺,是全世界海拔最高的行政首都)一直到亞馬遜低地雨林,境內還擁有全世界最大的鹽沼——烏尤尼鹽沼(Salar de Uyuni)。
  • 人口: 約 1,240 萬人(2024 年估計),境內有超過 40 個獲得官方承認的原住民族;約 40% 的人口自我認定為原住民,是美洲地區比例最高的國家之一。
  • 宗教: 以羅馬天主教為主(約占 70%),新教/福傳派信仰增長迅速(約占 15% 至 19%);原住民傳統信仰、尤其是對「大地之母」帕查瑪瑪(Pachamama)的崇敬,至今仍廣泛實踐,通常與天主教信仰並存融合,而非被取代。
  • 經濟: 高度依賴自然資源,以人均 GDP 而言屬於南美洲較低所得的經濟體,但天然氣與錫礦資源豐富,近年來戰略地位日益重要的鋰礦儲量更被估計是全世界最大之一,主要集中在烏尤尼鹽沼地下;農業(大豆,以及玻利維亞是全球數一數二的藜麥出口國)也具重要地位。玻利維亞的特殊之處在於擁有兩個首都:蘇克雷(Sucre)是憲法/司法首都,拉巴斯(La Paz)則是政府與國會實際所在地。
  • 歷史: 曾是印加帝國的一部分,後成為西班牙「上秘魯」(Alto Perú)轄地,波托西(Potosí)的富饒山(Cerro Rico)所產的白銀,曾支撐西班牙帝國數百年的財政。1825 年獨立,國名取自解放者西蒙・玻利瓦爾(Simón Bolívar),首任總統安東尼奧・何塞・德・蘇克雷(Antonio José de Sucre)的名字則成為憲法首都之名。玻利維亞在「太平洋戰爭」(1879-1884)中被智利奪走太平洋沿海領土,又在「查科戰爭」(1932-1935)中被巴拉圭奪走部分領土;長期以來政局動盪不安,常被引述為獨立以來政權更迭次數最多的國家之一。2005 年當選的埃沃・莫拉萊斯(Evo Morales)是玻利維亞史上首位原住民出身的總統,以「社會主義運動黨」(MAS)執政近 14 年(2006-2019)。
  • 現任領導人: 總統羅德里戈・帕斯・佩雷拉(Rodrigo Paz Pereira),為第 68 任總統,於 2025 年 11 月 8 日就任,終結了 MAS 黨近二十年的執政;上任第一年即面臨嚴峻的治理危機,包括 2026 年 4 月的抗議潮,以及 2026 年 6 月因民生物價與燃料/食品供應中斷相關的封路行動而宣布的緊急狀態。
  • 語言: 官方語言以西班牙語為主流通用語,憲法另承認 36 種原住民官方語言,其中以克丘亞語(Quechua)與艾馬拉語(Aymara)最為普及。

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 傻眼貓咪("Blank/Stunned Cat")

  • 名稱: 「傻眼貓咪」——字面意思是「目瞪口呆、傻掉的貓」,原本是一句四字流行語,而非一開始就是圖片哏。
  • 起源/歷史: 這句流行語的爆紅起點頗為特殊——並非來自笑話或圖片,而是一起真實刑案:2017 年 4 月至 5 月間,高雄警方在查緝毒趴時,查扣了一批稱為「毒咖啡」的混合式毒品咖啡包,外包裝印有一隻藍色卡通貓,搭配大大兩個字「傻眼」。當地媒體報導此案時,這款吸睛的包裝圖案迅速廣為流傳。
  • 如何爆紅: 這句流行語出現時,社群上早已醞釀出接受這類四字反應語的氛圍——先是 2016 年的「藍瘦香菇」(源自廣西口音「難受,想哭」的諧音哏)與「黑人問號」(源自一張爆紅 NBA 球星照片),再加上更早的「無言薯條」圖片哏。等到 2017 年 5 月又一起更受矚目的毒品查緝案曝光後,「傻眼貓咪」便在華語社群媒體上迅速蔓延;主流媒體則在 2017 年 8 月大量刊出解釋文章,當時該詞在 Google 搜尋趨勢上也達到高峰。
  • 意義與使用情境: 用來表達說不出話的傻眼、無奈,或是「不知該如何反應」的情緒狀態,功能上近似英語的「I'm speechless」或「wtf」;引伸用法也可以用來形容某人行為失常、看起來「很嗨/很茫」(呼應其毒品相關的起源背景)。
  • 變體與演變: 衍生出大量搭配貓咪吉祥物形象的貼圖包(在 LINE 上廣泛流通)、把這句話搭配各種反應圖的哏圖模板,而且在日常口語中,經常和它同世代的「無言薯條」搭配使用,成為表達「對此我無話可說」的固定組合語。
  • 目前現況: 雖然已經過了 2017 年的爆紅高峰期,但這句流行語早已完全融入台灣/華語圈的日常網路與口語用語之中——至今仍常被使用,尤其是透過貼圖的形式,作為一種輕鬆表達「傻眼、無言以對」的方式,不需要多加解釋。
    資料來源:zh.wikipedia.org、news.ltn.com.tw

7) 電影/表演藝術介紹 — 芭蕾舞劇《天鵝湖》(Swan Lake / Лебединое озеро,1877/1895;柴可夫斯基作曲,馬呂斯・佩提帕與列夫・伊凡諾夫編舞)

  • 名稱/創作者: 《天鵝湖》,四幕古典芭蕾舞劇。音樂由彼得・伊里奇・柴可夫斯基(Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky)創作(1875-76 年);如今被視為經典版本的編舞,則歸功於馬呂斯・佩提帕(Marius Petipa,編創第一、三幕)與其助手列夫・伊凡諾夫(Lev Ivanov,編創第二、四幕),樂譜則經指揮家里卡多・德里戈(Riccardo Drigo)修訂。
  • 時代背景: 由莫斯科波修瓦劇院(Bolshoi Theatre)委託創作,1877 年首演時由朱利葉斯・雷辛格(Julius Reisinger)編舞——這個版本普遍被認為是失敗之作,舞台呈現備受批評。直到柴可夫斯基 1893 年逝世之後,聖彼得堡皇家馬林斯基劇院(Mariinsky Theatre)才搬演由佩提帕與伊凡諾夫重新編排的版本(1895 年),自此成為這部舞劇歷久不衰的標準版本。
  • 劇情概述: 齊格菲王子愛上了公主奧黑塔(Odette),她被巫師馮・羅特巴特(Von Rothbart)施下詛咒變成天鵝,唯有從未對他人許下真愛誓言的男子起誓,才能破除詛咒。在一場宮廷舞會上,馮・羅特巴特設計讓齊格菲誤將愛的誓言,說給了奧黑塔的黑暗分身「黑天鵝」奧迪兒(Odile),使詛咒無法破解;至於最終結局究竟是悲劇收場(戀人唯有以死相守)還是圓滿收場(愛戰勝了巫師),則依製作版本與時代不同而有差異,尤其是蘇聯時期前後的版本差異最為明顯。
  • 藝術風格與技巧: 全劇建立在佩提帕「大型芭蕾」的結構範式之上——正式的雙人舞(pas de deux)、大篇幅的群舞(corps de ballet)場面,以及第三幕的獨舞展技段落;其中最為人熟知的,是第二、四幕群舞演員排列成幾何隊形、動作齊一如「天鵝群」的場面,以及奧黑塔/奧迪兒一人分飾兩角所帶來的強烈技巧對比——奧黑塔柔美、雙臂如羽的「白天鵝」慢板舞段,對比奧迪兒銳利耀眼的「黑天鵝」變奏,並以著名到近乎折磨舞者的連續 32 圈「揮鞭轉」(fouetté)作為壓軸。
  • 文化/歷史意義: 柴可夫斯基這部情感濃烈、交響化程度極高的樂譜,起初曾被認為過於複雜、不夠「適合芭蕾」,但其最終獲得的成功,證明了芭蕾音樂同樣能承載音樂會或歌劇作品般的作曲重量,也重新塑造了後世芭蕾配樂的創作方式(包括柴可夫斯基自己後來創作的《睡美人》與《胡桃鉗》)。
  • 影響與傳承: 白天鵝那彎曲的背部與如翅膀般展開的手臂舞姿(port de bras),是整個舞蹈藝術領域中全球辨識度最高的意象之一;黑天鵝的 32 圈揮鞭轉,則成為全世界芭蕾舞者公認的技巧試煉指標。這部舞劇也啟發了無數重新詮釋的版本,從馬修・伯恩(Matthew Bourne)1995 年全男班編制的《天鵝湖》,到它在 2010 年電影《黑天鵝》(Black Swan)中扮演的核心角色。
  • 現況與觀賞管道: 是全世界演出頻率最高的芭蕾舞劇之一,幾乎每個樂季都會由全球各大重要舞團搬演——包括俄羅斯的波修瓦劇院與馬林斯基劇院、英國皇家芭蕾舞團、巴黎歌劇院芭蕾舞團、美國芭蕾舞劇院等,此外也有大量巡演版本與錄影版本,可供在家觀賞。
    資料來源:en.wikipedia.org、roh.org.uk

8) 看片方法 — 凝視理論與觀影主體性 The Gaze / Spectatorship Theory(第十四課)

第一到十三課教了場面調度、剪輯與蒙太奇、攝影與燈光、音效、色彩、敘事結構、走位與空間調度、取景與構圖、象徵與視覺隱喻、作者論、長鏡頭、表演/演技分析,以及類型與慣例分析——這些視角關注的,是「畫面裡有什麼」以及「一部電影是怎麼被建構出來的」。今天的分析視角則反過來問:攝影機呈現的不是「看到了什麼」,而是攝影機正在扮演誰的目光,以及這樣的安排,把觀眾放進了什麼樣的位置。

方法說明: 電影理論家蘿拉・莫維(Laura Mulvey)在 1975 年發表的論文《視覺愉悅與敘事電影》(Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema)中提出:古典好萊塢電影的敘事結構,普遍建立在一種主動、具支配性的「男性凝視」之上——攝影機、故事中的男性角色,以及觀眾三方的目光被安排成一致地「看著」女性、把她當成視覺上的觀賞對象,而她本人卻鮮少被賦予同等具結構性的目光。運用這個分析視角時,可以自問:(1)攝影機借用了誰的眼睛? 留意主觀鏡頭(POV shot),或是單純長時間停留在某個角色注視畫面的臉部特寫——電影正藉此告訴你,誰是「目光的持有者」,誰又被安排成「被注視的對象」。(2)劇情是否因「注視」這個行為本身,而受到懲罰、獎賞,或獲得解決? 古典敘事常常整個劇情都建立在某個男性角色「必須調查、占有,或解開他所注視女性身上謎團」的需求之上——「凝視」本身變成了推動劇情的引擎,而不只是一種風格選擇。(3)觀眾的目光,是與某個角色的目光重疊在一起,還是被刻意區隔開來? 一部電影可以毫不加以質疑地邀請你分享某個角色帶有偷窺或支配意味的目光,也可以揭露並使這種目光變得複雜——讓你「看見那個正在看的人」,使這整套機制本身變得可見、可以被檢視與批判,而不只是被單純消費。(4)誰的目光被剝奪了? 留意哪些角色從未獲得對等的主觀鏡頭——莫維指出,在古典好萊塢電影中,這種缺席通常沿著性別界線劃分,但同樣的問題,也能延伸去檢視任何一部電影中,關於種族、階級等其他結構性缺席的安排。

實例示範——《迷魂記》(Vertigo,1958,導演亞佛烈德・希區考克 Alfred Hitchcock)的旅館房間變身場景: 《迷魂記》是凝視理論相關學術討論中最常被引用的單一電影作品,主要正是因為這一段場景。退休警探史考提,一直執著地試圖把店員茱蒂改造成瑪德蓮的模樣——瑪德蓮是他深愛卻未能救下的女子(表面上已經死亡)。當茱蒂終於梳著和瑪德蓮完全相同的髮型、穿著相同的洋裝從浴室走出來,整個人被窗外旅館霓虹燈招牌的綠光完全籠罩時,希區考克先讓鏡頭停留在史考提出神凝望的臉上,接著攝影機以完整不間斷的 360 度環繞運鏡,拍下兩人親吻的畫面——就在旋轉的過程中,旅館房間畫面溶接成史考提記憶中、他未能阻止瑪德蓮「死亡」的西班牙教堂馬廄場景,隨後又轉回當下。整場戲的場面調度、鏡頭語言與節奏,完全是圍繞著史考提的「目光」建構而成:茱蒂在這一刻,並沒有獲得任何對等的主觀鏡頭;攝影機的旋轉運動,與其說是在向我們展示這個房間的空間,不如說是在演繹史考提那股執念、渴望把眼前的女人與記憶中的形象合而為一的支配性欲望。

該注意什麼、為什麼這樣的手法有效:(1)攝影機的運動本身就是論點,而不只是裝飾——這段 360 度環繞運鏡,並沒有揭露任何關於這個空間的新資訊;它唯一的功能,就是以視覺方式演繹史考提心理上的全然沉溺,這正是這個分析視角訓練你去留意的「形式在扮演凝視」的典型案例。(2)茱蒂本身的主體性,恰好在最關鍵的時刻被剝奪了視覺呈現——我們看著她被注視、被改造,但這場戲完全沒有給我們對等的機會,去了解她自己究竟在想什麼、想要什麼,這正是莫維理論所指出的性別不對等現象。(3)綠色霓虹燈光,標記出這種凝視是不自然、被刻意建構出來的——希區考克選擇用人工、超現實的色光,而非自然寫實的光線來呈現這場「揭曉」戲,視覺上等於明白告訴觀眾:史考提所注視的,其實是他自己投射出來的幻想,而不是一個真實、擁有自主性的人——這正是凝視理論賦予你、能夠明確指出的那種批判視角。
