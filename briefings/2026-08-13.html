3) Spanish Lesson — Day 35 (10 min, builds on Day 31 and Day 34)

Day 31 flagged, but did not yet teach, that swapping the verb after Ojalá from present subjunctive to imperfect subjunctive pivots the same word from a hopeful future wish to a wistful, contrary-to-fact one. Today delivers that missing tense: the imperfect subjunctive (Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo). Its formation is unusually convenient — take any verb's preterite "ellos" form (from Days 16-19's preterite lessons), drop -ron, and add -ra/-ras/-ra/-ramos/-ran (an equivalent, more literary -se family also exists). Because it inherits the preterite's own irregularities rather than introducing new ones, a student who has mastered the preterite gets this tense almost for free. Three uses were introduced: Ojalá + imperfect subjunctive for wishes about the present that are unlikely or contrary to fact (Ojalá lloviera); a first preview of "Si + imperfect subjunctive, + conditional" contrary-to-present-fact conditional sentences (full classification to come later); and sequence-of-tenses — when a WEIRDO-triggering main verb sits in a past tense, its subjunctive clause must likewise shift from present to imperfect subjunctive (Mi profesora quería que estudiáramos más).

  • Ojalá lloviera hoy, con este calor. — I wish it were raining today, in this heat. (regretting that it isn't)
  • Si yo tuviera un abanico, te lo prestaría. — If I had a fan, I'd lend it to you. (I don't have one)
  • Mi profesora quería que estudiáramos más antes del examen. — My teacher wanted us to study more before the exam.

Grammar note: the imperfect subjunctive is derived, not memorized separately — every irregular preterite family taught so far (u-stem tener→tuvieron, j-stem decir→dijeron, the ir/ser-shared fueron) carries its irregularity straight through to tuviera, dijera, and fuera. The sequence-of-tenses rule introduced today (a past-tense trigger verb pulls its subjunctive clause back to the imperfect subjunctive) will be built out further in future lessons.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 35: 晉 (Jìn) — "Progress" (Fire above, Earth below — Lí over Kūn). The Xugua (Sequence) commentary follows directly from yesterday's Hexagram 34: "物不可以終壯,故受之以晉。晉者,進也。" ("Things cannot remain forever strong; hence Dà Zhuàng [The Power of the Great] is followed by Jìn [Progress]. Jìn means to advance.") Where yesterday's hexagram was about the raw, potentially reckless surge of gathering strength, Jìn describes what should happen once that strength has matured: a steady, sanctioned, outward advance rather than an untamed show of force. The Judgment reads: "晉,康侯用錫馬蕃庶,晝日三接。" ("Progress: the powerful, pacifying prince is honored with a gift of many horses, and is received by the king three times in a single day.") — an image of advancement being met with recognition and reward, repeated and reaffirmed rather than granted once and forgotten. The Tuan (Commentary on the Judgment) explains the image directly: "晉,進也,明出地上,順而麗乎大明,柔進而上行,是以康侯用錫馬蕃庶,晝日三接也。" ("Jìn means to advance: light rising above the earth. [The lower trigram] is docile and clings to the great light [of the upper trigram]; the yielding element advances and moves upward — hence the prince is honored with many horses and received three times in one day.") The hexagram is built from Lí (Fire/Light, radiant clarity) sitting above Kūn (Earth, receptive docility) — the image of the sun rising above the horizon, its light spreading further and further as it climbs, a natural, unforced picture of expanding illumination rather than sudden conquest. The Image commentary draws the ethical lesson: "明出地上,晉;君子以自昭明德。" ("The light rises above the earth: this is the image of Jìn. Thus the superior person brightens their own inherent bright virtue.") Read together, the hexagram frames genuine progress as something closer to the sunrise than to a military campaign: advancement that unfolds through clarity, docile persistence, and self-cultivated inner light — earning recognition through steady illumination rather than seizing it by force, a natural sequel to yesterday's warning that raw strength must still answer to propriety.

5) Country / Region / Faction Spotlight — Eswatini (史瓦帝尼)

  • Location: A small landlocked kingdom in Southern Africa, bordered almost entirely by South Africa (on the west, north, and south) with a shorter eastern border against Mozambique.
  • Territory: About 17,364 km² — remarkably close in size to Kuwait (17,818 km²), and roughly 48% (just under half) of Taiwan's land area (~36,193 km²), making it one of the smallest countries on mainland Africa.
  • Population: Approximately 1.2 million.
  • Religion: Overwhelmingly Christian (around 90%, split across Protestant, Zionist/African-independent, and Roman Catholic traditions), with many practitioners blending Christian worship with traditional Swazi ancestor veneration and customary rites rather than treating the two as separate systems.
  • Economy: Heavily reliant on sugar production and refining (Eswatini is one of Africa's leading sugar exporters), soft-drink concentrate manufacturing (the country hosts a major regional Coca-Cola concentrate plant supplying bottlers across Africa), textile exports, and Southern African Customs Union (SACU) revenue-sharing; its currency, the lilangeni, is pegged 1:1 to and used interchangeably with the South African rand.
  • History: The Swazi nation was consolidated in the early-to-mid 18th century under King Ngwane III, founder of the Dlamini royal dynasty that still reigns today; became a British protectorate in 1903 amid the scramble for southern Africa; gained independence in 1968 as the Kingdom of Swaziland; in 2018, King Mswati III renamed the country Eswatini ("land of the Swazis") to mark the 50th anniversary of independence and shed a name seen as a colonial-era holdover resembling Switzerland.
  • Current leader: King Mswati III, sub-Saharan Africa's last remaining absolute monarch, on the throne since 1986, who appoints the Prime Minister and retains ultimate authority over the legislature, judiciary, and armed forces; political parties remain banned from contesting elections for parliamentary seats, which are instead partly appointed by the king and partly filled via nonpartisan tinkhundla constituency elections.
  • Language: siSwati and English, both official.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, cia.gov/the-world-factbook

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — "Damn Daniel"

  • Name: "Damn Daniel," a viral catchphrase-and-video meme from early 2016.
  • Origin/history: In February 2016, 14-year-old Riverside, California high schooler Josh Holz posted a compilation of Snapchat videos to Twitter documenting his classmate Daniel Lara repeatedly showing off the same pair of plain white Vans sneakers, narrated with the deadpan catchphrases "Damn, Daniel!" and "Back at it again with the white Vans."
  • How it became popular: The 30-second compilation exploded across Twitter and Vine, racking up tens of millions of views within days and pushing #DamnDaniel to the top of Twitter's US trends; both teenagers were subsequently invited onto The Ellen DeGeneres Show, and Vans reportedly gifted Daniel a lifetime supply of shoes in response to the sudden brand attention.
  • Meaning/usage: The phrase "Damn, Daniel!" is used as an exclamation of surprised, mock-impressed admiration — often deployed ironically toward someone doing something mundane or repetitive with unwarranted confidence or consistency, echoing the original clip's tone toward the unremarkable white sneakers.
  • Variations/evolution: Spawned countless remixes, parody dubs, and sports-highlight/pop-culture caption reuses of "back at it again with the ___"; the phrase became detachable from its original video, applied generically to any repeated minor spectacle.
  • Current status: A well-preserved artifact of the 2016 Vine-era meme cycle, now mostly invoked nostalgically or ironically as shorthand for that specific moment in internet culture rather than actively circulating as fresh content.
    Sources: knowyourmeme.com, en.wikipedia.org

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — Waiting for Godot (等待果陀, 1953, by Samuel Beckett)

  • Name/creators: Written by Irish playwright Samuel Beckett, originally in French as "En attendant Godot" (1948-49), later translated into English by Beckett himself (1954); premiered on stage January 5, 1953 at the Théâtre de Babylone in Paris, directed by Roger Blin.
  • Era/background: Written in the aftermath of World War II, amid the existentialist intellectual climate of postwar Paris, where Beckett (who had served in the French Resistance) was grappling with questions of meaning, waiting, and futility.
  • Plot/content overview: Two tramps, Vladimir and Estragon, wait by a barren tree beside a country road for a figure named Godot, who sends word each evening that he will come "tomorrow" but never appears across either of the play's two acts; their waiting is interrupted by the arrival of the domineering Pozzo and his abused servant Lucky, whose master-slave relationship visibly deteriorates between the acts.
  • Artistic style and techniques: A radically minimalist set (a single tree and a road), circular and repetitive dialogue structure (Act II largely mirrors Act I with variations), absurdist non-sequitur exchanges alternating with music-hall-style physical comedy, and a deliberate absence of conventional plot resolution — Beckett himself labeled it "a tragicomedy in two acts."
  • Cultural/historical significance: Widely regarded as the founding, defining text of the "Theatre of the Absurd" (a term later coined by critic Martin Esslin to describe this and related postwar drama), it radically broke with conventional dramatic structure by refusing exposition, resolution, or even clear meaning, forcing audiences and critics to confront theatre that portrayed existence as inherently absurd and directionless.
  • Influence and legacy: Critic Vivian Mercier's famous description — "a play in which nothing happens, twice" — captures both its initial reception as bewildering and its later canonization as a landmark of 20th-century drama; a celebrated 1957 production at San Quentin State Prison, performed for convicted inmates, became legendary for how directly its themes of waiting and confinement resonated with that audience; the play's influence extends across absurdist and postmodern theatre, film, and literature broadly.
  • Current status/where to experience it: Continuously in production worldwide in professional, community, and academic theatre; a staple text in drama and literature curricula; published editions and filmed stage productions (including a 2001 Beckett on Film adaptation directed by Michael Lindsay-Hogg) remain widely available.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, britannica.com

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Shot Scale / Size: Close-Up vs. Wide Shot (Lesson 21)

Shot scale (also called "shot size") is one of the most basic yet most consequential choices a director makes: how close or far the camera sits from its subject, ranging from the extreme wide/establishing shot down to the extreme close-up. Three things to watch for: first, contrast — cutting between a tight close-up and a wide shot within the same sequence can build tension purely through the rhythm of scale change, independent of what's actually happening in the frame; second, withholding — a director who refuses to grant a close-up on a character keeps the audience at emotional arm's length, while an unexpected extreme close-up on an otherwise minor detail signals that the object suddenly matters; third, orientation — an establishing shot grounds the audience in a space's geography, and a director who deliberately withholds one is choosing disorientation as a storytelling effect rather than an oversight. This is distinct from Day 8's depth-of-field lesson (which concerns what's in focus within a shot, not the camera's distance from the subject), Day 10's aspect-ratio lesson (frame shape, not shot distance), and Day 17's framing/composition lesson (arrangement within the frame, not scale).

Worked example: The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (1966, dir. Sergio Leone, cinematography by Tonino Delli Colli) — the climactic three-way standoff at Sad Hill Cemetery. Leone builds nearly the entire sequence out of shot-scale contrast alone: extreme close-ups of Blondie, Angel Eyes, and Tuco's eyes dart and narrow, cut against extreme close-ups of their twitching hands hovering over holsters, all intercut with wide establishing shots of the circular cemetery arena that keep the audience oriented to the geometry of the three-way threat. As Ennio Morricone's score escalates, the cuts between scales accelerate, generating unbearable suspense through shot-scale rhythm alone — with almost no dialogue and minimal camera movement doing the dramatic work instead.

3)西班牙文課程 — 第 35 天(10 分鐘,承接第 31 天與第 34 天)

第 31 天曾經預告、但尚未正式教過:只要把 Ojalá 後面的動詞從虛擬式現在式換成虛擬式過去未完了式,同一個字就能從「對未來仍抱有希望的願望」,轉變成「對現況感到惋惜、甚至明知不太可能實現」的假設語氣。今天正式補上這個缺漏的時態:虛擬式過去未完了式(Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo)。它的變化方式格外方便——取任何動詞簡單過去式(第 16~19 天教過)的「他們」形,去掉字尾 -ron,再加上 -ra/-ras/-ra/-ramos/-ran(另外還有一組意義相同、較書面語的 -se 字尾家族)。因為這個時態直接繼承簡單過去式本身的不規則變化,而不是另外自成一套新的不規則系統,只要簡單過去式學得紮實,這個新時態幾乎可以說是「免費」附贈的。今天介紹了三種用法:Ojalá + 虛擬式過去未完了式,表達對現況「不太可能、甚至與事實相反」的惋惜願望(Ojalá lloviera);初步預告「Si + 虛擬式過去未完了式,+ 條件式簡單式」這種與現在事實相反的條件句結構(完整分類留待後續課程);以及「時態呼應」——當 WEIRDO 類型的觸發動詞是過去式時,後面的虛擬式子句也必須跟著從現在式退回過去未完了式(Mi profesora quería que estudiáramos más)。

  • Ojalá lloviera hoy, con este calor. — 這麼熱,真希望今天在下雨就好了。(可惜此刻並沒有下雨)
  • Si yo tuviera un abanico, te lo prestaría. — 如果我有一把扇子的話,我會借給你。(其實我沒有扇子)
  • Mi profesora quería que estudiáramos más antes del examen. — 我的老師(當時)希望我們考前多讀一點書。

文法筆記:虛擬式過去未完了式是「推導」出來的,不需要另外死背——目前為止教過的每一組不規則簡單過去式家族(u-字根 tener→tuvieron、j-字根 decir→dijeron、ir/ser 共用的 fueron),它們的不規則性都會直接延續到 tuviera、dijera、fuera 這些過去未完了式虛擬式形態上。今天介紹的時態呼應規則(過去式的觸發動詞,會把後面的虛擬式子句拉回過去未完了式),之後的課程還會進一步完整展開。

第 35 卦:晉 (Jìn) ——「前進、晉升」(離上坤下,火在上、地在下)。〈序卦傳〉直接承接昨天的第 34 卦而來:「物不可以終壯,故受之以晉。晉者,進也。」(事物不可能永遠停留在強盛壯大的狀態,所以〈大壯〉之後接著是〈晉〉。晉,就是前進、晉升的意思。)昨天的大壯卦談的是力量剛剛壯大、甚至帶有躁進風險的奮起狀態;晉卦則描寫這股力量成熟之後應有的樣貌:一種穩健、受到肯定、循序漸進的向外推展,而不是失控的武力展示。〈卦辭〉說:「晉,康侯用錫馬蕃庶,晝日三接。」(晉卦:能安定邦國的諸侯,獲賜眾多良馬作為獎賞,一天之內三度受到君王接見。)——這是一幅「前進獲得肯定與獎賞」的畫面,而且這份肯定是反覆確認、持續給予的,而非曇花一現、給了就忘。〈彖傳〉直接闡明了卦象:「晉,進也,明出地上,順而麗乎大明,柔進而上行,是以康侯用錫馬蕃庶,晝日三接也。」(晉,就是前進的意思:光明從地面升起。下卦柔順,依附著上卦的大放光明;柔順之德向上推進、逐步上升,因此諸侯獲賜眾多良馬,並一天三次受到接見。)這一卦是由離卦(火、光明,象徵光輝與清明)疊在坤卦(地,象徵柔順包容)之上組成——正是太陽從地平線升起、光芒隨著升高而逐漸擴散的景象,是一種自然而不勉強的「光明擴展」畫面,而不是突如其來的武力征服。〈象傳〉點出了其中的道德意涵:「明出地上,晉;君子以自昭明德。」(光明從地面升起:這就是晉卦的象。因此君子要以此彰顯、發揚自己內在原有的光明德性。)三段文字合在一起看,這一卦把真正的「前進」描繪得更接近日出,而非軍事征伐:前進是透過清明、柔順而持之以恆的堅持,以及自我修養而發散出來的內在光明來實現——是靠穩健的光輝逐步贏得肯定,而不是靠武力強行奪取,恰好呼應了昨天大壯卦「強大的力量仍須服膺於禮節分寸」這項警示的自然後續。

5)國家/地區/勢力介紹 — 史瓦帝尼(Eswatini)

  • 位置:史瓦帝尼是南部非洲一個袖珍的內陸王國,幾乎完全被南非包圍(西側、北側與南側皆與南非接壤),僅東側與莫三比克有一小段邊界。
  • 領土規模:國土總面積約 17,364 平方公里——與科威特(17,818 平方公里)的面積相當接近,約為台灣陸地面積(約 36,193 平方公里)的 48%,略小於一半,是非洲大陸上面積最小的國家之一。
  • 人口:約 120 萬人。
  • 宗教:絕大多數信奉基督教(約 90%,涵蓋新教、非洲獨立錫安教派與羅馬天主教等不同傳統),許多信徒同時融合基督教信仰與傳統史瓦濟祖先崇拜及慣習儀式,並不將兩者視為互斥的兩套體系。
  • 經濟:高度依賴蔗糖種植與精煉產業(史瓦帝尼是非洲主要的蔗糖出口國之一)、汽水濃縮液製造(境內設有供應非洲多國裝瓶廠的大型可口可樂濃縮液工廠)、紡織品出口,以及南部非洲關稅同盟(SACU)的關稅收入分潤;其貨幣里蘭吉尼(lilangeni)與南非蘭特採 1:1 固定匯率,並可互通使用。
  • 歷史:史瓦濟民族於 18 世紀中葉、在恩格瓦內三世國王(King Ngwane III)統治下逐漸整合成形,他也是至今仍在位的德拉米尼(Dlamini)王室世系的奠基者;1903 年在列強瓜分南部非洲的浪潮中淪為英國保護地;1968 年以「史瓦濟蘭王國」之名獨立建國;2018 年,姆斯瓦蒂三世國王(King Mswati III)為紀念獨立五十週年,將國名更改為「史瓦帝尼」(Eswatini,意為「史瓦濟人的土地」),以擺脫被認為是殖民時代遺留、且容易與瑞士(Switzerland)混淆的舊國名。
  • 現任領導人:姆斯瓦蒂三世國王(King Mswati III),是撒哈拉以南非洲僅存的絕對君主,自 1986 年即位至今,擁有任命總理、並對立法、司法與軍事握有最終決定權的實權;政黨至今仍被禁止參與國會議席選舉,國會議席一部分由國王直接任命,另一部分則透過不具政黨色彩的「廷孔達拉」(tinkhundla)選區制度選出。
  • 語言:史瓦濟語(siSwati)與英語,兩者皆為官方語言。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、cia.gov/the-world-factbook

6)網路迷因介紹 —「該死的丹尼爾」(Damn Daniel)

  • 名稱:「Damn Daniel」,一句 2016 年初爆紅的迷因口頭禪與影片。
  • 起源/歷史:2016 年 2 月,美國加州河濱市(Riverside)一名 14 歲高中生 Josh Holz,在 Twitter 上發布了一段由 Snapchat 短片剪輯而成的合輯,內容是他的同學 Daniel Lara 一再展示同一雙純白色 Vans 帆布鞋,並以毫無表情、一本正經的口吻反覆說出「Damn, Daniel!」(該死的,丹尼爾!)與「Back at it again with the white Vans」(又來了,還是那雙白 Vans)。
  • 如何走紅:這段 30 秒的合輯在 Twitter 與 Vine 上迅速爆紅,短短幾天內累積數千萬次觀看,並帶動 #DamnDaniel 登上美國 Twitter 熱門趨勢榜首;兩名少年隨後雙雙受邀上《艾倫秀》(The Ellen DeGeneres Show),而 Vans 也因這波突如其來的品牌關注,據報導贈送 Daniel 終身免費球鞋。
  • 意義/使用情境:「Damn, Daniel!」這句話被用來表達一種帶著驚訝、半是誇張讚嘆的感嘆語氣——通常用在諷刺某人明明做著平凡無奇、甚至重複性的小事,卻表現得異常自信或一貫堅持的情境,呼應原始影片中對那雙「普通白鞋」誇張反應的語氣。
  • 變體/演變:衍生出數不清的混剪、配音惡搞版本,以及「back at it again with the ___」這句話被套用在運動精華片段或流行文化梗圖上的各種再利用;這句台詞後來甚至脫離了原始影片,被廣泛套用在任何「重複出現的小型高光時刻」上。
  • 現況:如今是 2016 年 Vine 時代迷因週期中保存完整的一個經典案例,多半被用來懷舊或反諷式地指涉那個特定的網路文化時刻,而非仍在持續產生新內容的活躍迷因。
    來源:knowyourmeme.com、en.wikipedia.org

7)電影/表演藝術介紹 —《等待果陀》(Waiting for Godot,1953,山繆·貝克特)

  • 名稱/創作者:由愛爾蘭劇作家山繆·貝克特(Samuel Beckett)創作,原著以法文寫成,劇名為「En attendant Godot」(1948-49 年),後由貝克特本人親自譯為英文版(1954 年);於 1953 年 1 月 5 日在巴黎巴比倫劇院(Théâtre de Babylone)首演,導演為羅傑·布林(Roger Blin)。
  • 時代背景:創作於第二次世界大戰結束後不久,正值戰後巴黎存在主義思潮盛行的年代——曾參與法國反抗軍的貝克特,在此時深刻思索意義、等待與徒勞的命題。
  • 劇情概述:兩名流浪漢,弗拉迪米爾(Vladimir)與艾斯特拉岡(Estragon),在鄉間小路旁一棵光禿禿的樹下,等待一位名叫「果陀」(Godot)的人物——此人每晚都會捎來口信說自己「明天」就會到,卻在全劇兩幕之中始終沒有出現;兩人的等待過程,被跋扈的波佐(Pozzo)與遭受虐待的僕人幸運兒(Lucky)的到來所打斷,而這對主僕之間的權力關係,在兩幕之間出現了明顯的惡化。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:極度簡約的舞台佈景(僅有一棵樹與一條道路)、循環往復的重複性對話結構(第二幕在變化中大致重演第一幕的內容)、荒謬無厘頭的對話交錯著雜耍劇式的肢體喜劇橋段,以及刻意不提供傳統戲劇該有的情節解決——貝克特本人將此劇定位為「一齣兩幕的悲喜劇」。
  • 文化/歷史意義:普遍被視為「荒謬劇場」(Theatre of the Absurd,此術語後由評論家馬丁·艾斯林〔Martin Esslin〕提出,用以概括本劇與同時期相關的戰後戲劇)的奠基與代表之作,它徹底顛覆了傳統戲劇結構,拒絕提供明確的鋪陳、結局,甚至清楚的意義,迫使觀眾與評論界正視一種將人類存在本身描繪為根本荒謬且毫無方向的劇場形式。
  • 影響與傳承:評論家薇薇安·梅西耶(Vivian Mercier)那句著名的形容——「一齣『什麼事都沒發生』發生了兩次的戲」——精準捕捉了此劇最初令人費解的接受度,以及後來被奉為二十世紀戲劇經典的轉折;1957 年在聖昆丁州立監獄(San Quentin State Prison)為受刑人演出的著名製作,因其「等待」與「囚禁」的主題與該群觀眾產生強烈共鳴而傳為佳話;本劇的影響力廣泛擴及荒謬主義與後現代主義的戲劇、電影與文學領域。
  • 現況/觀賞方式:至今仍持續在全球職業劇場、社區劇團與學院製作中演出;是戲劇與文學課程中的必修經典文本;出版劇本與已拍攝的舞台演出影像紀錄(包括 2001 年由麥可·林賽-霍格〔Michael Lindsay-Hogg〕執導的「Beckett on Film」電影版)皆廣泛可得。
    來源:en.wikipedia.org、britannica.com

8)電影賞析方法 — 景別:特寫鏡頭與遠景鏡頭(第 21 課)

景別(shot scale / shot size,也就是攝影機與拍攝主體之間的距離遠近)是導演最基本、卻也影響最深遠的選擇之一,範圍從極遠景/建立鏡頭(establishing shot),一路涵蓋到大特寫(extreme close-up)。分析時要留意三件事:第一是「對比」——在同一場戲裡,將緊湊的特寫鏡頭與遠景鏡頭交錯剪接,單靠景別切換的節奏本身,就能營造張力,與畫面裡實際發生的動作內容無關;第二是「保留與釋放」——導演如果刻意不給某個角色特寫鏡頭,會讓觀眾與這個角色維持一種情感上的距離感,而在原本無關緊要的細節上突然給出意料之外的大特寫,則是在向觀眾宣告這個物件突然變得重要;第三是「空間定位」——建立鏡頭能讓觀眾掌握一個空間的地理方位,而導演若刻意不給建立鏡頭,則是主動選擇用「迷失方向感」作為敘事效果,而非疏漏。這與第 8 課的景深課程(關注畫面中「什麼是清晰對焦的」,而非攝影機與主體的距離)、第 10 課的畫面比例課程(關注畫框形狀,而非拍攝距離),以及第 17 課的取景與構圖課程(關注畫面內的元素排列,而非景別大小)都不相同。

實例演練:《黃昏三鏢客》(The Good, the Bad and the Ugly,1966,導演:塞吉歐·李昂尼 Sergio Leone,攝影:托尼諾·德利科利 Tonino Delli Colli)——片尾在「悲傷之丘」墓園的三方對峙戲。李昂尼幾乎完全靠景別的對比,撐起了這整場戲:金髮客(Blondie)、天使眼(Angel Eyes)與圖科(Tuco)三人眼神游移、瞇起雙眼的大特寫,交錯剪接著他們手指在槍套上方顫抖懸停的大特寫,再穿插著呈現整座圓形墓園場地的遠景建立鏡頭,讓觀眾始終掌握這場三方對峙的空間幾何關係。隨著顏尼歐·莫利克奈(Ennio Morricone)的配樂逐漸升溫,鏡頭在不同景別之間切換的速度也隨之加快,單靠景別節奏的變化,就營造出令人幾乎無法承受的懸疑張力——幾乎沒有台詞,運鏡本身也極為節制,真正推動這場戲戲劇張力的,正是景別本身。
