4) Daodejing (道德經) — Chapter 22: 曲則全 (Qū zé quán) — "Bent, Then Whole"

  • Context: The I Ching and Zhuangzi sequences are used up, so this section continues through the Daodejing in order. Yesterday was Chapter 21.
  • Text (received text): 曲則全,枉則直,窪則盈,敝則新,少則得,多則惑。是以聖人抱一為天下式。不自見,故明;不自是,故彰;不自伐,故有功;不自矜,故長。夫唯不爭,故天下莫能與之爭。古之所謂「曲則全」者,豈虛言哉!誠全而歸之。
  • Meaning: Bent, then whole; crooked, then straight; hollow, then full; worn out, then new; having little, then gaining; having much, then confused. So the sage holds to the One and becomes the model for the world. He does not show himself, so he is seen clearly; he does not insist he is right, so he stands out; he does not boast, so he has merit; he does not brag, so he endures. Because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him. The old saying "bent, then whole": is it empty talk? Truly, wholeness returns to it.
  • Key terms: 曲 (bent, yielding) and 枉 (crooked) are the images: a tree that bends survives the storm; a measuring line that is first bent can be pulled straight. 窪 is a hollow in the ground that fills with water. 抱一 ("embracing the One") repeats Chapter 10. The four "不自…" phrases (見, 是, 伐, 矜) are four kinds of self-display; Chapter 24 later restates them in the negative (自見者不明…).
  • Reading it: Chapter 22 turns the paradoxes of Chapter 2 into practical advice. Each pair says that the "lesser" state carries the seed of the "greater" one: emptiness can be filled, oldness can be renewed. The political point is in the middle: the person who does not push himself forward is the one others notice and follow. "夫唯不爭,故天下莫能與之爭" connects back to Chapter 8 (water does not contend). The last line claims the proverb is ancient wisdom that Laozi is quoting, not inventing.

5) Country Spotlight — Namibia (納米比亞)

  • Location: south-western Africa, on the Atlantic coast. Borders Angola and Zambia to the north, Botswana to the east and South Africa to the south and south-east. The narrow Caprivi (Zambezi) Strip in the north-east reaches the Zambezi River and almost touches Zimbabwe.
  • Size: about 825,600 km²: slightly larger than Türkiye, about twice California, and roughly 23 times Taiwan.
  • Population: about 3.0 million (2023 census: about 3.02 million), roughly the population of Taipei City in a country 23 times Taiwan's size. At under 4 people per km², it is one of the most thinly populated countries in the world, second only to Mongolia among sovereign states. Capital and largest city: Windhoek.
  • Religion: about 90% Christian; Lutherans are the largest group, a legacy of German and Finnish missions. Traditional beliefs are often practised alongside Christianity.
  • Economy: upper-middle income but among the world's most unequal by Gini coefficient. Mining dominates exports: diamonds, much of them now dredged from the seabed by ships (a Namdeb/De Beers joint venture), and uranium from the Rössing and Husab mines, making Namibia one of the largest uranium producers. Also fishing, beef, tourism, and large new hopes: offshore oil finds in the Orange Basin (Shell's Graff and TotalEnergies' Venus, both 2022) and a green-hydrogen project near Lüderitz. The Namibian dollar is pegged one-to-one to the South African rand.
  • History: home for millennia to San hunter-gatherers, later to Nama, Herero, Ovambo and other peoples. Germany colonised it as German South West Africa from 1884. In 1904-1908 German troops killed tens of thousands of Herero and Nama, now widely called the first genocide of the 20th century; Germany formally acknowledged it as genocide in 2021. South Africa took over in 1915, ruled under a League of Nations mandate and later in defiance of the UN, and extended apartheid. SWAPO fought a long independence war, and Namibia became independent on 21 March 1990 under President Sam Nujoma.
  • Current leader: President Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah (SWAPO), sworn in on 21 March 2025 as the country's first woman president (from general knowledge; not re-checked today).
  • Language: English is the only official language, chosen at independence as a neutral language. Most people speak Oshiwambo languages at home; Afrikaans is a widespread lingua franca; German, Otjiherero, Rukwangali, Setswana and Khoekhoegowab (a click language) are also spoken.
  • Notable: the Namib is often called the world's oldest desert. Sossusvlei's red dunes and the dead camel-thorn trees of Deadvlei, the shipwrecks of the Skeleton Coast, Etosha's salt pan and wildlife, and Fish River Canyon draw visitors. In 1990 Namibia became the first country to write environmental protection into its constitution, and its community conservancies are a well-known model.

6) Internet Meme — "Two Buttons" / "Daily Struggle"

  • What it looks like: two panels. In the top panel, a hand hovers over two red buttons, each labelled with an option. In the bottom panel, a man in a blue suit sweats and wipes his forehead with a handkerchief, unable to choose.
  • Origin: a webcomic panel titled "Daily Struggle" by the American artist Jake Clark, generally dated to around 2014. People soon blanked out the button labels so anyone could fill them in.
  • How it spread: it became an "exploitable" template on Reddit, Tumblr, Twitter and meme generators around 2016-2017, and has stayed near the top of template sites such as Imgflip since then.
  • Meaning and usage: a dilemma between two choices that are both tempting, or one where the choices contradict each other. Typical jokes: "Lose weight" vs. "Eat the whole pizza"; "Hate my job" vs. "Need money"; brands caught between two contradictory promises. A popular sub-type shows two opinions that a person or group holds at the same time to expose hypocrisy.
  • Variations: three or more buttons; the sweating man alone, as a reaction image for any stressful decision; buttons replaced by doors, apps or political parties; "galaxy-brained" versions where a third button appears.
  • Current status: an evergreen, low-effort template. It is no longer fashionable, but most people still recognise it instantly, and it often appears in classroom, corporate and marketing slides. Chinese-language users usually call it 「兩個按鈕」 or 「流汗選擇」.

7) Film / Performing Arts — Les Misérables (the musical, 1980 / 1985)

  • Creators: music by Claude-Michel Schönberg; original French lyrics by Alain Boublil and Jean-Marc Natel; English lyrics by Herbert Kretzmer; produced by Cameron Mackintosh; the London production was directed by Trevor Nunn and John Caird of the Royal Shakespeare Company.
  • History: it began as a French concept album in 1980, staged that year at the Palais des Sports in Paris. Mackintosh's reworked English version opened with the RSC at the Barbican Centre in London on 8 October 1985, moved to the Palace Theatre that December, and later to the Queen's Theatre (now the Sondheim Theatre). First reviews were poor, but audiences kept coming. It opened on Broadway in 1987 and won eight Tony Awards, including Best Musical.
  • Content: based on Victor Hugo's 1862 novel. The released convict Jean Valjean breaks parole, rebuilds his life, and raises the orphan Cosette, while the police inspector Javert pursues him for decades. The story builds to the student uprising in Paris in June 1832. Main characters include Fantine, the Thénardiers, Marius, Éponine and Enjolras.
  • Style and technique: almost entirely sung ("sung-through"), with recitative linking the songs. Recurring melodies are reused for different characters to make connections: Javert's "Stars" and Valjean's soliloquy share musical material, marking them as mirror images. "One Day More" combines several characters' themes in one ensemble before the barricade. The original staging used a large revolving stage (designer John Napier) so that scenes flowed like film and the barricade could turn to show both sides.
  • Significance: with Cats, The Phantom of the Opera and Miss Saigon, it led the 1980s "megamusical" era: large scale, through-composed scores, global franchised productions and a recognisable logo (the young Cosette). It is the West End's longest-running musical.
  • Legacy: songs such as "I Dreamed a Dream", "On My Own", "Bring Him Home" and "Do You Hear the People Sing?" became standards. Susan Boyle's 2009 Britain's Got Talent audition made "I Dreamed a Dream" a global viral moment. "Do You Hear the People Sing?" has been sung at protests in many places, including Hong Kong. Tom Hooper's 2012 film recorded the singing live on set; Anne Hathaway won an Oscar as Fantine.
  • Where to see it: still running in London at the Sondheim Theatre (in the newer staging without the turntable, first toured in 2009), plus touring and international productions; the 10th and 25th anniversary concerts and the 2012 film are widely available.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 105: Three-Act Structure & Plot Points

  • The method: divide a film into Setup (Act 1), Confrontation (Act 2) and Resolution (Act 3), and locate the plot points: the turns that push the story from one act into the next. The model comes from Aristotle's beginning-middle-end and was popularised for screenwriting by Syd Field's Screenplay (1979), which placed Plot Point 1 about a quarter of the way in and Plot Point 2 about three-quarters in. Earlier lessons covered the Hero's Journey (mythic stages) and scene-level turning points; this lesson is about the large-scale proportions of a whole film.
  • What to look for: (1) Setup: what normal life, characters and problems are established, and which details are planted for later. (2) Plot Point 1: the event that the hero cannot undo, which locks in the main goal. Note the minute it happens. (3) Midpoint: often a new piece of information or a raised stake that changes how the hero pursues the goal. (4) Plot Point 2: the low point or decisive move that launches the final act. (5) Climax and resolution: how the setup details pay off and how the "normal world" from Act 1 has changed. Then time the acts: a long Act 1 or a short Act 3 says something about what the film values.
  • Worked example: Back to the Future (1985, dir. Robert Zemeckis; screenplay by Zemeckis and Bob Gale). Act 1 (1985): Marty McFly's family is defeated (his father George is bullied by Biff), the town clock has been stopped since lightning struck it, and a woman collecting money hands Marty a "Save the clock tower" flyer giving the date and time of the strike. Marty writes his girlfriend's number on the back. Plot Point 1: at the Twin Pines Mall parking lot at night, Doc Brown demonstrates the DeLorean time machine; Libyan terrorists shoot Doc, and Marty escapes in the car, hits 88 mph and lands in 1955, about half an hour into the film. He has no plutonium and cannot go back: the goal is locked.
  • Act 2 (1955): Marty accidentally stops his parents from meeting, and his teenage mother Lorraine falls for him instead. The family photo in his pocket starts to fade, a visible clock on the second goal (get his parents together). Midpoint: Marty still has the flyer; Doc reads it and realises the lightning strike can power the return trip, giving an exact deadline: Saturday night, during the school dance. Now both goals run to the same moment. Plot Point 2: at the "Enchantment Under the Sea" dance, George finally punches Biff and kisses Lorraine, and the photo is restored.
  • Act 3 and payoff: the race to the clock tower, the cable that comes loose, and the lightning strike that sends Marty home. Back in 1985, the opening is reversed: George is confident and successful, and Biff waxes his car. Everything in Act 1 (the stopped clock, the flyer, the bully) returns with a new meaning.
  • Why it works: the structure gives each act a clear question (Act 1: what is wrong with Marty's life? Act 2: can he fix the past and get out? Act 3: will the plan work?). The midpoint merges two goals into one deadline, so the climax answers both at once. The Act 1 plants are tied to objects (a flyer, a photo, a clock), which makes them easy to remember and satisfying to pay off.
  • Limits: three-act structure describes much Hollywood storytelling but is not a law. Kristin Thompson argued that many studio films divide into four parts of similar length, with the midpoint as a real turning point. Films such as Jeanne Dielman (1975) or Yi Yi (2000) are built on rhythm and accumulation instead. Use the model as a ruler for measuring, not as a test of quality.
  • Practice: for the next film you watch, note the minute of the event that makes the hero's goal unavoidable, the midpoint, and the start of the final sequence. Divide by the runtime. Then list three objects or lines from the first act that come back in the last.

  • 白話:能彎曲,才能保全;能屈枉,才能伸直;低窪,才能充盈;破舊,才能更新;少取,反而有得;貪多,反而迷惑。所以聖人持守「一」,作為天下的範式。不自我顯揚,反而看得清楚、被人看見;不自以為是,反而是非彰明;不自我誇耀,反而有功;不自大,反而長久。正因為他不與人爭,天下沒有人能與他相爭。古人所說的「曲則全」,難道是空話嗎?確實能保全而回歸於此。
  • 關鍵字:曲(彎曲、委順)與枉(屈曲)是比喻:能彎的樹在風暴中存活,先彎的繩墨才能拉直。窪是地上的凹處,低了才會積水。抱一呼應第 10 章。四個「不自……」(見、是、伐、矜)是四種自我展示;第 24 章會從反面再說一次(自見者不明……)。
  • 解讀:第 22 章把第 2 章「有無相生」的對立觀,轉成可以實踐的處世建議:每一組都在說「較低」的狀態裡藏著「較高」的種子,空才能裝滿,舊才能更新。中段點出政治含義:不把自己推到前面的人,反而被眾人看見、願意追隨。「夫唯不爭,故天下莫能與之爭」與第 8 章「水善利萬物而不爭」相呼應。最後一句說「曲則全」是古語,老子是引述而非自創。

5) 國家介紹 — 納米比亞(Namibia)

  • 位置:非洲西南部,西臨大西洋。北接安哥拉與尚比亞,東鄰波札那,南與東南接南非。東北角細長的卡普里維(尚比西)走廊延伸到尚比西河,幾乎碰到辛巴威。
  • 面積:約 82.56 萬平方公里:比土耳其略大,約是美國加州的兩倍,約為台灣的 23 倍。
  • 人口:約 300 萬(2023 年普查約 302 萬),大約等於台北市人口,卻分布在 23 個台灣大的土地上。每平方公里不到 4 人,是世界上人口最稀疏的國家之一,在主權國家中僅次於蒙古。首都暨最大城市:溫荷克(Windhoek)。
  • 宗教:約 90% 信仰基督教,其中路德宗最大,源自德國與芬蘭傳教士;不少人同時保有傳統信仰。
  • 經濟:屬中高所得國家,但以吉尼係數衡量是全球最不平等的國家之一。出口以礦業為主:鑽石(如今多由船隻從海床開採,為政府與戴比爾斯的合資事業)與鈾(Rössing 與 Husab 礦,使納米比亞名列主要產鈾國)。此外有漁業、牛肉、觀光,以及新的期待:奧蘭治盆地的海上油田發現(殼牌的 Graff 與道達爾的 Venus,皆在 2022 年),和呂德里茨附近的綠氫計畫。納米比亞元與南非蘭特一比一掛鉤。
  • 歷史:數千年來是桑人狩獵採集者的家園,之後納瑪、赫雷羅、奧萬博等民族遷入。1884 年起成為德屬西南非。1904-1908 年德軍殺害數萬名赫雷羅人與納瑪人,今日普遍被稱為 20 世紀第一場種族滅絕,德國於 2021 年正式承認為種族滅絕。1915 年南非接管,先依國際聯盟委任統治,後來不顧聯合國反對繼續統治並實施種族隔離。人民組織 SWAPO 長期進行獨立戰爭,納米比亞於 1990 年 3 月 21 日獨立,首任總統為努喬馬(Sam Nujoma)。
  • 現任領導人:總統奈圖姆博·南迪-恩戴特瓦(Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah)(SWAPO),2025 年 3 月 21 日就職,是該國首位女性總統(依一般知識撰寫,今天未再查證)。
  • 語言:英語是唯一官方語言,獨立時被選為中立語言。多數人在家說奧萬博語系語言;南非語(Afrikaans)是普遍的共通語;另有德語、赫雷羅語、卡萬戈語、茨瓦納語,以及使用搭嘴音的科伊科伊語(Khoekhoegowab)。
  • 特色:納米布沙漠常被稱為世界最古老的沙漠。索蘇斯維雷的紅色沙丘與死亡谷(Deadvlei)的枯樹、骷髏海岸的沉船、埃托沙鹽沼的野生動物、魚河峽谷都是知名景點。1990 年,納米比亞成為第一個把環境保護寫進憲法的國家,其社區保育區制度也廣為人知。

6) 網路迷因 —「兩個按鈕」Two Buttons/「每日掙扎」Daily Struggle

  • 長什麼樣:兩格漫畫。上格是一隻手懸在兩個紅色按鈕上方,每個按鈕標著一個選項;下格是一位穿藍色西裝的男子滿頭大汗,用手帕擦額頭,不知道該按哪一個。
  • 起源:美國漫畫家傑克·克拉克(Jake Clark)的網路漫畫〈Daily Struggle〉(每日掙扎),一般認為約發表於 2014 年。網友很快把按鈕上的文字塗掉,讓任何人都能自己填。
  • 如何爆紅:約 2016-2017 年成為 Reddit、Tumblr、Twitter 與迷因產生器上的熱門「可套用模板」,此後一直名列 Imgflip 等模板網站的前段班。
  • 意義與用法:表達兩個選項都很誘人,或兩個選項互相矛盾的兩難。典型笑點:「減肥」vs.「吃完整個披薩」;「討厭我的工作」vs.「需要錢」;品牌被夾在兩個互相矛盾的承諾之間。一種常見變體是列出某人或某群體同時抱持的兩種立場,用來諷刺雙重標準。
  • 變化:三個以上的按鈕;只用下格流汗男子,當作任何壓力抉擇的反應圖;把按鈕換成門、App 或政黨;出現第三個按鈕的「腦洞大開」版本。
  • 現況:屬於長青、好上手的模板。雖然不再新潮,但多數人一眼就認得,常出現在課堂、公司簡報與行銷素材中。中文圈常稱為「兩個按鈕」或「流汗選擇」。

7) 電影/表演藝術 — 音樂劇《悲慘世界》(Les Misérables,1980/1985)

  • 主創:作曲克勞德-米歇爾·勳伯格(Claude-Michel Schönberg);法文原詞亞蘭·鮑伯利(Alain Boublil)與 Jean-Marc Natel;英文歌詞赫伯特·克雷茲莫(Herbert Kretzmer);製作人麥金塔(Cameron Mackintosh);倫敦版由皇家莎士比亞劇團的崔佛·努恩(Trevor Nunn)與約翰·凱爾德(John Caird)執導。
  • 歷史:最初是 1980 年的法文概念專輯,同年在巴黎體育館(Palais des Sports)演出。麥金塔改編的英文版於 1985 年 10 月 8 日在倫敦巴比肯中心由皇家莎士比亞劇團首演,同年 12 月移至皇宮劇院,後來再移至女王劇院(現名桑坦劇院)。首演劇評不佳,觀眾卻絡繹不絕。1987 年登上百老匯,獲八座東尼獎,包括最佳音樂劇。
  • 內容:改編自雨果 1862 年的小說。假釋犯尚萬強違反假釋、重新做人,並撫養孤女珂賽特;警探賈維數十年來緊追不捨。故事最後推向 1832 年 6 月巴黎的學生起義。主要角色還有芳婷、德納第夫婦、馬留斯、艾潘妮與安灼拉。
  • 風格與技巧:幾乎全劇都用唱的(sung-through),以宣敘調銜接歌曲。旋律會在不同角色之間重複出現,藉此建立關聯:賈維的〈Stars〉與尚萬強的獨白共用音樂素材,暗示兩人互為鏡像。〈One Day More〉在街壘之戰前把多位角色的主題疊在同一段合唱中。原版舞台使用大型旋轉舞台(設計約翰·納皮爾),讓場景像電影般流動,街壘也能轉動,呈現兩側。
  • 意義:與《貓》《歌劇魅影》《西貢小姐》一起代表 1980 年代的「巨型音樂劇」時代:大規模、全劇譜曲、全球授權複製的製作,以及一眼可辨的標誌(小珂賽特)。它是倫敦西區演出時間最長的音樂劇。
  • 影響:〈I Dreamed a Dream〉〈On My Own〉〈Bring Him Home〉〈Do You Hear the People Sing?〉都成了經典曲目。2009 年蘇珊大嬸(Susan Boyle)在《英國達人秀》演唱〈I Dreamed a Dream〉,成為全球爆紅的一刻。〈Do You Hear the People Sing?〉曾在多地的抗議活動中被傳唱,包括香港。湯姆·霍伯 2012 年的電影版在片場現場收音演唱,安·海瑟威以芳婷一角獲得奧斯卡。
  • 現在哪裡看得到:倫敦桑坦劇院仍在上演(採 2009 年起巡演的新版製作,不再使用旋轉舞台),另有巡演與各國製作;十週年、二十五週年紀念音樂會與 2012 年電影版都很容易取得。

8) 看片方法 — 第 105 課:三幕劇結構與情節轉捩點

  • 方法:把一部電影分成建置(第一幕)、衝突(第二幕)、解決(第三幕),並找出情節轉捩點(plot points):把故事從一幕推進到下一幕的轉折。這個模型源自亞里斯多德的「開頭、中段、結尾」,由席德·菲爾德(Syd Field)1979 年的《實用電影編劇技巧》(Screenplay)推廣到編劇界:第一個轉捩點約在全片四分之一處,第二個約在四分之三處。先前的課程教過「英雄旅程」(神話式階段)與場景層級的轉折;這一課看的是整部電影的大比例。
  • 看什麼:(1) 建置:建立了什麼日常、角色與問題?埋下了哪些之後會用到的細節?(2) 第一轉捩點:主角無法回頭的事件,把主要目標鎖定下來;記下發生在第幾分鐘。(3) 中點:常是一項新資訊或升高的賭注,改變主角追求目標的方式。(4) 第二轉捩點:最低點或決定性的行動,開啟最後一幕。(5) 高潮與結局:建置中的細節如何回收?第一幕的「日常世界」有什麼改變?接著計算各幕長度:第一幕特別長或第三幕特別短,都透露電影重視什麼。
  • 實例:《回到未來》(Back to the Future,1985,導演勞勃·辛密克斯;編劇辛密克斯與鮑伯·蓋爾)。第一幕(1985 年):馬蒂一家很失敗(父親喬治被畢夫欺負);鎮上的鐘自從被雷擊中後就停了;一位募款的女士塞給馬蒂一張「拯救鐘樓」傳單,上面寫著雷擊的日期與時間,馬蒂在背面寫下女友的電話。第一轉捩點:深夜在雙松購物中心停車場,布朗博士示範迪羅倫時光機;利比亞恐怖分子槍擊博士,馬蒂開車逃走,時速衝到 88 英里,回到了 1955 年,約在開演半小時左右。他沒有鈽燃料,回不去:目標就此鎖定。
  • 第二幕(1955 年):馬蒂意外讓父母沒有相遇,年輕的母親羅琳反而愛上了他。口袋裡的全家福照片開始消失,成了第二個目標(讓父母在一起)的看得見的倒數計時。中點:馬蒂身上還留著那張傳單;博士看到後發現可以用雷擊的電力送他回去,於是有了明確的期限:週六晚上,學校舞會期間。從此兩個目標指向同一個時刻。第二轉捩點:在「海底的魔力」舞會上,喬治終於揍了畢夫並親吻羅琳,照片恢復。
  • 第三幕與回收:衝向鐘樓、電纜鬆脫、雷擊把馬蒂送回家。回到 1985 年,開場被翻轉:喬治自信又成功,畢夫反而替他們打蠟洗車。第一幕的每一樣東西(停擺的鐘、傳單、惡霸)都帶著新的意義回來。
  • 為什麼有效:結構讓每一幕都有清楚的問題(第一幕:馬蒂的生活出了什麼問題?第二幕:他能修正過去並離開嗎?第三幕:計畫會成功嗎?)。中點把兩個目標合併成同一個期限,所以高潮能一次回答兩個問題。第一幕埋的伏筆都綁在具體物件上(傳單、照片、時鐘),容易記住,回收時也特別過癮。
  • 限制:三幕劇能描述許多好萊塢敘事,但不是定律。學者克莉絲汀·湯普森(Kristin Thompson)主張許多片廠電影其實分成長度相近的四段,中點是真正的轉折。《珍妮·德爾曼》(1975)或《一一》(2000)等電影則靠節奏與累積來構成。把這個模型當作測量用的尺,而不是評斷好壞的標準。
  • 練習:下一部電影,記下「主角的目標變得無法迴避」那一刻、中點,以及最後段落開始的分鐘數,除以片長。再列出第一幕中三個在最後一幕又出現的物件或台詞。
  • 備註:第 4-8 項內容來自一般常識,今天未查詢即時來源;日期、人名與場景細節為約略值。
