3) Spanish Lesson — Day 13 (10 min, builds on Day 12)

Days 11-12 covered the present tense of regular -ar, -er, and -ir verbs (habitual/current actions). Today's new concept is a very practical one: ir a + infinitivo — using the (highly irregular) verb ir ("to go") plus a plus an unconjugated verb to talk about what someone is going to do, Spanish's everyday "near future" tense.

  • ¿Qué vas a hacer este fin de semana? (keh bahs ah ah-SEHR EHS-teh feen deh seh-MAH-nah) — What are you going to do this weekend?
  • Voy a viajar a Kenting con mis amigos. (boy ah byah-HAR ah Kenting kohn mees ah-MEE-gohs) — I'm going to travel to Kenting with my friends.
  • Ella va a trabajar el sábado. (EH-yah bah ah trah-bah-HAR el SAH-bah-doh) — She is going to work on Saturday.

Grammar note: ir conjugates irregularly — voy (yo), vas (tú), va (él/ella) — bearing no resemblance to the infinitive at all, so these three forms simply have to be memorized. The formula is always: [conjugated ir] + a + [infinitive verb], with the linking a never dropped.

4) I Ching Hexagram

Hexagram 13: 同人 (Tóng Rén) — "Fellowship with Men" (Heaven over Fire). After yesterday's Hexagram 12 (否, Standstill) closed off all connection between high and low, the King Wen Sequence's answer is Fellowship: fire's flame naturally rises to join heaven's light, symbolizing people setting aside narrow self-interest and clan loyalty to unite around a shared, open purpose. The Judgment reads: "Fellowship with men in the open. Success. It furthers one to cross the great water. The perseverance of the superior man furthers." True fellowship, this hexagram teaches, cannot be confined to one's own family or clique — the superior person organizes distinctions among people (rank, ability, background) without letting those distinctions curdle into exclusion, so that cooperation stays broad, principled, and genuinely shared rather than a narrow faction seeking private advantage.

5) Country / Region Spotlight — Fiji

  • Location: A South Pacific archipelago of over 330 islands (about a third inhabited), roughly 3,100 km northeast of New Zealand and east of Vanuatu; the two main islands, Viti Levu and Vanua Levu, hold the vast majority of the population.
  • Territory: About 18,300 km² of land — roughly half the size of Taiwan (~36,200 km²) — but scattered across an exclusive economic zone of well over a million square kilometers of ocean.
  • Population: Approximately 930,000 people.
  • Religion: Predominantly Christian (around 64%, mostly Methodist) among indigenous iTaukei Fijians; Hinduism (~28%) and Islam (~2%) are practiced mainly by the Indo-Fijian community, descended from Indian indentured laborers the British brought over from 1879 to work sugar plantations.
  • Economy: Tourism is the largest sector (resorts, diving, cruise stopovers), alongside sugar exports, bottled water exports (Fiji Water), fishing, and significant remittances; the economy remains highly exposed to cyclones and rising sea levels.
  • History: Settled by Melanesian/Austronesian peoples roughly 3,500 years ago; became a British colony in 1874; the colonial-era importation of Indian indentured labor created a lasting indigenous-Fijian/Indo-Fijian demographic divide that has driven much of its post-independence political turmoil, including coups in 1987, 2000, and 2006. Fiji gained independence from Britain in 1970 and returned to full parliamentary democracy in 2014.
  • Current leader: Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka, in office since December 2022 at the head of a coalition government — the same figure, notably, who led the 1987 military coups before later returning to power through the ballot box.
  • Language: Three official languages — English, Fijian (iTaukei), and Fiji Hindi.
    Sources: en.wikipedia.org, en.wikipedia.org

6) Internet Meme Spotlight — "Lying Flat" (躺平, Tǎng Píng)

  • Name: "Lying Flat," Chinese: 躺平 (tǎng píng).
  • Origin/history: Coined in April 2021 when a former factory worker named Luo Huazhong posted an essay titled "Lying Flat Is Justice" (躺平即是正義) on the Chinese forum Tieba, describing a deliberately minimal lifestyle: doing only the bare minimum to get by while opting out of "996" overwork culture, marriage, homeownership, and consumer striving.
  • How it became popular: The post spread rapidly on Weibo and WeChat among young Chinese exhausted by hyper-competitive schooling and job markets (a pressure widely nicknamed "involution," 內卷). State media initially condemned the mindset as irresponsible and defeatist, and censors scrubbed related merchandise and hashtags — but the crackdown only amplified public awareness, cementing "lying flat" as shorthand for an entire generation's quiet rebellion.
  • Meaning/usage: Describes voluntarily disengaging from the rat race — working just enough to survive, refusing to chase a house, car, marriage, or children under systemic pressure — used earnestly to describe burnout and resignation, and casually/jokingly to mean simply "not trying today."
  • Variations/evolution: Gave rise to related terms such as "let it rot" (擺爛, bǎi làn — actively embracing failure rather than passively resting) and is frequently invoked alongside "involution" (內卷) as the two poles of the same pressure-cooker dynamic; English-language commentary has often drawn a direct parallel to the West's "quiet quitting."
  • Current status: Still a widely cited touchstone in coverage of China's youth unemployment and economic slowdown, and remains one of the defining sociological catchphrases to emerge from Chinese internet culture in the early 2020s.

3) 西班牙語課程 — 第十三天(10 分鐘,銜接第十二天內容)

第十一、十二天學了規則 -ar、-er、-ir 動詞的現在式(描述習慣性或當下的動作)。今天的新內容非常實用:ir a + 原形動詞——用(高度不規則的)動詞 ir(去)加上 a,再接一個不需變化的原形動詞,來表達某人「打算/即將要做」某件事,也就是西班牙文日常生活中最常用的「近未來式」。

  • ¿Qué vas a hacer este fin de semana?(克誒 巴斯 阿 阿誒瑟 誒斯貼 芬 得 誰媽那)—— 你這個週末打算做什麼?
  • Voy a viajar a Kenting con mis amigos.(波以 阿 逼阿哈爾 阿 墾丁 空 米斯 阿米勾斯)—— 我打算跟朋友去墾丁旅行。
  • Ella va a trabajar el sábado.(誒亞 巴 阿 特拉巴哈爾 誒爾 薩巴多)—— 她星期六要工作。

文法重點:ir 的變化完全不規則——voy(我)、vas(你)、va(他/她)——跟原形動詞長得一點都不像,只能直接背起來。公式永遠是:[ir 的變化] + a + [原形動詞],中間的連接詞 a 絕不能省略。

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 「躺平」(Tǎng Píng)

  • 名稱: 「躺平」(tǎng píng)。
  • 起源/歷史: 2021 年 4 月,一位曾任工廠工人、名為駱華忠的網友在中國論壇「貼吧」發表一篇題為〈躺平即是正義〉的文章,描述一種刻意極簡的生活方式:只做維持生存所需的最低限度工作,拒絕參與「996」過勞文化、婚姻、購屋與消費主義式的拚搏。
  • 如何爆紅: 這篇文章迅速在微博、微信上於被教育與職場「內卷」逼到身心俱疲的中國年輕世代之間流傳開來。官方媒體起初批評這種心態不負責任、消極頹廢,審查機構也開始清除相關周邊商品與話題標籤——但這樣的打壓反而擴大了公眾關注度,使「躺平」成為整個世代無聲反抗的代名詞。
  • 意義與使用情境: 用來形容自願退出激烈競爭——只做剛好能餬口的工作,拒絕在系統性壓力下追求購屋、買車、結婚或生育;此詞既可以認真使用,形容身心倦怠與放棄掙扎,也可以是輕鬆玩笑地說「我今天躺平」,意指「今天不努力了」。
  • 變體與演變: 衍生出「擺爛」(bǎi làn,意指乾脆放棄、主動迎接失敗,而非被動休息)等相關詞彙,也經常與造成這種壓力鍋現象的「內卷」一詞相提並論,視為同一現象的正反兩面;英語媒體也常將其與西方的「安靜辭職」(quiet quitting)相互類比。
  • 目前現況: 至今仍是討論中國青年失業與經濟成長放緩議題時經常被引用的指標詞彙,並被視為 2020 年代初期中國網路文化中,最具代表性的社會現象流行語之一。
