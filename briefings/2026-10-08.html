3) Spanish Lesson — Day 84: Todo / Cada / Ambos (all, each, both)

Todo el día (singular) means "the whole day"; todos los días (plural) means "every day." Cada never changes, is always followed by a singular noun and takes no article (cada semana). Ambos/ambas means "both" (colloquially los dos / las dos), and todo alone is a pronoun for "everything" (Todo está bien; todo lo que dices).

  • ¿Trabajas todos los días? — Do you work every day?
  • Trabajo todo el día, pero cada viernes salgo temprano. — I work all day, but every Friday I leave early.
  • Mis dos hermanos viven en Tainan; ambos son médicos. — My two brothers live in Tainan; both are doctors.
  • Cada vez hablo mejor. — I speak better and better.

4) Zhuangzi — Essay 20: 山木 (Shān Mù) — "The Mountain Tree"

The next chapter of the Outer Chapters (外篇), a collection of short parables about how to live safely and freely in a dangerous world. Zhuangzi sees a huge tree in the mountains that loggers leave alone because its wood is useless; it lives out its full years. That evening at a friend's house, the host has two geese killed for dinner: the one that cannot cackle is chosen, the one that can is spared. A disciple asks which side the master takes. Zhuangzi first answers he would settle "between usefulness and uselessness," then admits that this still falls short: the real answer is to ride on the Way and its virtue, drifting free of praise and blame, neither rising nor falling with things.

The chapter's famous image is the empty boat (虛舟): if a boat drifts into yours you may be irritated if someone is steering it, but not if it is empty. Be like the empty boat and nobody can fight you. In another story, Taigong Ren (大公任) tells the besieged Confucius that the straight tree is cut first and the sweet well runs dry first, and advises him to give up fame and return to the crowd. The bell-maker Bei Gong She (北宮奢) raises funds for a bell with no effort: he does not refuse what comes or hold what goes. The chapter also holds the saying that the gentleman's friendship is as plain as water while the petty man's is as sweet as wine (君子之交淡若水).

The chapter ends with Zhuangzi in a chestnut grove with a slingshot: he sees a strange magpie, then a cicada, a mantis stalking the cicada, and the magpie stalking the mantis, each so focused on its prey that it forgets its own danger. He puts the slingshot away, sighs that things harm one another and profit calls profit, and the grove keeper chases him as a thief. For three months afterward he was unhappy. Last, an innkeeper's two concubines: the ugly one is honored and the beautiful one neglected, because the beautiful one thinks herself beautiful. "Act with virtue, but drop the heart that thinks itself virtuous."

Takeaway: Neither usefulness nor uselessness is a safe place to hide; what protects you is not clinging to a position, an image or a result, and drifting like an empty boat. (Chapter details are from general knowledge of the text, not from today's search.)

5) Country / Region Spotlight — Ireland (Éire, 愛爾蘭)

  • Location & territory size: The Republic of Ireland covers about five-sixths of the island of Ireland, in the North Atlantic west of Great Britain (Northern Ireland, part of the UK, holds the rest). Area about 70,300 km², roughly 1.9 times Taiwan (36,200 km²), a little larger than Lithuania or Sri Lanka and slightly smaller than South Carolina.
  • Population: About 5.5 million (Central Statistics Office: 5,525,600 in April 2026, up 1.2% in a year), only about a quarter of Taiwan's. Dublin, the capital and largest city, has about 1.4-1.5 million in its metro area.
  • Religion: Historically overwhelmingly Roman Catholic (about two-thirds identified as Catholic in the 2022 census), with a fast-growing share, around one in seven, reporting no religion, plus Church of Ireland and other Christians, Muslims and Orthodox communities.
  • Language: Irish (Gaeilge) is the first official language and English the second in practice; Irish-speaking regions called the Gaeltacht survive mostly on the western coast, and Irish is taught in all schools.
  • Economy: A small, very open euro-zone economy and a hub for US multinationals (tech, pharmaceuticals, medical devices) attracted by low corporate tax (12.5%, 15% for large multinational groups after the OECD deal) and an English-speaking, EU-based workforce. Because of those corporate profits, Irish GDP overstates local activity, so the government also uses a modified measure (GNI*). Famous exports also include Guinness and whiskey.
  • History: Celtic Ireland was Christianized in the 5th century (traditionally by St. Patrick), had monastic learning that preserved much Latin and Irish writing, then Viking towns (Dublin from the 840s) and the Anglo-Norman invasion of 1169. English and later British rule, the Penal Laws, and the Great Famine of 1845-52, in which about a million died and another million emigrated, shaped the diaspora of tens of millions of people of Irish descent. After the 1916 Easter Rising, the War of Independence (1919-21) and the Anglo-Irish Treaty, the Irish Free State was founded in 1922 and became a republic in 1949. It joined the EEC (now the EU) in 1973; the 1990s "Celtic Tiger" boom, the 2008 crash and bailout, and the 1998 Good Friday Agreement on Northern Ireland are the modern landmarks.

7) Film / Performing Arts Spotlight — The Nutcracker (ballet, 1892)

  • Key figures: Composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (his last ballet); libretto and choreographic plan by Marius Petipa, adapting Alexandre Dumas père's French version of E.T.A. Hoffmann's 1816 tale "The Nutcracker and the Mouse King"; choreography actually completed by Lev Ivanov after Petipa fell ill. First staged at the Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg on December 18, 1892 (Dec 6 in the old Russian calendar) on a double bill with Tchaikovsky's one-act opera Iolanta.
  • Plot overview: On Christmas Eve the girl Clara (Marie in some versions) receives a nutcracker doll from her godfather Drosselmeyer. At night the tree grows, mice invade, and the nutcracker leads toy soldiers against the Mouse King; Clara helps win the fight by throwing her slipper. The nutcracker becomes a prince and takes her through a snowy pine forest to the Land of Sweets, where the Sugar Plum Fairy welcomes her and presents divertissements: Spanish (chocolate), Arabian (coffee), Chinese (tea), Russian (trepak), the Mother Ginger and the Waltz of the Flowers, then the grand pas de deux.
  • Style & technique: Late Romantic Russian "imperial" ballet: classical academic technique, large corps de ballet and children's roles. Tchaikovsky wrote a lush, color-driven orchestral score and used the celesta, a new keyboard instrument he had found in Paris, for the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy's glassy bell-like sound. The ballet is built as a story in Act I and a showcase of dances in Act II.
  • Reception & significance: The 1892 premiere had a lukewarm response (critics disliked the weak plot and the child-heavy first act), but Tchaikovsky's concert suite had been a hit in March 1892. The ballet spread slowly: the first full US production was by the San Francisco Ballet in 1944, and George Balanchine's 1954 New York City Ballet version made it a Christmas institution in America.
  • Influence & legacy: Its melodies are among the most widely recognized classical music; it appears in Disney's Fantasia (1940), many films, adverts and holiday events, and it funds many ballet companies, for which December performances can be a large share of annual ticket sales.
  • Where to experience it today: Productions run every December at major companies (Mariinsky, Bolshoi, Royal Ballet, New York City Ballet and Pacific Northwest Ballet, among many) and at local and school companies worldwide, including in Taiwan. Filmed and streamed versions are available. Check each company's 2026 season listing for dates.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 70: The Reaction Shot

The method: A reaction shot shows a character (usually the face) responding to something said or done, instead of showing the event itself. Film editors use it to decide where the audience's feelings go: the meaning is in the response, not the stimulus. It can be a surrogate for the viewer (we feel what they feel), a source of comedy (the double take or slow burn), or a way to show a feeling that words cannot carry. Different from the Kuleshov Effect (Lesson on editing meaning) and Eyeline Match (spatial connection): here the question is whose face are we given, when, and for how long?

How to analyze a scene: (1) Identify the event and the person who reacts. (2) Note the timing: do we see the reaction before the event is fully revealed, after it, or is it withheld? (3) Ask whose perspective the reaction gives us; is it an audience stand-in, an antagonist, a bystander? (4) Measure the duration of the hold: a long, unbroken hold makes the viewer search the face and fills the silence. (5) Check whether the reaction confirms what we expected or complicates it (ambivalence, the "wrong" emotion). (6) Ask what is not shown, such as the other person's face.

Worked example — City Lights (1931, dir. Charlie Chaplin), the final scene: The Tramp's money has restored a blind flower seller's sight (played by Virginia Cherrill). Released from jail, he wanders by and she, now running a flower shop, laughs at the shabby man through the window with her assistant. Moved, she comes out to give him a rose and a coin; as she presses the coin into his hand she recognizes his touch, the same hand she once held while blind. An intertitle gives the only dialogue: "You?" and "You can see now?" "Yes, I can see now." The film's last shot is not hers but his: a close-up of Chaplin's face. He is shy, afraid he will be a disappointment, the flower held to his mouth, yet his expression slowly opens into an uncertain smile. The camera simply holds, with no more titles. Why it works: the film trusts the reaction shot to carry the emotion of the whole story. The audience has watched his sacrifice and now reads in one face his hope, shame and joy, and the unresolved ending (does she accept him?) is left in his expression. The critic James Agee later called it one of the greatest moments of acting in movies. Compare the comic reaction in Oliver Hardy's famous look-to-camera glances, where the same device turns exasperation into laughter.

4) 莊子 — 第 20 篇:山木(Shān Mù)—「山中之木」

〈達生〉之後的下一篇,屬於外篇,是一組講「如何在險惡世界中安全而自由地生活」的短寓言。莊子在山中看到一棵巨木,伐木的人不砍它,因為木材沒有用處,所以能終其天年。晚上他借宿朋友家,主人叫僕人殺鵝款待:不會叫的那隻被殺,會叫的那隻留下。弟子問老師要站哪一邊。莊子先說他會處在「材與不材之間」,隨即承認這樣還是不夠:真正的答案是乘著道與德悠遊,不受讚譽與毀謗牽制,不隨事物起落。

本篇最有名的意象是「虛舟」:有船撞上你的船,若船上有人駕駛,你可能會生氣;若是空船,就不會。像虛舟一樣的人,沒有人能與他爭鬥。另一段中,大公任對被困的孔子說:直木先被砍,甘井先枯竭,勸他捨棄名聲、回到眾人之中。北宮奢為鑄鐘募款毫不費力:來的不拒絕,去的不挽留。本篇也收錄「君子之交淡若水,小人之交甘若醴」。

全篇結尾,莊子拿著彈弓走進栗林:看到一隻怪異的鵲,然後是一隻蟬、一隻伺機撲蟬的螳螂,和一隻正要捕螳螂的鵲,每個都專注於獵物而忘了自身的危險。他收起彈弓,嘆道物與物互相傷害、利益彼此招引,結果被看守栗林的人當成小偷追趕。事後三個月他都不開心。最後是旅店老闆的兩個妾:醜的受尊重,美的被冷落,因為美的自以為美。「行賢而去自賢之心」:做有德的事,但要拋開自以為有德的心。

重點:有用與無用都不是安全的藏身處;真正的保護是不執著於位置、形象或結果,像空船一樣漂流。(本篇內容細節來自對原文的一般知識,而非今日搜尋。)

  • 語言:愛爾蘭語(Gaeilge)是第一官方語言,實際上英語是第二官方語言;稱為 Gaeltacht 的愛爾蘭語區主要殘存於西海岸,所有學校都教愛爾蘭語。
  • 經濟:小型、高度開放的歐元區經濟體,也是美國跨國企業(科技、製藥、醫材)的樞紐,吸引力來自低企業稅(12.5%,依 OECD 協議對大型跨國集團為 15%)與說英語、位於歐盟的勞動力。由於這些企業利潤,愛爾蘭的 GDP 會高估本地經濟活動,所以政府也使用修正後的 GNI* 指標。知名出口品還有健力士啤酒與威士忌。
  • 歷史:凱爾特時期的愛爾蘭在 5 世紀被基督教化(傳統上歸功於聖派翠克),修道院學問保存了大量拉丁文與愛爾蘭文書寫;接著是維京人的城鎮(都柏林始於 840 年代)與 1169 年的盎格魯-諾曼入侵。英國統治、刑罰法與 1845-52 年的大饑荒(約一百萬人死亡、另一百萬人移民),造就了數千萬愛爾蘭裔的海外社群。經過 1916 年復活節起義、1919-21 年獨立戰爭與《英愛條約》,愛爾蘭自由邦於 1922 年成立,1949 年成為共和國;1973 年加入歐洲經濟共同體(今歐盟);1990 年代「凱爾特之虎」繁榮、2008 年金融危機與紓困,以及 1998 年關於北愛爾蘭的《耶穌受難日協議》,是近代重要里程碑。
  • 現任領導人(最後已知,今日未重新驗證,搜尋也未能確認):總理(Taoiseach,政府首腦)為共和黨(Fianna Fáil)的米歇爾·馬丁(Micheál Martin),自 2025 年 1 月起與統一黨(Fine Gael)聯合執政;總統(象徵性國家元首)為凱薩琳·康諾利(Catherine Connolly),自 2025 年 11 月就任。

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 彈力豬(Moo Deng,爆紅的小河馬)

  • 名稱:Moo Deng(泰語 หมูเด้ง),字面意思是「彈力豬」,是一種軟彈烤豬肉丸的名稱(彈力豬肉)。
  • 起源與歷史:一隻 2024 年 7 月 10 日出生在泰國春武里府考基奧開放動物園(Khao Kheow Open Zoo)的雌性侏儒河馬。園方在社群媒體上舉辦公開投票替她取名,她的照片與影片發佈在動物園的臉書專頁上。
  • 如何爆紅:2024 年 9 月,這隻圓滾滾、大眼睛的小河馬在圍欄裡潑水、啃飼養員、把嘴藏進水裡的短片在 TikTok、X 與 Instagram 上瘋傳。人潮湧入動物園(園方因有人向她潑水的報導而限制遊客行為、加強規範),她在數週內成為國際新聞。2024 年 9 月底,《週六夜現場》(SNL)在 Weekend Update 單元由克蘿伊·芬曼(Chloe Fineman)模仿她。
  • 意義與用法:「Moo Deng」成了「你超愛的、小小的、混亂但無所謂的生物」的代稱,也是一種心情:「我今天就是 Moo Deng」,意思是可愛、野性、疲倦,或單純懶得理。品牌、同人創作、玩偶、萬聖節服裝與妝容潮流隨之而來。
  • 變體與演變:引發一波「動物當人格」迷因,從更早爆紅的動物(企鵝 Pesto、生氣貓、Harambe)到「Moo Deng 能量」的說法。動物園註冊商標並販售官方周邊,其社群帳號也成為泰國旅遊的重要吸引力。
  • 現況:高峰期是 2024 年秋天;她如今是辨識度高的網路懷舊梗與動物園吉祥物。作為迷因,已落在「療癒動物偶像」的類別而非每日潮流(2026 年最新狀況未驗證)。

7) 電影/表演藝術介紹 — 《胡桃鉗》(芭蕾,1892)

  • 主要人物:作曲家柴可夫斯基(Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky,他的最後一部芭蕾);劇本與編舞構想來自馬里烏斯·佩提帕(Marius Petipa),改編自大仲馬的法文版本、源於 E.T.A. 霍夫曼 1816 年的故事〈胡桃鉗與鼠王〉;佩提帕生病後,實際編舞由列夫·伊凡諾夫(Lev Ivanov)完成。1892 年 12 月 18 日(舊俄曆 12 月 6 日)在聖彼得堡的馬林斯基劇院首演,與柴可夫斯基的獨幕歌劇《約蘭達》同場演出。
  • 劇情概要:平安夜,女孩克拉拉(部分版本稱瑪麗)從教父德羅塞爾梅爾手中收到一個胡桃鉗娃娃。夜裡聖誕樹變大,老鼠入侵,胡桃鉗率領玩具兵對抗鼠王;克拉拉丟出拖鞋助戰獲勝。胡桃鉗變成王子,帶她穿過雪松林到糖果王國,糖梅仙子迎接她並獻上各國舞蹈:西班牙(巧克力)、阿拉伯(咖啡)、中國(茶)、俄羅斯(特列帕克)、大肚薑媽媽與花之圓舞曲,最後是雙人大雙人舞。
  • 風格與技法:晚期浪漫派的俄國「帝國」芭蕾:古典學院派技巧、龐大的群舞與兒童角色。柴可夫斯基寫出華麗、色彩豐富的管弦樂,並在糖梅仙子之舞使用他在巴黎發現的新鍵盤樂器鋼片琴(celesta),發出玻璃鈴般的聲音。結構上,第一幕講故事,第二幕是舞蹈的展示。
  • 評價與意義:1892 年的首演反應冷淡(評論家不喜歡薄弱的劇情與以孩子為主的第一幕),但柴可夫斯基在 1892 年 3 月發表的音樂會組曲大受歡迎。這部芭蕾傳播緩慢:美國首次完整演出是 1944 年舊金山芭蕾舞團,1954 年巴蘭欽的紐約市立芭蕾舞團版本使它成為美國的耶誕節傳統。
  • 影響與傳承:它的旋律是世界上最廣為人知的古典音樂之一;出現在迪士尼《幻想曲》(1940)、許多電影、廣告與節慶活動中,許多芭蕾舞團靠它支撐營運,十二月的演出常佔全年票房很大的比例。
  • 今日可在哪裡欣賞:每年 12 月,各大舞團(馬林斯基、波修瓦、英國皇家芭蕾、紐約市立芭蕾與太平洋西北芭蕾等)與世界各地的地方及學校舞團(包括台灣)都有演出,也有影片與串流版本。演出日期請查各舞團的 2026 年度節目表。

8) 看片方法 — 第 70 課:反應鏡頭(The Reaction Shot)

方法:反應鏡頭是指拍攝某人(通常是臉)對所說或所做之事的反應,而不是事件本身。剪接師用它來決定觀眾的情感落在哪裡:意義在於反應,而不是刺激。它可以是觀眾的替身(我們感受他們的感受)、喜劇的來源(驚愕回頭或慢慢發火),也可以呈現語言無法承載的情感。不同於庫勒雪夫效應(剪接創造意義)與視線匹配(空間連接):這裡的問題是我們看到誰的臉、什麼時候、持續多久?

如何分析一場戲:(1)找出事件與反應者。(2)注意時機:反應是在事件完全揭露前、後,還是被保留不給?(3)問這個反應給了我們誰的視角:是觀眾替身、對手,還是旁觀者?(4)衡量停留時間:長而不切的停留,會讓觀眾在臉上尋找答案、填滿沈默。(5)確認反應是印證我們的預期,還是使之複雜化(矛盾、「不對」的情緒)。(6)問什麼沒有被拍出來,例如另一個人的臉。

實例分析 — 《城市之光》(City Lights,1931,導演查理·卓別林),最後一場戲:流浪漢的錢讓盲眼賣花女(維吉尼亞·雪莉飾)恢復了視力。他出獄後走過,她如今經營著花店,和店員隔著櫥窗取笑這位衣衫襤褸的男人。她心軟,走出來要給他一朵玫瑰和一枚硬幣;當她把硬幣塞進他手裡時,她認出了他的觸感,那是她失明時握過的那隻手。唯一的對白由字幕給出:「是你?」「你現在看得見了?」「是的,我現在看得見了。」電影的最後一個鏡頭不是她的,而是他的:卓別林臉部的特寫。他害羞,害怕自己令她失望,花朵貼在嘴邊,表情卻慢慢綻放成不確定的微笑。鏡頭只是靜靜停留,沒有更多字幕。為什麼有效:電影把整個故事的情感交給反應鏡頭。觀眾一路看著他的犧牲,現在在一張臉上讀到他的希望、羞愧與喜悅,而沒有解決的結局(她會接受他嗎?)就留在他的表情裡。評論家詹姆斯·艾吉(James Agee)後來稱之為電影表演中最偉大的時刻之一。對照奧利佛·哈台(Oliver Hardy)著名的對鏡頭無奈眼神,同樣的手法把惱怒變成笑聲。
