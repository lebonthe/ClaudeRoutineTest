5) Country Spotlight — Maldives (馬爾地夫)

  • Location & size: an island nation in the Indian Ocean, southwest of Sri Lanka and India, strung along about 26 atolls with roughly 1,190 coral islands (about 200 inhabited). Land area only about 298 km², roughly the size of Taipei City (about 272 km²) and about 1/120 of Taiwan (about 36,200 km²); in the sea, its territory spreads over about 90,000 km². It is the lowest-lying country on Earth: average elevation about 1.5 m and highest natural point about 5 m, so sea-level rise is an existential issue.
  • Population: about 520,000 (the 2022 census counted about 515,000, including a large share of foreign workers). The capital Malé is one of the world's densest cities, home to roughly a third of the population on a tiny island.
  • Religion: Sunni Islam is the state religion and citizens are required by law to be Muslim; public practice of other religions is not permitted.
  • Language: Dhivehi (written right to left in the Thaana script); English is widely used in tourism and business.
  • Economy: tourism (resort islands: one resort per island) is the dominant sector, followed by fishing (tuna); GDP is on the order of US$6-7 billion (approximate), and GDP per capita is among the highest in South Asia. Public debt is high and has raised debt-distress concerns in recent years (approximate; no fresh 2026 figures confirmed). Currency: the rufiyaa.
  • History: Settled by peoples from South Asia in antiquity; Buddhist until the conversion to Islam traditionally dated to 1153. Portuguese rule in the 1550s-1570s ended when Muhammad Thakurufaanu led a revolt; British protectorate from 1887; independence on 26 July 1965; a republic from 1968. Maumoon Abdul Gayoom ruled 1978-2008; Mohamed Nasheed won the first multi-party presidential election in 2008 and held a famous underwater cabinet meeting in 2009 to highlight climate change; later presidents were Abdulla Yameen (2013-2018) and Ibrahim Mohamed Solih (2018-2023). The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami hit the islands hard.
  • Current leader: President Mohamed Muizzu (in office since 17 November 2023). I did not find reporting dated Nov 2026, so any recent change is unverified.

6) Internet Meme — "Storm Area 51, They Can't Stop All of Us" (2019)

  • Name: "Storm Area 51" (also the "Naruto run" jokes and "Let's see them aliens dodge this").
  • Origin: On June 27, 2019, a Facebook event titled "Storm Area 51, They Can't Stop All of Us" was created by a user, Matty Roberts, as a joke: gather on September 20, 2019 and run into the secretive US Air Force site in Nevada ("Area 51"), arguing that the military couldn't stop everyone if enough people ran at once. Area 51's existence had only been officially acknowledged in declassified CIA documents in 2013.
  • How it spread: The event went viral in July 2019 on Facebook, Reddit and Instagram, passing about two million "going" RSVPs within weeks. An Air Force spokesperson told the Washington Post that any attempt to enter the area would be dangerous and that the military was ready to protect it. Meme pages produced "Naruto run" jokes (runners with arms swept back) and parody posts about "alien defense."
  • Meaning & usage: A joke about mass participation without consequence: people use "they can't stop all of us" for any plan that sounds absurd when announced to millions of strangers, and Area 51 became shorthand for conspiracy culture and alien lore.
  • Variations & evolution: Roberts re-framed the stunt as "Alienstock," a festival in Rachel, Nevada (Sept 2019), but safety and logistics worries scaled it down; reports say only a small crowd (hundreds to low thousands at Rachel, far fewer at the gates) showed up, with a few arrests. Spin-offs included Naruto-run videos, fake "Area 51 raid" Pokémon-style flyers and songs.
  • Current status: A 2019 time capsule, still used as a template for "joke event goes viral" and as a nostalgic reference to alien memes; it comes back on anniversaries and whenever UFO reporting makes the news. (From general knowledge; attendance numbers are approximate; no live source was checked today.)

7) Film / Performing Arts — The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (Il buono, il brutto, il cattivo, 1966, dir. Sergio Leone)

  • Name & creators: directed by Sergio Leone; screenplay by Age & Scarpelli, Luciano Vincenzoni and Leone; music by Ennio Morricone; cinematography by Tonino Delli Colli; starring Clint Eastwood ("Blondie", the Good), Lee Van Cleef ("Angel Eyes", the Bad) and Eli Wallach ("Tuco", the Ugly). An Italian-Spanish-West German co-production, the third film of Leone's "Dollars" trilogy.
  • Era & background: Released in December 1966 during the "spaghetti Western" boom, set in the American Civil War. Much of it was filmed in Spain (the Tabernas desert in Almería, and Burgos for the Sad Hill cemetery). Its low budget (reportedly about US$1.2 million) became a worldwide hit.
  • Plot overview: Three gunmen chase a buried Confederate cash box (US$200,000) against the backdrop of the war. Tuco and Blondie run a bounty-swindle partnership that sours; Angel Eyes hunts the gold for himself. Each learns one half of the secret: the name of the grave (Arch Stanton) versus the name of the cemetery (Sad Hill). The film climaxes in a three-way standoff in a circular graveyard.
  • Style & techniques: extreme close-ups of eyes, cut against wide landscape shots; drawn-out silence before violence; stylized, often post-synced sound; Morricone's coyote-like main theme and the building "Ecstasy of Gold" (with soprano Edda Dell'Orso) as music-as-character; deep-focus compositions that put faces and horizons in the same frame.
  • Significance & legacy: It defined the visual grammar of the modern Western and the "Mexican standoff" scene, and it influenced directors from Quentin Tarantino to generations of Hollywood action directors. The film turned Eastwood into a global star and made Morricone's score one of film's most recognizable.
  • Where to experience it today: available on home video; a restored extended cut (about 3 hours) exists; and Sad Hill cemetery in Burgos was restored by volunteers in 2015-2016 and can be visited. Streaming availability varies by region. (From general knowledge; no live source was checked today.)

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 99: Screen Time vs. Story Time (Real-Time & Time Dilation)

  • What it is: Every film has two clocks: story time (how long the events take in the story) and screen time (how long the film runs). Editors compress (ellipsis), stretch (dilation: cutting among many shots to extend a moment) or match them (near real time). Reading the gap between the two clocks tells you where the film puts pressure. This differs from Lesson 64 (average shot length and pacing) and from compressed time (ellipsis), since here the question is how long the story takes versus how long we watch.
  • How to analyze: (1) Compare: roughly how long is the story versus the running time? (2) Find the clocks: does the film show watches, church bells, calendars or deadlines to anchor time? (3) Find stretching: where does the film linger (repeated cutaways, slow build) on a short moment? (4) Find compression: what is cut out? (5) Ask what each choice does: pressure, anxiety, dread, or release. (6) Check where the film breaks its own clock: the moment it speeds up or stops is the one it wants you to feel.
  • Worked example — High Noon (1952, dir. Fred Zinnemann; screenplay Carl Foreman; score Dimitri Tiomkin), the countdown to noon: Marshal Will Kane (Gary Cooper) learns a killer is arriving on the noon train, and the film runs roughly 85 minutes for about the same stretch of story time. (a) Clocks: clocks are everywhere: on walls, in the saloon, and in the sheriff's office, and the film cuts to them repeatedly, with pendulum close-ups; as noon nears, the shots of the clocks get more frequent. (b) Stretching: as Kane asks town after town for help, the film keeps returning to empty streets and waiting faces, so the minutes feel longer than they are. (c) Sound: Tiomkin's ballad "Do Not Forsake Me" returns like a tolling clock. (d) Why it works: because story time and screen time nearly match, we cannot skip ahead, so we wait with Kane. The film ends when the clock does, and the arrival of the train delivers the release. (The production's blacklist-era backstory is often read as a parable; that reading is interpretive.)
  • Practice: pick any thriller scene with a deadline and write down (a) story time, (b) screen time, (c) each clock or time marker, (d) what you'd feel if the scene were half as long.

6) 網路迷因 —「Storm Area 51, They Can't Stop All of Us」(衝進 51 區,2019)

  • 名稱:「Storm Area 51」(衝進 51 區),也衍生出「火影跑」(Naruto run)與「Let's see them aliens dodge this」等玩笑。
  • 起源:2019 年 6 月 27 日,使用者 Matty Roberts 建立了一個玩笑性質的 Facebook 活動「Storm Area 51, They Can't Stop All of Us」:2019 年 9 月 20 日大家一起衝進內華達州的美國空軍秘密基地「51 區」,理由是只要人夠多,軍方就擋不住所有人。51 區的存在要到 2013 年 CIA 解密文件才被正式承認。
  • 如何爆紅:活動在 2019 年 7 月於 Facebook、Reddit 與 Instagram 瘋傳,幾週內「會參加」的人數超過 200 萬。美國空軍發言人對《華盛頓郵報》表示,任何進入該區的企圖都很危險,軍方隨時準備保護該區。迷因頁面製作了「火影跑」(雙臂向後甩著跑)與「抵禦外星人」的惡搞貼文。
  • 意義與用法:一種「集體參與但沒有後果」的玩笑:當一個計畫對上百萬陌生人宣布時聽起來荒謬,大家就用「they can't stop all of us」;51 區也成為陰謀論與外星人文化的代名詞。
  • 變體與演變:Roberts 後來把活動改成在內華達州雷契爾(Rachel)舉辦的「Alienstock」音樂祭(2019 年 9 月),但因安全與後勤顧慮而縮小規模;報導指出實際到場的人不多(雷契爾約數百到數千人,抵達大門的人更少),有少數人被逮捕。衍生內容包括火影跑影片、假的「51 區突襲」傳單與歌曲。
  • 現況:屬於 2019 年的時代記憶,仍被拿來當「玩笑活動爆紅」的範本,也是外星人迷因的懷舊梗;每逢週年或 UFO 新聞又會重新出現。(來自一般常識;出席人數為概數;今天沒有查證即時來源。)

7) 電影/表演藝術 —《黃昏三鏢客》(The Good, the Bad and the Ugly,1966,導演瑟吉歐·李昂尼)

  • 名稱與創作者:導演瑟吉歐·李昂尼(Sergio Leone);劇本由 Age & Scarpelli、Luciano Vincenzoni 與李昂尼完成;音樂恩尼歐·莫利克奈(Ennio Morricone);攝影托尼諾·德利·科利(Tonino Delli Colli);主演克林·伊斯威特(「金髮仔」Blondie,善)、李·范克里夫(「天使之眼」Angel Eyes,惡)與伊萊·瓦拉赫(「圖科」Tuco,醜)。這是義大利、西班牙與西德合拍的作品,也是李昂尼「Dollars 三部曲」的第三部。
  • 時代背景:1966 年 12 月上映,正值「義大利式西部片」(通心粉西部片)熱潮,故事設定在美國南北戰爭。大部分在西班牙拍攝(阿爾梅里亞的塔韋爾納斯沙漠,以及布爾戈斯的 Sad Hill 墓園)。低預算(據報導約 120 萬美元)卻成為全球賣座片。
  • 劇情概述:三名槍手在戰爭背景下追逐一箱埋藏的南軍金幣(20 萬美元)。圖科與金髮仔搭檔詐領賞金,關係後來惡化;天使之眼則想獨吞黃金。兩人各知道祕密的一半:墳墓的名字(Arch Stanton)與墓園的名字(Sad Hill)。影片在一座圓形墓園的三方對決中達到高潮。
  • 風格與技法:眼睛的極端特寫與廣闊風景的遠景交替剪接;暴力之前拉長的沉默;風格化、常為後期配音的聲音;莫利克奈如土狼嚎叫的主題曲與逐步堆高的〈Ecstasy of Gold〉(女高音艾妲·戴爾·奧索演唱),音樂本身就像一個角色;深焦構圖讓臉孔與地平線同時入鏡。
  • 意義與影響:它定義了現代西部片的視覺語法與「三方對峙」(Mexican standoff)場面,影響了從昆汀·塔倫提諾到後來好萊塢動作剪接的許多導演。這部片讓伊斯威特成為全球巨星,也讓莫利克奈的配樂成為影史上最容易辨認的作品之一。
  • 在哪裡體驗:有家用影片發行;也有約 3 小時的修復加長版;布爾戈斯的 Sad Hill 墓園在 2015-2016 年由志工修復,現在可以參觀。串流平台依地區而異。(來自一般常識;今天沒有查證即時來源。)

8) 看片方法 — 第 99 課:銀幕時間與故事時間(即時與時間延展)

  • 是什麼:每部電影有兩個時鐘:故事時間(故事裡的事件經過多久)與銀幕時間(影片放映多久)。剪接師可以壓縮(省略)、延展(用多個鏡頭切換把一瞬間拉長),或讓兩者接近(近乎即時)。看懂兩個時鐘之間的差距,就能知道影片把壓力放在哪裡。這與第 64 課(平均鏡頭長度與節奏)以及壓縮時間(省略)不同:這裡問的是故事過了多久、我們看了多久。
  • 如何分析:(1) 比較:故事大約多長?片長多久?(2) 找時鐘:影片是否用手錶、教堂鐘聲、日曆或期限來錨定時間?(3) 找延展:哪裡在短暫的片刻上停留(反覆插入鏡頭、慢慢堆高)?(4) 找壓縮:被剪掉的是什麼?(5) 問每個選擇做了什麼:壓力、焦慮、恐懼,還是釋放?(6) 看影片在哪裡打破自己的時鐘:加速或停住的那一刻,就是影片要你感受的地方。
  • 實例分析 —《日正當中》(High Noon,1952,導演佛列·辛尼曼;編劇卡爾·佛曼;配樂迪米特里·提奧姆金),等待正午的倒數:警長威爾·肯恩(賈利·古柏)得知殺手將搭正午的火車抵達,影片長約 85 分鐘,對應的故事時間大致相同。(a) 時鐘:時鐘無所不在:牆上、酒館裡、警長辦公室,影片一再切到時鐘,還有鐘擺特寫;越接近正午,時鐘鏡頭越頻繁。(b) 延展:肯恩一個又一個地向鎮上的人求助時,影片不斷回到空蕩的街道與等待的臉孔,讓這些分鐘感覺比實際更長。(c) 聲音:提奧姆金的民謠〈Do Not Forsake Me〉像鐘聲般反覆出現。(d) 為什麼有效:因為故事時間與銀幕時間幾乎相等,我們無法跳過,只能和肯恩一起等待。時鐘走到底影片就結束,火車抵達帶來釋放。(此片拍攝時的黑名單時代背景常被解讀為一則寓言;這種解讀屬於詮釋。)
  • 練習:挑任何一個有期限的驚悚片場景,寫下 (a) 故事時間、(b) 銀幕時間、(c) 每個時鐘或時間標記、(d) 如果這場戲縮短一半,你的感受會怎樣。
