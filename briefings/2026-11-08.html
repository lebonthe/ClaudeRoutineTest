3) Spanish Lesson — Day 115

  • Topic: Prestar / pedir prestado / dejar / devolver — lend, borrow, "let me have" (colloquial) and give back. New topic, not covered in Days 1-114.
  • Key points: Chinese has one word for lend and borrow; Spanish splits it by direction: prestar = the owner lends (Te presto mi coche), pedir prestado = the user borrows (Le pedí prestado el coche a mi tío; prestado agrees with the object). 4) Daodejing (道德經) — Chapter 18: 大道廢,有仁義 (Dà dào fèi, yǒu rén yì) — "When the Great Way Is Abandoned"

  • Context: The I Ching and Zhuangzi sequences are used up, so this section continues through the Daodejing in order. Yesterday was Chapter 17.
  • Text: 大道廢,有仁義;智慧出,有大偽;六親不和,有孝慈;國家昏亂,有忠臣。
  • Meaning: When the great Way is abandoned, "benevolence and righteousness" appear; when cleverness emerges, great deceit appears; when the six family relations are out of harmony, filial piety and parental kindness are praised; when the state is in chaos, loyal ministers appear. The chapter reads as a diagnosis: virtues get named and promoted only once their natural, unforced form has been lost. (Interpretation varies; some commentators read it as a sharp critique of moral teaching, others as a description of how praise follows failure. The excavated Guodian bamboo text is often cited as having a milder wording, from general knowledge.)
  • Link to earlier chapters: Chapter 17 described rulers who are barely noticed; Chapter 18 describes what follows when that natural order is lost and moral labels must be introduced. It echoes Chapters 2 and 3 on how naming a virtue creates its opposite.

  • Location & size: The narrow isthmus linking Central and South America, between Costa Rica to the west and Colombia to the east, with the Caribbean to the north and the Pacific to the south. Area about 75,400 km², roughly 2 times Taiwan (about 36,200 km²) and a bit smaller than South Carolina and a bit larger than Ireland. At its narrowest the isthmus is less than 60 km wide.

The 1977 Torrijos-Carter treaties led to the canal's handover on 31 December 1999, after a 1989 US invasion that ousted Manuel Noriega.

  • Origin: A roughly five-second clip from a Japanese TV program in which a small animal turns toward the camera as a dramatic sting plays. A YouTube upload from 2007 spread widely, and the clip reportedly came from a Japanese show (details of the show and the uploader are from memory and not verified).
  • How it spread: It was passed around as a short looping clip and then remixed with different music and fake "reveal" scenes, and became one of the first widely recognised YouTube "reaction" memes, reaching tens of millions of views across copies.

  • Variations & evolution: countless re-scored versions (horror, soap-opera, action music), "dramatic" parodies using other animals or people, and appearances in mainstream TV parodies. It is an early example of a "reaction clip" that later formats like GIFs and TikTok sounds inherited.
  • Current status: a nostalgic classic of the early YouTube era; still re-uploaded and cited, but rarely a trending format. From general knowledge; origin details and view counts approximate and not verified.

7) Film / Performing Arts — Throne of Blood (蜘蛛巣城, 1957, dir. Akira Kurosawa)

  • Name & creators: Throne of Blood (Japanese: Kumonosu-jō, "Spider Web Castle"), produced by Toho, directed by Akira Kurosawa; screenplay by Shinobu Hashimoto, Ryūzō Kikushima, Hideo Oguni and Kurosawa; starring Toshiro Mifune (Washizu), Isuzu Yamada (Lady Asaji), Takashi Shimura and Minoru Chiaki; cinematography by Asakazu Nakai; music by Masaru Satō.
  • Era & background: Made in the 1950s, the peak of Japan's postwar studio cinema, as Kurosawa's adaptation of Shakespeare's Macbeth moved to feudal Japan's age of civil war (Sengoku). It was shot partly on the slopes of Mount Fuji and in studio sets, with heavy fog.
  • Plot overview: The warlord Washizu and his friend Miki are told by a spirit in a fog-bound forest that Washizu will become lord of Spider Web Castle and Miki's son will follow. Urged by his wife, Lady Asaji, Washizu murders his lord, then Miki, and descends into paranoia. In the final sequence his own archers turn on him and shoot him as the forest seems to march on the castle.
  • Style & techniques: Heavily influenced by Noh theatre: Mifune's stylised, stamping movement; Yamada's mask-like face and deliberate, gliding walk; sparse, stage-like sets; chanting and flute-led music. Fog, wind and bare landscapes give a stark, fatalistic mood. In the climax Kurosawa reportedly had real archers shooting real arrows near Mifune, which adds to the terror in his performance (as commonly told).
  • Significance & legacy: Often ranked among the finest Shakespeare film adaptations, notable for translating the play's meaning rather than its words (the film uses none of Shakespeare's dialogue). It helped establish Kurosawa and Mifune internationally and showed how Noh could be turned into cinema; it influenced later Shakespeare adaptations and samurai-genre tragedies.
  • Where to experience it today: available on home video, notably in Criterion's restored edition, and in repertory screenings and streaming that vary by region. (From general knowledge; details approximate; no live source was checked today.)

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 101: The Crane Shot (Vertical Camera Movement)

  • What it is: A crane shot (or jib shot) moves the camera vertically, up or down, often while also swinging or pulling back, on a counterweighted arm. Earlier lessons covered pans, dollies and tracking shots, which move the frame sideways and forward; the crane adds height. Because height reads as perspective on the story (a god's-eye view, a revelation of scale, a shift from the personal to the vast), crane moves are usually chosen for emphasis.
  • How to analyze: (1) Name the direction: crane up (away from characters, revealing more) or crane down (arriving into the scene, focusing in). (2) Ask what is revealed or concealed: what information enters the frame as the camera rises, and what shrinks? (3) Check the scale change: does a character become small, and is that the point (isolation, insignificance, a larger consequence)? (4) Check motivation: is it tied to a character's movement or emotion, or is it an authorial comment? (5) Check placement: crane moves often open or close a sequence or a film, so ask what the shot is summarising.
  • Worked example — Gone with the Wind (1939, dir. Victor Fleming; production design William Cameron Menzies; cinematography Ernest Haller), the Atlanta depot sequence: Scarlett O'Hara walks through the railway yard looking for a doctor and finds a few wounded soldiers. As she keeps walking, the camera cranes up and back, and the few bodies become hundreds, then thousands, spread across the whole yard, until a tattered Confederate flag fills the foreground in the final image. Watch the sequence for the three effects above: revelation (the camera hides the scale until it rises), scale change (Scarlett, our guide, shrinks to a dot in a sea of suffering), and authorial comment (the shot says the war's cost is far greater than one person's story, and the flag in the foreground gives an ironic, mournful frame). The shot works because the camera's slow rise delays the full reveal, so the audience feels the horror grow along with Scarlett's.
  • Practice: find a film scene that uses a crane or drone rise or descent, then write one sentence on what the rising or falling camera reveals, one on what scale change happens, and one on what the shot comments on.

7) 電影/表演藝術 —《蜘蛛巢城》(Throne of Blood,1957,導演:黑澤明)

  • 名稱與創作者:《蜘蛛巢城》(日文 Kumonosu-jō,英文片名 Throne of Blood),東寶出品,黑澤明導演;編劇橋本忍、菊島隆三、小國英雄與黑澤明;主演三船敏郎(鷲津武時)、山田五十鈴(淺茅夫人)、志村喬與千秋實;攝影中井朝一;音樂佐藤勝。
  • 時代背景:1950 年代,日本戰後片廠電影的高峰。黑澤明把莎士比亞的《馬克白》搬到封建日本的戰國時代。部分在富士山山坡拍攝,其餘在片廠搭景,大量使用濃霧。
  • 劇情概要:武將鷲津與好友三木在霧鎖的森林裡遇到一個靈體,預言鷲津將成為蜘蛛巢城的城主,而三木的兒子將繼位。在妻子淺茅的慫恿下,鷲津殺了主君,又殺了三木,逐漸陷入偏執。最後一場戲,他自己的弓箭手反過來對他射箭,而森林似乎朝城堡逼近。
  • 風格與技法:深受能劇影響:三船敏郎風格化、踏地的動作;山田五十鈴面具般的臉與刻意、滑行般的步伐;簡約如舞台的場景;吟唱與笛聲為主的音樂。霧、風與荒蕪的地景營造出冷峻、宿命的氣氛。據一般說法,高潮戲中黑澤明讓真正的弓箭手對著三船敏郎周圍射真箭,增加了他表演中的恐懼感。
  • 意義與影響:常被列為最優秀的莎士比亞電影改編之一,特色在於轉譯劇作的意涵而非台詞(片中沒有使用莎士比亞的台詞)。它讓黑澤明與三船敏郎在國際上聲名大噪,也展示了如何把能劇轉化為電影;影響了後來的莎士比亞改編與武士類悲劇。
  • 現在哪裡可以看:有家庭影音版本,特別是 Criterion 的修復版;另有影展/重映放映與串流平台(依地區而異)。(一般常識;細節為概數;今天沒有查證即時來源。)

8) 看片方法 — 第 101 課:搖臂鏡頭(垂直運鏡)

  • 是什麼:搖臂鏡頭(crane shot / jib shot)是把攝影機架在配重臂上,垂直上升或下降,常同時橫擺或後拉。之前的課講過搖攝、推軌與跟拍,那些是讓畫面左右或前後移動;搖臂多了「高度」。因為高度會被解讀為對故事的視角(上帝視角、揭示規模、從個人轉向宏大),所以搖臂運鏡通常用在強調的地方。
  • 如何分析:(1) 說出方向:往上(遠離角色、揭露更多)還是往下(進入場景、聚焦)。(2) 問「揭露了什麼、遮住了什麼」:攝影機上升時,畫面中進來了什麼資訊,什麼縮小了?(3) 檢查尺度變化:角色是不是變小了,這是不是重點(孤立、渺小、更大的後果)?(4) 檢查動機:是跟隨角色的動作或情緒,還是作者的評論?(5) 檢查位置:搖臂常用在段落或全片的開頭或結尾,問問這個鏡頭在總結什麼。
  • 實例 —《亂世佳人》(Gone with the Wind,1939,導演:Victor Fleming;美術設計 William Cameron Menzies;攝影 Ernest Haller),亞特蘭大車站段落:郝思嘉走過火車站場找醫生,起初只看到幾個傷兵。她一路走,攝影機一邊上升一邊後拉,幾具軀體變成幾百、幾千,鋪滿整個站場,最後一格畫面前景是一面破爛的邦聯旗。看這一段時,對照上面三個效果:揭露(攝影機升起之前,規模被隱藏)、尺度變化(帶領觀眾的郝思嘉縮成苦難之海中的一個小點)、作者評論(這個鏡頭說明戰爭的代價遠大於一個人的故事,前景的旗幟則給出帶著反諷與哀傷的構圖)。這個鏡頭有效,是因為攝影機緩慢上升,延遲了全貌的揭露,讓觀眾與郝思嘉一起感受恐怖逐漸擴大。
  • 練習:找一個用到搖臂或空拍升降的電影場景,各寫一句:升降的攝影機揭露了什麼、發生了什麼尺度變化、這個鏡頭在評論什麼。
