3) Spanish Lesson — Day 101: Pasar (to happen, to spend time, to pass by, "I'm not into it")

Pasar is a regular -ar verb with many meanings: to happen (¿Qué pasa?), who is affected (¿Qué te pasa?, like gustar), to spend time (pasarlo bien/mal, with a fixed neuter lo), to pass by or drop in (pasar por), to come in (¡Pasa!), to hand over (Pásame la sal), and in Spain colloquially pasar de = not be interested in. Preterite: pasé, pasó (the accents matter).

  • ¿Qué te pasa? Tienes mala cara. — What's wrong with you? You look unwell.
  • No me pasa nada. Solo que no lo pasé bien en la fiesta. — Nothing. I just didn't have a good time at the party.
  • Pasé tres horas esperando. Paso de ir otra vez. — I spent three hours waiting. I'm not going again.
  • Pasa por mi casa y tomamos un café. — Drop by my place and we'll have a coffee.

4) Daodejing — Chapter 4: 道沖而用之 (Dào chōng ér yòng zhī) — "The Way Is Empty, Yet Used"

Series note: continues the Daodejing track begun on Oct 22, one chapter per day in order (chapters 1-81), never repeated.

Text: 道沖而用之,或不盈。淵兮,似萬物之宗。挫其銳,解其紛,和其光,同其塵。湛兮,似或存。吾不知誰之子,象帝之先。

Plain-English rendering (paraphrase): The Way is like an empty vessel, yet in use it never seems to fill up. Deep, it seems the ancestor of the myriad things. It blunts sharpness, unties tangles, softens glare and joins the dust. Clear and still, it seems barely to exist. I do not know whose child it is; it seems to have been there before the Lord (or ancestor).

Notes: Chapters 1-3 spoke of naming, opposites and social display; Chapter 4 describes the Way itself through the image of an emptiness that stays useful (compare the hub and the pot in Chapter 11, which is a later chapter). Some editions read 沖 as 盅 (an empty cup), and the line 挫其銳,解其紛,和其光,同其塵 reappears in Chapter 56. "Lord/Di (帝)" is read by some commentators as a supreme deity and by others as an ancestor figure, so the final line is debated: it says the Way precedes even the highest power.

Takeaway: Usefulness often comes from what is left open; softening one's own edges and glare lets a person join others without friction.

7) Film / Performing-Arts Spotlight — Giselle (ballet, 1841)

  • Name: Giselle, ou les Wilis, a two-act romantic ballet and one of the cornerstones of the classical repertoire.
  • Creators / key figures: Music by Adolphe Adam; libretto by Théophile Gautier and Jules-Henri Vernoy de Saint-Georges; choreography credited to Jean Coralli, with major contributions by Jules Perrot (partner of the first Giselle, Carlotta Grisi). Marius Petipa later revised it for the Imperial Ballet in St Petersburg, and most modern productions descend from his version.
  • Era / background: Premiered at the Paris Opéra on 28 June 1841 during the Romantic movement, which prized the supernatural, the pastoral and the tragic heroine. Gautier drew on Heinrich Heine's account of the Wilis, ghosts of jilted brides, and on a poem by Victor Hugo.
  • Plot: Act I (a Rhineland village): the frail peasant girl Giselle loves dancing and falls for "Loys", who is really the nobleman Albrecht, already engaged. The gamekeeper Hilarion exposes the deception; Giselle goes mad and dies of a broken heart. Act II (a haunted forest): the Wilis, led by the queen Myrtha, force men to dance to their deaths. Hilarion perishes, and Giselle's forgiving love shields Albrecht until dawn breaks and she returns to her grave.
  • Artistic style and techniques: Contrast between the earthly first act (folk-style dances, a famous mad scene) and the ghostly second act (white long "romantic" tutus, pointework that makes dancers seem weightless, flying wires, ensemble symmetry). Adam's score uses recurring motifs for characters, an early use of leitmotif in ballet.
  • Cultural / historical significance: It defined the Romantic ballerina and the "ballet blanc" (white act); the title role is a test of both technique and acting for dancers worldwide. Its theme (love, betrayal, forgiveness) still resonates.
  • Influence and legacy: Its structure influenced later works such as La Bayadère and Swan Lake. Famous Giselles include Anna Pavlova, Galina Ulanova, Margot Fonteyn and Carlos Acosta as Albrecht; Mats Ek (1982) and Akram Khan (English National Ballet, 2016) made radical reinterpretations.
  • Current status / where to experience: Performed regularly by companies such as the Paris Opera Ballet, the Royal Ballet, American Ballet Theatre and the Bolshoi, and available in cinema relays and recordings. Current schedules were not checked today.
  • Notes: General knowledge, not verified against a source today.

8) Film-Appreciation Method — Lesson 87: Motivated Lighting & Practical Light Sources

What it is: Lighting is "motivated" when the light on screen appears to come from a source that exists, or could exist, in the scene: a window, a lamp, a fire, a candle, a TV. Those visible sources are called practicals. The cinematographer shapes and supplements them with off-screen lights so that the image feels natural. The opposite, unmotivated or stylized lighting, announces itself (a hard blue rim light in an apartment with no blue source). Neither is "better"; the question is whether the film wants the world to feel found or constructed.

How to analyze: (1) Ask of any lit shot: where is the light coming from, and can I point to it in the frame? (2) Note its quality: hard or soft, warm or cool, strong or weak, direction (front, side, back). (3) Check whether practicals are doing the real work or whether extra lights are faking them. (4) Watch what the source implies: a single candle isolates faces and darkens space, a window gives time of day, a TV flicker gives a mood. (5) Look for the moment when the motivation is dropped, which is often a sign that the film is moving from realism into a character's feeling.

Worked example — Barry Lyndon (1975, dir. Stanley Kubrick, cinematography John Alcott), the candlelit card-table scenes: Kubrick wanted interiors to look as they would have in the 18th century, lit only by candles. The crew used very fast lenses (Zeiss Planar 50mm f/0.7 lenses originally made for NASA) so that the actual candle flames could be the main light. The result is a soft, warm, low-contrast pool of light around the gamblers' faces, with the rest of the room falling into dark gold shadow. In the card-table scene where Barry first meets Lady Lyndon, the glow concentrates our attention on glances across the table. The shallow depth of field at that aperture keeps focus on a narrow plane. Notice that the motivation also dates the scene: the light looks like a period painting, while the daylight exteriors echo Gainsborough and Constable landscapes. The method shows how a lighting choice can carry historical atmosphere and mood without a word of dialogue.

Try it: In your next film, pick an interior scene and find the light source. Is it visible? Then switch off the sound for a minute and ask what the light alone says about the time, place and mood, and whether the lighting changes when the emotion changes.

6) 網路迷因介紹 — 「Shut Up and Take My Money!」(閉嘴,拿走我的錢!)

  • 名稱:「Shut up and take my money!」,常搭配《飛出個未來》(Futurama)角色菲利‧弗萊(Fry)舉著鈔票的圖片。
  • 起源 / 歷史:這句台詞出自動畫《飛出個未來》,2010 年(第 6 季)「Attack of the Killer App」那集,由角色菲利普·J·弗萊在迫切想買「eyePhone」卻被拒絕時所說。這一幕被截圖並做成梗圖。
  • 如何爆紅:約 2010 至 2011 年起,在 Reddit、Tumblr、4chan 與 Facebook 上,網友把它放在自己立刻就想買的產品、遊戲、電影、群眾募資專案與小工具的圖片上。它的流行與 Kickstarter 及科技小工具熱潮的興起同時發生。
  • 意義 / 使用情境:對於一個你會馬上買單的產品或點子的熱烈反應。可以是真心的(「這東西存在?我要!」),也可以對過度昂貴的商品或潮流反諷。幾乎總是出現在回覆或圖說。
  • 變體 / 演變:後來有人換成其他角色,或只用「Take my money!」的說法;品牌與群眾募資活動也使用過這句話,甚至在行銷中引用這個迷因。它也成為「顯然有市場」的簡稱。
  • 現況:已過巔峰,現在多半帶著懷舊感出現在回覆裡,但仍被廣泛理解,尤其在科技與遊戲社群。
  • 備註:一般常識;確切日期與傳播過程今日未以來源查證。

7) 電影 / 表演藝術介紹 — 芭蕾舞劇《吉賽爾》(1841)

  • 名稱:《吉賽爾,或維莉絲》(Giselle, ou les Wilis),兩幕浪漫芭蕾,是古典劇目的基石之一。
  • 創作者 / 關鍵人物:音樂為阿道夫·亞當(Adolphe Adam);劇本由泰奧菲爾·戈蒂耶(Théophile Gautier)與朱爾-亨利·韋爾努瓦·德聖喬治(Vernoy de Saint-Georges)撰寫;編舞掛名讓·科拉利(Jean Coralli),儒勒·佩羅(Jules Perrot,首位吉賽爾卡洛塔·格里西的伴侶)有重大貢獻。馬里烏斯·佩提帕(Marius Petipa)後來為聖彼得堡帝國芭蕾修訂,現代多數製作源自他的版本。
  • 時代背景:1841 年 6 月 28 日在巴黎歌劇院首演,正值重視超自然、田園與悲劇女主角的浪漫主義時期。戈蒂耶取材於海涅對「維莉絲」(被拋棄新娘的亡魂)的描述,以及雨果的一首詩。
  • 劇情:第一幕(萊茵河畔村莊):體弱的農家少女吉賽爾愛跳舞,愛上了「洛伊斯」,但他其實是已有婚約的貴族阿爾伯特。獵場看守人伊拉里翁揭穿了謊言;吉賽爾精神崩潰,心碎而死。第二幕(鬧鬼的森林):維莉絲在女王米爾塔率領下,逼男人跳舞至死。伊拉里翁死去,吉賽爾寬恕的愛保護阿爾伯特直到天亮,她才回到墓中。
  • 藝術風格與技巧:世俗的第一幕(民俗風舞蹈、著名的瘋狂場景)與幽靈般的第二幕(白色長「浪漫」紗裙、讓舞者顯得毫無重量的足尖舞、吊鋼絲、對稱的群舞)形成對比。亞當的樂曲為角色使用反覆出現的動機,是芭蕾中早期的主導動機運用。
  • 文化與歷史意義:它定義了浪漫芭蕾女伶與「白色芭蕾」(ballet blanc);主角對全球舞者而言,既考驗技巧也考驗演技。它的主題(愛、背叛、寬恕)至今仍引起共鳴。
  • 影響與傳承:其結構影響了後來的《舞姬》與《天鵝湖》。著名的吉賽爾包括安娜·巴甫洛娃、加琳娜·烏蘭諾娃、瑪歌·芳婷;卡洛斯·阿科斯塔演過阿爾伯特;馬茨·艾克(1982)與阿克拉姆·汗(英國國家芭蕾舞團,2016)做過大膽的重新詮釋。
  • 現況 / 哪裡可以欣賞:巴黎歌劇院芭蕾舞團、英國皇家芭蕾、美國芭蕾舞劇院與大劇院等團體定期演出,也有電影院轉播與影像紀錄。今日未查證最新檔期。
  • 備註:一般常識,今日未以來源查證。

8) 看片方法 — 第 87 課:有動機的打光與實景光源(Practicals)

這是什麼:當銀幕上的光看起來來自場景中真實存在(或可能存在)的光源,例如窗、燈、火、蠟燭、電視,這種打光就叫「有動機的」(motivated)。畫面中看得見的光源稱為「實景光源」(practicals)。攝影師會用畫面外的燈光來塑形與補強,讓影像感覺自然。相反的做法是無動機或風格化的打光,會讓人感覺到它的存在(例如沒有藍色光源的公寓裡出現硬藍色輪廓光)。兩者沒有誰比較好;問題在於電影要讓世界感覺像「被發現的」還是「被建構的」。

怎麼分析:(1) 看任何打光的鏡頭,先問:光從哪裡來?我能在畫面裡指出光源嗎?(2) 記下光的質感:硬或軟、暖或冷、強或弱、方向(正面、側面、背光)。(3) 檢查是實景光源真的在發揮作用,還是額外的燈在假裝。(4) 看光源暗示什麼:一支蠟燭會讓臉孔孤立並讓空間變暗,窗戶指出時間,電視閃爍帶出情緒。(5) 注意何時放棄動機,這通常表示電影從寫實轉向角色的內在感受。

實例 —《亂世兒女》(Barry Lyndon,1975,導演史丹利·庫柏力克,攝影約翰·艾考特),燭光牌桌戲:庫柏力克希望室內場景看起來就像十八世紀,只靠蠟燭照明。劇組使用極大光圈的鏡頭(原為 NASA 製造的 Zeiss Planar 50mm f/0.7 鏡頭),讓真正的燭火成為主要光源。結果是賭客臉孔周圍一圈柔和、溫暖、低對比的光,房間其餘部分則沉入暗金色的陰影。在巴瑞初遇萊登夫人的牌桌戲中,光暈把我們的注意力集中在桌子兩邊的視線交會上。那樣的光圈下景深很淺,焦點只落在一個狹窄的平面。請注意,這種動機也標示了時代:光看起來像一幅時代畫作,而日光外景則呼應庚斯博羅與康斯塔伯的風景畫。這個方法顯示打光的選擇可以不靠一句對白就傳達歷史氣氛與情緒。

練習:下次看電影時,挑一場室內戲,找出光源。它在畫面裡嗎?接著把聲音關掉一分鐘,問自己:光本身說了時間、地點與氣氛的什麼?情緒改變時,打光是否也跟著改變?
